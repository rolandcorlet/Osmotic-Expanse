The Atlas tells you what each lineage *is* (Atlas §2). This chapter tells you how they **regard each other**, what **pressures** their bodies put on their lives, and where they **live** in a colony. None of it is fair. All of it is common knowledge.

## 8.2 Lineage pressures

Every lineage's Frailty (Atlas §2) is a social fact as well as a physical one. This is what each body does to a career.

# 9 · THE UPHILL ECONOMY

Goods in the Expanse behave like molecules. **Downhill** — from where a thing is plentiful to where it is scarce — they drift on their own, slowly, for free. **Uphill** — against the concentration — they must be *carried*, and carrying costs energy. The whole economy is the fight over who pays for uphill.

## 9.7 The Hanging Market of Nine Tolls

*The Codex's market strung between four cables in the Mid-Strata, where everything is priced in Cohesion, Oaths and Specimens as well as Substrate (Codex §6.3).* It is the showcase for this chapter:

* **Four currencies at once.** Every stall posts four prices: Substrate, a Specimen grade, an Oath box, or Cohesion. A Cohort may pay any column. *(The Ledger, Ch. 6, records Oath and Cohesion payments.)*
* **Nine tolls.** Each cable-stair charges a toll in a different currency; a Cohort that pays all nine in coin is *Dry* there for a Beat, whatever it is elsewhere.
* **Debts for sale.** A Relic-bearer is selling a Gene-Debt they cannot pay (Codex §6.3) — and the Market's Convocations (Ch. 4) are the most dangerous rooms in the Mid-Strata.

# 10 · CARCASS LAW

Precept 4 says **tonicity is truth**: you may not lie about what you are, and in the Expanse a body can be *read*. Justice follows. An accused organism is not merely questioned; it is **assayed** — tested, sampled, read by the Lysosome — and the colony decides what the reading means. The law is called *carcass law* because, in the end, it is about **what is owed**, and the Expanse settles debts in bodies.

## 10.2 The trial

A trial is a **Convocation** (Ch. 4) whose Motion is *what is owed*, and whose verdict the colony confirms by **Quorum**.

* **The room.** Accuser, accused, the Reader (or the colony's assayer), a Quorum Speaker, and up to two interested parties — a faction, a budline, a creditor.
* **The oath.** Each party stands and states lineage, tonic and Oaths (Precept 4). An organism that refuses starts one rung down with the room.
* **The Assay.** In the second round, the Reader assays the accused's body: RTC + Sequence Reading vs **AT 12** (**AT 16** for an Elite or a Lysosan). A success is a **Reveal** of the truth (Ch. 4) — the Director knows what it shows. The accused may resist (§10.3).
* **Argument** is open to both sides **on its printed terms and no others**: a sequence altered without consent; AFF vs the violator's RTC, AT 16; once per violator (Codex §8.2, §8.3).
* **Memory Vials** and live extraction carry their printed costs: AT 16, 2 Stress on every participant, and Oath boxes limit what can be pulled (Core §11.7).
* **The verdict.** When the Table Clock fills, the leading side's Motion goes to the colony as an **Issue** (Ch. 4) starting at the side's rung count. The colony *knows* the verdict when it reaches Quorum. A hung Issue means *Clear but watched*: −1 die on all rolls at the Membrane until the next Rereading.

# 11 · THE SCENT-WEB

*"Chemotaxis is the Expanse's internet"* (Core §8.6). News is a molecule; to know something is to have smelled it; and a message is a thing someone carried.

## 11.1 Four networks

| Network | Run by | Speed | Can it be censored? | Price |
| :-- | :-- | :-- | :-- | :-- |
| **Currentpost** | the Flow Guilds | one region per Beat, on charted currents | yes — the Guilds price and block it | 10–50 S |
| **Bridge post** | the Hegemony, on the Ribbon Bridges (Codex §6.5) | reliable and registered — and **read** | routinely | 10–30 S, stamped |
| **Lipidpost** | the Reclaimers' Ash, by corpse-wagon | one region per Tide | **no** — censoring a funeral breaks Precept 2 | 5–20 S and a Specimen |
| **the Fade** | the Sequence Underground | hours, by decaying molecules; deniable | no — but every relay knows | a favour, always |

## 11.2 Rumour grades

Every rumour has a grade. A rumour's grade is how hard it is to *disprove*, on the AT ladder (Core §1.2).

| Grade | Lives for | AT to disprove |
| :-- | :-- | --: |
| **Fresh** | a Beat | 8 |
| **Repeated** | a Drift Season | 12 |
| **Settled** | until the next Great Cycle | 16 |
| **Law** | until the Rereading | **cannot be argued** — only erased: an archive entry destroyed, or a register line forged (Ch. 10), plus a segment on someone's Clock |

**Buying a rumour** uses the Core's rule: name what you want to know, pay 5–50 S in fresh samples, roll AFF + Signal Discourse at AT 10–20 (Core §8.6). **Forging one** is a Deceptive act (Core §11.1) *and* a Clock segment. A Settled rumour that turns out to be forged advances the Colony Clock: a colony that acted on a lie still acted.

## 11.3 A scent-perch

A Cohort can own a listening post: a **Scent-perch** — 250 S, 5 S a Beat upkeep, established as a colony asset (1 Tide, AT 16, Codex §5.7). It gives **one rumour per Drift Season** off the Codex's rumour table (Codex §11.1, truth rolled by the Director as usual), one extra Core §8.6 query per Beat, and a chance to **notice a planted scent** (OSM + Chemoreception vs the forger's total).

## 11.4 Signal relays

A message carried hand to hand (Ch. 7, *Signal Relay*) moves a region in hours, and every relay organism knows it. Each relay is a chance to leak: the Director rolls a d6 per hop, and on a 1 the message reaches someone it shouldn't.

## 12.4 The duel that replaces a war

The books print the pieces in five places: two organisms **duelling by Oath, formally, in the open** — *"killing either is a crime; interrupting is a scandal; watching is a legal act"* (Codex §5.5); an **arena** whose walls are registered (Codex §9.2); a Vector to *"win an Oath duel as a champion for someone who cannot fight"* (Codex §11.4); the Osmotic Brutes, who *"will often accept a challenge, a wager, or a formal Oath-duel instead of a fight"* (Bestiary, Order V); and the Hypertonic Zenith, when *"duels are shorter and meaner"* (Codex §5.3). This is the page that joins them.

1. **Name the Motion** the duel decides — a debt, a water right, a grazing claim, an insult. Both sides swear to it as an Oath (Core §11.4).
2. **Champions.** Each side names one. A champion may fight *for* another party, spending that party's Standing, not their own.
3. **Seconds.** Each side may name one second, who can **Reveal** evidence (Ch. 4) once, before blood.
4. **The bout** is a Pulse War between the two champions, in a registered arena or an open circle. It ends at the first **Yield**, Dormancy, or a ring-out.
5. **Stopping.** Either champion may **Yield** — *"a chemical white flag it cannot fake"* (Core §4.14). Whoever kills a yielder marks 2 Stress on every witness and drops Cohesion 1: killing a duellist in front of a crowd is expensive, and that is the world's own argument for stopping. The yielding side concedes the Motion and drops one rung with the champion's faction. *(derived)*
6. **Betting** is in Substrate and in Ledger lines.

A duel settles in one scene what a Mass Conflict (Codex §3.6) would settle in a Tide and a hundred dead. The colonies prefer it, and the Muster's widows prefer it most of all.
