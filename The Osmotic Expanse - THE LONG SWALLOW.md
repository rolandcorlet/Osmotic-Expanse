# THE LONG SWALLOW
### A Vector for four organisms, Levels 7 → 11 — *getting inside a person who is being digested*

**Book:** Director's material. This Vector sits beside *The Bloom of Ash* (CODEX Ch. 12) and uses the same shape: one page of situation, faces, five sites, a visible Clock, an honest ledger.
**Region:** the Lysosomal Wastes — the Vat Road, and then the interior of **the Unread** (CODEX §6.7, §7.4).
**Spine link:** Act II of *The Long Argument* (CODEX §8.5) — the record of the Closing. It also runs perfectly standalone; if you run it first, it will hand you Act II by the end of the last scene.
**Tone:** dry institutional body-horror. The comedy is **legal and procedural**; the horror is **physiological**. Ash paperwork, warm acid.
**Content note:** ingestion, digestion, being held inside a body, the dissolving of people, loss of bodily autonomy. **§2 is a session zero with a hard consent rule; read it before you run this, and put its one-line summary in your invitation.**

> *A Vector is a person with a door in it. Everything in this book exists to answer one question the players will ask in the first ten minutes: **how do we get in?** Let them answer it. The answer is the adventure.*

---

# 0 · THE VECTOR ON ONE PAGE

**The situation.** On the Vat Road, the Reclaimers' Ash have posted a **Dissolution Licence** for **the Unread** — the Barophil Osmotic Brute that has been half-digested in the Wastes for **nine Great Cycles** and has never been allowed to stop (CODEX §6.7: *The Undissolvable*). A Warden register-audit found **an unlisted sequence inside its body**, and Precept 1 says what a body contains that the state cannot name must be named or destroyed (CODEX §8.2). The licence executes in **six Beats**: Vat Crews will open the Unread and dissolve it under legal supervision, and everything still alive inside it goes with it.

**The host is a person, and it asked for you.** The Unread cannot finish one digestion. Nine Great Cycles ago it swallowed something it *refuses* to dissolve — a body whose consent it needs, still thinking, kept in a nodule the Wastes never reached. It has been holding a promise inside itself ever since, and holding it is what keeps it from dying. It wants to stop. It needs someone to go in and **cut the thing out**. Only it remembers the Era of Closing, and it will trade that memory for death.

**The yield.** At the core: **the Receipt** — the chemical record of *who consented* to end the Prime Serum at the Closing (the Act II reveal, CODEX §8.5). In the Unread it is stored **as a person**: the **Undersigned**, still half-digested, still reasoning, still *under oath*. A document you can harvest, or a witness you can carry. Not both.

**Who else wants it.** The **Brine Office** (Hegemony) wants the sequence *printed*, and knows its serial number. The **Warden Circles** want the audit error buried, including whoever made it. The **Deep Choir** wants the Unread *dead* — a body that will not stop is their claim made flesh, and it has gone wrong. The **Sequence Underground** wants the Receipt *destroyed*, because the Undersigned signed for a lineage that never agreed to be signed for. **Foreman Ock's** missing Vat Crew is in there too, and Ock is not filing a rescue; he is filing a *reclaim*.

**The shape of play.** Four movements. **INTAKE** (the Cohort chooses how it gets inside: §4, twelve vectors, each with a landing site, an advantage, and a debt) → **THE FIRST CHAMBER** (the interior is a society, not a corridor) → **THE PROBLEM ROOM** (the nine-Cycle Churn, still trying to finish one job; recognition begins) → **THE COST ROOM AND THE YIELD** (the Shelf of Not-Finished Things, the Nodule, the exit).

**The three Clocks.** **LICENCE** (6 Beats — the unavoidable problem, §3), **IMMUNE** (5 — what the body learns about you; *this is the price of loot*), **CHURN** (4 — peristalsis; it moves the map, and it always moves **deeper**).

**The loop the players will feel.** A nine-Cycle body is the richest harvest in the Expanse. Every cut you take advances the IMMUNE Clock. At 3 the body's Sentinels *Mark* you and its Goodwill toward you drops. At 5 recognition is complete: the Host can no longer choose to open for you, and the way home becomes a Breach. **Taking things closes the door behind you, and the Clock that tells you so sits on the table in plain sight.**

**What this Vector is about.** Being inside a person: an anatomy that tolerates you, a map with feelings, and the fact that every useful thing you do to it *hurts it*.

**Three omens for the table** (read one aloud when the Cohort first sights the Unread; an omen always bodes ill for *someone*, and never for everyone at once):

1. *The Vat Road's notice board lists the Unread's exports for this Tide, item by item. Item fourteen is a cradle.*
2. *The Congregation is on nine. They count flushes as prayer, and a Barophil pilot will not tell you what she is counting to.*
3. *Something has already been cut out of the Host from the inside, and the cut was made by a licensed tool.*

# 1 · THE CORE VISION

## 1.1 The Big Three Questions

| Question | The answer for this Vector |
| :-- | :-- |
| **What experience is this game about?** | *Infiltration where the target is a living person and the walls are its own.* Wonder first, then claustrophobia, then a conversation. The players should feel **tolerated** before they feel hunted, and the tolerance must be renewable, and revocable. |
| **How do the mechanics reinforce that mood?** | (1) **Goodwill** uses the printed Chemotactic rungs (CORE §11.3): the Host is a *mood*, not a wall. (2) **TOLERANCE** replaces Stealth: inside a body you hide by *being chemically correct*, so the infiltration axis is rite, paperwork, sponsorship and forgery — not damage. (3) The **CHURN Clock** gives the dungeon a direction: going deeper is free, coming back costs PP. (4) The Host is blocked with phases but is never an *enemy*; hurting it advances the story, not the initiative. |
| **What reward and punishment loop keeps players touching the rules?** | Harvest inside a nine-Cycle body is the best in the Expanse (Pristine Sequences, Epic material, one campaign-revealing object). **Every harvest advances the IMMUNE Clock**, which spawns recognition, drops Goodwill, and at 5 removes the Host's ability to open an exit. Loot = a closing way home. Players must choose *what to carry before they know the price* — and the price is a visible track they can plan against. Against that, **§5.2's twelve Keys** pay Codon for playing the premise out loud: the Vector rewards the player who makes their own scene harder, and the punishment falls on the organism, never on the player. |

## 1.2 Three pillars (and what they cut)

| Pillar | What it means at the table | What it is allowed to cost |
| :-- | :-- | :-- |
| **1 · INTAKE** | How you get in is chosen by the players, priced in front of them, and decides where Act 2 starts. Prep *is* play. | One full session before any combat. If your table wants to skip it, use the compression in §14. |
| **2 · ANATOMY AS ECOLOGY** | Every scene answers: *what does the body do about you now?* Three visible clocks, pH and current as terrain, recognition as escalation. | You will improvise from tables (§13). Do not pre-write rooms beyond the five compartments. |
| **3 · CONSENT AND EXTRACTION** | The Host has a will; the exit is a relationship, not a route. The Vector ends in a decision about a document that is also a person. | Three endings plus one the table invents (§12). Someone will be angry at you by Session 5. That is the design working. |

**The cut list — things deliberately *not* in this Vector.** No Mass Conflict (CODEX §3.6). No new Lineages, Phenotypes, Orders, Bursts, or Pathways: everything here is a printed thing wearing a new hat. No Wastes region-crawl (the Vat Road plus five compartments, nothing else). No colony, retainer, or vessel management. No Act III membrane cosmology beyond one optional hook (§8.5). **Eight named NPCs, nine new blocks, four movements.** If an idea needs a tenth block, it is a different Vector.

## 1.3 Terminology discipline (read this once, then police it all Vector)

The Expanse's books are strict, and this Vector is stricter, because *inside a body* the wrong word ends a friendship.

| Say this | Not this | Why it matters here |
| :-- | :-- | :-- |
| **the Cohort** (the players' organisms) | "the party", "the characters" | A Cohort is a chemical unit; inside the Host it is *one* scent. |
| **the Host** (the Unread) | "the dungeon", "the monster" | The Host has Goodwill. A dungeon does not. |
| **Intake** | "entry", "infiltration check" | An Intake is a *chosen vector with a printed debt*, not a roll. |
| **Tolerance** | "stealth", "sneak" | You are not unseen; you are *unread*. |
| **the organism suffers / gains** | "the player loses" | The player **chooses a Vector**; the organism **suffers the Debt**. Player decisions are never retroactive; organism consequences always are. |
| **Marked, Jostled, Turgid, Flaccid, Sequestered, Brittle, Locked, Masked, Leaking, Catabolic** | invented conditions | Use CORE §4.7 only. New conditions are a rules tax you do not need. |
| **Dormancy / Total Lysis / Reconstitution / Dormant Cycle / Beat / Fugue Interval** | "unconscious", "death", "rest", "day" | CODEX §5.1 and CORE V. |
| **BSP** (encounter cost) and **Codon** (progression) | "XP" | Two economies, never added (CODEX §4). |

# 2 · SESSION ZERO FOR THIS VECTOR

CODEX §1.3 requires a session zero with four questions. This Vector adds three questions of its own, because it is the one Vector in the line where the *players' bodies* are the plot. Ask them out loud, before dice.

## 2.1 The four standing questions (CODEX §1.3)

1. **What bodies are on the table?** Consent around forced grafting, parasitism, reproductive content, ingestion of people, Total Lysis of named NPCs, identity loss.
2. **How graphic is a death?** Clinical (*your contents join the world*) or explicit. Pick a register and stay there.
3. **What is the tone dial?** This Vector is written for **dry institutional body-horror with a legal comic register**. If your table wants straight survival horror, cut the Form 11-B Intake and the toll-booth; if it wants absurdity, keep them and play the Wardens deadpan.
4. **Who can pause, and how?** One word. The fiction rewinds one sentence and the scene restarts adjusted. No explanation, ever.

## 2.2 Three extra questions, specific to being inside someone

| Ask | Because |
| :-- | :-- |
| **Is "being swallowed / absorbed / held inside" on the table for everyone — and is it *chosen*, or never used?** | This is the Vector's premise. If any player lines it out, the Intake is still available: they run the **Honest Road** or the **Storage Token** and are *met at the door* rather than passing through one. Nothing breaks. |
| **Do we want entrapment and "buried alive" beats, and at what intensity?** | The Churn pushes. The Licence Clock closes. Some tables find that thrilling and some find it unbearable; the dial is *how often you name the countdown aloud*. Naming it more is the *kinder* setting, not the harsher one. |
| **How much of the "foreign body / immune response" language do we want, given what it echoes?** | Sentinels *Mark* you as not-self; the state decides which bodies are lawful inside other bodies. That is a loaded metaphor and this Vector will not pretend otherwise. Play it as **bureaucracy** (the books' own reading: CODEX §7.2's red line — registered bodies have rights) and never as a punchline about people. |

## 2.3 The Consent Rule (this Vector's only hard rule)

> **An Intake is never sprung. An Intake is always chosen.**

The Director never imposes a swallowing, a dissolution, an implantation, or an involuntary harvest. Every one of those beats is on the Intake table (§4) or the Immune table (§13.4) **and the players must pick it, accept it, or line it out.** If a player declines a Vector, it does not exist for them, and no roll, penalty, or "inevitable" consequence replaces it — the Cohort simply takes another road and the Clock runs one Beat shorter.

This is not a softening of the Vector. It is the Vector's thesis: **a membrane decides what gets in.** The game already does involuntary body-horror mechanically (Parasitic grafts, Hijacked, Latency, Chimeric Growth — CODEX §1.3). Make those beats *chosen* and they become the best scenes of your campaign.

# 3 · THE UNAVOIDABLE PROBLEM — THE LICENCE CLOCK

The opening scene is not a hook. It is a **notice**, read aloud on the Vat Road at the start of every Beat, by a Licence-Reader with a shell trumpet:

> *"By licence of the Warden Circles and the Reclaimers' Ash, counter-signed nine Great Cycles past, the body known as the Unread is to be opened and dissolved for the removal of one unlisted sequence. Six Beats. All claims to be filed before the third cut. Nothing unwasted."*

**LICENCE CLOCK — 6 segments.** Advance one per **Beat** spent anywhere in the Vector (a Beat is the Wastes' working day; CODEX §5.1). Advance it *in front of the players* and name it, every time, even when they are deep inside: the sound carries through the Host's tissue.

| Segment | What changes in the fiction |
| --: | :-- |
| 1–2 | Crews mustering. The outer price of everything rises; the Choir begins a public vigil at the Weep. |
| 3 | The **first cut** is made: the Weep (compartment 1) gains an enzyme wash — 3 Lytic per Cycle until the Cohort is past it. Goodwill −1 rung if the Cohort did nothing to stop it. |
| 4 | The Congregation's chapel floods with Neutraliser runoff; the ferry stops running. Your sponsor is now *inside with you*, which is either a gift or a catastrophe. |
| 5 | The **Churn is decoupled**: peristalsis goes from schedule to flood — CHURN advances every Cycle, not every Fugue Interval, and the Churn Clock's fill throws **2 bands** instead of 1. |
| 6 | Dissolution. The Vector's Act 4 becomes an escape (CODEX IV chase subsystem) with the Receipt in someone's hands, and everything the Host was holding dies. See §12, ending **LET IT BE FINISHED**. |

**Three ways to slow it** (the clock is playable, not a rail; the players only need to find one, and each is findable by a different kind of organism):

1. **Buy the third cut.** The licence's fee is 600 S to the Ash and 200 S to the Warden household that issued it, and the fee can be **held in dispute** by any licensed party. Foreman Ock's crew will do this for 3 Beats of foundry labour or a Pristine Sequence (§9, E4). *Slows 2 segments.*
2. **Attack the counter-signature.** The licence is nine Great Cycles old and its counter-signature belongs to a clerk who is now **Licence-Reader Od**, who posted it. A forged or withdrawn counter-signature does not void the licence: it forces a **Warden audit**, which takes Beats (§10, Od's block; Tolerance/Argument contest at AT 16). *Slows 3 segments and advances ORDER 1.*
3. **Invoke Precept 6.** If the Cohort can *name a body inside the Unread that the state intends to dissolve without consent*, any organism may invoke **Argument** (CODEX §8.2: free contested AFF vs RTC at AT 16). The Undersigned is exactly that body. Win, and the licence is stayed until the argument is heard — *slows the clock to a stop, and starts the biggest problem in the Vector*, because now the Closing's consent is a public case (§12, ending **CARRY IT OUT**).

**Backup solutions, because the players will miss the primary one:** the notice is read aloud; the Congregation will explain it for free; the Unread itself says *six* (in a long pause, with enormous emphasis) the first time anyone speaks to it. Three channels. Move on once they have any one.

# 4 · HOW YOU GET IN — THE INTAKE

A body is a thing that decides what gets in and what stays out. So **the players make that decision first, in the fiction, with prices visible.** This is the whole first session, and it is where the Vector's agency lives.

## 4.1 The rules of an Intake

1. **Every Intake prints four things:** how it works, what it costs, **where the Cohort surfaces inside**, and its **Advantage** and **Debt**.
2. **Choose one Intake as a Cohort**, or split the Cohort across two (some tables love being split; it costs the CHURN Clock one segment and one PC's Bulk, and it is very fun).
3. **Advantages are real and Debts are promises.** The Director spends a Cohort's Debt **once per movement**, always in the exact shape printed. Never invent a worse one.
4. **Roll to prepare, not to enter.** A failed Intake *prep* roll does not stop the Intake — it applies CODEX §2.3's *Cost*: it works, and something is paid (ATP, Stress, Substrate, noise, one ORDER or IMMUNE segment, a faction rung). Being inside is the premise; it is never gated on one roll.
5. **The Rule of the Twelve.** If the Cohort invents a thirteenth way in, **it works**, and you price it out loud in thirty seconds using three questions: *What does it cost (S / ATP / a Clock / a faction rung)? Where do they surface? What Advantage and what Debt?* Write both on the card. A Cohort idea that beats these twelve is a better idea than mine; print it and move.

## 4.2 THE INTAKE (d12, or choose)

**1 · THE HONEST ROAD (Consent).** A Choir-accompanied rite at the Weep. You ask. The Unread opens a door it has not opened in nine Great Cycles, because asking is the one thing nothing has done in nine Great Cycles.
*Cost:* one name to carry for the Choir (say what it is: it may be a *rule* — CODEX §7.6) + a rite, 40 S. *Surfaces:* the Chapel, together, at full Bulk. *Advantage:* **Goodwill starts at Client**, and every Tolerance contest in the Vector is at **+1 die**. You may leave at any time until IMMUNE 5. *Debt:* mid-Vector the Host asks one favour, and the favour is owed (1 Oath box). What it asks is always the same thing, and it is §11's problem.

**2 · FORM 11-B — SYMBIOSIS PERMIT (the bureaucratic horror).** The Warden Circles will, for a fee, register you as **lawful organs of the Unread**. The immune system reads you as *self* because the state said so, which is the most Expanse answer possible.
*Cost:* 50 S, three forms, one witness with OSM 3+, and one PC must be named the Host's **Excretory Officer** on the record. *Surfaces:* the Churn, sealed in legal gel, on a schedule. *Advantage:* Gastric Sentinels **never initiate**; they read the state's chemistry, and a Sentinel that will not challenge you is a corridor you can walk. *Debt:* when the Excretory Officer is present, the Host may order a flush and that PC must obey: **CHURN advances 1 for free** once per movement. And at the exit you must file a **Return of Tissue**; leave with unlisted harvest and you are legally *stolen goods* (ORDER 2, and the Wardens of Arch Four hunt you for the Tide).

**3 · THE FREIGHT ROUTE (sell yourselves as biomass).** An Ash corpse-broker itemises the Cohort at **12 S each**, bags you as consignment, and dumps the consignment into the intake channel. The broker will not issue a refund while you are alive, and the Wastes pay less for live Cohorts than dead ones, which everyone has already noticed.
*Cost:* 0 S — you are paid 12 S each, and will spend it. *Surfaces:* the Churn, **loose**, in the wrong end of the compartment, and your Bulk is impounded in a numbered sac in the Chapel (fetch, with its own 3-segment Clock). *Advantage:* cargo is not suspected: the **first** in-host social contact for every faction begins one Chemotactic rung better than it otherwise would. *Debt:* your numbered sac is claimed by the Congregation as an *unwasted* gift, and taking it back is a theft from pilgrims.

**4 · ARRESTED, LAWFULLY (the Disposal Ledger).** Get read as unlisted contraband at an arch, let a **Phagocyte Marshal** *Detain* you (BESTIARY Order VI), refuse bail, and be legally disposed of **inside** the Unread — the Wastes' contract for state property the state does not want dissolved anywhere else. The fastest way in is to be a criminal, and the state will carry you there itself.
*Cost:* a deliberate crime worth an arrest, and two Beats. *Surfaces:* the Churn, **Sequestered** in lawful gel; allies cut out at AT 14. *Advantage:* every Sentinel in the Host will pause **one Cycle per PC** to re-read your warrant — three free Cycles of conversation with the only people in the Expanse who will talk to you while holding you. *Debt:* the Cohort begins with **ORDER 1**, and Goodwill cannot rise above **Curious** while any PC is still the state's tissue (the Host does not make deals with inventory).

**5 · THE WEDDING (an Oath of Intake).** Encode an Oath to the Host (CORE §11.4: a promise folded into your own chromatin): *"I will be inside you and finish what you cannot."* The membrane opens for the sworn, because an Oath is chemistry the Host can read.
*Cost:* **1 Oath box each**, witnessed (Chemotactic Rite, 10 S). *Surfaces:* anywhere in the Host the Oath names. *Advantage:* a **Bond with the Host** (CORE §11.5): shared sense, metabolic gift (5 ATP/5 MI at 1 PP), **Reserve relay** — you may spend your Reserve to defend an organ of the Host, which is how you save a compartment you have already ruined. *Debt:* the Host may invoke the Oath **once**, and it will invoke it at the worst possible moment for whoever made it. It never asks for violence. That is what makes it frightening.

**6 · THE WEEP (the nine-Cycle fistula).** The Unread has a wound that has never closed. It is now a neighbourhood: a hermit, domesticated Haunters, an abandoned toll-booth still issuing fines, and a water market (§13.3). Free, awful, and full of information.
*Cost:* a Beat, the walk, and E1 (§9). *Surfaces:* the Weep → Chapel, together, at full Bulk. *Advantage:* you arrive knowing the Host's **outflow**: a Chemoreception contest at AT 12 tells you what is moving inside and where it is going, which means the CHURN Clock is public information to you for the whole Vector — you may spend 1 PP to *predict* the next flush out loud and gain +1 die against it. *Debt:* the Weep's neighbours saw you arrive, and either charge you later or tell the first Sentinel they meet.

**7 · THE TROPHIC LIE (be eaten).** Hide inside something the Host is currently eating: a corpse-husk on the Vat Road, a Drifter shoal, a Yolk Bloom. Ride the bolus through the intake and be *filed* like food.
*Cost:* an Amoeban **Reshape** (change apparent size class by one, ATLAS) or the BESTIARY **HOST / VESSEL** template installed in a carcass; Lytic 4 per Cycle while in the bolus, and it is the worst part of it. *Surfaces:* the Churn, **unregistered**: the immune system has no file on you at all. *Advantage:* **IMMUNE cannot advance during Movement 1** — provided you travel light: Bulk ≤ 4. *Debt:* Bulk ≤ 4 is the price. Your gear stays outside with someone who will not hold it for free. And if a Sentinel ever reads you carrying an Apparatus, IMMUNE advances **2 at once**, loudly, in front of everyone.

**8 · THE STORAGE TOKEN (forge a bank receipt).** The Unread is a nine-Cycle **bank**: the Warden Circles have stored things inside it, because a body that cannot be opened is the safest vault in the Wastes (precedent: the Vacuole Behemoth's *Storage Law*, BESTIARY Order V). The Underground sells a forged storage token for 200 S (CODEX §7.5). You are *inventory*, and inventory walks in the side door.
*Cost:* 200 S + a forgery/Concealment contest at AT 14 + one Warden audit check per movement. *Surfaces:* the Shelf of Not-Finished Things, directly — you are filed where your paperwork says. *Advantage:* Sentinels ignore you, the Congregation *respects* you, and you may formally **request the Nodule from the Host's own storage system** — which is, if you have the nerve, an entire solution to Act 4. *Debt:* the token is forged onto a **real account** (Brine Office, Shift Nine). Someone notices the withdrawal mid-Vector and comes to collect what they believe they own.

**9 · THE CHAPEL FERRY (pilgrimage).** The **Undissolvable Congregation** (CODEX §7.4) has been letting the Wastes digest them *slowly and deliberately* for Beats beyond counting. They run a ferry and chapel network inside the Unread, and their chemistry is a passport: the body has been tolerating them for years.
*Cost:* a rite, and **one name to carry into the Nodule and say aloud**. *Surfaces:* the Chapel, together. *Advantage:* **sponsored** — Tolerance contests at AT 10 instead of AT 14, and the Congregation will hide the Cohort once, inside themselves, which is a scene nobody forgets. *Debt:* the name must be spoken **at the Nodule, in front of the Undersigned**. Choir truths are true and every one costs something (CODEX §7.6): if it is a rule, saying it advances **CORE 1** and one region of the Wastes changes its physics for the rest of the campaign.

**10 · THE GENE-DEBT DOOR (something inside wants to come home).** The Underground lends you a Relic fragment whose Gene-Debt *pulls* toward the half of itself the Unread swallowed nine Great Cycles ago (CORE §9.7; CODEX §10.5). You do not go in. The Host opens for the Relic, and you go through the opening.
*Cost:* a **4-segment Gene-Debt Clock** from the first hour, and when it fills the collector (a Choir rite-official) knows exactly where you are — including, on the worst roll, *inside*. *Advantage:* the Host's tissue is **friendly**: Sentinels read you as kin of a stored object, and the Relic itself is a real tool (one Rule Break, once, at a price). *Debt:* the Debt is the Vector's mid-point reversal: the collector arrives *inside the body*, in the only compartment where nobody can leave.

**11 · THE BREACHER'S HATCH (the uncreative answer, priced honestly).** A Membrane Breacher charge, 40 S of acid, three Beats of noise, and a hole in a person who was not asked.
*Cost:* the Host feels all of it. *Surfaces:* the compartment of the Cohort's choosing, braced, armed, at full Bulk. *Advantage:* you choose *where*, and no Debt arrives in Movement 1. *Debt:* **Goodwill starts at Wary**, **IMMUNE starts at 2**, **LICENCE advances 1** (the Ash are lawyers and damage to a licensed body is a lawsuit), and the wound will not close: CHURN advances 1 per Fugue Interval, plus **+1 Gastric Sentinel spawn per movement**. Sometimes the right answer is a hole. It is never free.

**12 · THE INVERSION (make it come to you).** Do not go in. **The Churn exports continuously** — nine Great Cycles of not-finished material leaves the Unread through the drainage of the Vat Road, and the Wastes have built a whole industry on catching it. Rig a catch (Synthesis AT 14, 30 S), read the Churn schedule, and let a body post you what you want.
*Cost:* **three Beats** (the LICENCE Clock runs while you build), and one thing the catch cannot do alone: for the Nodule to be *exported*, something inside must **file it for export**. That means a Congregation pilot, Miro, or the Undersigned itself has to do it — and one of them will not survive doing it. *Advantage:* no IMMUNE Clock, no Goodwill track, full Bulk, and you meet the Receipt in open water, with leverage. *Debt:* you have handed custody of the Closing's consent to a faction. It will arrive with an invoice, and the invoice is the ending.

> **If your Cohort is clever, they will pick 12, or invent 13.** Good. The Vector is a *situation*, not a funnel: the Host has nine openings, four of them legal, and the Director's job is to price them honestly and be delighted.

# 5 · THE INTAKE WEB AND THE KEYS

## 5.1 THE INTAKE WEB — why they are a Cohort by Cycle one

Twenty minutes, before dice, after the Intake is chosen. Each PC answers; every answer is one line and one mechanic. This is the built-in reason they travel together and it uses the printed **Bond** (CORE §11.5) rather than a new subsystem.

| # | Question | Mechanic it buys |
| :-- | :-- | :-- |
| 1 | **Who told you the Unread had asked for *you*, and what did they take for the message?** | Each PC names one faction contact → the Cohort starts at **Neutral** with that faction and **Wary** with the one the contact hates (CORE §8.7). |
| 2 | **Which companion is carrying something for the Host — a name, a vial, an unspent Oath box — and how do you know about it?** | Declare one **Bond** (CORE §11.5, 1 Oath box each). Two Bonds minimum across the Cohort, so nobody is alone when the Churn moves. |
| 3 | **What have you already done to this body?** *You cut a piece of it. You sold a piece of it. You healed a piece of it. You registered it.* | The Cohort's starting **Goodwill** rung, one rung better than the Intake alone grants (never above **Client** before the Intake's own advantage applies). |
| 4 | **Whose debt will the Ash call in if you are late?** (Each PC names another PC's.) | A **Debt line** per PC: the Director may convert one into a *Cost* on a failed roll instead of a plain failure (CODEX §2.3). |
| 5 | **What is the one thing inside that body you want for yourself, and have not said out loud?** | Write it on a card. §13.2's d8 result 8 makes one of these **literal**, in the compartment where the Cohort is least prepared for it. |
| 6 | **If the Host changes its mind, what do you do?** *PERSUADE / CUT / APPEAL TO LAW / LEAVE SOMEONE BEHIND.* | Your **Compass**. When Act 4 turns (and it turns), every PC's first instinct must be their Compass. Play it straight and you get +1 Cohesion for the scene (CORE §11.4, keeping something costly). |

**Worked example (the Atlas voice, so you can hear it at the table).** *Pell (Barophil Tank) answers 3: "I registered it. Six Beats ago, as a clerical favour, and the audit came back wrong because of my sample." Pell's Goodwill contribution is one rung better — the Host remembers being *named* wrongly by a friend. Torr (Flagellan Current Rider) answers 5: "the Peristaltic Anchor, and I'm not telling anyone why." Vane (Lysosan Replicator Agent) answers 6: "APPEAL TO LAW," which the Director will pay off in Movement 4 when the licence, not the acid, saves them. And when the Churn throws Pell into the enzyme wash at Cycle 3, Torr's Bond Reserve relay is the only thing that keeps a Barophil from Leaking in a body that was never going to digest them anyway.*

## 5.2 KEYS OF THE SWALLOW (Codon for making your own scene worse)

Twelve player-facing hooks, printed in the module rather than invented at the table. **A Key is stated by the player, in the fiction, and it pays when it costs them.** This is the Vector's answer to "why would my character do the difficult thing?" — the answer is that the *player* is being paid, honestly, in the game's own currency. (The shape is borrowed from a free module everyone should have read; see §15.)

**How to run them.** Each Organism picks **three** Keys at the start of the Vector (one from their Intake, one from the Intake Web, one free). Say the Key aloud the moment you honour it; the Director pays Codon **immediately**, in front of everyone, and marks it off. If the Key got them into genuine danger, pay double. A **buyoff** ends the Key permanently and pays once, big. Twelve Keys is a lot of Codon: expect **+100 to +200 per organism** across the Vector, and let that be the reason the Level range is 7 → 11.

| Key | Codon | Buyoff (ends the Key) |
| :-- | --: | :-- |
| **I did not want to be let in.** Refuse the easy door: take the route the body would genuinely reject. | 20 / 40 | 80 — you find a lawful way in nobody has tried, and the Wastes hear about it |
| **I answer when the body asks me what I am.** Precept 4 out loud, at cost, even to a Sentinel. | 20 / 40 | 80 — you are registered inside a Host; the record exists and cannot be unwritten |
| **I carry a name that is not mine.** Speak it where it helps someone else and hurts you. | 20 / 40 | 80 — you give the name away and lose the leverage it bought |
| **I cut only what is already dead.** Precept 2 in practice: leave a body whole, in the richest harvest site in the Expanse. | 20 / 40 | 100 — you harvest something you told the table you would not |
| **I keep an Oath the Host gave me, when keeping it costs ATP, Substrate, or standing.** | 20 / 40 | 80 — break it in front of a faction, and take the rung drop as written (CORE §11.4) |
| **I ask the smallest thing inside before I hurt the largest.** Get permission from a person-sized victim first. | 20 / 40 | 80 — you decide the crowd inside does not get a vote |
| **I am the one who counts the Beat.** Name the LICENCE Clock out loud when nobody wants to hear it. | 20 / 40 | 60 — you stop counting, and let someone else own the panic |
| **I pay for a way out with something I came here to take.** Sell, spend, or hand over yield to buy time or passage. | 20 / 40 | 100 — you keep the yield and pay in a faction rung instead |
| **I believe the Host is a person, and I say so in front of someone who can act on it.** | 20 / 40 | 100 — you sign a paper that calls it tissue, and take the money |
| **I finish the job of whoever went in before me.** Honour a corpse's filing, claim, or unpaid debt in the Shelf. | 20 / 40 | 80 — you loot the previous Cohort's last works |
| **I will not be the one who leaves.** Volunteer for the flush, the hold, the rearguard, the seal-down. | 20 / 40 | 80 — you take the exit when it opens, and someone else pays |
| **I want to see what the Closing actually was.** Chase the Receipt's truth even when a faction pays not to. | 20 / 40 | 120 — you destroy, sell, or surrender the record, and the Core Clock moves |

**Director's discipline:** pay instantly, never negotiate a Key down, and never assign one to a player. The whole engine works only because the players own it.

# 6 · THE BODY — RULES OF THE INTERIOR

Four module-local rules. All of them are printed tracks or re-pointed printed rules; none of them is a new subsystem.

## 6.1 GOODWILL (the Host's mood toward the Cohort)

Use the **Chemotactic Table** (CORE §11.3) as a five-rung track: **Hostile / Wary / Curious / Client / Kin**. Read it aloud every time it moves. The Unread is a Barophil: it does not gesture, it **emits**, it pauses long and puts enormous weight on small words (CODEX §7.7).

| Move Goodwill up | Move it down |
| :-- | :-- |
| a Tolerance contest won cleanly (+1) | Striking Host tissue, any type (+1 rung down, once per scene) |
| a **Vent** or rite performed *with* the Host's Congregation (+1) | harvesting anything without asking (−1 rung **and** IMMUNE 1) |
| healing, Mend, clearing an enzyme wash the Host cannot clear (+1) | Lytic damage anywhere in a compartment; leaving a body unharvested (Precept 2!) |
| carrying a name it asked for; saying it (+1) | Breaching a wall (−1), killing a Congregation pilot (−2) |
| a PC *keeping* an Oath when keeping it costs them (CORE §11.4) | being *lawful at* the Host: serving a Warden warrant while inside it (−1) |

**What Goodwill is for.** Only **Client or better** lets the Host open a door you ask for. Only **Kin** lets the Host move you anywhere inside for free, once per movement, and to *hold a compartment still* for you. At **Hostile** the Host stops being a place: every CHURN fill throws 2 bands, Tolerance ATs become AT 18, and the exit is §6.4's Breach. **The Host never attacks the Cohort on its own initiative unless the Cohort has made it Hostile — and if it does, print the weakness first (CODEX §4.4).**

Treat Goodwill as the Expanse's version of an old OSR habit, the **reaction roll**, with modifiers unique to this monster: casting a Burst inside a body is −2, drawing a weapon is −3, warmth and politeness are +1, a valid token is +2. The point of that older rule is exactly the mood this Vector wants: **the body does not hate you. It is indifferent, and small signals decide whether it clears you or files you.**

## 6.2 THE IMMUNE CLOCK (5) — the price of taking things

Advance **only** for things the body can notice: a Strike against Host tissue, Lytic damage, a harvest cut, a Burst at Tier II or above, a Breach, a corpse left dissolving unharvested, or an unlicensed Total Dissolution (Precept 2 — the Wastes' favourite crime). **Never advance it for a failed roll** (CODEX §5.2 rule 3: advance it because they succeeded at something loud).

| Segment | Recognition stage | What it costs |
| --: | :-- | :-- |
| 1 | *scented* | nothing yet. Say it out loud: "something has read you." |
| 2 | *filed* | first **challenge**: a Gastric Sentinel issues a Tolerance contest (AT 14; AT 10 if sponsored, AT 18 if Goodwill is Wary). |
| 3 | ***Marked*** | one random PC is **Marked** (CORE §4.7: +2 Reagents against them; no Masked). **Goodwill −1 rung.** Sentinels spawn +1 per encounter. |
| 4 | *cascade* | **Antigen Gnats** in every register villus; any challenge you lose now Marks another PC. The Congregation's sponsorship costs double, and one pilot will not sell you anything ever again. |
| 5 | **COMPLETE** | **Ninth Response** arrives (§10) and the Host **can no longer open an exit for you** — Goodwill is capped at Curious for the rest of the Vector. The way out is §6.4's Breach. Say this exactly as written, at the table, the moment it happens. |

## 6.3 THE CHURN CLOCK (4) — the map has a direction

Peristalsis. Every compartment has a **flow direction** and a schedule.

- On **fill**: everything in the compartment is pushed **1 band** toward the next compartment and takes **6 Osmotic**; anything hitting a wall takes +4 Kinetic (CORE §4.9, OSMOTIC). Brace, Anchor, Immovable, or a Navigator's shear negates.
- The Churn runs **one segment per Fugue Interval** by default, and one per **Cycle** at LICENCE 5.
- **Deeper is free**: moving with the flow costs 1 PP and grants **Momentum** (+1 die on your next Strike, CORE §4.3). **Coming back costs**: Fluid Navigation at AT 12 (14 in the Churn proper) or 2 PP per band.
- Say the spine aloud at the start of every compartment. *Every good site has a direction, and this one has a schedule.*
- The Churn can be **jammed** (Synthesis AT 14, 20 S of Substrate, or a Barophil wedge): peristalsis stops in that compartment. This strands everyone there, freezes the CHURN Clock, and makes the compartment a *dead-water* market where the Cohort can rest, trade, or be trapped. That is a whole scene, and it is usually the players' idea.

## 6.4 TOLERANCE, and the Breach

**Tolerance** is this Vector's stealth: *presenting chemistry the body reads as own*. It is a contested contest: `OSM + Pathway (Chemoreception)` or, for Immunan PCs, `AFF + Pathway (Antigen Discipline)` against the Sentinel's `AFF + (Antigen Register)`, at **AT 14** (10 sponsored / 18 if the Host is Wary or worse).

The printed answers to a Sentinel challenge, in ascending cost:

1. **Say what you are.** Precept 4 (*Tonicity is truth*): state lineage, tonic, Oaths. +1 die on the next contest this Beat, and a Sentinel that will not fight an honest answer is a corridor.
2. **A valid token** — Form 11-B, a Warden storage receipt, a Congregation rite-scar, a Warrant (if you are inventory).
3. **An Antigen Rite** (CORE §11.6): 25 S and a witness; you are registered as *self* to one faction's wards for one Tide. Inside a body that is a master-key, and it is **legal**, which is the point of this Vector.
4. **Argument** (Precept 6): free contested AFF vs the violator's RTC at AT 16 — the Undersigned's own weapon, and available to the Cohort once they learn what is inside.
5. **A Breach.** Cut your way out through the Host's wall: the wall has **integrity 40, MT 5 (8 braced)** (CORE §4.8, §7.5). It takes 2 Cycles, costs 60 S of acid or a Breacher's charge, advances IMMUNE 2 and LICENCE 1, drops Goodwill to Hostile, and the compartment floods: **10 Osmotic and a band shift for everyone nearby** (BESTIARY, *Breacher*). It works. It is the last resort and it should feel like one.

## 6.5 THE BARGAIN — a Clock instead of a body

A player may **offer a Clock** to buy dice: advance **IMMUNE, LICENCE, ORDER, CORE, or CHURN** by one segment to add **+2 Reagents** to any contest, or to lift one ally's pool by +1 die. The Director accepts, always, and moves the Clock in front of everyone. Not the same Clock twice in one scene.

This is the Vector's safest escalation valve, and it belongs to the players: they set the temperature of their own session, and every bargain prints exactly what it cost. It is *not* Overdrive (CORE §1.8), which spends ATP and MI; the Bargain spends the world. Use it when a table is under-using the Clocks, and stop offering it if they are already at IMMUNE 4.

## 6.6 TELLS AND TRIGGERS (how the interior escalates without an encounter list)

**Trigger the d8 in §13.2** when the Cohort: (1) spends more than one Fugue Interval in a compartment; (2) moves fast or loud — Strikes, Bursts, Breaches, running, cutting; (3) enters a compartment for the first time since the last trigger; or (4) leaves anything dissolving unharvested. **Never** trigger it because a roll failed (CODEX §5.2 rule 3). One hazard, one roll, and every hazard announces itself first:

| Hazard | Its tell, said out loud before it acts |
| :-- | :-- |
| **A Churn flush** | the fluid goes **flat and sweet** on the tongue (a pH drop), the walls groan one Cycle early, and the Congregation counts the groan aloud. Free warning, always |
| **A Sentinel challenge** | the smell of **ammonia**; register villi standing up along one wall; your own scent starts arriving *after* you do |
| **The Licence advancing** | the **shell trumpet** through the tissue, once per Beat. It carries. It always carries |
| **Near the Nodule** | **the enzyme stops.** Lytic apparatus goes quiet, corpses nearby are whole, and anything you were dissolving starts healing |
| **An enzyme bloom** | the mucus turns **yellow**, and the compartment's Osmotic pressure climbs one band before it bursts |

Say the tell, then stop and let them act. A hazard that cannot be anticipated is a rules-tax; a hazard with a tell is an *invitation*, and this Vector's whole interior runs on invitations.

## 6.7 THREE EXITS (print them; a host you cannot leave is a bug, not a premise)

An inside-a-body adventure fails at one table only: the one where the Cohort finds the object and has no way out, so the last act becomes a dice argument. This Vector ships **three exits from the first scene**, and the Director names them aloud whenever the Cohort is stuck:

1. **The door that opens.** Goodwill **Client or better**: the Host opens the Weep, or a fistula it makes for you, or hands you to its own drainage. Costs a Beat and one favour it will ask for later (§4, Intake 1's Debt). Available unless the Cohort made the Host Hostile.
2. **The body's own schedule.** Peristalsis exports continuously: ride the **Churn** to the drainage of the Vat Road (Synthesis AT 12 to rig a catch, §4 Intake 12) or be flushed by the same order that moves the Excretory Officer. Costs Bulk (you travel light) and 6 Osmotic at the seal. Always available, and the Wardens of Arch Four have a **licensed catch-net** that will absolutely take your harvest off you, for a fee.
3. **The Breach.** §6.4: cut a hole, pay for it in full, and be hunted out of the Wastes for the rest of the Tide. Always available. Never free.

And a fourth, which the Director should keep quiet until the Cohort has earned it: **the Host's death is an exit.** If the Cohort cannot get out, the Licence Clock will open the body for them. That is not a solution the players should reach for early — it is the Vector's worst ending arriving on schedule, and it *is* still an ending.

        ^                                  v

   BREACH -- the last              NOT-FINISHED THINGS

*Travel inside is measured in bands and Beats, not Drifts: the Weep to the Chapel is a Beat; Chapel to Churn is half a Beat and the Churn does it to you.*

## 7 · 1 · THE WEEP — *approach: a toll, a hazard, a scent*

The nine-Cycle fistula that never closed: a torn band of muscle the width of a street, its edges folded into a shantytown exactly like the Rim's breach-scar shanties (CODEX §6.1), because people do that everywhere there is a hole.

*Play it:* a **market and a warning**. Three Barophils sell water at 2 S a Drift; an abandoned Warden toll-booth is still reading passers-by and issuing fines to no one; the Hermit of the Weep (see §13.3) knows the Host's outflow and will trade it for something you have. Chemoreception AT 12 reads the whole schedule of the interior (§4 Intake 6).
*Danger:* **E1** — 2 **Flaccid Haunter** + 1 **Gnaw Swarm** (5 BSP). They are not evil; they are *thirsty*, and in the Weep water is currency: a Cohort that offers 20 S of sealed water ends this encounter in one sentence.
*What they can get:* the Churn schedule (huge), the Hermit's price list, and the nine-Cycle scar of an **earlier break-in** — gear, and a very short chemical record of what happened to the previous Cohort: *"the Nodule answers to a name."* (This is the primary clue to §11's cut-out rules and one of the three backups for §12.)

## 7 · 2 · THE CHAPEL OF THE UNWASTED — *the first room, wrong*

A swallowed Deep Choir shrine, nine Great Cycles old: it became a granary, then a hospital, then a graveyard, and now it is a **ferry terminus with a notice board**. Pews of folded membrane. A shrine bell no one rings because ringing it tells the body where everyone is.

*Play it:* **no forced combat.** The **Undissolvable Congregation** (CODEX §7.4) runs the interior here — forty-odd pilgrims being digested slowly and *on purpose*, chemically legible to the Host as own-ish tissue, which is why the body has tolerated them for Beats. They will sell sponsorship (Intake 9), hide you inside themselves (once), and refuse to lie about what is deeper. Their price is always a name, and always a true one.
*Clues (three, per CODEX §9.4's "three observations"):*
 - the **Churn's log**, kept by pilgrims who count flushes as prayer: the pump has been trying to finish **one object** for nine Great Cycles and never has (primary clue to the Nodule);
 - **numbered sacs** — the inventory of everything the body has taken in, and one of them is Foreman Ock's missing crew's (leads to the Shelf and to E4's negotiation);
 - a **fresh cut** in the chapel wall, made from the *inside*, with Hegemony tool-marks and a Brine Office charge-slug (the **Second Intake** — see §9, E4 — got here first, and they are harvesting).
*Danger:* if the Cohort is hostile, loud, or Lytic: **E2** — 1 **Gastric Sentinel** + 3 **Antigen Gnats** (5 BSP) and IMMUNE 1. Note that a Cohort with Form 11-B, a storage token, a warrant, or an honest Precept-4 answer **cannot lose this fight**, and should be allowed to walk out with a Sentinel escort, which is better than any victory.

## 7 · 3 · THE NINE-CYCLE CHURN — *the problem room, still running*

The Unread's digestion, mid-action, for nine Great Cycles: a channel of muscle walls contracting on a schedule, enzyme sheets, a pump-villus the size of a foundry flywheel, and a **bolus that never finishes** — the thing the Host cannot dissolve, being turned over and over, forever, politely.

*Play it:* a **terrain puzzle with a schedule**, which is the best kind of Expanse fight. Read the medium out loud (CODEX §1.1): **acidic / hypertonic, viscous (2 PP per Drift, 3 in the deep channel), Flow 4 toward the Shelf, Turbid.** The Tide moves this (CODEX §5.3): in HYPOTONIC ZENITH the Churn's MTs drop by 2, the pump over-primes, and the enzyme sheets *stop* — the season changes the solution, and the Choir pick that season to walk in.
*Three working answers to the Churn* (never gate on one clever idea):
 - **jam the pump** (Synthesis AT 14, 20 S): the compartment stops moving; everything in it stops being pushed, and the Cohort gains a safe room at the cost of being stuck in it;
 - **reverse a band** (a Turbulence Navigator's shear, or **Tidal Anchor** behaviour, BESTIARY Order V): the flow carries you the wrong way once;
 - **be patient** (a Barophil Brute with Anchor + Immovable simply *refuses*: ride the Churn to the Shelf for free and eat the 6 Osmotic at fill, which a Barophil does with its hands full of tea).
*Danger:* **E3** — the **Churn** (abiotic, 2 BSP) + 2 **Gastric Sentinels** + 2 **Protease Locusts** = **8 BSP**. At the fill, the compartment throws everyone toward the Shelf; a Cohort that fights the Churn *against* the flow is fighting the map and should be allowed to notice that.

## 7 · 4 · THE SHELF OF NOT-FINISHED THINGS — *the cost room. This is where the horror is.*

The horror of this Vector is not acid. It is **politeness**: the Unread has spent nine Great Cycles *not finishing* things. Everything it decided it would not dissolve is here, held in nodules of slow tissue, alive, and aware: cargo wrecks still broadcasting their owners' Oaths; a Vat Crew of twelve who went in looking for salvage in the Ninth Cycle and are still *in there*, thinking; Drift Whale calves it swallowed and could not bring itself to end; and at the centre of the Shelf, in a nodule the size of a chapel, **the Undersigned** — the Receipt — thinking for nine Great Cycles under an oath it cannot break and a promise it cannot keep.

*Play it:* **the moral room**, and it must be *playable*. Three things are true and the players can check all three:
 - the Shelf's inhabitants are **not** a fight — they are a *crowd* (no stats; one OSM/AFF contest at AT 12 mixed, 16 hostile, BESTIARY's Colonist table) who have learned to live as organs;
 - **harvesting the Shelf is possible and it is the best haul in the Vector** (Pristine Sequences stacked like cordwood, per CODEX §6.7's "the best harvest in the Expanse") and each harvest cut is IMMUNE +1 and Goodwill −1;
 - the **Ash's licence will dissolve this room first**. Anything the Cohort wants from the Shelf must be asked for, bought, taken, or argued for *now*.
*Danger:* **E4** — **Foreman Sulk Nine-Yards** (Brine Office harvest crew; Elite, 4 BSP) + 2 **Enzymatic Horrors** (4) + the Churn hazard (2) = **10 BSP**. Sulk is not a villain: he is a *foreman on a contract*, harvesting inside a body with a licence, and if the Cohort can name the crime he is committing (no Warden counter-signature for *interior* cuts), he will stop, sit down, and negotiate — because Sulk wants to retire.
*The room's knife:* **Ock's crew.** Twelve Ash workers, alive, thinking, and Ock's own licence makes them *salvage*. Any Cohort deal with Ock (the LICENCE slow-down, §3) is a deal about what happens to them. Let the table find this out on their own; it is the best scene in the Vector.

## 7 · 5 · THE NODULE — *the yield, defended by the owner*

A sealed knot of slow tissue at the centre of the Shelf, older than the Wastes' current politics, and the only part of the Unread that has never been touched by enzyme. Inside: the Receipt, and the promise.

**The Nodule (abiotic):** integrity **90**, **MT 5 (8 braced)**, and it is not merely a wall — it is *sealed to a person*, so Precept 6 applies to damaging it.
**Three ways to open it** (CODEX §9.4's working-answers discipline; BESTIARY's *Living Vault* precedent, AT 20):
 - **cut it** — a Synthesis contest at **AT 20**, or a Breacher's charge, or **30 S** of acid poured into the seam. Loud: IMMUNE +2, Goodwill −1;
 - **unseal it** — the Host's own storage system, if the Cohort arrives with a valid token (Intake 8) or a **Kin** relationship (§6.1): the Host opens it itself, in nine seconds, and asks them to be quick;
 - **be named to it** — the Hermit's clue, the Congregation's name-rite, or the Undersigned's own spoken consent. The Nodule answers to a name; saying the right one costs a CORE Clock segment and gets you everything.
**The guard:** **Ninth Response** (§10), the Host's own immune authority, plus its **Gastric Sentinels**: **E5**, 12 BSP Hard, 16 Deadly at IMMUNE 5. Ninth Response *opens by parley* (see its block): it will trade a route and a safe exit for one thing — that the foreign object leaves the body, which is the same thing the Host wants and the opposite of what three factions pay for.

# 10 · THE BLOCKS (nine, printed in house format)

Bestiary conventions hold (BESTIARY front matter): **MI already includes size**; Specials spend **PP** from the same economy the players use; every Special is **telegraphed**; every block prints a **findable weakness**; abiotic blocks have **no Frequency** and harvest nothing.

## 10.1 THE UNREAD — *the Host* (Barophil Osmotic Brute, Level 10)

```
THE UNREAD              Catastrophe · T3 · Vast · Order V (Osmotic Brutes)
MT 15/13/11        MI 80 / 60 / 50 (three phases)     soak 6     pool 7d
PP 5    Flow 1    Freq +1    BD 7 Osmotic
```
**These phases are not a fight line.** They are *how much of a person you can hurt before the person starts making decisions about you.* Phase 1 is the body's reflexes (churn, pH, walls). Phase 2 is its **response** (Sentinels, swelling, seal-down). Phase 3 is **the Host speaking**, and Phase 3 has no specials: it is a conversation that can end the Vector.
**Traits:** **Peristaltic Authority** (it sets the flow in any compartment it can feel: free, and it always sets it *deeper*) · **Nine Great Cycles of Not-Finished** (its interior holds sealed and *living* things; cut one out with a **Synthesis contest at AT 20**, and each cut ends one of its phase options — BESTIARY, *Living Vault*) · **Consent** (it does not act against organisms presenting valid chemistry, a Warden storage token, or a lawful warrant; it *has* tolerated the Congregation for years) · **Turgor** (as the Phenotype, ATLAS: it feeds on push and Osmotic exchange) · **Unfinishable** (nothing in the Wastes can dissolve it; Lytic damage against it is reduced by 4, and Total Dissolution requires the licence's machinery, not a Cohort's).
**Special — Close the Compartment (5 PP):** one compartment seals: exit MT 5→8 braced, integrity 60, and everyone inside gains **Jostled**. It does this to move you, not to hurt you. · **Special — Sweep (4 PP):** CHURN advances 1 immediately and throws everyone in the compartment 1 band, 6 Osmotic. · **Special — Offer Passage (free, Goodwill Kin):** move the Cohort anywhere inside, once per movement, and hold one compartment still for a Fugue Interval.
**Weakness (print it, Cycle 1):** *it cannot act against a registered guest, and its Weep cannot be closed while the Churn runs.* Also: **do not fight the Host — ask the Host.** The block is a puzzle with MI, and the puzzle's solution is consent (CODEX §4.4, §13.2).
**HARVEST:** C15 / G9 / P4 / T2 · **Codon 45 (as a Catastrophe: 3 × T3 base 15) · BSP 10** — **and harvesting the Unread is the single most consequential act in this Vector** (see §11.3 and CODEX §10.6: the social price of harvesting; the *Clonal Line*'s precedent is "this kill should cost the Cohort a faction").
**Tactics:** it does not fight. It **processes**. Everything it does has a direction and a schedule, which is why a Cohort that learns the schedule can walk through the most dangerous body in the Wastes unmolested.
**Why it exists:** nine Great Cycles of a promise it could not finish and would not break. It is the Expanse's oldest argument that a body is its own argument (Precept 6), and it is tired.

# 14 · RUN IT SHORT OR LONG

## 14.1 Four hours (the compressed Vector)

1. **Scene 1 — the Vat Road (40 min).** The notice, the Weep, the Intake chosen from §4 (offer six, not twelve), the Intake Web cut to **questions 1, 5, 6**.
2. **Scene 2 — Chapel and Churn (70 min).** Merge the Chapel into the Churn's approach; run E3 with the Churn Clock at 2; the Congregation's sponsorship offer is live.
3. **Scene 3 — the Shelf (60 min).** Ock's twelve, the Receipt's face, Sulk's crew at the far wall. The reversal lands here.
4. **Scene 4 — the Nodule (70 min).** Ninth Response parleys; the ending is chosen, not rolled. Then the ledger, read aloud (CODEX §12.6's discipline is the last scene's punctuation).
Cut: LICENCE to **3**, IMMUNE to **4**, the Weep's market to one neighbour, and *never* cut the Undersigned's scene — that scene is the Vector.

## 14.2 Six sessions (the crawl)

1 The notice and the Intake Web; the Heist of Paperwork (Form 11-B / Storage Token / arrest — the Wardens' own machinery does the carrying).
2 The Weep (E1, the Hermit, the previous Cohort's scar).
3 The Chapel (sponsorship, the Churn's log, the fresh cut from inside).
4 The Churn (E3, the pump jammed or not, the Tide's mood decides).
5 The Shelf (E4, Ock's twelve, the moral inventory: what will you carry out of here and what will you leave thinking?).
6 The Nodule (E5, the ending, the ledger). Then Act II (CODEX §8.5) is *already running*: the Cohort has the Receipt, a Gene-Debt, and a lineage in the record.

## 14.3 Five minutes of prep, tonight (CODEX §13.4 applied)

1. What does the compartment **taste of**? — *vinegar and old sugar; nine Cycles of one meal.*
2. Which **Clock** advances tonight, and what will the players do about it? — *CHURN, and it throws them toward the Shelf.*
3. What does the enemy want that is **not violence**? — *Sulk wants to finish his shift; Ninth Response wants to be asked.*
4. The **one printable weakness** of the strongest thing here? — *Gastric Sentinels: valid chemistry denies them, Desiccation ends them.*
5. Who is **counting** something the Cohort has not noticed? — *the Congregation counts flushes as prayer, and they are on nine.*

## 14.4 Pacing dials (the Vector is a deck you shuffle yourself)

**Where you bury the ending is a choice you make before Session 1, not a fact about the body.** Decide the depth of the Nodule — four hours, six sessions, or eleven — and every other quantity follows from it: Intakes offered (six for a one-shot, all twelve for a crawl), Keys in play (three, all twelve), Clock segments (LICENCE 3 compressed / 6 written / 9 crawl). This is the free-game trick of putting the End Card in the deck wherever you want the last act to fall, and it is the only dial that keeps a 20-page Vector from becoming a 60-page one.

**Frame every compartment by its question, then cut.** Each site below has one agenda; open it with a **bang** (the thing that forces a choice), escalate with sequenced bangs, and **cut the scene the moment the question resolves** — do not fade it out:

| Compartment | The scene's question | The bang that opens it |
| :-- | :-- | :-- |
| The Vat Road | *Which door, and what are we willing to pay for it?* | the licence being read aloud, and the queue of harvest crews filing their claims |
| The Weep | *Can we buy information from people who live in a wound?* | the toll-booth fining a PC for a pass they did not make nine Cycles ago |
| The Chapel | *Will the body's tenants trust us before the first cut?* | a pilgrim asking the Cohort to carry a name, in front of everyone |
| The Churn | *Do we fight the map or read it?* | a flush announced one Cycle early by a groan and a sweet taste |
| The Shelf | *What will we refuse to take?* | a salvage crew of twelve, alive, filing a claim on their own bodies |
| The Nodule | *Who decides what happens to a document that is a person?* | Ninth Response asking, politely, to be allowed to help |

**Two borrowed shapes worth knowing, because your table will drift into one of them.** The free horror-module idiom **Survive → Solve → Save** fits Movements 1–2 (stay alive, read the anatomy, save something); the longer **Transgression → Omen → Manifestation → Banishment → Slumber** fits the whole Vector (the audit that named an unlisted sequence; the licence; the Cascade; the Argument; the Unread finally stopping). If your session is drifting, ask which of the five words you are in, and play the next one.

## 15.2 The house rules this Vector obeys, line by line

So that you can run it without re-deciding them: never let a failure be "nothing happens" — apply a **Cost** instead (CODEX §2.3). Advance Clocks for **succeeding loudly**, never for a failed roll, and let the Cohort rewind one segment by spending time, Cohesion, or Specimens (CODEX §5.2). Print **one findable weakness** for anything bigger than the Cohort (CODEX §4.4, §13.2). Design a puzzle to fall to **three observations**, and a site to have a **spine** you say out loud (CODEX §9.1, §9.4). Read the medium aloud — pH, current, clarity (CODEX §1.1). Budget by **BSP**, pay **Codon** separately, and never add the two (CODEX §4). Write a creature **backwards from what it leaves behind**, and make the big kills **cost a faction** (BESTIARY front matter; *Clonal Line*). Never inflate an Activation Threshold because of level: change the fiction instead — +1 die for a good tool, −1 for a hostile tonic (CODEX §2.1). Prefer **complications from failed rolls** to pre-authored surprises: a Vector that only works if the players find the clue you wrote is a Vector with one play in it.

## 15.3 The premise's own lineage (nobody invents being swallowed)

The inside-a-body heist is old, and naming its relatives keeps this Vector from drifting into either sci-fi pastiche or gross-out. The screen's spine runs from **Fantastic Voyage** (1966: a shrunken submarine crew injected to dissolve a clot) through **Innerspace** (1987: the same device played as farce because the wrong person swallowed the ship) and **Magic School Bus: For Lunch** (a school trip down a throat, which is the exact beat of Intake 7 in a children's show) to **Ant-Man** (shrinking explicitly structured as a heist film) and **Last Wail of the Sea King** (a civilisation growing inside a dying beast, unaware it is inside one — the Jonah inversion this Vector is *actually* closest to, since the Shelf's tenants have made a society of the Unread's refusal). In this hobby: **Salt in Wounds** (a city built on a bound Tarrasque, butchered and regenerated daily — the industrial version of the Vat Road), **Legend of the Leukocytes** (you *are* four white blood cells, with real immunology as the mechanics), the free Troika! background **A/an 1d6¹ trapped inside the body of a 1d6²**, and Pathfinder's **Swallow Whole** rule, which for thirty years has been the hobby's only systematic answer to "how do I survive the stomach" (damage per round, cutting your way out, crawling up the gullet). *The Osmotic Expanse* has the one thing none of those have: **the host can be a legal person, and the law has opinions about who may be inside it.** That is why this Vector exists as a Vector, and not as a spell.

## 15.4 Safety lineage, stated properly

The consent frame in §2 is this line's own CODEX §1.3, extended with the standard toolkit and its real attributions: **Lines & Veils** (Emily Care Boss, in *Sex & Sorcery*, a supplement for Ron Edwards' *Sorcerer*, from Nordic larp — lines are never, veils fade to black), the **X-card** (John Stavropoulos), and **Script Change** (Brie Beau Sheldon: rewind, fast-forward, pause), plus a **debrief** at the end of any session that went somewhere heavy. Put the content note on the invitation, not in an appendix: this Vector is about ingestion, digestion, and being held, and a table that learns that at minute forty is a table that planned nothing. §2.3's **Consent Rule** is the Expanse-native version of the same instinct: the mechanic *is* a membrane, so ask before you cross one.

## 15.5 Why this book is laid out the way it is (and what would have made it cheap)

The published craft literature is unusually blunt about what makes a short module feel expensive, and the list is not art: **white space and real margins**; a **run-this-adventure page** carrying premise, Clocks, escalation ladder, exits, tone and content note; **one source of truth per fact** with cross-references; **keys short enough to read at a glance** ("no one wants to read a three-page key to a single room"); **stat blocks on the same spread as the room that needs them**; boxed text that is *actually* boxed, consistently; **labelled** dice tables; a back page of referential material (handouts, minion blocks, mini-map); and no "GM, when the players arrive…" plot assumptions anywhere. Its negatives are the cheapness this Vector tries to avoid: walls of boxed prose, an escalation ladder that is really an encounter list, random tables nobody labelled, a hook whose timeline cannot survive scrutiny (the licence Clock in §3 exists because a licence is the one timeline a *legal* world can hold to), and padding by reprint. So: §0 is the run-this-adventure page; §7's compartments are keyed and short; §10's blocks are together and §9 tells you which block is where; every table in §13 is labelled and always hands the players an action; §3's Clock is the spine, not a threat.

### THE ONE THING TO REMEMBER TONIGHT

The Host is not a dungeon. It is a person with a schedule, a government, and nine Great Cycles of manners. **It will tolerate almost anything from someone who asks.** Every hour the Cohort spends deciding *how* to be let in is an hour they are not spending deciding *what to cut out*, and the Licence Clock does not care which one they chose.
