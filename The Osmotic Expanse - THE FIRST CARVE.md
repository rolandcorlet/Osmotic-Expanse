# THE FIRST CARVE
### A Vector for four organisms, Levels 1 → 3 — *learning the Expanse by cutting up something enormous that somebody else already killed*

**Book:** Director's material, and the house **teaching Vector**. It sits *before* *The Bloom of Ash* (CODEX Ch. 12) in difficulty, not in the calendar, and well before *The Long Swallow* (Levels 7 → 11) in scale. If your group has never played, run this one. If they have, run it anyway and let a new player bring a friend.
**Region:** the **Toll Reach**, the 60 Drifts where three channels merge (CODEX §6.4), off **Tallow Ring** — a tether-farm colony of 2,400 that has never had a windfall and does not know what to do with one.
**Spine link:** optional Act I flavour for *The Long Argument* (CODEX §8.5). It runs standalone; if you do run a campaign, the last paragraph of §9.4 hands you a seed without committing you to one.
**Tone:** **workplace comedy with real stakes.** Nobody in this Vector is evil. Everyone is at work, on a deadline, and sure the others are the problem. The Expanse's horror is all here — a body, a harvest, a licence, a nursery of things that did not consent to be cut up — but it is reachable, survivable, and mostly *funny*, because four beginners with a permit should not be able to outrun a Guild schedule, and they do.
**Content note:** butchery of a dead animal (and of people, if the table chooses it), harvesting, Dormancy, drowning-in-fluid, a nursery of juveniles you can legally kill. Nothing here is forced. §2 is a short session zero; put its one-line summary in your invitation.
**For the Director who is also new:** every mechanic you need is in §6 and §7. You do not need to have read the four books. You need this book and the odds card (§6.6).

> *In the Mid-Strata the harvest is the dead, and the dead are property* (CODEX §6.3). This Vector is the sentence, made playable: a thing the size of a district has died in the busiest water in the region, everyone wants a piece of it, nobody can move it, and it is **rotting while you argue**. You are the crew with the permit. The rules you learn tonight are the rules that decide what you carry out.

# 0 · THE VECTOR ON ONE PAGE

**The situation.** A **Drift Whale** (BESTIARY Order III — Nutrient Drifters: Catastrophe, Tier 4, *Vast — "the largest living thing in the Expanse"*, *"the Expanse's weather with a will, and the largest moral problem a Cohort can bring home"*) has stopped in the middle of the **Toll Reach** and died. Its gut locked on an obstruction: a fused **print-canister and salt** the size of a hab, now called **the Gut Stone**. Three channels are blocked. Six hundred skiffs are behind it. The **Flow Guilds** have contracted a **Hegemony clearance crew** to set a **Render Charge** — blow the whale apart and dredge the channel — and the charge is on a schedule, not a request. The **Reclaimers' Ash** say the whale died inside a consecrated drift, so everything in it is *unwasted*, which is a legal category, not a mood. **Tallow Ring** holds a salvage licence and has, in the panic, sold the salvage rights **twice**.

Why nobody killed it, and why nobody can move it, is printed in the block: a Drift Whale is **protected in three regions, and killing one is a capital crime — the Wardens will *File* you, not fight you**. The whale died of its own accident, which is worse for everyone, because now there is nothing to blame and everything to sell. The Bestiary's own harvest line is this Vector's premise, verbatim: a Whale yields **C18 / G11 / P5 / T2 / T2**, and *"a Whale harvest feeds a colony for four Tides and starts a war over who owns the ground it died on."* Tallow Ring has already started the war, on paper, before anyone cut anything.

**The job.** Tallow Ring hires the Cohort as a **Carve Crew**: get inside, cut the *soft* meat — the part that is worth money *now*, before autolysis turns it into soup — and get out before the charge. Cutting is a job, not a heist: there are claim-markers, toll arches, a customs post in the wound, and a Guild claim-runner with a clipboard. The clipboard can beat you harder than anything with teeth.

**The prize.** Four things, and they are not compatible:

| The prize | What it is worth | Who objects |
| : : | :-- | :-- |
| **the soft cut** | the only harvest that survives the spoilage: ~30 Drifts of edible, gradeable tissue — a *regional* fortune the Cohort can carry a fraction of | the Guilds, the Ash, the Hegemony |
| **the Gut Stone** | a fused print-canister of Hegemony slag, warm, sealed, and stamped with a serial that does not exist | the Hegemony, loudly |
| **the brood** | something hatched *inside* the canister and is now swimming in the whale's brood-channels, alive, unregistered, and enormous in number: **the not-whale** | the Wardens (unregistered), the Choir (sacred), the market (delicious) |
| **a Name** | the colony's debt, the Ash's blessing, a Guild toll-free, a Warden register entry: legal personhood, in this world | nobody, which is why it is expensive |

**Who else is inside.** Guild **claim-runners** walking the cut-lines with a stamp. A Hegemony **clearance crew** planting the charge and refusing to say when. An Ash **reading crew** at the eye, taking the whale's last hours, which is a service and a lawsuit. A **customs post** of Chaperone Drones (Order II — *architecture that defends itself*, and the one Order that negotiates) who have folded a checkpoint out of whale gristle and want a toll in Specimens. And the scavengers: **Gnaw Swarms**, **Detritus Feeders**, and everything in the Reach that can smell a wound (BESTIARY Order I).

**The shape of play.** Five movements, one site each, one **lesson** each (§6.2): **THE TOLL-VENT** (the wound, and the rules of being inside a dead thing) → **THE SOFT FIELD** (harvest, and why a corpse is a bank) → **THE RENDER HALL** (the stomach, still warm, still working; the first real fight, and the first Dormancy) → **THE BROOD OF THE NOT-WHALE** (the cost room: the thing that should not be here, and the price of a licence) → **THE GUT STONE** (the yield, the owner, and the exit as the charge counts down).

**The three Clocks** (all three on index cards, in front of everyone; CODEX §5.2): **CARCASS** (6) — the whale is digesting itself, and value falls every segment (§3.1). **TOLL** (5) — the Guild's clearance schedule; at fill the Render Charge fires. **HUNGER** (4) — the scavenger convergence, and the price of leaving bodies unharvested (Precept 2: an unharvested corpse is a crime *and* a dinner). These are a **value** clock, a **law** clock, and a **danger** clock. The players can move all three. That is the whole teaching plan: the mechanics are the plot's handles.

**The loop.** Get hired (choose *how*, §4) → learn one rule at a site → spend a rule to save time or value on the next one → the Clocks move → the ledger settles (who owns what you carried out) → a Level lands on camera (§6.5).

**What this Vector is about.** *Everything in the Expanse is worth something, and the question is never whether to take it but who decided.* Four new players learn that in the same evening they learn to roll.

**Three omens** (read one, out loud, in the first ten minutes; Cairn's Omens — something is already true that the Cohort has not priced):
1. *The whale has been dead for three Beats, and the channels have been closed for two. Somebody knew before the Guild did.*
2. *There is a smell in the brood-channels that is not whale. It is warm, and it is sweet, and it is not in any register.*
3. *The Ash reading crew has already taken the whale's last hour. They are not sharing it, because what the whale saw at the end is a lawsuit.*

**The five lessons at a glance** (§6.2 has the numbers): **1** roll a pool, beat a number, keep the Overflow. **2** a body is a bank: harvest, Specimens, Substrate, Bulk. **3** in a Pulse War: PP, Reserve, Reactive Osmosis, Soak, damage type, terrain. **4** the social layer: contests, Oath boxes, Cohesion, Standing, and that a scene can pay better than a fight. **5** Dormancy, the Countdown, stabilising, and Growth — what you buy with what you have learned.

# 1 · THE CORE VISION

## 1.1 The Big Three Questions

| Question | This Vector's answer |
| :-- | :-- |
| **What experience is this about?** | Competence. Four organisms who have never done anything and are suddenly doing the biggest job in the region with a deadline on it. The feeling is not *heroic*; it is *first shift, and we are not getting fired*. |
| **How do the mechanics reinforce that mood?** | Every rule the engine has is a **workplace** rule. A harvest check is doing the job properly (CORE §2.4). A Bulk limit is what your cart holds (CORE §2.2). A claim is paperwork. A Clock is a shift schedule (CODEX §5.2). Stress is the eighth hour (CORE §5.8). The engine is not decorated as a job; it already is one — the Expanse's organisms are, literally, working bodies. |
| **What reward loop keeps players touching the rules?** | **Lesson Keys** (§5.3): a named rule, demonstrated correctly at the table, pays Codon on the spot — 5 Codon for *naming* the rule you used, 10 for using it to solve a problem the Director did not write. Players are paid for learning the game. The loop closes in §13's ledger, which is built so the Levels land where the Codex says they should (CODEX §12.6). |

## 1.2 Three pillars (and what this Vector cuts)

| Pillar | In play, every session |
| :-- | :-- |
| **The clock is a place** | the carcass is the map *and* the timer: it rots, it floods, it blows. Every route is priced in Drifts and Beats. |
| **Value is a mechanic** | Specimens, Substrate, Bulk, harvest grades, spoilage. Harvest decisions are made *before* the kill, which is what professionals do. |
| **The law is a monster** | claims, licences, tolls, Oaths, registers. The hardest thing in this Vector has a stamp, a filing fee, and four soak. The Threshold ladder never moves because the Cohort is new: *they* get dice (CORE §1.2: "Difficulty is a fixed number, not a moving target"). |

**Cut.** No dungeon crawl (there is no treasure room, there is a *cutting list*); no campaign-level conspiracy (the seed in §9.4 is one paragraph and may be deleted without breaking anything); no Tier 3+ creature anywhere (the tier rule is the wall: Levels 1–4 are Tier 1, CODEX §4.1); no moralising (the nursery is not a lesson, it is a *line item* the table will argue about, which is better). And no scene where the correct answer is one clever idea — three clues everywhere, §14.1.

## 1.3 Terminology discipline (read once, then police it all Vector)

New players arrive carrying vocabulary from other games. Replace it in the first ten minutes and they will never think in the old words again.

| Say this | Never say this | Why it matters here |
| :-- | :-- | :-- |
| "**roll n dice and beat the Threshold**" | "roll a check / save / skill" | the pool is a *reagent quantity*: more dice is not luck, it is chemistry (CORE Ch. II §1.2) |
| "**Overflow**" (what you beat it by) | "margin of success" | Overflow *is* damage, and it is why a good plan hits harder than a good die (CORE §1.4) |
| "**1s are Inhibitors**" | "crit failure" | Inhibitors cost **Stress**, not the attempt (CORE §1.1) — the failure is a resource, not a punishment |
| "**Threshold (AT)**" for the world; "**Membrane Threshold (MT)**" for being hit | "DC", "AC", "armor class" | the Threshold ladder never inflates for level; *you* gain dice (CODEX §2.1) |
| "**Membrane Integrity (MI)**" | "hit points", "HP" | MI is *containment*. Low MI is a leaking body, not a blood bar. |
| "**Dormant**" at 0 MI | "unconscious", "down", "dead" | the game's most important kindness, and the first rule a beginner must own (§6.5, Lesson 5) |
| "**Lysis Countdown**" (visible boxes) | "death timer" | boxes are *information*; players say "I have four boxes" and that sentence is play (CORE §5.11) |
| "**Metabolic Stress** (0–8)" | "sanity", "fatigue" | Stress is heat in the system: it removes dice. *Stress is what kills experienced organisms* (CODEX §1.4) |
| "**Pulse War**", "**Cycle**" | "combat", "round", "turn order" | nobody waits their turn; PP is the initiative system (CORE Ch. IV) |
| "**Grow**" (a Level, at a Reconstitution) | "level up" | Codon is never spent; a Level is where the total has carried you (CORE §2.3) |
| "**Cohort**", "**Carcass**", "**cut**", "**claim**" | "party", "dungeon", "loot", "quest" | the world's words are the mood. Nobody in this world says *loot*; they say *harvest*, and mean it. |

**House ban, enforced:** no *d20*, no *AC*, no *HP*, no *spell*, no *mana*, no *experience points*, no *proficiency bonus*, no *gold*. Substrate is **S**. Codon is Codon.

# 2 · SESSION ZERO FOR A FIRST TABLE

A Vector that teaches has to be *consented to*, not sprung. This is twelve minutes, and it is the best twelve minutes you will spend.

## 2.1 The four standing questions (CODEX §1.3, verbatim in shape)

1. **What bodies are on the table?** Consent around forced grafting, parasitism, reproductive/Hijack content, ingestion of people, Total Lysis of named NPCs, identity loss. In *this* Vector the live ones are: **harvesting a dead body (including a person's, if it comes to that)**, **a nursery of juveniles you can legally destroy**, and **Dormancy**. Ask. Decide.
2. **How graphic is a death?** Clinical ("your contents join the world") or explicit. This Vector defaults to **clinical**, and its horror is procedural: a form signed, a cut not made, a body left unharvested.
3. **What is the tone dial?** This Vector is written for **absurd xenobiological workplace comedy with real danger** (CODEX §1.3's third setting). If your table wants grim survival, keep the Clocks and cut the drone customs post; if it wants intrigue, keep the claim war and cut E1.
4. **Who can pause, and how?** One word at the table. Fiction rewinds one sentence; scene restarts adjusted; no explanation, ever. Say who is allowed to use it: *everyone, including you.*

## 2.2 Three more, for a table that has never played

5. **Have you played anything like this?** If anyone is new, the first scene is theirs, not the plot's: ask each new player to say one thing their organism is *good at* and one thing it is *bad at*. Write both on the sheet. Both will come up by Scene 3, and the bad one will save someone.
6. **Everyone gets the odds.** The Codex's exact probability table (§6.6) is **printed for the players**, not hidden. A beginner with the odds table plays like a veteran; a beginner without one plays like a victim. This is not cheating — the Codex computes those numbers by enumeration precisely so the world stays honest (CODEX §2.4).
7. **The Rehearsal Rule** (this Vector's only house rule): *the first time a rule would hurt you, it happens to someone else.* The first Breach is an NPC's. The first Dormancy is a hireling's, at a Threshold you announce. The first time a PC goes Dormant, the Cohort has already seen what it costs and what to do. Then the rule is theirs, and it is fair.

## 2.3 The Cohort's reason to be together

Do not spend a session on backstories. Use **THE HIRE** (§4): the table picks, together, who hired them and what they were promised, and that answer *is* their shared history. Four ties are already printed in §5.1. Each player writes, in one sentence: *what I was told this job was*, and *what I was told it was really*.

# 3 · THE UNAVOIDABLE PROBLEM — AND THE THREE CLOCKS

The problem is not a villain. It is **a deadline, a decaying asset, and a permit**. The whale is worth a fortune tonight and nothing on Thursday, and three organisations would rather destroy it than let the others have it. Read §3.1–3.3 out loud to the players at the end of Scene 1. Players who can see the schedule stop playing like tourists.

## 3.1 CARCASS (6 segments) — the value clock

The whale is digesting itself. Autolysis does not care that you are new.

| Segment | What the carcass becomes | What it means at the table |
| --: | :-- | :-- |
| 0 | **fresh** | every cut is at full grade; harvest checks at AT 12 are honest |
| 1 | **weeping** | free fluid everywhere: viscosity 0 in two chambers; +1 Drift per Move, and the first **Slough** patches appear |
| 2 | **bloated** | gas pockets: a ruptured pocket is a **Kinetic 8, Jostled, 1 band shift** for whoever is in the chamber (telegraph it: *"the floor is humming"*) |
| 3 | **sloughing** | soft tissue drops **one harvest grade** everywhere; harvest checks rise to AT 14 |
| 4 | **sour** | an **Enzyme Field** (BESTIARY Abiotic, Tier 2) is now active in two chambers; Salting is your only answer |
| 5 | **gaslock** | every chamber has a hazard; Move costs +1 PP; the **Gut Stone** shifts and groans once per scene |
| 6 | **the whale goes over** | the carcass bursts: **Kinetic 12, Jostled, 2 band shift** for everyone inside; every un-shipped cut is **worth nothing**; HUNGER jumps to 3; the Order Clock opens at 2 |

**Advance +1 when:** a Beat passes with no cut signed and no haul shipped · the Cohort opens a new panel (oxygen gets in; the job has a cost) · the Cohort is swept or stuck in the interior · a pump starts.
**Rewind −1 when:** the Cohort **salts** a panel (10 S of salt per 2 Drifts — *salt is the Expanse's refrigeration*, and Desiccation is a service people pay for: CODEX §6.4) · a Thermal **Cold Shock** or a Desiccation Specialist cools a chamber · a chamber is drained · the Ash perform an **Unwasted** rite over a panel (the Ash will do this for free *if the Cohort asked first*).
**Say it out loud, every time:** *"Carcass is at three. The soft cut is worth a third of what it was when you got here."*

## 3.2 TOLL (5 segments) — the law clock

The Flow Guilds have contracted a Hegemony clearance crew to set a **Render Charge** and dredge the Reach. The charge is on a schedule.

**Advance +1 when:** a Beat passes · the Cohort is loud in the channels (a Pulse War, a collapsed panel, a corpse left in the water) · the Cohort obstructs the clearance *legally* (refusing a stamp, disputing a line) · Tallow Ring defaults on its second sale.
**Rewind −1 when:** the Cohort **produces paper** — a valid claim, a licence, a toll chit, an Ash witness, a Guild tally receipt for a delivered haul · the Cohort keeps an Oath in front of a faction that notices · the Cohort turns in the **Gut Stone's serial** to a Warden register arch (a Warden *Name* is worth a Clock segment and a future).
**At fill — THE RENDER CHARGE FIRES** (§11, THE RENDER CHARGE). Not as punishment: as the schedule the players were shown on turn one and chose to race.
**Say it out loud:** *"Toll is at four. The charge is set for the end of the next Beat, and Foreman Vesk will not say which Beat."*

## 3.3 HUNGER (4 segments) — the danger clock

Everything in the Reach can smell a wound, and the Expanse's scavengers do not negotiate.

| Segment | What arrives |
| --: | :-- |
| 0 | nothing yet — but the **tells** start: bare patches of old biomass, a Gnaw Swarm reading the current (BESTIARY Order I: *"this is how you find a carcass"*) |
| 1 | signs: Gnaw activity at every open panel; one swarm will follow the Cohort at Far band for a scene without attacking |
| 2 | 1–2 **Gnaw Swarms** at the wounds; they want drink, not violence (offer water and it ends in one sentence) |
| 3 | **Detritus Feeders** begin **Render** on any corpse in the water: *"in a long fight, a Feeder becomes four Feeders"* — a corpse you did not harvest is a Feeder you did not kill |
| 4 | **the Render Cascade**: every un-shipped cut drops one grade, a swarm strip fires in the Soft Field, and the **Order Clock** opens at 2 (CODEX §5.2) |

**Advance +1 when:** the Cohort kills and leaves a body unharvested (Precept 2: the harvest belongs to whoever holds the licence — and in this world an unharvested corpse advances the Colony Clock, CODEX §8.2) · the Cohort fights loudly · a panel is left open.
**Rewind −1 when:** the Cohort **harvests** what it kills (each body harvested: −1, never below 0 — *this is the mechanical reward for looting everything*, and new players will not believe it until it works) · the Cohort salts a wound · the Cohort pays the drone toll and Order II closes the wound behind them.

## 3.4 Clock discipline (CODEX §5.2, printed, non-negotiable)

Advance **at most one segment per session per clock**. Advance it **in front of the players and name it**. Never advance a clock because the players **failed a roll** — advance it because they **succeeded at something loud**. Let players rewind segments with time, Cohesion, Specimens, or a promise; rewinding is a whole category of play, and in this Vector it is the *point*.

# 4 · HOW YOU GET THE JOB — THE HIRE

New players should not be handed a hook. They should **choose the terms of their own employment**, because the terms are the plot and the price is the debt. Pick one row together, or roll d12. **Whichever you choose, pay 15 Codon for choosing it and saying what you expected** (you are paying them for *thinking about it*).

| d12 | The Hire | What you were promised | The price, and who owns you now | Opens on |
| :-- | :-- | :-- | :-- | :-- |
| 1 | **Tally office contract** (Tallow Ring) | 25 S up front, 2 S per Drift of signed cut | the colony sold the salvage rights **twice**; you are the second sale, and the first crew is still inside | the wound mouth, with a stamped permit |
| 2 | **Guild escort licence** | toll-free passage in the Reach for a Tide | the Guild takes first pick of your haul, and a claim-runner walks with you | the channel queue, 600 skiffs deep |
| 3 | **Ash riteship** | the Ash bless your cut, and the dead of the whale are yours to process | the whale died in a consecrated drift: everything you take is **borrowed**, and borrowing is a legal category | the eye, where the Ash reading crew is already working |
| 4 | **Hegemony print-line contract** | 60 S and a **licence loan** (100 S at 5 S/Beat, and it does not end when you die) | you are a subcontractor: your salvage paperwork is *their* paperwork | the clearance crew's pump barge |
| 5 | **A buyer at the Hanging Market of Nine Tolls** | 40 S now for *"one thing, unopened"* | you do not know what it is; the buyer is not in the market any more | a sealed note with a serial number on it |
| 6 | **The nursery ring of Tallow Ring** | 30 S to *protect* the brood channels | nobody told you the brood is not whale | a warm channel that smells sweet |
| 7 | **You were on the whale** | nothing: your name is on the toll manifest as the escort | if the cargo was illegal, the signature is yours | the Gut Stone, and a lie you have not told |
| 8 | **A dropped permit** | a rival crew's permit, picked up while they fled a gas pocket | the permit has *their* names; presenting it is a Warden problem (an unlisted organism with a listed permit) | the toll arch, and a decision |
| 9 | **The drones hired you** | **a licence, issued by Order II** — Chaperone Drones file paperwork, and a fold is a legal document | Order II expects the brood **registered**, and they expect the toll | the customs post in the wound, three Drifts of folded gristle |
| 10 | **A favour owed sideways** | a clearance-crew foreman's sibling owes you; you work the carcass to make him a witness | you owe a witness *testimony*, and testimony costs Standing | the pump barge, off the record |
| 11 | **You already stole the first cut** | the colony hired you to make the theft legal | the **TOLL Clock starts at 2**; the first crew's families have opinions | the Soft Field, with a claim-marker in your wake |
| 12 | **Nothing at all** | the crew with the permit died of a gas pocket forty Drifts back, and you found them | you have their permit, their haul, and four bodies that will harvest at full value (**say what you do about that in the first ten minutes**) | a drifting skiff and a choice |

**Backup hooks (the players will miss the primary one):** the permit office reads the contract aloud; Ledger-Second Sool explains the second sale for free, angrily, twice; Foreman Vesk shows the charge casing on a tour he is giving to the skiff-caravan's passengers. Three channels. Move on once they have any one (CODEX §9.4).

# 5 · THE FOUR HANDS, THE WEB, AND THE KEYS

## 5.1 THE WEB — why they are a Cohort by Cycle one

Hand out the pregens (§5.2) if anyone is new; if everyone has a character, still read the four ties aloud and let players **steal** them. Then each player answers two questions in one sentence each: *what I was told this job was* / *what I was told it really was*. The two answers are the campaign.

| Hand | The tie (read it to the table) |
| :-- | :-- |
| **THE WALL** | Your name is on the toll manifest as the **escort** for the whale's last cargo. Whoever you are now, you signed for that animal reaching the Reach. |
| **THE LEDGER** | You **quoted the price of this carcass out loud**, in the Hanging Market, in front of a Guild claim-runner. Every number in this Vector is now your number. |
| **THE RIDER** | You were **riding the whale** when its gut locked. You are the only witness to the moment it died, and you have not told anyone what you saw. |
| **THE EYES** | Someone in Tallow Ring paid you **in advance** to bring back one specific thing. It is not the most valuable thing on the carcass. |

## 5.2 THE FOUR HANDS (four Level-1 organisms, printed from the Atlas's own builds, ATLAS §5.2)

These are not simplified characters — they are **the Atlas's worked builds with the arithmetic already done**, so a new player starts in ninety seconds and a veteran can check the math. Each hand carries one **Lesson flag**: the rule that organism exists to teach.

**H1 · THE WALL — Immunan Phagocyte Guard** *(Atlas build 1, "the wall of the Second Flow")*
Array 4,4,3,3,2 → **VSC 4 · AFF 5 · DFF 3 · OSM 3 · RTC 1**. Hull 6.
MI **34** (**39** with the Wrap's +5) · ATP **10** · PP **5** · MT **13** · Flow **6** Drifts · Countdown **9** boxes¹ · Slots 1.
Pathways: Pressure Tolerance 2, Membrane Penetration 1, Vigour 1, Chemoreception 1. Kit: **Chitin Flail** (15 S, Kinetic BD 4, +1 pool, Braced) · **Lipid Weave Wrap** (20 S, soak 1).
Pool you will use: Membrane Penetration **4+1 = 5d** (homeostatic 15). Oath boxes: 5.
**First move (say this in the first fight):** *"I put myself between."* Engulfment and Bulwark exist: the Guard is the only organism in the book who can remove an enemy from a fight **without killing it** — and in this Vector, "say who you want alive" is a plot move, not a mercy.
**Lesson flag:** **Soak, and the shape of damage.** The Wall is *supposed* to be hit; the table learns that being hard to hit (MT) and hard to hurt (soak) are different purchases (CORE §4.8).
¹ *The Atlas prints Countdown 8 for this build; the printed formula (VSC 4 + ⌈Hull 6 ÷ 2⌉ + 2) gives 9. Take 9 and tell the player why (§16.4).*

**H2 · THE LEDGER — Epithalian Amino Acid Smith** *(Atlas build 2, "ledger of the Ash market")*
Array → **RTC 4 · VSC 5 · DFF 2 · OSM 3 · AFF 2**. Hull 4.
MI **36** · ATP **16** · PP **4** · MT **12** · Flow **5** · Countdown **9** · Slots 1.

**Lesson flag:** **the harvest economy** — Specimen grades C/G/P/T, Substrate, Bulk, and the fact that a body is a bank (CORE §2.4).

Array → **DFF 5 · OSM 4 · RTC 3 · AFF 3 · VSC 1**. Hull 4.

# 6 · THE LESSON SPINE — HOW THIS VECTOR TEACHES

This is the chapter that makes the Vector a **teaching game** rather than an easy one. The engine's five core loops are taught one per movement, each one *paid for*, and none of them arrives before the scene that needs it. Play the movements in order for your first table; the sites are written so that order is also the natural route through the whale.

## 6.1 The disclosure order (what is in play, and what you must not say yet)

A new player can hold **three numbers**. Give them three, use them, then add two more. Everything in this Vector can be run with the left column only.

| Movement | Rules in play | Rules you **hold back** |
| :-- | :-- | :-- |
| **1 · THE TOLL-VENT** | the one roll (pool, AT, Inhibitors→Stress, Overflow); the Threshold ladder 8/12/16; **Homeostatic Baseline**; bands and Drifts; contested reactions; talking as a real action | PP, MT, Soak, damage types, Dormancy, Cohesion, Oaths, Specimen grades. Do not *name* them yet. |
| **2 · THE SOFT FIELD** | + harvest checks (AT = tier × 4 + 8), Specimen grades C/G/P/T, Substrate, **Bulk**, group attempts, **Overdrive / Inhibition**, Cohesion (1 point = pass a die) | Bursts, graft families, conditions, Countdown boxes |
| **3 · THE RENDER HALL** | + the whole **Pulse War**: Frequency, PP and the Action Matrix, Reserve and Reactive Osmosis, MT vs Soak, damage types, hazards at Cycle end, **Stress bands**, **Dormancy / Breach / Countdown / stabilising** | Baseline purchases, Gene-Debt, Relics, Mass Conflict, anything above Tier 2 |
| **4 · THE BROOD OF THE NOT-WHALE** | + the social layer: **three registers**, the **Stakes Ladder**, **Oath boxes**, Standing rungs, mood rungs, buying information by chemotaxis; Clock rewinds | new combat rules — none. Let them *use* what Movement 3 taught. |
| **5 · THE GUT STONE** | everything, at once, on a schedule. Then **Growth**, on camera (§6.5) | — |

**The rule behind the rule:** never hand a player a rule they will not use in the next scene. If a player asks about something in the right column, say *"that is real, and it matters in the stomach"* and write their question on a card. They will ask again in Movement 3, and the answer will land.

## 6.2 The five lessons

### LESSON 1 · THE ROLL — *"roll the dice, beat the number, the ones cost you, the extra is yours"*
**The rule, in words a twelve-year-old gets:** you roll a handful of six-sided dice; the number of dice is *how good you are at that thing*, not how lucky you feel. Add them up. Beat the number the Director said. Every **1** is an **Inhibitor**: it scores nothing and it marks **1 Stress** on your body. Whatever you beat the number by is **Overflow**, and Overflow is never wasted — it makes the thing *bigger*.
**The numbers they will actually use:** AT **8** common · **12** standard · **16** severe (CORE §1.2 — the ladder does not move because you are new; *you* get more dice). Typical Level-1 pool: **4–6 dice**. Baseline shortcut: an unhurried, unopposed task with a pool of 4 automatically succeeds at AT 12 (Baseline = dice × 3, CORE §1.6).
**Rehearsal (an NPC pays for it):** the drone at the toll arch rolls in front of the table to open the wound lip: 5d vs AT 12, one 1, total 17, Overflow 5 — *"the flap tears wide enough for three of you."* Then the Ledger points out that the drone is now **1 Stress** and does not care.
**The test (a PC):** the crossing of the wound lip, AT 12, viscosity 1. Success moves them in; failure is a **Cost**, not a wall: *you get across, and the panel groans; mark CARCASS +1* (CODEX §2.3: Cost / Turn / Complication / Reveal).
**Say it out loud:** *"Six dice, beat twelve. What did you beat it by?"*
**Paid for:** a player who **names** Overflow and spends it buys the outcome they described (a wide hole, a clean cut, a promise) — and 5 Codon. A player who uses Overflow to solve a problem you did not write: 10 Codon.
**They've got it when:** someone says, before rolling, *"if I get big Overflow I'm taking the whole panel, not just a hole."*

### LESSON 2 · THE BODY IS A BANK — *three accounts, and you cannot trade them freely*
**The rule:** **MI** is your body's containment (spend it and you leak); **ATP** is your energy (spend it and you cannot catalyse); **Stress** is heat in the system (and at 3 it starts taking dice away). A Level-1 organism has roughly **20–36 MI**, **10–16 ATP**, **Stress 0**.
**The numbers:** MAX MI = 12 + (VSC × 4) + Hull (CORE §5.1). ATP max = 8 + (RTC × 2), **+2 at the end of each Cycle, +1 more if you spent none** (CORE §2.1). Stress: **0–2** nothing · **3** −1 die · **5** −2 dice · **6** −3 dice and ATP costs +1 · **7** a roll on the Cascade table · **8** Autophagic Collapse: 2 Countdown boxes, Stress drops to 5 (CORE §5.8). Clearing: −1 at the end of a Cycle in which you spent no ATP and took no damage · **Vent** (1 PP, 2 ATP) = −2 · a **Reconstitution** clears all of it (and heals MI = VSC + Hull, not all of it).
**The two universal levers** (CORE §1.8 — teach these in Movement 2, because they are how a *player* beats a bad roll without cheating): **Overdrive** — 4 ATP for **+2 dice**, or 4 MI for **+4 dice** (marks 1 Stress; never twice on one roll). **Inhibition** — 3 ATP + 1 **held** PP to force a re-roll of one chosen 6 or subtract 2 dice.
**Rehearsal:** a clearance-crew worker Overdrives to hold a pump hose (4 MI for +4 dice), succeeds hugely, and takes 12 damage from the burst hose. The table sees the price before they pay it.
**The test:** the first gas pocket. Somebody will Overdrive. Let them, and say the number out loud as it happens.
**Say it out loud:** *"What are you paying with?"*
**Paid for:** each time a player *chooses* which account to pay from, 5 Codon. Once per session, a player who **Vents** instead of swinging pays their own lesson fee and gets 10 Codon.
**They've got it when:** a player says *"I'm at Stress 3, I'm down a die, I should Vent"* — unprompted, in a fight.

### LESSON 3 · THE HARVEST — *"a body is a bank, and the Expanse does not have garbage"*
**The rule:** harvesting is an **action**, not a treasure chest. It costs **1 Bulk** and needs an **OSM or RTC check at the creature's tier × 4 + 8** (CORE §2.4). You get: **Substrate (S)** always, and **Specimens** by grade — **C** common tissue, **G** greater organ, **P** pristine sequence, **T** terminal codon. Specimens are what buy your next ability (CORE §9.9).
**The numbers:** harvest check for Tier 1 material = **AT 12** · 1 Specimen ≈ **5 S** · every **20 S** of loose goods = **1 Bulk**, and Bulk carried = **VSC × 5** · Tier-1 wealth is **30–60 S earned per Level**, and a Level-1 organism starts with **40 S** (CORE §9.2).
**Rehearsal:** a Guild crew harvests a Fold Sentinel in front of the Cohort, and the Ledger watches them get **three C and one G** out of a two-BSP creature in one action, because they had the tool, the time, and rank-2 Synthesis.
**The test:** the Soft Field. A Smith with Synthesis 2 has a pool of 6d and Baseline 18: **they cannot fail an unhurried harvest of Tier-1 tissue.** Say that to the player, out loud. It is the moment a new player realises the game *rewards competence instead of punishing it*, and it is why this Vector starts with a harvest.
**Say it out loud:** *"What do you cut, and what do you leave, and can your cart hold it?"*
**Paid for:** 15 Codon the first time the Cohort **harvests a kill instead of walking past it** — and HUNGER −1, which is the same lesson in the world rather than on the sheet.
**They've got it when:** someone asks *"what's the Bulk cost of that?"* before they pick it up.

### LESSON 4 · THE PULSE WAR — *"nobody waits their turn; you pay to act"*
**The rule, taught in four sentences:** at the start of your turn you get **PP = 2 + DFF**. Everything costs PP: **Drift 1 · Vent 1 · Sense 1 · Strike 2 · Synthesise 2 · Burst 3 or more**. Hold **1 PP** and you get one reaction in somebody else's turn: **Deflect** (your MT becomes +4 against one attack), **Inhibit**, **Brace** (halve Kinetic/Osmotic, ignore push), **Shed**. Unspent PP are gone at the end of the Cycle. Attacks do not roll against a defence pool: you roll your pool and beat the target's **MT**, and damage is **Overflow + Effect Value + apparatus BD − soak, minimum 1**.
**The numbers:** MT = **10 + DFF** (so 13–15 for these hands) · soak: light 1–2, medium 3–4, heavy 5–6, viscous fluid 1 · a Tier-1 **Standard** foe is **MT 13 · MI 22 · BD 2 · soak 1 · 4d · PP 3**; a Tier-1 **Swarmling** is **MT 12 · MI 10 · BD 1 · soak 0 · 3d · PP 2 · Segments 6** · hazards resolve **at the end of the Cycle, after everyone** (CODEX §3.4, *Hazards act too* — so the Cohort gets one Cycle to react; this is a kindness, use it).
**Bands** (the only positioning rule that matters at Level 1): **Contact 0 · Near 1–3 · Mid 4–9 · Far 10–20 · Loose 21+** Drifts. **Momentum:** Drift 3+ Drifts in a straight line and Strike in the same Cycle = **+2 dice, and the damage becomes Kinetic** (+3 if you started inside a current) — the Rider's whole personality, printed.
**Swarms, taught once, remembered forever:** a swarm is not one creature. A hit removes **⌈Overflow ÷ 3⌉ + 1 Segments**, and a swarm gets **one Strike per 2 remaining Segments, to a maximum of three**. *Area effects are its doom.* A player who hits a Gnaw Swarm with 9 Overflow removes 4 of its 6 segments and understands, permanently, why big Overflow beats small attacks.
**Rehearsal (this is the one that saves a table):** in the Render Hall, the Hegemony foreman's worker **Osk** takes a Cytoplasmic Bleeder hit that exceeds his remaining MI by 8+: **Breach** → Dormancy and a Countdown box, in front of the Cohort, at a Threshold you announced. Then the fight continues for one Cycle while the table notices he is lying there, and then someone stabilises him: a **Synthesise** action at Contact, **RTC + Synthesis vs AT 12 + Level**, and **MI restored = Overflow + 4, and one Countdown box closed** (CORE §5.4). The Cohort has now *saved a life with a rule*. Do not let this be skipped: **do not let Osk die**, and if the Cohort does not stabilise him, the foreman does, angrily, and tells them what they failed to do.
**Say it out loud:** *"Two Pulse Points. Do you want to Strike, or do you want to hold one?"*
**Paid for:** 5 Codon whenever a player *holds* a Reserve and says why. 10 Codon whenever a player uses terrain, a band shift, or Momentum to change a fight instead of adding a Strike.
**They've got it when:** a player asks *"can I Drift three and hit in the same Cycle?"* — which is the correct question, and the answer is yes, with +2 dice.
**And the kindness, printed here so the Director cannot forget it:** **0 MI is Dormancy, not death.** A Dormant organism can always be stabilised by an ally while one member can act (CORE §5.11). If the whole Cohort is going down, **the Director asks: *who arrives?*** In this Vector the answer is almost always Foreman Vesk's clearance crew, and their arrival is a scene, not a rescue.

### LESSON 5 · THE LAW AND THE LEDGER — *"the hardest thing in this Vector has a stamp"*
**The rule:** a social scene is **three exchanges**, each one a contested roll, and the **Overflow decides how far the other side moves** on the Stakes Ladder: **Refusal → Concession → Compliance → Binding → Conversion**, at **4 Overflow per rung** (CORE §11.1–11.2). You never "roll to persuade": the Director states what success *buys* first.
**The three registers** (pick the one the fiction is in): **SEMANTIC** (facts, trade, contracts): your **AFF + Signal Discourse** vs their **OSM + Chemoreception** · **AFFECTIVE** (status, threat, comfort): **AFF + Symbiosis or Vigour** vs their **VSC + Pressure Tolerance** · **DECEPTIVE** (lying, forgery, false scent): **DFF + Concealment** vs their **OSM + Chemoreception**, and *any* antigen register they can check against.
**Buying information:** name what you want, pay **5–50 S in fresh chemical samples**, roll **AFF + Signal Discourse at AT 10–20**. Success is a true answer; Overflow is a second, related secret; **failure is a confident wrong answer** (CORE §8.6) — which is the best engine in the world for a table of beginners, because a wrong answer is still a decision.
**Oaths are not flavour:** encoding an Oath takes a **Chemotactic Rite** (1 Fugue Interval, 10 S, one witness with OSM 3+) and marks **Oath boxes = AFF**. **Keeping** an Oath that costs you something real: **+1 Cohesion per scene**. **Breaking** one: **2 Countdown boxes, the witness faction drops a rung, and a scar**. Oath-breaking is a plot, not a punishment (CORE §11.4).
**Cohesion** is the group's shared metabolism (0–10). Spend **1** to pass a die (+1 die to anyone's pool), refuse a Stress mark yourself, add +2 to a Frequency Check, or wake a bonded symbiote. At **Cohesion 0** every Baseline is −1. Gain it by keeping Oaths, shared meals (**Chemotactic Rite: 3 S each, +2 Cohesion, all Stress to 0**), and carrying someone's body out.
**Rehearsal:** the Chaperone Drones. Order II *"will negotiate"* (BESTIARY), and the drones want **paperwork**: a toll in Specimens, and a registration entry for the brood. Run it as three exchanges with the Stakes Ladder written where everyone can see it. A Concession is a discount; a **Binding** costs the Cohort an Oath box, and a new player who has just *spent an Oath box on purpose* has understood this world better than most veterans.
**Say it out loud:** *"What does success buy here? Say it, then roll."*
**Paid for:** 20 Codon for a social scene that **changes a Clock** without anyone drawing an Apparatus. 10 Codon for an Oath that costs the Cohort real money or real risk and is kept anyway.
**They've got it when:** the table argues about the *claim* while the whale is rotting, and nobody reaches for a dice pool.

## 6.3 The rehearsal mechanics (how to teach without lecturing)

1. **Sixty-second creature rule** (CODEX §13.2): before any foe appears, say the block in three sentences: *what it wants · what it does · what it is afraid of.* Never read a stat block aloud.
2. **Announce the shape of a fight before it starts:** *"Three of them, in the enzyme haze, and the floor is dissolving. If you get past them, the pump is behind them. This one can put someone down."* A Deadly fight announced is a plan; a Deadly fight sprung is a grudge.
3. **The first Cycle in slow motion.** In the first Pulse War, run Cycle 1 aloud as narration: *"The Bleeder reaches for the Wall — Wall, you're up: five PP. Do you want to Strike for two, or hold one and see what it does?"* Do not enforce order rigidly in Cycle 2. By Cycle 3 nobody needs it.
4. **Say the numbers as they happen:** *"that's Overflow 9, so it takes four segments off the swarm."* New players learn the engine from the Director's vocabulary, not from the book.
5. **Never roll for something the players could have handled by talking, and never let a plan that is good in the fiction be blocked by a number.** Use the Homeostatic Baseline constantly: a prepared, unhurried specialist **just succeeds**, and the world gets to be believable without dice.
6. **End every fight with three questions** (house, in the spirit of CODEX §10.1's *"a session should pay in at least three of the four currencies"*): *what is harvestable? what did it announce? what is the Cohort's state?* The first two teach the economy; the third teaches the sheet.

## 6.4 THE FIRST CYCLE, PRINTED (read this aloud if you have never run a Pulse War)

> **Setting:** the wound lip. Two Gnaw Swarms (6 Segments each) in the torn flap; the drones' customs fold at Near.
> **Detection:** nobody is hiding. Skip the contest. **Frequency** (`d6 + DFF + Pulse Discipline`, CORE §4.1): Eyes d6+4 = 10, Wall d6+3 = 9, Rider d6+6 = 11, swarms d6+0 = 5. Order: Rider, Eyes, Wall, swarms.
> **Rider** (7 PP): **Drift** for 1, closing the Near band to Contact and bringing the Wall with you — *towing, in this Vector, costs the Rider their straight line: no Momentum this Cycle, and that is a price the player chose.* Holds 1 PP. 5 left.
> **Eyes** (6 PP): **Sense** for 1 — *"one swarm is in the flap, the other is under the lip and it is drinking, not hunting. Offer it water and it leaves."* 5 left.
> **Wall** (5 PP): **Strike** for 2: Chitin Flail is a Weight apparatus, so pool = **VSC 4 + Membrane Penetration 1 + apparatus +1 = 6d** vs swarm **MT 12**. Dice: 6,5,4,4,2,1 → **one Inhibitor: Stress 1**. Total 21, **Overflow 9**. Damage = Overflow 9 + BD 4 − soak 0 = **13**. Kinetic consequence, and the Wall takes the printed option: **push 1 Drift per 4 Overflow = 2 Drifts**, so the swarm comes off the lip into the **Near** band. Swarm damage: **⌈9 ÷ 3⌉ + 1 = 4 Segments removed → 2 left.** The Wall spent 2 PP on the Strike and **1 on Reserve**, leaving 2 PP to be lost at the end of the Cycle — which is the next line's point.
> **Swarms** (2 PP each): the swarm on the lip now has 2 Segments, so it gets **one Strike** (1 Strike per 2 Segments). It hits the Wall for BD 1; the Wall's **Lipid Weave Wrap** soak 1 leaves the printed **minimum 1** damage. MI 34 → 33. *This is the moment to say out loud: "soak is why you bought the wrap."*
> **The held Reserve, spent in the same Cycle:** the Wall kept **1 PP** on its turn, and when the second swarm climbs toward the Rider — still inside this Cycle — the Wall spends it on **Deflect**: the Rider's MT against that one attack becomes **15 + 4 = 19**, and the swarm's 3d cannot reach it. *This is the rule the whole transcript exists to teach:* **unspent Pulse Points are lost at the end of the Cycle, and a held Reserve is the only thing that lets you act on somebody else's turn.**
> **End of Cycle 1.** Hazards resolve last: the weeping current pushes everyone 1 Drift.
> **Cycle 2** is the Rider's idea: Drift 3 + Strike = **Momentum, +2 dice, Kinetic**, and the last swarm is driven into the drones' **air-lock** — Order II keeps its paperwork dry, so this is a **Dry Zone**, and a dry swarm **does not re-form Segments** (BESTIARY: Dry Zones are a Gnaw Swarm's weakness; CORE: +1 Segment per Cycle *unless Dry*). Fight over in two Cycles: one Stress mark, one swarm driven off, one swarm **harvested** for **C3 / G1** and 1.5 Codon, and the Cohort has learned Lesson 4 without a lecture.

**What to notice:** nobody ever "took their turn" in seat order; the fight ended because of **Overflow, a band shift, and a held point** — not because of a big number. That is the game.

## 6.5 THE GROWTH, ON CAMERA (the sixth lesson, and it is the last one)

Somewhere around the Reconstitution between the Render Hall and the Brood (roughly 40–60 Codon each — check the ledger in §12.1), a Level lands. **Do not hand out a Level in a footnote.** Run it as a scene at the hearth:

1. Read each player their total and the ladder: Level 2 at **40**, Level 3 at **100**, Level 4 at **180** (CORE §2.3). Say *"Codon is never spent. A Level is where the total has carried you."*
2. Say the mechanical gift out loud: **+2 max MI**, **+1 Bulk capacity**, and your Phenotype's feature for the new Level, **plus one Synthesis Choice** — a Pathway rank (60 S to rank 2), a Baseline grade (100 S **and** one P Specimen), a Burst, an Adaptation, or a creature's harvest trait from a Specimen you are holding (CORE §9.9).
3. Then ask the question that makes it stick: **"what is different about your body now, in one sentence?"** Write the sentence on the sheet next to the number. A new player who can say *"my blade arm has a second joint"* has stopped playing a character sheet.
4. Ask what Specimen they are holding and whether it enables that sentence. This is the loop the CORE says is deliberate: *harvest → Specimen → the ability that survives the next thing* (CORE §9.9). In this Vector the Ledger will have plenty; make sure the Wall has something too, or the economy teaches the wrong lesson.

**Do not teach Level 4 mechanics before they have Level 3.** Bursts beyond Tier I, graft families beyond their own, Gene-Debt, Mass Conflict: all of it waits.

## 6.7 The three questions when a player is lost

Say these, in order, at the table, out loud. They take nine seconds and they are the whole tutorial:

1. **"What is your Threshold?"** — the Director names it. The player never guesses.
2. **"What does your body have left?"** — MI, ATP, Stress, PP. Read them off the sheet *for* them the first three times.
3. **"What do you want to be true?"** — then build the pool together from that sentence. If the answer is *"I don't know"*, use the Atlas's printed ladder (ATLAS §5.5): **Vent · Drift to a better band · Sense · spend Cohesion to pass a die to someone else · then Strike. Never "wait."**

## 6.8 When to stop teaching

**Two new rules per movement, maximum.** If a movement needs three, one of them is not needed. If a player is quiet, the Clock advanced wrong, not the player: check whether the last three scenes could all be answered by the same organism. If the answer is yes, put the others' lesson in front of them (the Hands each carry a Lesson flag in §5.2 for exactly this reason). And when the table has clearly got a lesson, **say so, take the Codon off the table, and move on.** A teaching Vector that keeps teaching after the lesson is learned is a school, not a game.

## 6.9 Four cards, printed for the table (one per player, plus one spare)

Print these. A beginner holding a card plays the game; a beginner listening to you explain it plays your explanation. They are all player-facing, they contain nothing secret, and each one is a rule the books already print.

**CARD 1 · WHAT YOU CAN DO, EVERY CYCLE**
*Your points:* **PP = 2 + DFF**, spent fresh each Cycle, **unspent points are lost**. You also get **one free thing**: a short signal, one step, dropping something, or one word to a bonded ally.
**Drift 1** (move ~2 m; your Flow is **3 + DFF** Drifts; three Drifts in a straight line then Strike in the same Cycle = **Momentum: +2 dice and the hit becomes Kinetic**) · **Vent 1** (−2 Stress, costs 2 ATP) · **Sense 1** · **Strike 2** (a second Strike the same Cycle costs 2 more) · **Synthesise 2** (craft, heal, or stabilise an ally) · **Burst 3–5** (your Phenotype's big thing; costs the same in ATP) · **Reserve 1, held** (this is the heartbeat: spend it *during somebody else's turn* to **Deflect** +4 to your MT, **Inhibit**, **Brace** to halve Kinetic and Osmotic and ignore a push, or **Shed** a condition).
**Never "wait."** If you have nothing to say: **Vent · Drift to a better band · Sense · pass a die using Cohesion · then Strike.**

**CARD 2 · HOW A ROLL WORKS, AND WHAT IT COSTS**
Roll **N d6**, add them. **Every 1 is an Inhibitor: it counts as 0 and marks 1 Stress.** Beat the **Threshold** on the table (**8** common · **12** standard · **16** severe · **20** extreme · **24** nucleoid · **28** apotheotic). **Beat it by 4 or more and you buy something extra** — more damage, more distance, a secret, an extra hole. **No 1s and two or more 6s = Runaway Catalysis: your effect happens twice.** Failing is never "nothing": you pay a **Cost**, lose a **Turn**, eat a **Complication**, or learn a **Reveal**. **Ask for the Threshold before you roll. It is a published number and the Director is not allowed to move it.**

**CARD 3 · WHEN SOMETHING GOES WRONG WITH YOUR BODY**
**0 MI is not death.** You are **Dormant**: no actions, you cannot sense past Contact, your Baselines count as **1** for saves. You are **Leaking**: −1 MI and **+1 Countdown box** each of your turns. **Anyone at Contact can stabilise you** with a **Synthesise** action: **RTC + Synthesis vs AT 12 + your Level**, restoring **Overflow + 4 MI** and closing **one box**. A hit that beats your remaining MI by **8 or more** is a **Breach**: Dormancy *immediately*, plus a box. Fill all your boxes and that is **Total Lysis** — and then your body is harvestable, and somebody will. **Stress:** at **3** you lose a die from everything, at **5** two, at **6** three and your ATP costs go up, at **8** you collapse. **Vent clears 2. A rest clears all of it. Do not get to 5.**

**CARD 4 · WHAT THINGS ARE WORTH**
**1 Substrate (S) = 1 MI, out of combat only.** **20 S of goods = 1 Bulk**, and you carry **VSC × 5** Bulk — the rest is left behind, and that is a decision you make on purpose. **Harvest a kill:** a Synthesise action or ten minutes, costs **1 Bulk**, needs an **OSM or RTC check at AT 12** for Tier-1 things; a failure turns the sample into plain Substrate. **A corpse is worth more than most live things, and everyone can smell that you know it.** **A Coagulant Pack (14 S) stabilises an ally with no check and closes one box** — buy one tonight. **A stamped cut is worth money; an unstamped one is worth a fight.**

**Tonic is a rule, not weather:** working in the wrong tonic is −1 to −2 dice (CORE §1.3). Say it every time they cross into hypertonic fluid, and let them *answer* it with a Mucoid graft, a Turgor Shot, or by going around.

## 7.2 Autolysis: harvesting a site instead of a creature

The books price the harvest of a *creature*; they do not print a rule for harvesting a **site the size of a district**. Here is one, and it obeys the printed formula: **loose, unresisting tissue is Tier 1 material, so the harvest check is AT 12** (tier × 4 + 8), and the **grade** of what you get depends on the CARCASS Clock, not on the roll:

| CARCASS | Best grade available from the soft cut | What the check is really for |
| --: | :-- | :-- |
| 0–1 | **C** freely, **G** with a good cut (4+ Overflow) | finding the good panels |
| 2–3 | **C**, and **G** only in sealed chambers | finding a chamber the gas has not reached |
| 4–5 | **C** at best; **G** costs a fight with the hazard | salt, cold, and time |
| 6 | nothing | the whale is not a resource any more, it is a crime scene |

**The whale's bulk is not an economy, it *is* the economy.** The Bestiary prints a Drift Whale's harvest as **C18 / G11 / P5 / T2 / T2**, and says what that means: *"a Whale harvest feeds a colony for four Tides and starts a war over who owns the ground it died on."* A Drift Whale is Tier 4 and its full harvest is worth more than Second Flow's water tally; the Cohort can carry a rounding error of it (Bulk = VSC × 5, so the Wall carries 20 Bulk ≈ 400 S of goods, the Rider 5 Bulk ≈ 100 S). Print that number at the start of Movement 2 and watch the table do arithmetic: **Bulk is not a limit on treasure, it is a limit on greed you can physically act on.** What they *legally* sell is decided by the claim (§9), which is why the law is worth more than the cart.

## 7.3 Five hazards, in the printed grammar (Discovery AT / Trigger / Counter)

A hazard is **chemistry with a trigger**, priced at `damage = 4 + (2 × tier)` and `save = 8 + (2 × tier)`, and **always gets one exploitable property** — *"a hazard without an exploit is just weather"* (CORE, *Building a hazard in three steps*).

| Hazard | Discovery | Trigger | Damage / effect | Counter (print it, players find them) |
| :-- | :-- | :-- | :-- | :-- |
| **GAS POCKET** (T1) | Sense, Chemoreception AT 12: *"the floor is humming"* | a Kinetic hit, a Strike, Momentum | **6 Kinetic, Jostled, 1 band shift** to all in the chamber | vent it slowly (Synthesise, 2 PP); ride it (Brace for the push, gain 2 Drifts free) |
| **SLOUGH** (T1) | free; it is visible after CARCASS 1 | standing in it | viscous fluid: **soak 1 for you, −1 Drift per Move**; at CARCASS 3+, 1 Lytic damage per Cycle | cross on the hanging panels (Mid band the whole way); salt it |
| **ENZYME FIELD** (T2, BESTIARY Abiotic) | Sense AT 14: the haze has a *sheen*; integrity 50 | entering Mid band; a Strike inside it | **8 Lytic**; −1 soak until repaired (max −3) | it has no Frequency and cannot be reasoned with; **drain the chamber** (the pump!) or walk the dry ribs |
| **THE PUMP** (abiotic) | free: it is loud | the clearance crew starts it | **Osmotic**: a current that drags 1 band per Cycle, Fluid Navigation or be swept | close the valve (VSC 12 AT contest); or *use it* to move the Cohort for free |
| **THE RENDER CHARGE** (§11, THE RENDER CHARGE) | known; it is on a Clock | TOLL 5 | 20 Kinetic, 2-band shift, the carcass ends | not survived. **Raced.** |

## 7.4 Time, and what a Beat costs here

A **Metabolic Beat** is about a day; a **Fugue Interval** is an hour; a **Cycle** is six seconds (CORE §8.1). **The whole Vector runs in roughly 4 Beats.** Say this in Scene 1: *"the charge is set, the guild's schedule says four days, the whale has less."* Then the Clocks are honest: CARCASS 6 and TOLL 5 fit inside 4 Beats only if the Cohort works, and each **Reconstitution is a Fugue Interval and costs a CARCASS segment.** That single trade — *rest costs value* — is the Vector's teaching masterpiece, and it will make players choose to be uncomfortable, which is the Expanse in one decision.

## 7.5 What a Level-1 Cohort can afford (so the harvest *means* something)

Four Level-1 organisms start with **40 S each** (160 S together) and Tier-1 play earns **30–60 S per Level per organism** (CORE §9.2). A **Chitin Flail is 15 S; a Peptide Blade 20 S; a Lipid Weave Wrap 20 S; an Amino Drip 6 S; a Coagulant Pack 14 S** (it stabilises without a check and closes a Countdown box — *tell the Ledger this exists before the Render Hall*). A **Harvest Claw is 90 S** (Lash, Contact, Kinetic BD 2/4/6/8/10) and is the single best purchase in this Vector: **Harvest checks +2 dice, and one free Harvest per Pulse War** (CORE §9) — which is a Clock rewind disguised as a tool. A **licence loan** of 100 S at 5 S per Beat is the Atlas's own advice for a first character, and in this Vector the Hegemony will offer it *twice*.

# 8 · THE WHALE, AND THE FIVE SITES

**The anatomy, in the order the Cohort meets it.** The carcass lies athwart the channel, head downstream, mouth open into the Reach. A wound 40 Drifts wide has been cut in the flank near the head. Everything the Cohort needs is **inward and upstream**: wound → soft field → stomach → pyloric channels → the stone in the gut's far curve. The route back out is the same, and at the end it is the whole game.

```
  THE TOLL REACH — 3 channels merge · 600 skiffs queued · the Guild's toll arch
        │  (the only legal way in: a stamp, a toll, or a drone's paperwork)
        ▼
  [1] THE TOLL-VENT ── the wound lip, the drone customs fold, gas pockets
        ▼
  [2] THE SOFT FIELD ── hanging lipid panels · the TALLY POST · the first crew's
        │                cut-lanes, abandoned mid-swing        ← CARCASS is priced here
        ▼
  [3] THE RENDER HALL ─ the stomach: still warm, hypertonic, churning · the
        │                Hegemony pump · the Enzyme Field · the first crew's bodies
        ▼
  [4] THE BROOD OF THE NOT-WHALE ─ warm, sweet, lit · 300+ juveniles · Nine-Folds'
        │                customs post for something that has no name
        ▼
  [5] THE GUT STONE ── a 30-Drift plug of fused print-slag, still running, stamped
                         with a serial that does not exist · and its owner
```

**Pointcrawl discipline (Cairn 2e's pointcrawls, and the Codex's spine, CODEX §9.1):** each site is one scene, has one **key fact**, and can be left at any time. Players choose the route; the Clocks punish *slowness*, never curiosity. If they skip a site, it still happened, and §9.3 says what it did without them.

## 8.1 [1] THE TOLL-VENT — the wound mouth

**What it is.** A torn flank panel the size of a square, current running through it, and — folded out of the whale's own gristle with a precision that is obscene on a dead animal — a **Chaperone Drone customs post**. Three Drifts of folded membrane, a chitinous gate, a queue of drones, and a **toll board** with prices in Specimens. Order II *"will negotiate"* (BESTIARY), and these drones have found their calling.

**How to play it.** The Cohort wants in, and the wound is legal only with **a Guild stamp, a drone licence, or a toll paid**: **2 C Specimens per body**, **15 S per body**, or **a promise** (an Oath box, witnessed by an Order II fold, which is a document that Guild courts honour). Refusing the toll and fighting is a *decision*, not a mistake — see the DANGER line — but it costs the **TOLL Clock a segment** and makes the drones' testimony available to whoever asks.

**The Lesson scenes here.** The drone's own roll at the lip (Lesson 1 rehearsal), the crossing at AT 12, and **the first hazard**: a gas pocket under the lip. *Discovery:* Chemoreception AT 12 — *"the floor is humming."* Let them walk into it anyway if they want; the **Counter is printed** (§7.3), and the Expanse's rule is that hazards are knowable, not hidden (CORE, *Building a hazard in three steps*: "a hazard without an exploit is just weather").

**DANGER.** 2 **Gnaw Swarms** + 1 **Fold Sentinel** = **6 BSP** (Standard band for a Cohort of four at Levels 1–4, CODEX §4.1). **The Fold Sentinel does not start the fight**: it demands, in chemical semiotics, that the Cohort show its claim. If the Cohort has paid or sworn, it stands aside even after a strike — Order II defend, they do not avenge.
**Wants:** drink, not violence (a Gnaw Swarm offered water leaves in one sentence). **Weakness, printed:** area effects. A Lytic Fog, a shove into the current, or the pump wash removes Segments by the handful.
**What it pays:** the toll chit (**TOLL −1**), a legal route in, the Bestiary’s harvest for each harvested swarm (C3 / G1; 1.5 Codon each), and **15 Codon** for the objective *get inside without making an enemy of Order II*.
**Tells:** bare patches of old biomass on the wound lip (the scavengers found it first); the drone fold is **facing the wrong way** — its gate points *inward*, toward the whale. They are not guarding the toll. They are guarding something from what is inside. *(First clue to the brood.)*
**Exit:** the cut-lane down (to the Soft Field), or the hole a gas pocket blew.

## 8.2 [2] THE SOFT FIELD — the licensed cut-ground, and the Tally Post

**What it is.** The good part of the whale: hanging sheets of lipid and connective tissue, cut lanes marked with Guild claim-line pheromone, tally-sleds on a cable line, and at the centre the **TALLY POST** — a station where a cut is weighed, graded, stamped, and thereby *becomes property*. **Nothing carried out of this Vector is worth anything unless it passed a post.** Say this out loud in this room, because it is the whole economy of the Expanse in one sentence, and it converts looting into a *decision with a deadline*.

**What happened here.** The first crew — Tallow Ring's *first* sale — stopped working mid-cut. Their tools are unblooded and stacked. Their sleds are empty. The post's ledger has their last entry: **CLAIM VOID — SEE NETH**. This is not a mystery to solve; it is a fact to *find*, and it teaches the table that in this world a **paper can stop a crew faster than a monster** (clues: three, all findable in one scene, §14.1).

**The problem in the room.** A pair of **Glaze Medusa** have nested in a hanging panel (*Transparent* in clear water — Chemoreception is the only reliable way to find one; its **Glaze** coats a struck target at −2 to all pools and leaves a scent every Drifter in the Reach can read **for a Tide**), and **Hijack Mites** are in the lipid sheets: a hit installs a **Lattice**, and on your next turn the Mite **spends your held Reserve PP** and heals itself 4 MI (BEST). *This is the engine teaching Lesson 4 from the other side:* holding a point is correct, and something in this whale eats holders. A Genetic hit's signature consequence can also take a Vial or a held Specimen (CORE §4.9) — the players' **harvest is a target**.

**DANGER.** 2 **Glaze Medusa** + 2 **Hijack Mites** = **6 BSP** (Standard). Add 2 more Mites (**8 BSP**, Hard-lite) once the Cohort has got Lesson 4. **Wants:** the Medusa are nesting — they are *defending*, and a Cohort that backs off and takes the far panels gets the same harvest with less blood. **Weakness, printed:** the Medusa's glaze needs line of sight and turbid fluid kills it — cut a panel loose and the water clouds (a Kinetic hit, 4+ Overflow: the lane goes opaque, and every contest in the room is −2 dice for everyone).
**What it pays:** the harvest proper (§7.2 — this is the Ledger's room; a Smith with Synthesis 2 cannot fail an unhurried Tier-1 harvest); a stamped cut is **40–120 S**, but **only if the claim stands**; the salt cache of the first crew (**60 S of salt = a CARCASS rewind**, Key 5); and **20 Codon** for getting a cut *stamped* rather than merely taken.
**The priced choice (d12, roll once when they reach the post):**

| d12 | The post's clerk offers you this | Price |
| :-- | :-- | :-- |
| 1–2 | stamp your cut under the first crew's void claim | the Ash will call it a grave-robbing; the Guild will not notice for two Beats |
| 3–4 | stamp it under Tallow Ring's second sale | free, and the *first* buyer's crew will come to collect |
| 5–6 | a Guild tally-stamp | the Guild takes a third of the haul, and **TOLL −1** |
| 7–8 | an Ash **Unwasted** seal over the panel you are cutting | the cut is slower (CARCASS −1 for that panel only) and worth **one grade more** |
| 9 | drone licence: Order II stamps it | **TOLL −1**, and the drones now consider you registered — including for what the brood needs |
| 10 | no stamp: the clerk is a client of Neth, and refuses | the cut is worth nothing legally; a **contest** or a **Bribery in samples (10–30 S)** fixes it |
| 11 | the clerk will trade: a stamp now, a favour at the Gut Stone later | you owe; the favour arrives in §8.5 |
| 12 | the post is empty, and the scale is still warm | someone was here minutes ago; **HUNGER +1**, and a **Reveal**: the pump shaft is open |

**Tells:** the sweet current coming up from the pyloric channels; a drone escort moving *into* the whale, not out; a tally-sled loaded with nothing but tools.
**Exit:** the cut-lane to the stomach, or the sled-cable back to the surface (which teaches the Cohort the route they will need in §8.5).

## 8.3 [3] THE RENDER HALL — the stomach, still running

**What it is.** The whale's main stomach, and it is **still warm**: digestion was underway when the gut locked, and four Beats later the chamber is 40 cycles of chemistry away from cooling. **Hypertonic** (−1 to −2 dice if you are not prepared), **churning** (Flow 3: every Drift is a contest if you are in the churn), an **Enzyme Field** across the mid floor (BESTIARY Abiotic, Tier 2: 8 Lytic, −1 soak until repaired, integrity 50, no Frequency, harvest nothing), and a **Hegemony pump barge** crew of nine already running a hose, draining fluid to slow the autolysis — because the clearance crew would rather sell a firm carcass than dredge a soup.

**How to play it.** This is the **Pulse War room** (Lesson 4), and it is built so that terrain is the main character: the **churn** (Osmotic drag, 1 band per Cycle unless you win Fluid Navigation), the **dry ribs** of the stomach wall (walkable, no Enzyme Field, Mid band the whole way), the **still** at the bottom (hot, contact, worth harvesting: the whale's last meal is here), and **the pump**.
**The pump is the best object in this Vector.** Seizing or opening it *drains a chamber*: **CARCASS −1**, the Enzyme Field dies with the fluid, and the whole Soft Field firms up. It is also the loudest thing in the carcass: **HUNGER +1**, and if the Guild sees the Cohort operating Hegemony equipment, **TOLL +1**. Every table should get this choice and pay for it.

**The Lesson-4 rehearsal.** The foreman's worker **Pump-Hand Osk** is in the room, on a hose, at MT 12 and MI 24, and he is *not* protected by the Cohort. On the Cycle after the Cohort does something loud (a Burst, a Runaway, a Kinetic hit near the churn), the churn takes him: a hit that exceeds his remaining MI by **8+** is a **Breach** → **Dormancy** and a Countdown box, announced the moment it happens (*"Osk is down: his membranes have gone, and he has boxes."*). Then one Cycle of silence while the table realises the game did not end, then the stabilisation (CORE §5.4: Synthesise at Contact, **RTC + Synthesis vs AT 12 + Level**, restores **Overflow + 4 MI** and closes a box). **If the Cohort fails to act, the foreman does it himself and tells them, bitterly, what they let happen.** Never let this be a lesson nobody learned.

**DANGER.** 3 **Cytoplasmic Bleeders** + 2 **Detritus Feeders** = **8 BSP**, *plus* the Enzyme Field and the churn. **Wants:** the Bleeders want the warm fluid and the plasma in it; they attach (a hold: break with a contested VSC, 1 PP) and they drink. The Feeders **Render** — any corpse in the water, including the first crew's four bodies if the Cohort left them unharvested, becomes Feeders: *"in a long fight a Feeder becomes four Feeders."* **Weakness, printed:** drain the chamber and both Orders lose their footing and their medium; a **Braced** Cohort on the dry ribs turns the fight into a shooting gallery.
**What it pays:** the **first hard fact** (the Gut Stone's location, visible from the still: a warm plug lodged in the pyloric curve); the first crew's **four bodies**, which are worth a full Tier-1 harvest each — *and the Ash will not let you take them*: **"they do not harvest a body whose Oath boxes are unspent… and they will enforce it for you whether you like it or not"** (CODEX §7.4). So the bodies are worth **a read, not a haul**, and the read is the Vector's key fact. If the Ash are not present, the Cohort may harvest them, and Reader-Behind-Nine-Urn will hear about it by the next Beat: that is a Standing rung and a Clock. And **20 Codon** for the objective *stabilise a stranger and get the fact in the same room*.
**Tells for the Gut Stone:** everything in the stomach is stone-clean except one plug, which is **warm**; the Ash read (buy it: 20 S in samples) shows the whale swallowing something that rang; the sweet current starts here.
**Exit:** the pyloric channels (warm, sweet, going up), or the pump shaft (the way out you will want later).

## 8.4 [4] THE BROOD OF THE NOT-WHALE — the cost room

**What it is.** The pyloric channels open into a chamber that should not exist in a dead animal: **warm, lit, and sweet**. Three hundred and more juveniles, blind, whale-shaped and *wrong* — six fins where a Drifter has four, no feeding-plates, and a chemoreception so active that they are **reading the Cohort's chemistry as they swim**, which is why the room feels like being asked questions. They are clustered around a warm shard of slag that broke free of the stone. Something hatched in it.

And at the chamber's gate: **Nine-Folds**, a **Chaperone Drone** elder, who has done the only thing Order II knows to do with an unregistered population — **set up a customs post and started filing a registration**. There are forms. There are *toll receipts*. The drones have been feeding the brood for two Beats and have recorded themselves as its **toll-paying guardians**, which under Guild law is a **claim**.

**How to play it.** Nothing here wants to fight. What is here wants to be **legal** — and the Cohort is the only power in the Reach that can make that happen, because a claim needs a witness, a signature, and a body that can hold paper. Run the whole room as **Lesson 5**: three exchanges, Stakes Ladder visible, and *state what success buys first*. The Wardens will register or destroy an unregistered population; the Guild will toll it or void it; the Choir will call it sacred and price the blessing; Tallow Ring will eat it (three hundred Tier-2-equivalent carcasses is more value than the entire soft cut, and Ledger-Second Sool will say so, apologetically, in this room).

**DANGER.** If the Cohort startles the brood, the **Receptor Lurker pair** that has been eating juveniles for two Beats comes: 2 **Receptor Lurkers** + 3 **Drifter Shoal** = **7 BSP** (Standard). A Lurker **reads you for free** — one Burst you know, one Vial you carry, one condition you can shed — and then **announces what it intends to do**, which is the single most useful teaching tool in the Bestiary: a new player who hears *"I will cancel your blade-arm for two Cycles"* now knows what **Preempt** is and what **Locked** costs. Its **Camouflage** reads as a neutral organism to Chemoreception at AT 14, so the Eyes' contest is a real contest. **Weakness, printed:** it must be *near* to read — blind it with **Turbid water** (cut the brood-current loose) or a **Scent Dampener**, and it becomes a mediocre brawler. **Preempt** is saved by **OSM at AT 14**, which the Eyes clear on Baseline.
**What it pays:** **25 Codon** for the objective *learn what the not-whale actually is*; a **G Specimen** that makes a Growth purchase real (§6.5); the **Choir's goodwill** (free passage, one blessing, no more); and — if the brood is registered rather than harvested — **a Name**: legal standing in the Reach, a toll-free, and the drones' testimony on demand. If it is harvested instead: **400–900 S**, **HUNGER +2**, **TOLL +1**, and the Choir's price. **Both are legitimate play. Do not moralise; charge for both.**
**The three observations (CODEX §9.4 — three ways to learn the truth, at least two must be missed):** the Eyes can *smell* that the brood's chemistry matches the slag, not the whale; Nine-Folds will say it in exchange for a registration signature (the Cohort must sign as witness, which makes them legally liable for the brood forever — print that cost); and the Ash read, if anyone bought it, shows the whale **herding** something into the Reach on purpose, which is the Act I seed in §9.4 and can be cut without breaking the Vector.
**Exit:** the pyloric duct to the Gut Stone (the shard's source, and the last fact), or out — the brood's fate stays open, and §9.3 resolves it.

**The printed gift in this room (BESTIARY, Drift Whale).** The Bestiary's own Drift Whale block runs in **phases**, and Phase 3 is not a fight: *"the passengers: the Whale's internal colony of Symbiotes defends the corpse-heart, MI 60"* — and the weakness line says the way to end a Whale problem is to **"reach Phase 3 and talk to the passengers, who have been living inside a god and have opinions about the surface."** This room is Phase 3. The **Passengers** are the whale's own internal colony: warm, organisationally terrified, four Beats into a collapse of their economy, and they were **inside the gut when the stone went down.** They are not a foe (MI 60 of *integrity of their warren*, not of anybody's body), they are an **information market with a grudge**, and they know: what the stone is, where the brood came from, and precisely which Guild officer signed the clearance. Buy what they know with **warmth** (a Cold Shock is a kindness here; the pump's outflow is a gift), **Cohesion** (they will trade for an Oath), or **salt** (they hate it, and paying in salt is an insult that costs you a secret). If the Cohort never finds them, the Gut Stone room still works; if they do, the last scene of the Vector is a **conversation with three witnesses instead of one**.

## 8.5 [5] THE GUT STONE — the yield, the owner, and the schedule

**What it is.** A **30-Drift plug of fused print-line slag, gut-crud, and salt** wedged in the far curve of the gut: the thing that killed the whale by stopping it. It is **warm** and it is **running** — inside the slag a Hegemony print-canister is still executing a job, and its serial is stamped on the casing in a format the Ash will tell you was **retired before your lineage was written**. Around it: a shard field (Difficult terrain, Kinetic, everything cutting loose), the acid halo where the canister leaks, and one exit shaft up to the Render Hall's pump.

**The owner.** Two things claim this room, and neither is evil. The **Lattice Guardian** (BESTIARY Tier 2 Elite, Order II) has been guarding the canister since it was swallowed, in the only way Order II knows: it *folded itself around the job* and considers the print run a defended structure. And a **Hegemony recovery team** under a **Protease Warden** (Tier 2 Elite) wants the canister intact and will kill anyone who is not holding a licence.

**DANGER — E5, announced as Deadly.** 1 **Protease Warden** (Tier 2 Elite: **MT 15 · MI 60 · soak 4 · BD 4 · 6d attack · PP 4 · Codon 14**) + 2 **Cytoplasmic Bleeders** (4 BSP) + 4 **Antigen Gnats** (4 BSP, Swarmlings) = **12 BSP**, against a Deadly budget of 13 for a Cohort of four at Levels 1–4 (CODEX §4.1). **Say it before it starts:** *"This is the one that can put all four of you down. Somebody's body is going to be harvested here. You may still walk away with the fact and not the stone."*
**Its printed weakness (BEST), and it is the Eyes' whole job:** the Warden's kit runs on **Marks**, and **Mark-denial breaks it** — *Masked*, *Latency* (the Eyes' Lysosan Gift, once per scene), or a scent dampener turns it into a plain bruiser with MT 15 and nothing else. **Purge** (4 PP) does 10 Voltaic to a Marked target in Mid and transfers a condition as **Locked**; **Cascade** (1 PP) lets its allies free-Strike anything it has Marked. ## 10.2 The three difficulty dials (house, for a first table — printed here so you do not fudge in secret)

CODEX forbids secretly moving a Threshold, and it is right: **difficulty never moves; the world moves.** So move the world, out loud:

1. **Dial one: the theatre.** The same foes in *dry, braced, well-lit* ground are a third easier. Say so: *"you got there first, and you picked the ground."*
2. **Dial two: the supports.** The Codex's own simulations are blunt — *elites are more dangerous than numbers* (CODEX §4.2). Dropping the Gnats from E5 takes it from 12 BSP to 8 and the fight from *Deadly* to *Hard* without touching the Warden's numbers at all.
3. **Dial three: who arrives.** If the Cohort is going down, the Codex's rule is a question, not a rescue: ***who arrives?*** (CORE §5.11). In this Vector the answer is Foreman Vesk's crew, a drone fold, or the brood itself — and their arrival **costs something**: a Clock, a favour, a debt. A rescue you charge for is not a rescue; it is a scene.

**Fight length:** 3–6 Cycles is the printed shape (CODEX §4.2), and the Codex's Monte Carlo says a Standard budget against a Cohort of four ends in ~3–6 Cycles with **0–1 Dormancies**. If your fights are running to eight Cycles, you have too many Standards and not enough terrain. If a fight ends in one Cycle, the Cohort just played it correctly — *take the Codon and move on.*

| :-- | --: | --: | --: |
| **THE WALL** | 4 | **20 Bulk** | 400 S |
| **THE LEDGER** | 5 | **25 Bulk** | 500 S |
| **THE RIDER** | 1 | **5 Bulk** | 100 S |
| **THE EYES** | 3 | **15 Bulk** | 300 S |
| **Cohort total** | | **65 Bulk** | **≈1,300 S**, *and only if they carry nothing else* |

A **Vacuole Sac** (110 S) is **+10 Bulk**, which is why the Ledger will want one and why the colony will want four.

Now the squeeze. The Bestiary prices a whole Drift Whale at **C18 / G11 / P5 / T2 / T2**, and at the printed Specimen prices (C 5 · G 25 · P 100 · T 500) that is roughly **2,800 S ≈ 140 Bulk of goods**, before you count the corpse itself, which at Vast size is **twice its size class in Bulk** and cannot be moved by anything this Cohort owns. The **soft cut** — the part still worth cutting when they arrive — is the fraction the Ledger appraises at **900–1,400 S**, and it is **going soft the whole time**.

So the Vector's economy has three clamps, and you should say all three out loud in the first site:

1. **Bulk** — they can carry about a thousand Substrate if they empty their pockets. They will not empty their pockets.
2. **Beats** — every haul out is a trip, every trip is time, and **every rest costs a CARCASS segment** (§7.4). The soft cut is not a pile; it is a *melting* pile.
3. **Stamps** — what they *legally sell* is decided by the claim (§9), and a stamped cut is worth money while an unstamped one is worth a fight. This is why the law in this Vector is worth more than the cart.

**Wanting more than you can hold is what the Clocks are for.** A table that harvests the brood has to decide who *carries* it, and carrying it means staying another Beat, and another Beat is a CARCASS segment. Every greed decision in this Vector is arithmetic, not advice.

## 12.3 Where the Levels come from (Codon thresholds: 40 → 100 → 180 → 280)

**The design intent, stated plainly:** this Vector is built to take a **Level-1 Cohort to Level 3 by the final scene**, which means landing the total between **100 and 179 Codon**. A table that fights all five rooms, takes every escalation line and turns every Key will brush **Level 4**, and that is a *reward for greed, not a bug* — run the Level-4 growth scene the same way and carry it into the next Vector. The dial is not the Threshold ladder (which never moves, CORE §1.2) and it is not foe maths; it is **how many fights end in a kill**.

| Cohort outcome | Codon | Where they land |
| :-- | --: | :-- |
| skips E4 and E5's fight, two Keys, ending C or D | ≈120–140 | **Level 3**, banked toward 4 |
| typical clear (all five sites, one fight resolved socially, three Keys) | ≈180–245 | **Level 4**, partway to 5 — the greed tax, on camera |
| every site, every fight, escalation lines, five Keys | ≈250 | **Level 4 with Level 5 in sight** — house dial: if you want them to stop at Level 3, do not pay the escalation lines and cut E5's kill (−26 Codon: win the room legally and the Codon is not there) |
| **starting at Level 2** (already banked 40) | as above | finishes at **Level 4–5**: promote E3 and E4 by the Bestiary's **Veteran** modifier (+1 pool, +1 BD, ×2 Codon) and add one Standard |
| **starting at Level 3** (already banked 100) | as above | finishes brushing **Level 5**: cut E1 and E2's fights, promote E5, and treat the Warden as *the* fight |

**Growth is on camera** (ATLAS §4.1): each Level = **+2 max MI, +1 Bulk capacity, one Phenotype feature, one Synthesis Choice**. At **Level 2** each pregen should *say* what they take — the Smith takes a Burst or a Pathway, the Wall takes *Engulfment*, the Rider takes a Drift feat, the Eyes take Chemoreception 2 — and then, with Substrate, the printed prices apply: **+1 Pathway rank 60 S** (then 150, then 400), **a new Burst 20–200 S plus the Specimen it needs**, **a harvested trait = Specimen ×4 plus a contest**, **+1 Baseline 100 S and 1 P** (CORE §9.9). *Nobody in this Vector should be Level 3 without having bought one thing out of a whale.*

# 13 · ENDINGS

Four endings, and none of them is "you win". Each is a **shape the ledger took**, and each prints what the Reach does next, because a Vector that ends without telling you what you changed is a Vector that taught you nothing.

### A · THE CLEAN CARVE
**How:** out before **TOLL** fills, at least one stamped cut, the serial read, and the brood **registered**.
**Pays:** full ledger (≈200 Codon) · **Standing:** the drones' testimony on demand (Order II, *Bound*-adjacent), the Choir *Obliging*, the Guild *Neutral* at worst, the Ash *Confided* · a **Name** in the Reach: the Cohort is registered, toll-free, and legally able to *witness* things.
**The Reach next:** the channel reopens on the Guild's schedule; the drones file the brood's registration with the Cohort's names on it; Tallow Ring pays its second buyer and defaults on the first, and Ledger-Second Sool either takes the fall or hands the Cohort the proof (which is a Vector hook for next time, not a debt to fix now).
**Epilogue question, asked out loud:** *"You signed as witness. What do you do when they come to you for the favour?"*

### B · THE RENDER
**How:** **TOLL** hits 5 and the charge fires. Everything un-shipped is worth nothing.
**Pays:** survival Codon only — **10 per organism out, 15 per body carried out** (§11) — plus whatever was already stamped and already on a sled.
**Standing:** the Guild is **Wary** (you were on the schedule; you were slow), the Ash **Wary-to-Hostile** (there is whale meat in the water and nobody asked them), the Hegemony **Neutral** (Vesk will still talk to you, and will not help you).
**The Reach next:** the blockage clears, three channels run again, six hundred skiffs move, and the Reach stops talking about the Cohort inside a Beat — which is the cruelest outcome this world has: not punishment, **irrelevance**.
**Epilogue question:** *"What did you actually carry out, and who is going to buy it?"*

### C · THE LONG LEDGER
**How:** the brood is harvested, sold, or handed to a buyer. The Cohort is rich.
**Pays:** **600–900 S**, more money than Level-1 organisms are supposed to touch — **and 0 Codon** (§11). Gear for two Levels, a Vacuole Sac, a graft each, and a debt cleared.
**Standing:** the Choir **Hostile** (they do not lie, and they will say what you did, in every colony on this cable); the drones **Hostile** and *litigious* — Order II will fold your name into a document that follows you; the Ash **Wary** and charging double everywhere you go; the Guild **Confided**, which is its own kind of stain.
**The Reach next:** a **Recession** in the brood-channels (BEST: a Shoal that stays is a Recession; a population that ends is worse) — the sweet current stops, the scavengers move on, and the panels you cut begin to fail. Tallow Ring buys the Cohort's silence with a *second* contract. Somebody in the Reach starts calling the Cohort by a Name that is not a compliment.
**Epilogue question:** *"You can afford anything now. What does the Ash charge you for a read, and do you still want to know what the whale was herding?"*

### D · THE STONE STAYS
**How:** the Cohort walks out with the **fact** and nothing else — no cut, no stone, no brood-deal, or they left it all running on purpose.
**Pays:** the smallest ledger (≈90–130 Codon: objectives, Keys, one or two fights) — and the **largest single piece of Standing**: they are the only organisms in the Reach who know what is inside the whale, and they told someone.
**Standing:** the Ash *Confided* if they brought the read; the Underground suddenly *interested* (the serial is a forgery opportunity worth more than the cut); the Hegemony *Wary* and about to pay.
**The Reach next:** the Hegemony comes for the serial. If the Cohort sold it, they have enemies with catalogue prices on their heads; if they published it, they have a Name that *means* something.
**Epilogue question:** *"You are poor and known. Which of those two is going to feed you next Beat?"*

### · A HOLE IN THE LEDGER (if someone Takes the Fall)
If an organism reaches **Total Lysis** inside this Vector — and in E5 some body very likely will be harvested — the books are blunt and this Vector does not soften them: the body is **harvestable at full value** (CORE §5.6), the Ash will want a read, the colony will want the plot, and **debt to the Hegemony is chemical and cannot be escaped by dying** (CORE §8.4; ATLAS Step 5).
The **successor** is not a new character: it is *"a continuation of the same argument by different means"* (CORE §12.2), inheriting two of a graft / a Pathway rank / a Specimen cache / faction standing one rung lower / a scar / a half-strength Bond — **and the dead's plot, clients, and debts pass to them if they perform a Mourning Rite** (the Ash's fee, CODEX §10.8: *"the Ash will not do it free, and the Cohort will pay anyway"*).
**Say this in the same voice you use for a hazard:** a Tier-1 table that watches a Total Lysis priced properly learns more than one that is rescued. And then **pay the survivor's table**: 15 Codon to the Cohort for carrying the body out, and one free Mend.

# 14 · IMPROV, ORACLES, AND THE THREE-CLUE RULE

## 14.1 Every fact in this Vector has three ways to find it

CODEX §9.4: design the puzzle to fall to **three observations**, and expect at least two to be missed. Never gate the Vector on one roll.

| The fact | Clue one | Clue two | Clue three |
| :-- | :-- | :-- | :-- |
| **The whale is already dead and someone is lying about the schedule** | the CARCASS Clock is on the table at segment 1 | the wound is a *cut*, not a bite, and the cut edges are healed over at the corners (it is older than the closure notice) | six hundred skiffs queued, and nobody on them is allowed to move |
| **Tallow Ring sold the salvage rights twice** | two claim-markers on the same panel, one under a Guild stamp | Ledger-Second Sool quoting *two* different contract numbers in one breath | the first crew's ledger at the Tally Post: **CLAIM VOID — SEE NETH** |
| **Something is alive inside the whale** | the drone customs fold faces **inward** | a **sweet current** coming up the pyloric channels | the brood's chemistry on the Cohort's coats before anyone sees the brood |
| **The stone is the thing that killed the whale** | everything in the stomach is stone-clean *except one warm plug* | the Ash read (25 S or three Cycles): the whale swallowed something that **rang** | the brood huddling around a shard of the same slag |
| **The charge is set earlier than the announced day** | Vesk will not say the Beat, and he keeps checking it | the pump crew is working **faster than the schedule needs** | the charge's firing-line runs to the *gut curve*, not the wound — the Guild means to open the gut, not the flank |
| **The Warden can be beaten without killing it** | its own block, read aloud, printed weakness | Mark-denial already in the Cohort's kit (the Eyes' *Latency*) | it **retreats to report** (BEST): break the supports and it leaves |

# 15 · RUN IT SHORT OR LONG

## 15.1 The four-hour cut (the default for a first table)
**Hire (20 min) → Toll-Vent (40 min, one fight, one Clock) → Soft Field (45 min, harvest + one fight) → Render Hall (40 min, Pulse War + Osk) → one named face and the Stone (45 min) → ending and ledger (30 min).**
Cut: **[4] the Brood** becomes a *heard-about* room (the sweet current, the drone's paperwork, one Lurker at the channel mouth) and **E5's supports** drop to the Warden plus two Gnats (8 BSP, Hard). Keep: **the Tally Post, Osk, and the ledger read-aloud.** If you cut one thing from a teaching Vector, cut content, never the accounting.

## 15.2 The five-session crawl (the same Vector at full weight)
One site per session, **one Clock segment per session per Clock** (CODEX §5.2's cap), and a **Fugue Interval** between sessions means CARCASS advances by one every time they rest (§7.4 — *rest costs a segment*, and this is the sentence that makes a new table understand pacing).
1. The Reach, the queue, the Hire, the toll → **CARCASS 1**, **TOLL 1**.
2. Soft Field, the Tally Post, E2, the first crew's ledger → **CARCASS 2**, **HUNGER 1**.
3. Render Hall, E3, Osk, the pump choice → **CARCASS 3**, and one **rewind** if they earned it.
4. The Brood, the Lurkers, the registration, Neth's claim walk → **TOLL 3**, **HUNGER 2**.
5. The Gut Stone, E5, the chase, the ledger → ending.
Between sessions, run **one d10 WHAT THE ASH SAY** and **one d6 NEIGHBOURS AT THE WOUND**. That is the whole prep for the next night, and it is four lines.

## 15.3 Five-minute prep (the Codex's own drill, CODEX §13.4, applied to this Vector)
1. Read **§0** (one page). 2. Read the three Clocks' segment tables out loud. 3. Pick **one** Hire from §4. 4. Read the **E-block** for the site you are running and the one after it. 5. Write **three clues** from §14.1 on an index card. 6. Write the four PCs' names, Baselines, and PP. That is the whole book you need to run tonight.

## 15.4 What to skip, and what never to skip
**Safe to skip:** any fight (all five sites can be won socially or with terrain), E4's Lurkers, the escalation ladder, Key 9's sibling, the Ash read, the Act I seed in §9.4.
**Never skip:** the **first fight at the Toll-Vent** (it is where Lesson 4 gets rehearsed on someone else), **the Tally Post** (without it the money is decoration), **Osk** (the Dormancy rehearsal), and the **ending ledger read aloud**. A Vector that skips those is a different, worse Vector.

## 15.5 Pacing inside a site
Each site is **two to four Beats** of in-world work and **one scene with teeth**. The Codex's spine (approach → wrong room → problem room → cost room → yield, CODEX §9.1) is already in §8's route: **[1] approach · [2] wrong room (the money, not the monster) · [3] problem room (the fight and the body) · [4] cost room (the choice) · [5] yield (the thing with an owner).** Say that sentence to your players at the end of session one. A new table that knows *where they are in the shape* stops asking *"what do I do?"* and starts asking *"what is this going to cost me?"* — which is the whole game.

## 16.2 Teaching lineage: how a new table learns, specifically

There is **no tutorial chapter in any of the four printed books**, and no disclosure ladder, no session-length advice, and no no-wipe policy anywhere in the line (see §16.4). So §6 is house material, built from these habits:

| Habit, and where it comes from | How it is used here |
| :-- | :-- |
| **Rehearse the lethal rule on an NPC first** — the standard survival-horror and action-game teaching pattern (*safe room, then twist*; the *training ground with a real hazard in it*) | §2.3's **Rehearsal Rule**: *the first time a rule would hurt you, it happens to someone else.* Osk's **Breach** in the Render Hall is the single most important page in this Vector |
| **Tell the player the number before they roll** — the published advice behind Cairn's *"only Saves"*, Dungeon World's *"the DM says what happens, then asks what you do"*, and every modern convention convention about transparent difficulty; the house version is CODEX §2.4 printing the odds table | §6.6's **Odds Card** is in the *players'* hands, and §6.3 makes the Director say ATs and Overflows out loud |
| **Name the rule you are teaching, and hide the rest** — the disclosure design of every good board-game tutorial (learn one mechanic per round; the second mechanic arrives as a *twist* on the first) | §6.1's disclosure table: five movements, five lessons, and what is **withheld** in each |
| **Give the strong thing one findable weakness** — CODEX §4.4, and the Bestiary's own discipline of printing a *Weakness* line on every block | every encounter in §10 has a weakness in its own row, and §6.3 makes reading blocks aloud the default |
| **Teach economy by making the players do the accounting** — CODEX §10.1 (*a session pays in three of four currencies*) and the Lady Blackbird school of *paying for hooks* | §12's ledger is read aloud at the end of each session, and the brood pays **0 Codon** on purpose |

## 16.3 The premise's own lineage (nobody invents a whale fall)

**The biology is real and is the mechanic.** A dead whale on the seafloor is not scenery; it is a **successional ecosystem** that runs for years in a fixed order — a mobile-scavenger stage (sleeper sharks, amphipods: strip the blubber), an enrichment-opportunist stage (polychaetes and crustaceans colonising the fatty residue), and a **sulphophilic stage** where the skeleton's lipids are chemosynthesised by bacteria and the whole community switches to a chemistry of its own, in the dark, with no sun at all. That is *exactly* this Vector's spine: **[1] scavengers at the wound → [2] the licensed strip → [3] the warm fermenting gut → [4] a population living on chemistry alone → [5] the object that started it.** The **HUNGER** Clock is carrion succession; the **CARCASS** Clock is autolysis and liquefaction; the brood is what happens when a sulphophilic stage gets *warm* and *big* and someone puts a running machine inside it. Anyone who wants the real thing, free: search **whale fall succession** and read the NOAA Ocean Explorer and Monterey Bay Aquarium (MBARI) whale-fall explainers before naming your next three rooms.

**The story relatives.** **Jonah** (the belly as a place you are put, not a place you go — and the reckoning that happens there). **Moby-Dick**, where the whale is an *industry* with a payroll, a quota, and a share price: the Tallow Ring's two sales are the same arithmetic with the names changed. **Nausicaä of the Valley of the Wind** — the Sea of Corruption is a poisoned ecosystem that turns out to be **purifying the world**, and the "monsters" are the purification: this is what the not-whale is, if the table is smart enough to ask. **Alien's Derelict** (a dead giant containing a cargo that was *being transported by someone*, with paperwork, and the engineers who lost it). **Annihilation** (the Shimmer: expansion by **grafting**, and a boundary you cross knowing you will not come out as the same organism — which is what a Growth looks like from the inside). **Ghostbusters** (an infestation handled as municipal law: the unavoidable problem is a *permit*, the villains are the city's schedule, and the funny part is the liability). And the one that should be named out loud in any game about being inside a body: **Legend of the Leukocytes** and **Magic School Bus: For Lunch**, the hobby's two great *inside* games, one of which is a children's programme.

**The law is not a gimmick either.** Real courts have granted **legal personhood to a non-human body**: New Zealand's Te Awa Tupua Act recognised the **Whanganui River** as a legal person with guardians who speak for it (2017); Ecuador's 2008 constitution granted rights to nature; the Whanganui settlement's logic — *a person with guardians, whose interests are argued by people who did not cause the harm* — is the exact legal shape of **Nine-Folds registering a brood, the Ash claiming a whale, and a Cohort signing as witness.** If a player asks whether that is a real thing, the answer is yes, and the answer is why the brood room is not a fight.

## 16.4 House rules obeyed — and the printed conflicts this Vector had to work around

**Obeyed, line by line:** a failed roll is never "nothing happens" — it is a **Cost** (CODEX §2.3) · Clocks advance on **loud successes**, never on a failed roll, and are rewindable by spending time, Cohesion, or Substrate (CODEX §5.2) · **Activation Thresholds do not move because the Cohort grew**; the fiction moves instead (CORE §1.2) · **BSP budgets a fight, Codon pays growth, and the two are never added** (CODEX §4) · one **findable weakness** on anything bigger than the Cohort (CODEX §4.4, §4.6) · **three ways to learn any critical fact** (CODEX §9.4) · every hazard prints **Discovery / Trigger / Counter** and a **tell** (CODEX §9.3; Cairn's rule) · hazards resolve at **Cycle end, after everyone** (CODEX §3.4, *Hazards act too*) · fleeing is **first-class**, with the chase subsystem printed rather than improvised (CORE §4.14) · **players roll everything that matters**, including information (CORE §1.7's group rule, and the §2.3 rule that the Director never rolls *at* them) · no creature has been reskinned: every foe in §10 and §11 is a printed **LYTIC BESTIARY** entry at its printed numbers or its printed **Veteran** modifier · **abiotics harvest nothing and have no Frequency** (BESTIARY, Abiotic) · and any number this Vector adds sits **at or above** the published value, never below.

# 17 · THE ONE THING TO REMEMBER TONIGHT

**You are not exploring a monster. You are working a deadline on somebody else's corpse.**

Four organisms, Level 1, standing in front of a thing bigger than their colony, all holding paper that says different things, while a Clock they can see eats the value out from under them. Everything else in this book is plumbing.

If the table learns five things, they have learned the Expanse:

1. **Roll the pool, beat the number, 1s are Inhibitors, the leftover is never wasted.**
2. **Hold one Pulse Point.** It is the difference between being attacked and being *in* a fight.
3. **Zero Membrane Integrity is not death.** Somebody fixes you. You learn the Countdown by watching someone else fill a box.
4. **Value is a body part, and Bulk is the reason you leave money behind.** If you did not stamp it, you did not own it.
5. **The problem in the last room has an owner.** You are allowed to walk out with the fact instead of the thing. Most tables will not. That is fine. The Ledger remembers either way.

*The whale is worth more than you can carry, the schedule does not care about you, and the smallest thing inside this carcass is asking to be legal. Cut carefully.*
