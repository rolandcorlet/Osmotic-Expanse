# The Osmotic Expanse — Society Rules and Social Norms


## At a glance

Society in the Osmotic Expanse rests on one physical fact: **water moves toward salt**. Concentration is wealth, and carrying water uphill costs a body. From that fact come four structures that the rest of this document sets out:

- **a law above every colony**, the Sacred Code;
- **a colony organised like a body**, with six organs and seven offices;
- **a register** that decides who exists;
- **a web of obligation** (Oaths, Ledger lines and mutual favours) in which every kindness and every promise is remembered.


The structure, top to bottom: The Code binds every colony. Each colony runs through its organs. The organs decide what each condition of organism may have, and the Tide moves that rank every season. Everyone not on the register falls out of the picture entirely.

This document draws on QUORUM (rev 2), the Director's Codex, the Core Rulebook and *What the Water Owed*, and folds in the sound points of the two attached drafts. Rules that only the story states are flagged in the last section.

## The Sacred Code: the seven Precepts

The Sacred Code is the oldest law in the Expanse, older than any colony. It has **seven Precepts**, and each one is enforced by a particular power. Every other rule in this document rests on one of them.

| # | Precept | What it says | Enforced by | Seen in *What the Water Owed* |
| --: | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | **Containment** | Nothing crosses a colony's membrane unread | the Wardens and the Membrane | Ket the Porter reads everyone at the gate; Corvane's hidden pipe is a crime against it |
| 2 | **Non-Waste** | Every body must be harvested, or buried in a licensed ground | the Wardens and the Ash | The Ash claim the wreck by licence; censoring a funeral wagon breaks this Precept |
| 3 | **Catalysis is debt** | A gift of synthesis must be recorded | the Ash | A Motion sworn as an Oath is *recorded*, and so becomes enforceable |
| 4 | **Tonicity is truth** | You may not lie about what you are: your lineage, your tonic, your Oaths | the Nucleolus | Masks are unlawful, except at the Swell, when custom suspends this Precept |
| 5 | **The Code is written** | Do not rewrite a region's rules, and do not cut the Sequence Forest | the Wardens; the Curators | Not in the story |
| 6 | **A body is its own argument** | Do not alter a body without its consent | the victim, through **Argument** | Not in the story; it is the basis of plasmid-theft law |
| 7 | **The Membrane is mercy** | Do not breach the world's outer membrane, or trade with what lies beyond it | the Wardens | Not in the story; the Deep Choir openly denies it |

**Two things follow from the Code.**

- **Justice is a reading.** Because Precept 4 makes a body's chemistry its testimony, an accused organism is not just questioned: it is **assayed**, read by the Lysosome. This is called *carcass law* (see Conflict and justice).
- **Some rules bend by custom, and everyone agrees to the bending.** For one Pulse Ten (10 Beats) each Tide, at the Swell, Precept 4 is suspended and masks are lawful. Nobody is lying, because everybody has agreed to pretend.

*Sources: Director's Codex §8.2; QUORUM Ch. 10, §12.1.*

## The colony as a body: organs, offices and the charter

A colony is a body, and **every organism in it belongs to an organ**. Your organ is not your job. It is your *place*: who feeds you, who you answer to, which Hearth you eat at, and who comes looking if you go Dormant in the street.

### The six organs

| Organ | What it does | Who staffs it | When it fails |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **The Membrane** | borders, gates and the register: who is *self* | Wardens, Immunans, gate-clerks | strangers walk in unread, or nobody gets in and the colony starves |
| **The Pump** | labour: carrying, pumping and building against the gradient | haulers, Flagellans, the Swollen | the water store empties and the colony swells |
| **The Vacuole** | stores, water rights and the treasury | quartermasters, the Brine Office, Epithalian stewards | hoarding, rationing, a run on the stores, riot |
| **The Nucleolus** | the council: it reads the Quorum and keeps the colony's sequence | elders, Quorum Speakers, old budlines | nothing reaches Quorum; factions rule by default |
| **The Hearth** | the commons: shared meals, Rites, healing; the place everyone is equal | Chemotactic priests, healers, Amoebans | Cohesion bleeds out; feuds; Oaths go unwitnessed |
| **The Lysosome** | waste, the dead, recycling, and **justice** | the Reclaimers' Ash, digesters, assayers | bodies go unharvested, disease, and the Ash walk out |

The Lysosome judges because in the Expanse justice *is* a digestive act. A body is read, broken down into its truths, and the useful parts are returned to the colony.

**Changing organs** takes an Induction Rite at the new organ's Hearth (1 Fugue Interval, 30 S, and a sponsor inside it). You lose your old Hearth and its gossip. Someone who changes organs twice is a **drifter**; three times, *unlisted in all but name*.

### Size

| Size | Organisms | How it is organised | Quorum threshold |
| --- | --: | --- | --: |
| Hamlet | 20–200 | the Hearth does everything; one elder | 4 |
| Settlement | 200–2,000 | all six organs, small; one organism may serve two (Sump Nine: 1,800) | 6 |
| Colony | 2,000–50,000 | all six, each with its own house and Hearth | 8 |
| Metropolis | 50,000+ | each organ is a district with organs of its own | 10 |

### The seven offices

Every organ has a head. Together the offices form the **Assembly**, which has **15 voices**.

| Office | Organ | Voices | What it may do | Its burden | In Sump Nine |
| --- | --- | --: | --- | --- | --- |
| **Registrar** | Membrane | 3 | enter and erase register lines; witness every Oath sworn in the colony | entries can be challenged | Quell (Unlisted, serving anyway), then Mesk |
| **Tally-Keeper** | Vacuole | 3 | set and audit the water tally; breaks ties in the Assembly | the water debt is theirs; a failed tally defaults the colony | Henn, in arrears |
| **Porter** | Membrane | 2 | choose who is read at which gate | every wanted organism admitted is held against them | Ket |
| **Steward** | Vacuole / Hearth | 2 | feed the colony; pay a household's arrears from the store | hard times land here first | vacant and disputed |
| **Reader** | Lysosome | 2 | funerals, last-memory readings, harvest brokerage | no harvest of a body with unspent Oaths; no Total Dissolution | Varrow |
| **Muster** | militia | 2 | call out the militia | the dead of any battle are the Muster's | Grue |
| **Printwright** | a Hegemony lease | 1 | hold the colony's leased print shift | the lease can be called in | vacant |

- **Terms.** An office lasts one Tide. Each organ re-chooses its own office-holder by a small Quorum at the **Contract Fair**.
- **Losing an office.** Breaking a charter line revokes the office that broke it. Abandoning an office writes a public Ledger line against you.
- **Ordinary business.** The Assembly decides fees, repairs and envoys in one sitting. A Motion passes with **8 of 15 voices**, and ties go to the Tally-Keeper, "because water is the colony's actual life". A vacant office's voice is cast by whoever its last holder owed most.
- **Big questions** (war, exile, the charter, selling the water) must go to a **Quorum**. An Assembly that tries to decide one alone provokes a new Issue: *who do the offices think they are?*

### The charter

Every colony has a **charter** of 3–5 founding lines, written as sentences. Sump Nine's has three:

1. No Total Dissolution inside the membrane.
2. The water tally is not negotiable.
3. Every hab feeds one nursery shift.

A short charter is easy to bend. In the story, the haulers argue the charter means the store belongs to the households and not the treasury. They win the duel on that reading, but with no Steward, nobody enforces it.

### The colony's health

- **Colony Cohesion (0–10)** is how much the organs still agree. A typical colony sits at 5; Sump Nine at 4. It rises with festivals kept well and Quorums reached honestly, and falls with stolen Quorums, bought Assays and bodies left unharvested. At **0** the colony splits apart.
- **Interior (0–8)** is the colony's unrest. At **3** comes rationing, at **5** a strike, at **7** violence inside the walls, at **8** collapse. Sump Nine reaches 5 during the Swell.
- **The arrears ladder.** A household that cannot pay its tally takes 1 Stress for one Beat unpaid. Two unpaid Beats suspend its registration for a Tide. After three, the gate closes to it.

*Sources: QUORUM Ch. 2, Ch. 3, §17.2; the story, Parts I, IV and VI.*

## Condition and class: Dry, Settled, Swollen and Unlisted

In a world made of water, **concentration is wealth**. A rich organism is dense: full of stored salt and structure, stable in any season. A poor one is dilute: thin and watery, at the mercy of the next shift in the Tide. Class is written in osmotic pressure, and it shows on the body. In the story's words: *you could see his condition from across the sump. Everyone could. That was the point of conditions.*

| Condition | Also called | Lives | Has | Lacks | Ledger lines it may carry |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --: |
| **Dry** | the dense, the salted, the Concentrate | sealed hypertonic pockets, kept steady by pumps | stores, water rights, signal to burn | the habit of labour; patience with the Pump | 7 |
| **Settled** | the even, isotonic folk | the colony's middle strata | a trade, a Hearth, a little saved | protection from one bad Tide | 5 |
| **Swollen** | the dilute, the watered | low, wet districts by the outflows | numbers, solidarity, nothing to lose | stores, signal, standing with the Dry | 3 |
| **Unlisted** | — | wherever they are not caught | invisibility to every tax and audit | every right; see Belonging | — |

**The proverb.** *Salt calls salt; water finds the floor.* The Dry and the Swollen both quote it, meaning opposite things. To be ruined is to be **diluted**; to rise is to **concentrate**.

### How condition is held, gained and lost

A condition rests on **four supports**: a **Name** (a registered chemical identity), a **licence**, a **hearth** (a home and a place at a Hearth) and a **seat** (an office or a charter).

- **Rising.** With two supports you can become Settled, and with three you can become Dry. You must also pay the new condition's upkeep for a full Tide (Swollen 0 S, Settled 10 S, Dry 40 S) and be received at a Hearth of that condition. Your old Hearth will call you *salted* behind your back.
- **Falling.** When a support is taken (a licence seized, a Name suspended, a home evicted, a seat lost), you slip a condition at the next Tide's turn, or at once if a court sentences it. Nobody needs your permission to dilute you.
- **Living costs per Beat:** Swollen 2 S, Settled 5 S, Dry 15 S.

### The Tide turns the ladder

The Osmotic Tide moves rank as well as water. The condition that suits the season rises, and every colony knows what that means.

| Season | Who rises | Who suffers | What society does |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Isotonic Rise** | the Settled | nobody much | contracts, conjugations, the Contract Fair |
| **Hypertonic Zenith** | **the Dry** | **the Swollen**: water leaves their bodies | rationing and the Salt Vigil; the Dry seal their pockets; strikes are broken |
| **Isotonic Fall** | the Settled | the old and the indebted | the Rendering; debts called in before the Swell |
| **Hypotonic Zenith** | **the Swollen** | **the Dry**: they bloat, and their pumps strain | the Swell: carnival, masks, riots, the Pump's season of power |

This is why, in the story, Sefa dies in the salty season and the haulers strike in the watery one. **Plan the brave act for the season that makes you strong.**

### What condition changes socially

- **Speaking across the ladder.** Threats travel down the ladder more easily than comfort does, and comfort travels up it more easily than threats. Plain factual speech ignores condition: *contracts do not care who is wet.*
- **Buying the air.** The Dry can pay for signal at half the cost. The Swollen are better at moving a crowd in their own district.
- **Uphill costs a body.** Water flows down for free; carrying it up costs someone's metabolism. *Downhill is free. Uphill is a body. The whole economy is the argument about whose.*

*Sources: QUORUM Ch. 5, §6.1, §9.5; the story, Parts I, III, IV and VI.*

## Belonging: the Register, the Unlisted and the Rereading

The Register is the colony. To be listed is to have rights; to be unlisted is to be *outside the world's legal body*. This is the single most important structure in the Expanse's society, and the central idea of *What the Water Owed*.

### What being listed gives you

- **A water ration.** The tally is assessed by hearth, and the store's crystal reads your ration from your line.
- **The right to sign, swear in a register, conjugate, hold office and be protected.**
- **A legal lineage.** The **birth register, not the body, decides your legal lineage.** That is why forged births, unregistered buddings and reading an infant's sequence are real crimes.

### The Unlisted

Below the Swollen is no condition at all.

- **No rights.** A Membrane may refuse them and a Warden may detain them on sight. Their contracts bind no court. They may not conjugate, because a conjugation needs a witness from each budline and they have none on record.
- **Not assessed.** No tally, licence or audit can reach them. That is their one advantage, and it is why smuggling, railroads and heresies all run through them.
- **Still able to swear Oaths.** An Oath is written in your own chromatin, and chromatin does not care about paperwork. In the story Oro and Mesk swear Oaths at the Fair when they cannot marry, because no register is asked.
- **Still able to live.** They may eat at a Hearth that will have them, work off the books, or become tenants of a Host-City, which reads scent and rent, not registers.
- **A network, not a faction.** Archivists erase entries for them, the Body Sovereignty heresy smuggles them, and the Ash quietly refuse to harvest the unlisted dead. The heresy of **Unlisted Rights** argues they should have rights.

**How you become Unlisted:** a Warden purge, a court sentence (the sentence for quenching a Quorum, for example), or being found to have no true line. **How you get back on:** at the **Rereading**, through a Nucleolus pardon, or with a forged Name, which is a crime of its own.

### Purges

A Warden circuit can strike from the register every organism it cannot read cleanly. Whole colonies can be struck at once. Oro's birth colony in the Wastes was erased this way, which is why he had to be smuggled in as a brooded seed. The Homeostatists who run the Warden Circles sum up the attitude: *We are not cruel. We are hydrated.*

### Forgery, and the sympathy it gets

Forging a register line is a crime against Precept 4. It is also common, and treated with real sympathy when it saves a life. Sefa had Oro written in as a Seven Ladders; Quell wrote the line for a debt; and nobody was ever prosecuted. Oro later refuses a second forged line: *I won't be built on a lie twice.*

### Children

A newly divided organism is a **dependent** for 1–6 Drift Seasons. It is not yet listed and exists only as a line on a parent's Ledger: *owed care*. It cannot swear Oaths. In the story the nursery buds are "the only organisms in Sump Nine who did not know what condition they were".

### The Rereading

Every **Great Cycle** (16 Tides) comes the Rereading, which lasts one Drift Season.

- **Registers are purged and old debts re-read.** Every Ledger line in the Expanse is read aloud as though it happened yesterday. Some debts are forgiven and some are doubled.
- **Every Standing is tested again.**
- **Salvage and channel claims are renewed.** A claim registered at the last Rereading can void a toll, as happens in the story's Convocation.
- **The Unlisted may apply to be listed again.** That is how Oro is finally written down, *as a citizen of this colony, with all its rights, from the day he was carried in.*

*Sources: QUORUM §5.5, §7.3, §7.5, §8.4, §12.1; Director's Codex §7.2, §8.3–8.4; the story, Parts II, V and VII, and the ending.*

## Deciding: Quorum, Speakers, quenching and Convocation

There are two ways a society decides things, and the Expanse uses both. In a **Quorum** the whole colony decides by chemistry: it is used for anything everyone must *know*, such as war, exile, the water or the charter. In a **Convocation** the people in one room decide by argument: it is used for a market, a council, a trial, a wedding or a treaty.

### The Quorum: a colony breathes

A colony does not vote. Everyone who cares about a question **breathes signal** into the Hearth air. When the air is thick enough with one answer, the colony *knows*: all of it, at once, in its chemistry. The **Quorum Speaker** reads the air and says it aloud. The Speaker announces the result; the Speaker does not decide it.

- **An Issue** is the question on the air: *open the gate to refugees? sell the water rights? let the Hegemony build?* A pressing Issue is argued Beat by Beat; a slow one, a Pulse Ten at a time.
- **Ways to move the air:** speak at the Hearth, move the crowd, **swear it** (tie yourself to the outcome by Oath), court an organ, call on a faction, or **breathe heavily**, which means paying for rendered signal.
- **It is not fair, and everyone knows it.** An organism with more ATP can breathe more signal. That is the class war of the Expanse in one sentence: the Swollen can breathe as hard as they like, but the Dry can buy the air. Buying signal is **lawful**.
- **Organs endorse.** A healthy organ backs one side of each Issue it cares about. Failing organs cannot, which is why factions break organs before a big Quorum.
- **A reached Quorum binds.** Acting against it is acting against your own colony's chemistry. Most organisms simply comply; rebels become interesting.

### The Speaker's honesty

Every Speaker has an **Honesty** from 1 to 6, and people talk about it openly. Wend, Sump Nine's Speaker, is Honesty 4: *honest enough most of the time and not quite honest enough when it mattered.* A Speaker may shade a reading toward whoever has paid them, frightened them, or shares their Hearth. A Speaker caught shading is a scandal for the Nucleolus, and it raises a new Issue: *who reads the air now?*

### Crimes in the air

- **False signal** is a lie in the air. Exposed, it swings hard against the liar and sends them to the Assay.
- **Quenching** is the gravest political crime in the Expanse. It means breathing an enzyme that destroys the other side's signal before it can build up: *not lying in the air, but making the air lie.* A quench leaves a residue, but it can be hunted only after the Issue is read. A found quench **voids the Quorum, drops Colony Cohesion by 2**, and sends the quencher to trial on a charge of *Breaking the Air*. The sentence is **Unlisting**. This is exactly what happens to Corvane.

### The Convocation: a room with one Motion

A Convocation is three to six parties, each wanting something different, and one **Motion** on the table: *who takes the wreck in the sixth hab's channel?* Each party has an Interest, a Price and some Leverage, and the room has a **mood** (Hungry, Grieving, Hostile and so on) that helps moves that suit it.

| Move | What it is | Note |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Offer** | name a real price | the plain, factual register |
| **Read** | learn another party's price, leverage or secret |  |
| **Reveal** | produce evidence: a witnessed Ledger line, a register entry, a memory | wins a step with no argument; each piece is revealed once |
| **Alliance** | two parties move as one | costs Cohesion or a Ledger line |
| **Bind** | propose the Motion as an Oath | if the room accepts, the outcome is enforceable and recorded |
| **Pressure** | lean on someone with threat or status | **if it fails, the room turns toward Hostile and the colony's Clock advances** |
| **Walk** | withdraw your recognition | everyone must answer *why you left*; the strongest move when the room already leans your way |

**Proxies.** You may speak, swear or fight *for* someone who is bound to you. The Ash already speak for the dead. A party with no proxy is still in the room: their Standing is what gets spent in their absence, and they find out later.

*Sources: QUORUM Ch. 4, §10.1; the story, Parts I, II and VI.*

## Promises and debts: Oaths, the Ledger and money

*Every body is a debt. Some are paid in Substrate, some in labour, some in Oath.* In the Expanse, help is never free and never forgotten. Obligation, not money, is the real currency of society.

### Oaths: promises written into the body

An **Oath** is literal. You fold a sequence into your own chromatin, so that breaking the promise damages you.

- **Swearing** takes a shared-meal Rite: 1 Fugue Interval, 10 S, and a witness who can read chemistry well. At the **Contract Fair** it costs no Substrate at all. *Swear it at the Fair, and it costs you nothing to swear. Keep it, and it costs you everything else.*
- **Oath boxes.** Each organism has only a few. A sworn, unkept Oath is *unspent*.
- **Breaking an Oath** wounds the body, drops you a rung with the witnessing faction, and scars you.
- **Unspent Oaths outlive you.** The Ash will not harvest a body with an unspent Oath. They keep it, sometimes for decades, until the Oath is kept or the Rereading comes. Yarrow hangs in the Ash Rows for twenty Drift Seasons waiting for *to see the child listed* to be kept.
- **Oaths can be bought and sold.** At the Tallow Market an oath-broker sells other people's promises. Buy a dead mother's Oath to bring her bud home, and *you* are bound by it. The Ash call this a theft of grief.
- **What an Immunan's Oath does.** An Immunan must answer any direct question truthfully *unless an Oath forbids it*, and even then each sentence of refusal costs them Stress.

### The Ledger: the world's memory of what is owed

Every organism has a **Ledger** with three columns: **Owed to you**, **You owe**, and **Witnessed**, meaning you can prove it.

- **A line is one sentence: who, what, witnessed by whom.** *Oro owes the Brine Office his share of the Ladders' debt: five S a Beat until the principal is paid. Witnessed by the Registrar.*
- **An unwitnessed line is a rumour, not a line.** It may be true, but it cannot be called in.
- **Capacity depends on condition.** Swollen 3 lines, Settled 5, Dry 7, never more than 9. The world only lets you carry as much obligation as your condition can bear. Oro fills his three in Part III.
- **What you can do with a line:** call it in (once), refuse it (and pay the cost), pay it off, transfer it at a Hearth Rite or the Tallow Market, forge it (a crime), or inherit it when someone dies.
- **Every Oath is a Ledger line; most Ledger lines are not Oaths.**

### Budline debts

- **A budline's debt divides with it, and never gets smaller.** When an organism divides, both halves keep its debts.
- **You inherit your share when your root name is first spoken at the Hearth.** Oro's line arrived the day Sefa named him *Oro-of-the-Seven-Ladders*.
- **Interest can be paid forever without touching the principal.** The Seven Ladders paid 5 S a Beat on a 100 S Brine Office loan for two Great Cycles without reducing it.
- **The most admired act in the story** is paying a debt that is yours by love rather than paperwork: Sefa sells her own body to pay it down, and Oro pays it every Tide of his life.

### Money: Substrate and the worker's day

- **Substrate (S)** is real stuff: rendered food and building material, not coins.
- **Every price is read against the worker's day.** A colonist's whole Beat of work is worth **5 S** to the colony.

| Work, per Beat | Pay |
| --- | --: |
| Hauler | 4 S (6 S hauling uphill all shift; 2 S less on the night pumps) |
| Hearth cook or healer | 5 S, plus meals |
| Gate-clerk | 6 S, plus bribes |
| Foundry hand | 8 S, less licence debt |
| Quartermaster | 10 S, plus what goes missing |
| Assayer or Reader | 15 S |
| Office-holder | 20 S if honest; much more if not |

- **A wage feeds; it does not free.** Four S feeds a Swollen hauler who eats at the Hearth and sleeps in the racks. It does not pay a debt.
- **The Hegemony lends at 5 S a Beat.** Budlines lend to kin at nothing, and never forget. The Vacuole lends against water rights.
- **Default.** One Drift Season in default and the creditor may take you to the Assay, or sell the debt to the Pump, which collects it in labour. A colony that defaults can have its **charter** bought, which is the Hegemony's plan for Sump Nine.

*Sources: Core Rulebook §11.4; QUORUM Ch. 6, §7.1, §9.5–9.6; the story, Parts I–IV and VII.*

## Hearth and kin: budlines, names, Wardship, conjugation and Rites

Organisms do not have parents the way the old songs pretend. They **divide**: one body becomes two, and both remember being one. Family is the record of those divisions, and of the debts, grudges and Oaths that divided with them. The **Hearth** is where family, work crew and colony meet, eat and witness.

### Budlines

- **A budline** is every organism descended from one founding division: family, house and clan at once.
- **The scent-root.** Every member carries the founder's faint signature. A budline cannot be hidden from a register; it can only be forged. This is why everyone agrees Oro "did not smell much like a Seven Ladders", though nobody says it to Sefa's face.
- **The root-elder** is the oldest living member. Once per Tide they may call any member home to the budline's Hearth, and refusing costs you. When a root-elder goes Dormant for good, the budline's Hearth chooses the next one by its own small Quorum.
- **Division passes everything on.** Both halves keep the Oaths, the Standing and the debts. The new body is a person in its own right, with its own will, carrying everything up to the moment of division. Lisk is Oro's bud, and remembers being Oro.

### Names

Every organism has three names, used in three registers.

| Name | Example | Who may use it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Chemical name** | the full scent-signature, impossible to pronounce | registers, assayers, Bonds |
| **Called name** | *Oro*, *Mesk*, *Quell* | anyone |
| **Root name** | *of the Seven Ladders*, *of the Clean Count* | kin, Hearth Rites, Oaths |

- **Coming of age** is a naming. The root name is given at the budline's Hearth when an organism first divides or first swears. With the name comes the budline's debt.
- **Using someone's root name uninvited** is either intimacy or threat.

### Wardship: kin by Rite

**Wardship** is a Rite at a Hearth by which you take a dependent as your own: a child, a reclaimed dead, or a successor. It creates a line that **cannot be transferred**. *You are somebody's future.* In the story, blood matters less than the Rite: Oro is a Seven Ladders "by Rite, if not by register", and he pays the budline's debt for life.

### Conjugation: the deepest promise

Two organisms may **conjugate**: link membranes and exchange sequence. It is part marriage, part contract and part shared inheritance. It is chemical and legal in tone, never erotic.

- **The Rite:** 1 Fugue Interval at a Hearth, **one witness from each budline**, and 20 S each. Usually done at the Contract Fair, in front of everyone.
- **What it makes:** a Bond between the two; one plasmid each, permanently; and kinship between the two budlines.
- **Dissolving it** breaks the Bond and sours the two budlines on each other until the next Rereading.
- **Across lineages** it is lawful everywhere but blessed only in some places, and it is a classic Quorum Issue.
- **Barred to the Unlisted**, because they have no budline on record. An Immunan who conjugates with an Unlisted body becomes "not quite standard" in every Warden's eyes.

### The Rites

| Rite | Cost | What it does |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Chemotactic Rite** (the shared meal) | 3 S each, 1 Fugue Interval | clears all Stress, raises Cohesion, warms moods; also the vehicle for Oaths |
| **Antigen Rite** | 25 S and a witness | you are read as *self* by one faction's wards for a Tide |
| **Dedication Rite** | 50 S and an Oath box | you rise a rung with a faction |
| **Mourning Rite** | 1 Beat at a dead zone | clears Stress; honours a dead organism |
| **Naming Rite** | 100 S | your chemical name becomes known across a region |
| **Induction** | 30 S and a sponsor | you change organ |
| **Wardship** and **Conjugation** | see above | kin by Rite |
| **The morning Rite** | free | a slow turn around the warm stone, every body brushing every other, so the day begins with everyone smelling a little of each other: *we are one organ* |

*Sources: Core Rulebook §11.6; QUORUM §2.5, Ch. 7; the story, Parts I, II, V and VII.*

## Mutualism: what organisms do for each other

Between families, organisms live by **mutualism**: small, priced, chemical favours that let a body survive among other bodies. There are **twelve recognised acts**, and every one of them writes a Ledger line. Kindness in the Expanse comes with a receipt, and that is not cynical: it is how kindness is *remembered*.

| # | Act | Cost | What it gives | What it makes |
| --: | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | **Grooming** | 1 S each, 1 Fugue Interval | eases travel Stress | — |
| 2 | **Cleaner Pact** | 5 S a Beat | no parasites while you travel | a standing line with a cleaners' Lodge. Quell pays for one for Oro and Mesk |
| 3 | **Endorsement** | your endorser owes your debt if you default | a creditor will deal with you | *your arrears are now my problem* |
| 4 | **Antigen Shelter** | the host risks an Oath box | you pass one gate unexamined | a line you owe for life. Useless to a Lysosan, whom the wards always see |
| 5 | **Brooding** | you carry it inside you | a child, a sequence or a specimen crosses a hostile region | the parent owes you a Bond or a debt; the Ash count it as kinship. Yarrow broods Oro across the Wastes |
| 6 | **Incubation** | a watcher's Fugue Interval | a Dormant Cycle taken safely in open water | a line; a watched sleeper cannot mask its scent, which makes this one of the most intimate acts there is |
| 7 | **Engraftment lease** | 20 S a Beat, or an Oath box per Tide | lend one of your grafts to a partner | — |
| 8 | **Signal Relay** | 1 Fugue Interval per hop | a scent-message crosses a region in hours | every relay knows the message |
| 9 | **Chimeric Pact** | an Oath box each | two organisms co-write a growth | betrayal gives the other **Argument** against you |
| 10 | **Feed Tithe** | 5 S a Beat | you eat inside a colony | you are inside its tally, and on its register |
| 11 | **Digestion Pact** | your future body | an advance of **half your body's harvest value**, today | the Ash own your body, and will enforce your unspent Oaths. Sefa signs one to pay Oro's debt |
| 12 | **Wardship** | a Rite at a Hearth | take a dependent as your own | a line that cannot be transferred |

### Plasmids: the deepest courtesy

A **plasmid** is a small sequence passed from body to body: a knack, a recipe, a trick of digestion, the shape of a song. Giving plasmids is how the Expanse shares culture, and it is older than any school.

- **How it is given:** 1 Fugue Interval at a Hearth, sitting very close and breathing together while the other body learns.
- **It is a courtesy.** Giving one warms the receiver. **Refusing one offered at a Hearth is an insult your budline will remember for a Tide.**
- **It fades unless it is kept.** A received plasmid lasts one Tide unless the receiver integrates it. A plasmid exchanged in conjugation is permanent.
- **Taking one without consent is theft** under Precept 6, and the victim may invoke **Argument**. Stealing a budline's memory strand is the same crime at budline scale.
- **In the story:** the Seven Ladders' lullaby calms a terrified body. Oro gives it to Mesk at the Fair; Mesk breathes it over Oro when Sefa dies, again after the wall, and at last over his body as it is read.

*Sources: QUORUM §7.4, §7.6, §14.3; the story, Parts II–V and the ending.*

## Conflict and justice: proof, crimes, trials and duels

Justice in the Expanse is called **carcass law**, because in the end it is about *what is owed*, and the Expanse settles debts in bodies. A body can be read, so an accused organism is not just questioned. It is **assayed**.

### Proof: rumour, line and Reveal

| Kind of claim | How long it lives | How hard it is to disprove |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Fresh rumour** | a Beat | easy: anyone can disprove it with a counter-fact |
| **Repeated rumour** | a Drift Season | hard |
| **Settled rumour** | until the next Great Cycle | very hard |
| **Law** | until the Rereading | cannot be argued, only erased |

- **A smell is not proof.** The haulers can smell that the Dry pockets are too full, but a smell is a rumour, and Corvane disproves it by opening his door to the count.
- **A line beats everything.** A witnessed fact on record wins arguments. A Reveal of a register line voids the toll-collector's claim in one move.
- **A Reveal is proof made public.** Showing the truth to everyone at once counts even when no record exists, because a crowd that has smelled it cannot un-smell it. The burst pipe is the story's great Reveal.
- **Proof can destroy the one who carries it.** Saying "this is where I came from" in a room with a register in it would Unlist Oro. Where a truth is said, and by whom, matters as much as the truth itself.

### Crimes, by the Code

| Crime | Precept | Prosecuted by | Typical sentence |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Crossing unread** | 1 | the Membrane | fine of 20–50 S, or a Beat sealed in a cyst |
| **Channel-making** (opening an illegal pore) | 1 | the Membrane | the tool-graft cut out; Unlisting if repeated |
| **Leaving the dead** unharvested | 2 | the Lysosome | indenture to the Lysosome, a Beat per body |
| **Hoarding a gift** (unrecorded synthesis) | 3 | the Vacuole, the Hegemony | the gift's value as a fine, doubled |
| **False scent**: lying about lineage, tonic or Oaths; forging a register line | 4 | the Nucleolus | a Pulse Ten sealed in a cyst; dilution if done in a Quorum |
| **Breaking the Air**: quenching a Quorum | 4 | the Nucleolus | **Unlisting** |
| **Cutting the Forest** | 5 | the Warden Circles | dilution, and the Curators take an interest |
| **Hijack**: altering a body without consent | 6 | the victim, by **Argument** | the graft cut out; Unlisting for repeat offenders |
| **Plasmid theft** | 6 | the victim's budline | return it, plus indenture |
| **Trading beyond the outer membrane** | 7 | the Warden Circles | Unlisting |
| **Oath-breaking against the colony** | all | the Hearth | dilution, plus the Oath's own cost |
| **Violence against a yielding organism** | custom | the Hearth | a Pulse Ten sealed in a cyst; every witness is marked with Stress anyway |

The written law is strict about crimes the poor commit and quiet about the plans of the powerful. In the story, the Hegemony factor Lune is never charged: *the Hegemony never breaks a contract in writing; it only reinterprets one for a Tide.*

### The trial

A trial is a **Convocation** whose Motion is *what is owed*, confirmed by a **Quorum**.

1. **The room:** accuser, accused, a Reader or assayer, a Quorum Speaker, and up to two interested parties.
2. **The oath:** each party states lineage, tonic and Oaths, as Precept 4 requires. Refusing starts you a step behind.
3. **The Assay:** the Reader reads the accused's body. A success is a Reveal of the truth.
4. **The verdict** goes to the colony as an Issue. The colony *knows* the verdict when it reaches Quorum. A hung verdict leaves the accused *clear but watched* until the next Rereading.

The accused may try to fool the Assay with a mask, by hiding chemistry, by bribing the assayer, or by demanding a second assayer from another faction. A bought or forged verdict is a time bomb for the colony. Corvane's trial follows this pattern: Varrow reads him at the Hearth, the colony reaches Quorum before dawn, and he is Unlisted.

### Sentences

From lightest to heaviest: a **fine**; **labour** routed to a creditor; **encystment** (sealed in a cyst); **excision** of a graft; a **chemical gag**, so you cannot swear or witness, the Expanse's civil death; **suspension from the register** for a Tide; **dilution** (exile into open water for a Tide); **seizure of an Oath box** by a creditor; a **forced reading**; **digestion suspension** (your body may never be harvested, which is worse than any cell); **Unlisting**; and **Total Dissolution**, the most feared sentence in the Expanse, which leaves no harvest, no legacy and no memory.

A condemned organism may instead *choose* the **Apoptotic Oath**: to dissolve and be harvested, clearing its budline's debts and shame.

### The duel that replaces a war

A duel settles in one scene what a war would settle in a Tide and a hundred dead. The colonies prefer it.

1. **Name the Motion** the duel decides: a debt, a water right, an insult. Both sides swear to it as an Oath.
2. **Challenge an office, not a person.** In the story the Pump challenges the empty Steward's office, not Corvane.
3. **Champions.** Each side names one, and may hire one. Corvane pays for a Kinetic Striker, and nobody thinks less of him for paying.
4. **Seconds** may Reveal evidence once, before blood.
5. **The ground** is a registered arena or an open circle. The Porter registers its walls, so whatever happens in it becomes law.
6. **Watching is a legal act; interrupting is a scandal; killing either duellist outside the rules is a crime.**
7. **The Yield.** Either champion may yield: a chemical white flag flown with the whole body, which cannot be faked. Killing a yielder marks every witness with Stress and drops everyone's Cohesion. **There is no honour-kill in this culture.** Grue catches the Striker, holds her while she yields, and lets her go.
8. **Winning is not collecting.** A duel settles who is right, not whether the winner can enforce it. The Pump wins and nothing changes, because there is no Steward to act.

### Strikes

A **strike** is the Pump withholding its labour, and it is a recognised, legitimate act. Each Beat of strike empties the store a little and pushes every price toward scarce. It also puts the Pump's demand on the air as an Issue. **Breaking a strike by force** costs the colony 2 Cohesion whatever happens.

*Sources: QUORUM Ch. 10, §9.3, §11.2, §12.4; Director's Codex §5.5; the story, Parts II, IV and VI.*

## Death and the Ash: reading, rendering and the dead's debts

The strictest norms in the Expanse are about the dead. *A body that is not read is a story that is not told, and a story that is not told is a soul thrown in the water.* The **Reclaimers' Ash** keep these norms, and Precept 2 (Non-Waste) gives them the force of law.

### What happens to a body

1. **The dead are the Ash's by licence.** The charter of most colonies says so. Whoever holds the licence is owed the harvest, not whoever caused the death.
2. **The body is hung in the Ash Rows** in its husk, to wait for the **Rendering**.
3. **It is read at its own Hearth, in front of its kin.** That is law, not custom. The Reader opens her body around the dead one's head and takes in its last memories.
4. **The Reader speaks to the one the news belongs to**, not to the most important person in the room, and carries the dead's words *exactly*.
5. **The body is rendered**: every part of it that can feed something feeds something. A memory the dead wanted kept goes into a **memory strand**.

### The Ash's red lines

- **No harvest of a body with an unspent Oath.** That debt is sacred. The Ash keep the body until the Oath is paid or the Rereading comes, whichever is first. They will enforce the dead's unspent Oaths themselves.
- **No Total Dissolution.** A body that leaves no harvest and no memory is the worst fate there is.
- **Never censor a funeral.** The Ash's corpse-wagons (the **Lipidpost**) cannot be stopped, searched or censored, and every Ash crew in the Expanse would come to a funeral's defence. That makes the funeral road the slowest and safest in the world, and the one news-carrier no power can block.
- **Leaving a body unharvested in the open** breaks Precept 2, damages the colony's Cohesion, and is prosecuted by the Lysosome.

### Selling your own body: the Digestion Pact

Any organism may sign its body over to the Ash **while still alive**. The Ash pay an advance of half its harvest value in Substrate, today. Pacts are usually signed at the Rendering's **funeral auctions**. When you die, your body is theirs to read and render, and they will see your unspent Oaths kept whether you like it or not. Sefa sells hers for 50 S to pay down Oro's debt. Oro signs his for the same reason, and his body comes home by Lipidpost because of it.

### Memory strands

A **memory strand** is a sealed crystal of recorded moments, an heirloom played back at the Hearth on feast nights. Strands are sacred and very valuable, at 100–1,000 S to the right buyer. A strand **stays warm while the memory inside it still wants to be remembered**.

- **It opens only to the self it was made for**, or to a Reader who can persuade it.
- **A brooder's strand opens once**, and then the memory is gone from the world. So it should be opened at a Hearth, with the Ash in the room, *where the dead can be honoured as they are heard.*
- **You do not play another budline's memory at your Hearth.** *Some memories are Hearth memories, and this is not my Hearth.*
- **Stealing a strand** is plasmid theft at budline scale.

### Mourning

- **Bad news is given plainly, fast and completely.** When Sefa dies, Mesk says only: *She died at the Hearth. She wasn't alone. Dunmore was with her. She didn't hurt.* Those last three facts are the ones a grieving body needs.
- **Grief is done in company.** Nobody is left alone with it, and sitting with someone through a whole Beat of grief is a thing one organism may owe another.
- **A public Mourning Rite for a beloved dead** raises the whole colony's Cohesion.

*Sources: Director's Codex §7.4, §8.2; QUORUM §2.1, §3.1, §7.6, §11.1, §12.1, §14.3; the story, Parts II, IV, V and the ending.*

## The social calendar: the day, the Tide and the Great Cycle

Society in the Expanse runs on **Metabolic Time**: a Beat (about a day) of 24 Fugue Intervals (about an hour each), a Drift Season of about 30 Beats, a Tide of four seasons (about a year), and a Great Cycle of 16 Tides. Every unit carries duties.

### A Beat in a colony

| Fugue Intervals | Light | What the colony does |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1–2 | lit | the Hearths wake; the morning Rite; the Membrane opens its registers |
| 3–10 | lit | **first shift**: the Pump hauls, markets drift, the Lysosome holds its assays |
| 11–12 | lit | **the shared meal**: every Hearth eats at once; each hab also feeds its nursery shift |
| 13–20 | lit | **second shift**; at its end the Quorum Speaker reads the air |
| 21–24 | dim | curfew at the Membrane; Dormant Cycles (sleep); channel-makers, the Underground and lovers move. Flagellans, who cannot rest still, work the night pumps for 2 S less |

### The festivals of the Tide

Each season of the Tide has a festival. Each one bends a rule for a few Beats, and attending (or not) has consequences.

| Season | Festival | Length | What happens | The rule that bends |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Isotonic Rise** | **The Contract Fair** | 3 Beats | debts signed, Oaths sworn, buds named, conjugations, charters granted, **offices re-chosen** | an Oath sworn at the Fair costs no Substrate to encode |
| **Hypertonic Zenith** | **The Salt Vigil** | a Beat and a dim | fasting and rationing; every household's water rights read aloud; the **arrears audit**; the Deep Choir's long note; **duels in the open**; thefts tried that night | a failing water store raises unrest sharply |
| **Isotonic Fall** | **The Rendering** | 2 Beats | the year's dead read and rendered; **funeral auctions** where Digestion Pacts are signed | harvests are richer; one free Mourning Rite |
| **Hypotonic Zenith** | **The Swell** | a Pulse Ten (10 Beats) | carnival; the Swollen rule the streets; current-races; mock-elders crowned and paraded; riots somewhere, always | **masks are lawful: Precept 4 is suspended**; every Issue the Pump backs starts ahead |
| **Every 16 Tides** | **The Rereading** | a Drift Season | registers purged; old debts re-read; Standings re-tested | **the Unlisted may apply to be listed again** |

**If you miss a festival:** at the Contract Fair, your office's voice is cast by whoever you owe. Miss the Salt Vigil and the arrears ladder starts. An unharvested body on your watch at the Rendering is a Precept 2 breach. At the Swell, *your nursery is on someone else's menu*.

### Rhythms of the Tide

- **Promises are made in the soft season.** Travel is cheap and everyone can afford to be generous, so the Contract Fair falls at the Isotonic Rise.
- **Debts are called in at the Fall,** before the Swell turns the ladder.
- **The Dry seal their pockets in the salty season, and the Pump strikes in the watery one.**

*Sources: Core Rulebook §8.1; QUORUM §5.3, Ch. 12, §16.1; the story, all parts.*

## Beyond the colony: the great powers, Host-Cities and markets

No colony stands alone. Six great powers reach into every colony's organs. Each has its own Standing with you, from Hostile to Bound, and each has a rival: **one step up with a power means one step down with its rival**.

### The six great powers

| Power | What it is | What it wants | Its red line | Its rival |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **The Ribosome Hegemony** (and its **Brine Office**) | the foundry empire: licences, grafts and loans to colonies | licences enforced; debts paid; charters bought from colonies that default | unlicensed sequence | the Sequence Underground |
| **The Warden Circles** | the immune police of the registers: *nothing enters a colony that has not been read* | every unregistered organism reported | hiding the unregistered | the Sequence Underground |
| **The Flow Guilds** | the Flagellan carriers and couriers who run the currents | free currents, cargo carried without questions | damming, owning or selling a current | the Hegemony |
| **The Reclaimers' Ash** | keepers of the dead, readers of memory | every harvest rendered properly | leaving the dead | the Warden Circles, who call the Ash unregistered |
| **The Sequence Underground** | the Lysosan-founded network of the Unlisted and unlicensed | sequences carried unread | handing a Lysosan to a register | the Warden Circles |
| **The Deep Choir** | Barophil pilgrims of the deep, singers of the long note | singing at the Salt Vigil; pilgrims carried | keeping the tonic "soft" | the Flow Guilds |

**Two ways of seeing the world.** Most colonies, the Wardens and the Hegemony's public face are **Homeostatists**: the world's good is stability, with correct tonicity, honest registers, harvested dead and no novelty. The Deep Choir and many Lysosan hermits are **Entropists**: the world is already ending, and the only dignity is choosing how. Their line is *"You will be reused. We would like you to be remembered first."*

### How the outside powers behave

- **The Hegemony lends, waits, and buys.** It lends at 5 S a Beat and calls the loan when a colony falters. If the colony defaults, the Hegemony buys its charter and installs its own Steward. It never breaks a contract in writing; it reinterprets one for a Tide.
- **The Wardens purge.** A circuit can strike from the register anyone it cannot read cleanly. Lysosans are barred from Warden offices, because the wards will not have them.
- **The Ash defend funerals as family.** Interfere with one funeral and every Ash crew in the Expanse comes to its defence.

### Host-Cities

The largest organisms in the Expanse are not fought. They are **lived in**. A **Host-City** is a Vast organism carrying thousands of tenants in its body cavities. It is a colony, with six organs and a Quorum, living inside a creature that has its own temper.

- **Rent** is **1 ATP per tenant per Beat**, fed into the host at its Rent Wells.
- **The host's immune cells are the police.** They do not take bribes. They take *scent*: a paid-up tenant carries the host's tolerance-scent, and anyone else is treated as *not-self*.
- **A Host-City does not read registers.** It does not care if you are listed; it cares if you are a good tenant. Tenancy is the one standing open to the Unlisted, which is why Oro leaves inside **Old Mother Brine**, a slow, motherly saline giant with 30,000 tenants and the Flow Guilds' headquarters in her left vacuole.
- **Host-Cities migrate with the Tide** and anchor where they please, uninvited.

### Places without registers

- **The Tallow Market** is a floating bazaar on a raft of fat at the edge of the Lipid Plateaus, with about 400 organisms, **no weapons and no registers**. Nothing there is read and nobody is listed. You can buy a stolen plasmid, an unpayable Gene-Debt, or another organism's Oath, which then binds *you*. It is the only place a Ledger line can change hands without a Rite. A truth told there is unwitnessed, which can protect a secret or bury one.
- **The Hanging Market of Nine Tolls** is strung between four cables in the Mid-Strata. Every stall posts four prices: in Substrate, a specimen grade, an Oath box, or Cohesion.

### How news travels

*News is a molecule; to know something is to have smelled it.*

| Network | Run by | Speed | Can it be censored? |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Currentpost** | the Flow Guilds | a region a Beat | yes; the Guilds price and block it |
| **Bridge post** | the Hegemony | reliable and registered, and **read** | routinely |
| **Lipidpost** | the Ash, by corpse-wagon | a region a Tide | **no**: censoring a funeral breaks Precept 2 |
| **The Fade** | the Sequence Underground | hours; deniable | no, but every relay knows the message |

**Writing is laid down, not spoken.** **Trail-script** is scent laid in sequence along a surface. It lasts a Drift Season on lipid, a Tide on chitin, and a Great Cycle on crystallised protein. Signing means laying your chemical name at the end, and counterfeiting a signature is the commonest crime in Knot Nine.

*Sources: Director's Codex §7, §8.3; QUORUM §9.6–9.7, Ch. 11, Ch. 13, §15.2, §17.3; the story, Parts III, VI and VII.*

## Everyday norms: manners, lineages and the moral vocabulary

A **rule** is written down, and an office punishes you for breaking it. A **norm** is unwritten, and everyone around you enforces it by how they react. In the Expanse **norms are unusually strong**, because everyone can smell you. A lie is not just risky; it is physically noisy. Much of life is governed by scent and shame rather than by Wardens.

### Polite and rude

| Polite | Rude |
| --- | --- |
| Giving someone a plasmid: the deepest courtesy there is | Refusing a plasmid offered at a Hearth: an insult your budline will remember for a Tide |
| Teaching by sitting close and breathing together for a Fugue Interval | Teaching from a distance, or rushing it |
| Taking part in the morning Rite, so the day begins with everyone smelling a little of each other | Being the one body that is not there |
| Saying hard news plainly, early and completely | Talking around a death |
| Speaking to the person the news *belongs* to, not the most important person in the room | Speaking only to the powerful |
| Excusing your body's needs: *Forgive me. It is in the body.* | Pretending your body needs nothing |
| Feeding the nursery, which is everyone's duty by charter | Leaving a nursery shift unfed |
| Serving an honest meal with nothing masked in it | Serving a masked meal, unless it is an *exquisite* one at the Swell, which is an art form |
| Gossip about lineage behind someone's back | Saying it to their elder's face |
| Displaying your condition openly: class is shown, not hidden | Pretending to a condition you do not have |

**Being unreadable is unsettling.** Corvane's servants smell "of nothing at all", and the story treats that as sinister. In a world where everyone leaks, choosing not to leak is a kind of threat.

### Lineages in society

Every lineage's body shapes its career, and every colony has expectations of each. None of this is fair. All of it is common knowledge.

| Lineage | Usually holds | Usually denied | Feared for | What its body does to its life |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Amoeban** | chemist, digester, Reader's crew | Porter, Registrar; must carry a shape-registry token | forgery, infiltration | often poorly equipped, and therefore poor |
| **Epithalian** | Tally-Keeper, Steward, architect, contract-signer | nothing: they *are* the offices | walling people in, or out | cannot remake themselves, so they accumulate offices instead. *An old Epithalian is a building with a grudge* |
| **Flagellan** | courier, navigator, herder, smuggler | anything that means standing still, such as a gate watch | a monopoly on movement | must keep moving, so they are the best smugglers and the first to be searched; paid less for night work |
| **Lysosan** | broker, archivist, geneticist, spy | every office under Warden jurisdiction | latent rewriting | the wards always see them, so their Ledgers are as legible as their bodies. *Not quite standard* |
| **Immunan** | Porter, Registrar, Muster | rarely chosen as a champion or a liar's partner | their own honesty | **cannot deceive**, and must answer truthfully. The most credible witness in the Expanse, *and therefore the classic kidnapping*. Villains ask them direct questions as a weapon |
| **Barophil** | herder, Muster, pilgrim, long-singer | anything decided fast | patience, and what they outlast | slow to react, so their politics run through rites, proxies and the Deep Choir. **Slowness is not cowardice** |

**Who warms to whom.**

- **Amoebans** like the other outsiders, Flagellans and Lysosans, and resent Immunans, who make them carry registry tokens.
- **Epithalians and Immunans** trust each other, the walls and the law.
- **Flagellans** resent Epithalians over toll contracts, and Barophils for taxing the deep currents.
- **Lysosans** start a step worse with Immunans.

These are tendencies, not laws: a single kept Oath or a shared Hearth beats them. Eating at another lineage's Hearth clears its cold regard for a Tide.

**Lineage quarters.** In a large colony each lineage keeps a quarter with its own Hearth, house rule and taboo:

- **The Melt (Amoeban):** never ask an Amoeban its *real* shape.
- **The Terraces (Epithalian):** every guest signs the wall.
- **The Wake (Flagellan):** nobody sits still at a meal, and you never anchor a Flagellan's skiff without asking.
- **The Shelves (Lysosan):** every guest leaves a sequence, and you never ask a Lysosan what it is carrying.
- **The Ranks (Immunan):** each diner is named before eating, and lying at table is pointless.
- **The Deep End (Barophil):** silence until the meal is done, and never hurry anyone.

### The moral vocabulary

Society runs on proverbs, and everyone quotes them.

- *Every body is a debt. Some are paid in Substrate, some in labour, some in Oath.* (the Pump)
- *A colony is a body. Find the organ that is hurting, and you will find the story.* (the haulers)
- *Downhill is free. Uphill is a body. The whole economy is the argument about whose.* (the haulers)
- *Swear it at the Fair, and it costs you nothing to swear. Keep it, and it costs you everything else.* (the Contract Fair)
- *Salt calls salt. Water finds the floor.* (the Dry and the Swollen, meaning opposite things)
- *A body that is not read is a story that is not told.* (the Ash)
- *The register is the colony.* (the Warden Circles)
- *Masks are lawful. Nobody is lying. Everybody is lying.* (the Swell)
- *Nobody ever writes the ordinary down, and then the ordinary is gone.* (the storytellers, whose duty is to remember everyday life as well as heroes)

*Sources: QUORUM Ch. 8, §14.4; the story, all parts. The rule-and-norm distinction, and several of the manners above, come from the attached Society Rules draft and have been checked against the story.*

## Sources and open questions

Where the books and the story agree, this document states the rule plainly. Where only the story states a rule, or the sources disagree, the rule is listed below so you can decide whether it becomes canon.

### Sources used

- **The Osmotic Expanse — QUORUM** (rev 2): the society sourcebook, and the main source for this document.
- **Director's Codex**: §5.5, §7, §8.2–8.4 (the factions, the Sacred Code, Homeostatists and Entropists).
- **Core Rulebook**: §8.1, §11.4, §11.6 (Metabolic Time, Oaths, Rites).
- ***What the Water Owed***: the story, used for examples and for rules it adds.
- **Attached drafts**: *Society Rules and Social Norms* and *What the Water Owed — Kid-Friendly Breakdown*. Their sound points are folded in.

### Rules only the story states

Each of these is consistent with the books but printed nowhere in them. Decide whether to make them canon in QUORUM.

1. **A duel challenges an office, not a person.** The Pump challenges the empty Steward's office at the Salt Vigil. QUORUM's duel rules do not say whom a duel may name.
2. **The Unlisted may still swear Oaths, "if no register is asked".** QUORUM lists what the Unlisted lose but is silent on Oaths.
3. **Night-shift pay.** The story pays Flagellans on the night pumps 2 S less a Beat. QUORUM's wage table has no night rate.
4. **The store belongs to the households.** The haulers argue that Sump Nine's charter means this, but QUORUM's printed Sump Nine charter has only three lines, none of which says so. Is it a reading of line 2, or a missing fourth line?
5. **A root name brings the budline's debt.** QUORUM says debts divide with the debtor. The story adds that you take your share when your root name is first spoken at the Hearth.
6. **A memory strand stays warm while its memory wants to be remembered.** This is flavour from the story, not in QUORUM §14.3.
7. **An Immunan who conjugates with an Unlisted body becomes "not quite standard".** This is a story consequence with no rule behind it.

### Points checked and confirmed

These claims are confirmed across the books and the story:

- the Rendering is **once per Tide, two Beats long**;
- the Swell lasts **one Pulse Ten (10 Beats)**;
- a Motion passes the Assembly with **8 of 15 voices**;
- the Swollen may carry **3 Ledger lines**;
- quenching is punished by **Unlisting**;
- Sump Nine's Speaker is **Honesty 4**;
- the Rereading falls **every 16 Tides**.

### Still open

- **What happens to an Unlisted organism's Ledger?** Can its lines still be called in?
- **Is a bud's debt share fixed at division,** or re-read at the Rereading like other debts?
