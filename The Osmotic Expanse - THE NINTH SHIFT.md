# THE NINTH SHIFT
### A Vector for four organisms, Levels 4 → 6 — *the Foundry Belt has stopped printing, and somebody has hired you to keep a district's muscles working*

**Story four of this campaign's line.** Chronologically it sits between *The First Carve* (Levels 1→4) and *The Long Swallow* (Levels 7→11): run it after the first and before the second, and it hands you both. It also runs standalone.
**Book:** Director's material. Same shape as *The Bloom of Ash* (CODEX Ch. 12) and *The Long Swallow*: one page of situation, faces, five sites, Clocks you run visibly, an honest ledger.
**Region:** the Foundry Belt — **Shiftless Row**, the print floor of **Shift Nine**, and the **Licence Court** (CODEX §6.6, §7.1, §7.2).
**Tone:** **workplace farce played deadpan.** Nobody in this Vector is winking. The comedy is procedural — forms, queues, clauses, grievances, a machine that keeps printing while everyone argues about it. The only thing that is genuinely frightening is that a district of ten thousand foundry-organs has run out of the enzyme that lets it *move*, and it will keep pretending otherwise for six Beats.
**This is the relaxed one.** Four of the five site problems can be ended with a conversation, a signature, or a very good errand. Lethality is announced before it is spent, and there is a printed mutuality rule (§6.9) that fishes you out of Dormancy. The Vector's teeth are economic and social, not mortal.
**Content note (put it on the invitation):** forced overtime, eviction, a nearly-alive person with a price on it, and a labour dispute that ends in a body count if you want it to. Nothing sexual. No bodily-autonomy horror — that is *The Long Swallow*'s business, not this one's.

> *A Vector is a person with a door in it. This one is a person with a **wage** in it. Everything below exists to answer the question the table will ask in the first ten minutes: **whose side are we on, and how much are they paying?** Let them answer it. The answer is the adventure.*

# 0 · THE VECTOR ON ONE PAGE

**The situation.** **Shift Nine** of the Foundry Belt — four hundred foundry-organs, one Line, one Overseer — has stopped printing. Officially there is no strike; officially the Row is in "scheduled maintenance suspension", a phrase invented nine Beats ago by a Hegemony auditor who has never touched a print-head. The Cohort is standing at the **Filing Gate** with four other hopefuls because the district posted an advertisement: **FOUR ORGANISMS. RTC 3 OR BETTER. TEMPORARY. BRING YOUR OWN APRON.** The pay is real. So is the fact that everything the Cohort owns — every licensed graft, every registered Apparatus, every Vial they can buy next Tide — is *printed here*, by the people who have stopped printing.

**What actually stopped.** Nine Beats ago a **print-canister** came off Shift Nine's Line still warm and unlisted (CODEX §6.6, *Shiftless Row*: *"what was printed in the silence is in the Cohort's hands already, and it is unlisted"*). The Hegemony registered the canister's **fold** as a patent — a *lineage trait*, a shape one organism's body has always known how to make (CODEX §7.1: *"Will lise you for: proving that a patented trait is prior art owned by a lineage"*). **Overseer Kel-Vasht of Shift Nine** filed the objection, and then withdrew it, because the trait in question is *Kel-Vasht's own*, and the receipt for buying it is folded somewhere inside its body. The Row found out. The Row stopped. Four hundred organs have been quiet for nine Beats, and the district's enzyme stock is a Tide deep.

**What the district is running out of.** Not food. **Fold.** A printed protein has to be *folded* before it works, and Shift Nine holds the Belt's only chaperone parlour. Without it, Vials go stale, grafts develop Minor Costs, the pump-villi of the Great Flow cramp, and the foundry-organs themselves begin to sit down. This is the joke and the clock: **the region's body is going stiff because two organisations cannot settle an argument about a shape.**

**The yield.** Two things are at the bottom of this Vector, and they are the same argument wearing different coats:
1. **THE NINTH TUNE** — the fold the Hegemony patented, which is Kel-Vasht's inherited lineage trait, and which, if it is proven to be *prior art owned by a lineage*, dies: one body becomes unlicensable, which is the most dangerous sentence in the Belt (CODEX §7.1, and Precept 3: *what is printed may be owned; what is inherited may not*).
2. **THE UNLISTED PRINT** — what **Foundry Mother "Hush"** has been printing, slowly, in an alley behind organ 41, for **nine Beats** (CODEX §7.1: *"it has been printing one unlisted organism, slowly, for nine Beats, and it is nearly finished"*). It is nearly finished. It is not finished. It has a Harvest line printed in §10.9 of this book, and that Harvest line is the most offensive object you will read all month.

**Who else is in the building.** The **Licence Court** wants the hearing closed in one Beat and has already written the decision (Auditor Eleven-Brief needs someone — *anyone* — to have found the filing error, so that the error is not theirs). The **Row** wants the Cohort to not work, and will feed them anyway. **THE RELIEF** is a hired strikebreaking Cohort who invoice for everything they break, and who are due on the third Beat. The **Warden Circles** will act if a register match appears, and Archivist Dol at Knot Nine has been quietly eating register pages for nine Beats (CODEX §7.2). **Foreman Vesk of the Clearance** (*The First Carve*) is in the Belt on a tool order and will help you, freely, and be furious about it afterwards.

**The shape of play.** Four movements. **WAGE** (the Cohort chooses *who is paying them and what they signed*: §4, twelve entries, each with a landing site, an advantage, and a debt) → **THE ROW** (a district of neighbours, a queue, and a machine that keeps running) → **THE PRINT FLOOR** (Shift Nine, the Line, the parlour; the problem room, still printing) → **THE COURT AND THE ALLEY** (a hearing with a decision already written, and an alley with something nearly finished in it).

**The three Clocks.** **SHIFT** (6 Beats — the district's enzyme stock; this is the unavoidable problem, §3), **BADGE** (5 — how the Belt reads you; *this* is the price of loot and of taking the good money), **PAPER** (4 — the Licence Court's schedule; at fill, someone's body becomes licensed property).

**The loop the players will feel.** The Belt has the best *gear* in the Expanse at list price, and the only way to keep it cheap is to keep the Line running. Every act that helps the Hegemony's print run advances **BADGE**; every act that helps the Row advances **SHIFT**. Both Clocks are on the table, both are named out loud, and neither one is the "wrong" one. **The Vector never tells you which side was right; it tells you, in writing, what your side cost.**

**What this Vector is about.** Being the temp. Four organisms with clean hands and a wage, standing exactly between an institution that can license a body and a neighbourhood that has decided to stop moving, and discovering that the shape being argued about is a shape *one of them is wearing*.

**Three omens for the table** (read one aloud when the Cohort first sights the Row; an omen always bode ill for *someone*, and never for everyone at once):

1. *The Belt's notice board lists Shift Nine's output for the last nine Beats, item by item, in the Overseer's own hand. There are no items. There is a line at the bottom: "the Line has not been asked."*
2. *Every licensed graft in the district has begun to hum, very quietly, on the beat the Line used to run.*
3. *The queue at the Filing Gate is forty-one long, and forty of those organisms are not applying for the job.*

# 1 · THE CORE VISION

## 1.1 The Big Three Questions

| Question | The answer for this Vector |
| :-- | :-- |
| **What experience is this game about?** | *Being competent, being hired, and being in the middle.* A workplace farce in a world where the workplace is a district's ability to move. The players should feel **useful and slightly embarrassed** — useful because only they can keep the Line running, embarrassed because they are the ones crossing a picket line for a wage. Wonder, then paperwork, then a genuine argument, then a decision about a nearly-alive thing. |
| **How do the mechanics reinforce that mood?** | (1) **BADGE** is one dial the whole district reads you by, and *both ends are useful*, which is what a dial is for. (2) **OFFERS** (§6.2) pay **Cohesion** for leaning into your own cliché: the table is rewarded for doing the funny thing, and the punishment lands on the organism, never on the player. (3) **THE LINE** (§10.1) is an abiotic hazard that *produces*: an argument, a hazard, and a slot machine in one block. (4) Every conflict is a **labour dispute with a body count** — the exits are printed, and violence is one exit among six. (5) **GRIEVANCES** (§6.3, §13.4) pay Codon for filing and more for winning: the rules literally reward reading the paperwork. |
| **What reward and punishment loop keeps players touching the rules?** | The Belt sells the whole Core §9 catalogue at list price and pays **2× for Constructs and Construct parts** (CODEX §6.6). The good gear is here, the good money is here, and the Hegemony's **Gene-Debt collectors** are also here (CODEX §10.5). Take the good money → BADGE advances → the Row's prices, repairs, and lies become unavailable to you, and your *own licensed gear* starts to develop Minor Costs the Hegemony will "fix" for a debt (CODEX §6.6, Order Clock at 3). Help the Row → SHIFT advances → the district goes stiff under you and the region itself becomes a hazard. Players choose *which door they are standing in front of* before they know which room it opens onto, and both Clocks sit on the table in plain sight. Against that, **§5.2's twelve Keys** pay Codon for playing the premise out loud. |

## 1.2 Three pillars (and what they cut)

| Pillar | What it means at the table | What it is allowed to cost |
| :-- | :-- | :-- |
| **1 · WAGE** | Who is paying you, and what you signed, is chosen by the players and priced in front of them (§4). It decides where Movement 2 starts and who owes you. Prep *is* play. | One session of arguing about a form before anything dies. If your table wants to skip it, use the four-hour cut in §14.1 — but keep the Form, because the Form is the Vector. |
| **2 · THE MACHINE IS A THIRD PARTY** | The Line, the Silence, and the parlour are not rooms: they are participants with schedules. Every scene answers *what is still running, and what did it just print?* (§10.1, §13.3, §13.7). | You will improvise from tables. Do not pre-write rooms beyond the five sites, and do not pre-decide what the Line prints. |
| **3 · A SHAPE SOMEONE IS WEARING** | The patent is on a body; the print is nearly a person. The Vector ends in a decision about **who may own a shape** (§12). Someone will be angry at you by the last scene. That is the design working. | Three printed endings plus one the table invents. Also: a Level-12 NPC and a Level-15 NPC who cannot be fought at Levels 4–6, and are not meant to be. Argue with them. |

**The cut list — things deliberately *not* in this Vector.** No Mass Conflict (CODEX §3.6). No new Lineages, Phenotypes, Orders, Bursts, or Pathways: everything here is a printed thing wearing an apron. No region crawl (the Row, the print floor, the Court, the alley — nothing else). No colony, vessel, or retainer management beyond the two symbiotes in §11. No Act III membrane cosmology. No Wastes acid-porn, no digestion horror: this is the **dry** one. **Eight named NPCs, nine blocks, four movements, one Form with eleven boxes.** If an idea needs a tenth block, it is a different Vector.

## 1.3 Terminology discipline (read this once, then police it all Vector)

The Expanse's books are strict; a comedy Vector has to be stricter, because a joke that uses the wrong word stops being a joke and becomes a rules dispute.

| Say this | Not this | Why it matters here |
| :-- | :-- | :-- |
| **the Cohort** | "the party", "the lads" | A Cohort is a chemical unit. In the Belt it is also a *wage unit*, and the Form makes that legal. |
| **the Row** (Shift Nine and its neighbours) | "the union", "the workers" | The Row has no legal name, because a name is a thing the Hegemony can license. This is the first joke and it is not a joke. |
| **the Line** | "the conveyor", "the machine" | The Line is an abiotic *entity* (§10.1). It has an integrity, a schedule, and a print output. It harvests nothing and it has printed nine Beats of something. |
| **Wage** | "the hook", "the job offer" | A Wage is a *chosen Vector with a printed debt* (§4), not a roll and not an imposition. |
| **Badge** | "reputation points", "standing" | Badge is a rung on a dial (§6.5), worn physically, giftable, and confiscatable in licensed ground. |
| **Offer** | "the GM tells you how to roleplay" | An Offer is *refusable, free to refuse, and pays Cohesion* (§6.2). If you cannot refuse it, it is not an Offer. |
| **Grievance** | "flavour text" | A Grievance is an objective with a printed remedy and a Codon line (§6.3). Filing pays. Winning pays more. |
| **the organism suffers / gains** | "the player wins" | The player **chooses a Wage**; the organism **carries the debt**. Player decisions are never retroactive; organism consequences always are. |
| **Marked, Jostled, Turgid, Flaccid, Sequestered, Brittle, Locked, Masked, Leaking, Catabolic** | invented conditions | Use CORE §4.7 only. *Misfolded* is **not** a condition; the correct word for what happens to an unfolded protein is **Brittle** or **Catabolic**. |
| **Dormancy / Total Lysis / Reconstitution / Dormant Cycle / Beat / Fugue Interval** | "unconscious", "dead", "rest", "day" | CODEX §5.1, CORE V. A Beat is the Belt's working day, which is why this Vector's clock is measured in them. |
| **BSP** (what a fight costs) and **Codon** (what a life costs) | "XP" | Two economies, never added (CODEX §4). The Row pays in Codon; the Hegemony pays in Substrate; never confuse the two at the table, because the NPCs certainly will. |

# 2 · SESSION ZERO FOR A COMEDY VECTOR

CODEX §1.3 requires a session zero with four questions. A comedy Vector needs the same four plus three, because the danger in a comedy is not gore, it is **a player being made the punchline**. Ask all seven out loud, before dice, and put the summary in the invitation.

## 2.1 The four standing questions (CODEX §1.3)

1. **What bodies are on the table?** Consent around eviction, forced labour, ownership of a body's own trait, the nearly-alive print, Total Lysis of named NPCs, the sale of a district. This Vector is *about* the last three, so ask specifically: **is "a body can be licensed" on the table as an argument, and for whom?**
2. **How graphic is a death?** Clinical (*your contents join the world*) or explicit. This Vector is written **clinical**, with the gore rationed to one scene (§7·5). Pick a register and stay in it.
3. **What is the tone dial?** Written for **deadpan institutional farce**. If your table wants a straight industrial tragedy, cut the Apron table, the queue, and THE RELIEF's invoice — the spine survives. If it wants chaos, keep all three and play the auditors *sincerely*, which is what makes chaos land.
4. **Who can pause, and how?** One word. The fiction rewinds one sentence and the scene restarts adjusted. No explanation, ever.

## 2.2 Three extra questions, specific to being the funny ones

| Ask | Because |
| :-- | :-- |
| **Who is allowed to be the butt of the joke?** | The house answer, printed here so you do not have to improvise ethics at minute nine: **institutions, procedures, and the Director's NPCs are the butt. A player, their character's body, and their character's dignity are not.** The Row can be ridiculous. A PC being humiliated is not a scene, it is a mistake. If it happens, rewind; do not discuss it. |
| **How loud is the slapstick?** | A Drift that ends in a wall is funny at the table exactly once per session for some people and constantly for others. The dial is *how often hazards physically throw people*, and it is free to change mid-session: ask once in the first scene, and ask again at the half. |
| **Does anyone want to play the company person?** | One player at almost every table quietly *wants* to be the one who reads the clause aloud and wins. Offer it out loud. If someone takes it, the Vector gets much better: put the Form in their hands and let them be terrifying. If nobody wants it, the auditor gets to be the one who does it, and the auditor is the enemy. |

## 2.3 The Rule of the Driest Thing (this Vector's only hard rule)

> **Nobody in the Foundry Belt knows they are in a comedy.**

Every NPC is sincere, competent within their own frame, and following a rule that seems obviously correct from where they are standing. The Auditor is not a bureaucrat; the Auditor is the only organism in the district who understands the schedule. The Steward is not a firebrand; the Steward is the one who read the wage book. The Line is not a villain; the Line is running. **If an NPC ever appears to be winking, they are wrong and the scene should be re-run.** The joke is always produced by the collision of two sincere procedures, never by a character being silly.

Three corollaries:
* **Specificity beats wit.** "There is a queue" is not funny. "There is a queue, and the fourth organism in it has been dead for three Beats and is still holding a numbered shell" is.
* **Never explain the joke; name the number.** Say *"nine Beats"*, *"eleven boxes"*, *"four hundred organs"*. Numbers are dry, dry is funny, and numbers are also what the ledger in §12.2 needs.
* **Escalate by register, not by volume** (§6.8). A memo is funnier than a threat; a lien is funnier than a memo.

# 3 · THE UNAVOIDABLE PROBLEM — THREE CLOCKS ON THE TABLE

## 3.1 SHIFT (6) — the district is running out of fold

Advance one segment per **Beat** spent anywhere in the Vector (a Beat is the Belt's working day; CODEX §5.1). Advance it in front of the players and name it, every time, even when they are deep in the Line: the pumps are part of the noise of the room.

| Segment | What changes in the fiction | Mechanical bite |
| --: | :-- | :-- |
| 1 | Prices move. Vials **×3**, graft re-tuning **10% → 25%** of list, and the canteen serves twice as little, half as often. | No roll. Say the new prices when they shop; the Cohort is allowed to notice. |
| 2 | **Registered gear is "scheduled for maintenance."** The Hegemony writes letters. Anyone with a licensed graft receives a Notice: it will be inspected, and it may be *suspended* pending inspection. | Each licensed graft may be **suspended** for one scene by a Hegemony functionary who is not present and does not need to be: the graft's benefit is off; its Minor Cost is not. CORE §9.4. |
| 3 | **The pump-villi cramp.** The Great Flow through the Belt stutters. Drifts cost +1 PP; the Medium's viscosity goes up one step. | CORE VII; Flow 2 → viscous 3. A Navigator in the Cohort becomes the most valuable organism in the district for two scenes. |
| 4 | **Organs sit down.** Foundry-organs across the Row stop mid-cycle. Aisle-fall: a T2 construct goes down in a corridor, and four hundred of them are going down. The district's own walls begin to be an obstacle. | **THE SILENCE** (§10.8) becomes a hazard everywhere in the Row: no repair, no print, no re-fold, and Dry — swarm regenerations stop, Osmotic builds lose their signature, and Lytic soak costs become real. |
| 5 | **The Print Convoy arrives**: the Hegemony's replacement crew, nine constructs and a re-registration crew, and THE RELIEF to escort it. Shift Nine's organs are re-registered to a new operator by clause; the Row is now *obstructing licensed production*. | Adds **THE RELIEF** (§10.6) to any scene in the Row, and every Row NPC becomes arrestable (Warden Circles, Order 3). |
| 6 | **Shift Nine is dissolved.** The Line is cut into four organs and sold. The fold in the alley has no one left who knows how to finish it, and the Nine Tune is granted. | Ending **THE ROW IS SOLD** (§12.1) is now the default; anything the Cohort has not done is done without them. |

## 3.2 BADGE (5) — how the Belt reads you

**Badge** is one dial, not five tracks (the shape is stolen from *Lasers & Feelings*; §15.1). The whole district reads you by it, and you wear it **physically**: a brass disc, issued at the Filing Gate, giftable, and confiscatable in licensed ground.

Advance BADGE by one for: taking the good money (the Relief's rate, the Hegemony's bounty, a Convoy contract); printing for the Hegemony; evicting, arresting, or re-registering anyone on the Row; breaking a picket line; **filing a grievance against the Row**.

Recover one BADGE for: carrying a grievance *for* the Row; feeding someone at the picket hearth; returning a badge to a Row organism in public; refusing paid work in a scene where you needed it.

| Badge | The Row's read of you (one cliché each — see §6.7) | What it gives | What it takes |
| --: | :-- | :-- | :-- |
| 0 | **Kin of the Row** — *"one of us, temporarily poor"* | The Row will hide, feed, and lie for you once each per scene-set. Repairs at cost. | The Hegemony reads you as an accessory to the strike: social contests with Hegemony NPCs at **−1 die**; your licences get audited first. |
| 1 | **The Temp** — harmless, rented | Both sides will talk to you. This is the best place to be, and it does not last. | — |
| 2 | **The Contractor** — you have a badge and a rate | **+1 die** in any contest with a Hegemony or Court NPC; the Gate lets you through without the queue. | Row prices double; no repairs from the Row; a Grievance filed against you is presumed true. |
| 3 | **Nine-Badges** — you have chosen | **+2 dice** Hegemony/Court; the Belt's shops sell you anything at list; THE RELIEF treats you as a colleague. | Nobody on the Row will trade with you. Nobody *attacks* you — that is the point. You are denied a service, which in a district of services is a slower death than a fight. |
| 4 | **The Bailiff's Friend** | You may serve a document in any scene, once, free. The Court hears you before it hears anyone else. | Any Row NPC may lawfully refuse you, in public, and be applauded. Archivist Dol's missing pages become a problem with your name on them. |
| 5 | **The Convoy's Own** | The Hegemony pays you 600 S and a licence, and calls you by the wrong name, warmly. | Ending **THE ROW IS SOLD** is now *your* ending; §12.1's cost is paid by you in front of everyone. |

**Never hide BADGE.** It is a brass disc. A PC can hand it to someone. Half the comedy in this Vector is a player discovering that **standing can be given away**, and that giving it away in front of an auditor is a legal act.

## 3.3 PAPER (4) — the hearing is already written

The Licence Court's hearing on the **Nine Tune** patent is scheduled for the end of Beat 4 and the Auditors have *already drafted the decision*; they are convening the hearing in order to have someone to blame it on.

| Segment | Advance when | What changes |
| --: | :-- | :-- |
| 1 | the Cohort first enters licensed ground; any scene at the Court | a summons arrives (it is a nice summons; someone spent an hour on it) |
| 2 | any scene in which the Cohort is *seen* with Row chemistry, or files a document | the Auditors request one witness. They have chosen one. It is someone the Cohort has been kind to. |
| 3 | any loud success, any seizure, any construct destroyed | the hearing is moved **forward** by one Beat. Clocks advance on loud successes, never on failed rolls (CODEX §5.2). |
| 4 | the end of Beat 4, or the Cohort's own filing | **the decision is issued.** Either the Nine Tune is licensed (§12.1), or the Cohort's filing has replaced it (§12.1, ending 4), or the patent is granted with a **counter-claim** the Cohort is now the surety for. |

**Rewinding a Clock** is printed and free: spend 1 **Cohesion**, or 2 Specimens, or one full Fugue Interval of *being in a queue*, and step one Clock back by one (CODEX §5.2). In this Vector the queue is not flavour — the queue is a **resource you can pay with**, and saying so once in Scene 1 changes how your players play for the rest of the Vector.

# 4 · HOW YOU GET HIRED — THE WAGE (d12, or choose)

The Intake in *The Long Swallow* asked *how do we get inside a body*. This Vector asks *whose wage are we on*, because in the Belt that is the same question: **the wage decides which door you are standing in front of, and the door decides who owes you.**

Each Wage prints: **where you land**, **what it gives you (mechanically)**, and **the debt** (which always falls on the organism, never on the player). Every Wage is chosen by the players. Nothing here is sprung.

# 5 · THE ROW WEB AND THE KEYS

## 5.1 THE ROW WEB — why they are a Cohort by Cycle one

Ask these at the table in order. Each answer gives the answering player **+1 Cohesion** and hands the answer to the person on their left, who must use it in the first scene. (The questions are the shape of *Lady Blackbird*'s pre-established ties; §15.1.)

1. **Which one of you put your name on the Form for the rest of you, and why did you use your real one?**
2. **What is the one thing each of you owns that is printed, licensed, and *here*?** Say what it is and what it would cost to lose it (CORE §9.2 price list; §10.7's honest breakage).
3. **Whose grievance are you carrying, and whose are you pretending not to carry?**
4. **Which one of you has worked a Line before, and what did the Line take from you?** (One Pathway rank at +1 as a *free* reward for a real answer: Membrane Penetration, Synthesis, or Fluid Navigation, the player's choice, at rank 1. This is not a discount, it is a **tell** about the character.)
5. **What did you each promise someone in the Row before you took the wage?** Say it in one sentence. If it is an Oath the group wants to see tested, encode it (CORE §11.4): it costs an Oath box and it will be worth Codon by Scene 4.
6. **Who among you is going to be the one who reads the clause aloud, and are you ashamed of being good at it?**

## 5.2 KEYS OF THE SHIFT (twelve; the players own these)

A Key is a fact, a promise, or a scar the **player** owns. Turn a Key by playing toward it: the player announces it, the Director says *yes, and here is the cost*, and the Codon is paid immediately. Turning a Key makes your own scene worse, and the Vector *rewards you for that*, which is the single mechanical idea this whole section exists to install (from *Lady Blackbird*; §15.1).

**Pay 10 Codon per Key turned. Ten keys may turn. Each Key has a buyoff: 20 S, one Specimen, or one Badge, whichever the player offers, paid to the fiction — you may buy off a Key you never want to see.**

| Key | When it turns | What the Director gets to say |
| :-- | :-- | :-- |
| **1. MY NAME IS ON THE FORM** | you are in licensed ground and someone reads the box you ticked | the box you ticked was a **claim on a lineage trait**, and it is yours (§10.9, §12.1) |
| **2. I OWE THE FUND** | the Mutuality's invoice arrives; or you ask for help and get it | your debt has been bought by the same office that issued the patent |
| **3. THE LINE KNOWS MY CHEMISTRY** | any scene where the Line acts | the Line has been printing **to your specification** for nine Beats without knowing it: your own grafts are its output (§10.1) |
| **4. I WAS ON THE NIGHT IT STOPPED** | anyone asks what happened nine Beats ago; or you lie about it | you saw the print come off the Line, and you **helped carry it** to the alley. The Row's list of the kind has your name on it, and the Court has the list |
| **5. MY GRANDMOTHER'S SHAPE IS THE PATENT** | the Nine Tune is read aloud, or your own body is scanned in an arch | your lineage has been making this fold since before the Code; you are **prior art**, in public, in your own body (Precept 3, CODEX §8.2) |
| **6. I SOLD SOMETHING I DID NOT HAVE** | the buyer arrives; the buyer is always a constructor | the thing you sold was a fold, a licence, or a person — pick one, the buyer has a receipt either way |
| **7. THE STEWARD SIGNED MY WAGE BOOK** | the Steward is arrested, or the wage book is read | the strike was legal, and your signature made it illegal, and you are the only witness who can prove which |
| **8. I TOOK THE GOOD MONEY** | Badge 3 or higher, or a Convoy payment received | the Convoy's rate was paid out of the **Row's own mutuality fund**, which is why they can afford you, which is why the Row is not angry, which is why they are furious |
| **9. SOMETHING IN THE ALLEY KNOWS MY NAME** | you enter the alley; or it enters anywhere else | the print has been folded **partly from your own chemistry**: it is not a copy of you, it is a *relative*, and the law has no word, and the Harvest line is still printed (§10.9) |
| **10. THE AUDITOR IS WRONG AND I KNOW IT** | the Court hearing, and only if you have read a document aloud in play | the patent filing names the wrong organ (organ 118, not Shift Nine). If you say it, the decision cannot be issued, and the Auditor will *thank you*, which is worse |
| **11. I HAVE A LICENCE I DID NOT READ** | the licence is invoked at the worst possible moment | clause 11: your licensed graft is **the Foundry's property while it is on your body**, and the Foundry has asked for it back, politely, in front of everyone |
| **12. I AM THE SURETY** | §10.9 is at any door, any faction, any hour | you co-signed a nearly-person. Every claim against it is now a claim against your membrane, and the Fund will collect it at any Beat |

**Keys are not a puzzle list.** A player who turns six Keys is not "solving" the Vector; they are telling you what kind of Vector they want to be in. Turn nothing you have not been offered, and pay the Codon in front of them.

# 6 · THE RULES OF THE SHIFT (all legal in the printed engine)

Everything in this section uses CORE/CODEX machinery that already exists, pointed at comedy. Nothing adds a new condition, a new economy, or a new roll.

## 6.1 The one roll, and why comedy still needs it

The Vector runs on CORE Ch. I: **n d6, beat the Activation Threshold**, 1s are Inhibitors and mark Stress, **Overflow** buys consequences, **Runaway Catalysis** buys two of them and is *the funniest thing that can happen in a scene*. Where the books print a Threshold, use it: 8 Common / 12 Standard / 16 Severe / 20 Extreme / 24 Nucleoid.

**Never raise a Threshold because the Cohort has grown** (CODEX §2.1). At Levels 4–6 the thresholds do not move; the *fiction* does. A Level-5 Cohort does not face AT 16 to find the missing register page — they face AT 12, and the page is in a room where something is running.

## 6.2 OFFERS (the compel, paid in the house's own currency)

When a PC's cliché, Wage, Key, or Oath would obviously make them do the wrong thing in front of people, the Director **offers**: state the complication, offer **1 Cohesion**, and stop. The player takes it or declines. Declining is free, costs nothing, and is never argued. (The shape is Fate's compel, from the free SRDs; §15.1. The currency is the house's own **Cohesion**, CORE §2.5, and Cohesion is built by exactly the things an Offer pays for: endurance in company, kept oaths, shared meals at a chemotactic hearth — and the picket hearth in §7·2 is a chemotactic hearth, printed, with soup.)

**An Offer is not a suggestion about roleplay.** It is always a *consequence the fiction can enforce*: a box ticked, a badge seen, a debt seen, a sentence heard through a wall. And: **you may Offer an NPC.** The Auditor's cliché is *the schedule*; an Offer to the Auditor is the single best tool in this Vector, and the players should learn it by watching you do it once, at someone else's expense, in Scene 1.

## 6.3 THE GRIEVANCE (the Expanse's favourite object)

A **Grievance** is a filed complaint against a licensed operator, held on the Row's ledger (and, since the Ledger is registered, also at the Court). It is an objective with two pay-outs:

* **Filing** a Grievance: takes one scene (a scribe, a witness, 5 S) and pays **1 Cohesion and 2 Codon**. It is a real legal act: the operator must answer within one Beat or the PAPER Clock advances against them.
* **Winning** a Grievance (the remedy is delivered): **15 Codon**, the remedy as printed on the d12 in §13.4, and one rung of standing for the Row.

Filing pays *even when the grievance is absurd*, because filing is procedural and the Row has learned that procedure pays. This is the joke, and the joke is load-bearing: **the Cohort can print Codon by reading their surroundings carefully**, which is the exact skill the Vector is testing.

## 6.4 THE APRON (d8; the players pick or roll one each at the first scene)

Stolen whole from *Honey Heist*'s d8 table of hats (§15.1). An Apron is a Common graft, **1 slot**, with one printed benefit and one printed Minor Cost (CORE §9.4's grammar), and it is the only gear in this Vector that nobody is licensed for, because the Row made it.

| d8 | Apron | Benefit | Minor Cost |
| --: | :-- | :-- | :-- |
| 1 | **The Too-Large Apron** (it belonged to someone bigger, and the Row is not going to say who) | +1 soak against Kinetic and Osmotic push; you can carry 2 extra Bulk | −1 Drift in any contest where being seen clearly matters (Concealment, Deceptive registers) |
| 2 | **The Apron of the Late Shift** (nine Beats of chemistry in the weave) | once per scene, name the last thing the room printed: Chemoreception free, no roll | every Warden arch reads you as **overdue**: social contests with Wardens at −1 die |
| 3 | **Nine Aprons, One Legal** | you may present as any one of nine registered workers once per Vector; one arch will believe you | the other eight aprons are *claims*: eight organisms can lawfully demand their apron back, in public, at any time |
| 4 | **The Apron With the Pockets Sewn Shut by Someone Else** | contains one thing you did not put there (§13.7, roll once at the start; the Director names it, the Cohort does not) | you cannot open the pockets during a search without admitting the search, and you cannot open them in licensed ground at all |
| 5 | **The Chaperone Apron** (a parlour reject; it *almost* folds you) | once per scene: reroll one Burst's Inhibitor (a 1 becomes a 2). It is not a repair; it is assistance | on a rerolled 1, mark **Brittle**: the fold was wrong, and wrong is a shape (CORE §4.7) |
| 6 | **The Quiet Apron** (mucoid weave; the Line's own scrap) | the Line and all Constructs read you as **in-frame**: they do not initiate, do not block, and do not report you for one scene | the Hegemony's graft-scan reads you as **unlicensed**: your registered gear is suspended for the rest of the Beat (§3.1, segment 2) |
| 7 | **The Steward's Apron** (pockets inside pockets, each with a form in it) | you may file one Grievance free per scene, in someone else's name | every filing names you, and the Court learns your name early: PAPER +1 per scene you file |
| 8 | **The Apprentice Apron** (small; someone made it for a young one) | while wearing it, any Row NPC will explain one thing fully, free, twice | you are a **minor** in the Belt's own legal grammar: your signature is not binding, and neither is anyone's signature *to* you |

## 6.5 BADGE as a dial (recap for play)

One number, 0–5, brass and physical (§3.2). **Both ends pay.** If your players have not noticed they can *give* the badge away, engineer it: an old printer asks to hold it while they cross the Row, and they do not give it back, and the Row cheers.

## 6.6 THE QUEUE (waiting is a scene, and the scene is the point)

The Filing Gate's queue is forty-one long. **Every significant wait rolls once on d6 (§13.3's neighbours), and a wait can be *spent* to rewind a Clock (§3.3).** Say this once, in Scene 1: *"You can wait, and waiting is not nothing."* Comedic pay-off guaranteed, because your players will absolutely try to solve a labour dispute by queueing. Let them; pay them.

## 6.7 THE CLICHÉ (every NPC is one line, and the line can be leaned on)

Stolen from *Risus*, the free cliché-dice game (§15.1). Every NPC in this Vector is printed with a **cliché and a number**: `Nine-Grudge — Steward Who Signed — 4 dice`. The number is their pool for social contests only, and it is *not* their combat pool.

A player may **lean** on a cliché once per NPC per Vector: state the cliché back to the NPC as the reason they will do the thing (`you are the sort of steward who would rather lose an argument than sign a wage book, so you will not sign`). That grants **+2 dice** to the social contest, and the NPC must behave in a way that is *credibly in character*. **A cliché never makes an NPC stupid.** It makes them *themselves*, harder. Leaning is the single most comic action available in this Vector, and it is also how a Cohort at Level 5 defeats a Level-12 Overseer: not by out-muscling it, but by being the ones who know which shape it is.

## 6.8 Escalation by register (say it out loud as it climbs)

No new monster type. **The same procedures, better at finding you.**

**Paper ladder:** a **memo** (free, funny, wrong) → a **form** (someone must sign; that is the Cohort) → a **fine** (Substrate, and it is small, which is the insult) → a **suspension** (a licence, a wage, or a hearth stops) → a **seizure** (an Apparatus, a graft, a Specimen: CORE §10.7's honest breakage, with printed fair warning) → a **lien** (your body is now collateral; CORE §9.7's Gene-Debt grammar, CODEX §10.5's collectors).

**Force ladder:** a **challenge** (say PRESENT; answer chemically, walk on) → a **charge** (a Warden states a register reason; it is a wrong reason) → an **escort** (you are walked somewhere; this is a scene, not a fight) → a **warrant** (four Wardens, and they would prefer not) → **THE RELIEF** (§10.6, and they invoice) → a **licensed crew with a bailiff** (which is the Vector's worst ending and it arrives with paperwork already stamped).

## 6.9 MUTUALITY (why this Vector is relaxed, printed so the table can trust it)

The Row runs a **mutuality fund**: an insurance scheme that every Row organism pays into and that the Cohort is, by Wage 5, inside.

* Once per movement, if a PC goes **Dormant** and the Cohort has *not left them behind*, the Steward's crew fishes them out at the next Beat: they wake with **2 Stress**, and the Fund's invoice is **60 S** (or one Grievance filed on the Fund's behalf, which the Steward will absolutely accept).
* This applies to **Dormancy only. Never Total Lysis**, and never if the Cohort left the organism where it fell. The books' own death-spiral guardrails (CORE §5.11) and Legacy (CORE §12.2) stand exactly as printed.
* **Announce the ceiling, once, out loud, at the start** (CODEX §3.5's discipline of saying what a fight is): *"In this Vector, Dormancy is survivable and funny, and Total Lysis only happens where I have said so beforehand."* Then never spend it. Your players will stop playing for safety and start playing for the funny idea, which is what the whole Vector is built to harvest.

# 7 · THE FIVE SITES (a pointcrawl with a spine)

**Say the spine out loud in Scene 1:** *"Four hundred organs have stopped. One machine has not. And a shape somebody is wearing is on the market."* Every scene is one of those three sentences wearing different shoes.

The sites are a **pointcrawl**, not a map: five nodes, printed routes between them, and a route list you can read at the table. Nothing is hidden; the difficulty is choosing which node to be standing in when the Clock fills. (The shape is Cairn's pointcrawl; §15.1.)

| From | To | The route | It costs |
| :-- | :-- | :-- | :-- |
| Filing Gate | Picket Hearth | the brine-walk under four hundred silent organs | 1 Beat if you walk it openly as a badge-holder; free if you hand your badge to someone and walk it as Kin |
| Picket Hearth | Print Floor | the service alley past organ 118's cage | a Warden arch (Badge 2+ passes; Badge 0–1 must be *escorted*, which is a scene) |
| Print Floor | Licence Court | the pump-lift, which has stopped | 1 Beat, and 2 PP per Drift through the stiff medium (§3.1 seg 3) |
| Licence Court | Alley behind 41 | you must cross the Row to get there, and the Row is watching everyone who comes out of the Court | BADGE +1 for the walk if you are carrying anything from the Court |
| Alley behind 41 | anywhere | Hush's route: a drainage channel that the pump-villi have forgotten | 1 Beat, and the print comes with you if you take it (Bulk 4; it helps; it is very polite about it) |

**Medium (whole Vector, CODEX §6.6):** hypertonic waste brine · **turbid** · **Flow 2, pump-driven, and the pumps stop at shift-change** — which is the first joke of the Vector and the last one too, because in nine Beats there has been no shift-change. Viscosity climbs one step per SHIFT segment (§3.1). A Turbulence Navigator in the Cohort is worth a Retainer's wage here, and the Row will offer it.

## 7 · 1 · THE FILING GATE — *approach: a queue, a form, a wage*

**The scene's question:** *Which door are we standing in front of, and what will we sign to be let through it?*
**The bang that opens it:** a clerk is already holding out a **badge** and a **Form with eleven boxes**, and the clerk is *kind*, and the kindness is the problem: "you'll only need three of these to be untrue, and I'm not allowed to say which."

**What is here.** A Warden arch (register read, free, unavoidable); a Hegemony licence clerk (**Clerk Second-to-Last**, §8.3, Colonist, Level 3, non-combat, five unspent Oath boxes, who makes excellent tea and will absolutely sign the wrong thing if you ask it properly); the **Mutuality desk** (the Fund's insurer, who will sell the Cohort coverage for 20 S and explain §6.9 better than this book does); the **badge rack** (nine hundred badges, most of them warm); the **notice board** (Shift Nine's output for nine Beats, itemised in the Overseer's hand, and the items are blank); and the **queue**, forty-one long.

**The queue is real.** Roll d6 on §13.3 for whoever is next to you when you arrive, and again for anyone you wait behind for more than one scene. Waiting is a **resource**: a Cohort that spends a Beat in the queue may rewind one Clock segment (§3.3). Say that in the first minute.

**The Form (handout, Appendix A).** Eleven boxes. Ticking a box is a legal act and some of them are **Keys** (§5.2) or Gene-Debt segments (CORE §9.7, CODEX §10.5). **Read the boxes aloud, one at a time, and let the players ask questions.** The clerk answers truthfully within the limits of what the clerk knows, which is the entire engine of the scene. Nothing here is a trap the players cannot see: three boxes are lies **about the Foundry**, not about the players.

**What can be taken here, lawfully (three of them, and each one is a scene):**
1. the **numbered shells** the queue-leaver left when they stopped being a queue-member (they are dead, they have been dead three Beats, they are holding a licence for a district two regions away — an unclaimed licence is *worth 100 S and a conversation*);
2. the **badge rack's ledger**, which lists nine hundred badge-holders including one who has been holding the same badge for nine Beats and whose name has been crossed out in three different hands;
3. the **blank notice board**: taking it down is a public act, and the Row will read your faces while you do it.

**Harvest / loot:** none (this is a bureaucracy). Take 30 S in "unclaimed wage float" if they find the Mutuality's error, and pay **15 Codon** for the Wage (§4) plus **10 Codon** if they catch the clerk's arithmetic mistake.

**If they miss it (three clues, §13.1 all live here):** the crossed-out name; the dead queue-member's licence; the clerk's own Oath boxes, which are visible, unspent, and *wrong for a clerk* (a clerk with five unspent Oath boxes has been told not to swear, which is a fact about the Court).

## 7 · 2 · THE PICKET HEARTH (SHIFTLESS ROW) — *the first room, wrong*

**The scene's question:** *Can we buy anything here, and are we willing to be seen buying it?*
**The bang that opens it:** someone hands the Cohort a bowl of hot chemistry from the **hearth** — a chemotactic hearth, CORE §2.5, the thing that builds Cohesion — and asks them, loudly and politely, to say **whose wage they are on**, in front of four hundred quiet foundry-organs.

**What is here.** Four hundred foundry-organs, silent, mid-cycle, some still holding a half-printed fold. A **hearth** with soup that is not good and is enough. **Steward Nine-Grudge** (§8.3, and §10.4), who did not organise the strike and has been made responsible for it. A **list of the kind** (Wage 10: the Cohort is on it, and it is read aloud in Court). The **alley behind organ 41**, visible from here, with a light in it that should not be on. A **collapsed organ, 118**, in its maintenance cage, which is where Wage 5's claim and Wage 11's real tenant both live.

**Rules of the Row.**
* **THE SILENCE** (§10.8) is the hazard: Dry, no print, no repair, no re-fold. It costs nothing and it ends builds. Say what it does the first time it matters, in one sentence, and never threaten it.
* **Nobody on the Row will attack the Cohort. Ever.** Even at Badge 5. The Row's weapon is **service denial** (§3.2), and it is a better tool than a fight: no repairs, no Vials, no lies on your behalf, no soup. Let the players discover that being *unpopular in a district of services* is a mechanical condition, not a mood.
* The **hearth pays Cohesion** (CORE §2.5): a shared meal at the hearth is +1 Cohesion, once per movement, and it is the best deal in the Vector. If the Cohort eats at the hearth while wearing Badge 3 or more, the meal still happens, and nobody speaks, and the soup is still given to them — which is the hardest, funniest image in this book and you should protect it.
* **Detritus Feeders are eating the strike** (E2, §9): they are on the corpses of the silent organs, they are *growing*, and the Row has domesticated two of them because the Row has always domesticated something.

**Loot worth having (three, each a scene):** a **Quiet Apron** from the scrap bins (§6.4, d8 result 6, free, and the Row will watch you put it on); a **Pristine Sequence** in organ 118's claim cage (200 S, and it is *part of the assessment*, which is Wage 5's whole problem); the **fold a silent organ was holding when it stopped**, which is unlisted and worth a conversation with Kel-Vasht (§10.3).

## 7 · 3 · THE PRINT FLOOR OF SHIFT NINE — *the problem room, still running*

**The scene's question:** *Do we stop the machine, feed it, or ask it what it is making?*
**The bang that opens it:** the Line is **running**. Nobody is running it. It has been running for nine Beats, on a specification nobody signed, and the output is on the far table, stacked, folded, and *nearly finished*.

**What is here.**
* **THE LINE** (§10.1) — abiotic, an entity, a third party. Roll its output on the d10 in §13.6 the first time anyone asks what it has made, and again whenever anyone does something loud near it. The Line is not haunted and not malicious: it has a specification and a schedule, and the specification was written by a body, not an office.
* **Overseer Kel-Vasht of Shift Nine** (§10.3, §8.3): Level 12, Immunan Complement Cascade Commander, and **not a fight at these Levels** — the Overseer's whole block exists so you can argue with it at AT 16 and *lose correctly*. It wants the Cohort to testify that the Smith's tune is prior art, so that the patent dies, and it does not want to be the one who says so, because the trait on the patent application is **its own**, and the receipt for having bought it is folded inside its membrane (CODEX §7.1, verbatim: *"its own lineage trait was bought, not inherited; it carries the receipt"*).
* **The Fold Parlour** — the district's only chaperone parlour: the room where a printed chain is *folded* into the shape that makes it work. It is, in every respect, a spa: towels, an appointment book, an apologetic attendant, and a queue of proteins with a ticket number. The attendant is a chaperonin-build (a construct with the **Assist** special, §10.2). It is the funniest room in the Vector and it is also where **Level 6 happens** (below).
* **The jam** in organ 118's feed: a **Synthesis contest at AT 16**, and it falls to three printed observations (§13.1), and the correct answer is boring, which is the point: the Line stopped *for a reason that has nothing to do with the strike*, and fixing it advances SHIFT **and** BADGE, because the machine is the third party.
* **THE RELIEF arrives on Beat 3** (§10.6), and they arrive *on time*, which is the only thing about them that is not funny.

**Growth on camera (this is where Level 6 lands, and it is a joke you get for free).** At **Level 6** a Stage-I organism takes its **Stage II features** (ATLAS §4.1: one Phenotype feature, one Synthesis Choice, +2 max MI, +1 Bulk). Stage it in the Parlour: the Cohort's new ability is **printed and folded on the machine that everyone is fighting about**, it arrives with a licence card and a warranty, and the attendant asks them to read the warranty aloud. Then, at the next arch, someone points out that a **folded-in public feature is now on the register**. The growth scene is the plot. Do not skip it.

**Loot / harvest:** Constructs and Construct parts sell at **2×** in the Belt (CODEX §6.6) — but harvesting a *Row* construct is a Precept-adjacent act the Ash will price socially (§11.3). The Line leaves **folded output** (harvest it on the d10 in §13.6; some of it is a Pristine Sequence, some of it is a form, one of them is a small organism that is not finished, and the Cohort must decide what to do with that one *now*, in the room, in front of everyone).

## 7 · 4 · THE LICENCE COURT — *the cost room*

**The scene's question:** *Do we win by their rules, and are we willing to become the kind of organism that does?*
**The bang that opens it:** the three Auditors already have the decision drafted. They are convening the hearing to find someone to have **found** it. Auditor Eleven-Brief wants, more than anything, to be *helped*. What it does not want is to be proved wrong in public, and those are the same sentence, said differently.

**What is here.** Three Auditors (Eleven-Brief and two who never speak, whose silence is doing enormous work); **Clerk Second-to-Last** with the tea; the **arch** at the door, which scans every organism and every graft (this is the comedy set-piece *and* a rules event: the arch finds the Cohort's licensed gear, and §3.1 segment 2 and Key 11 are live here); a public gallery of forty organisms who are all holding numbered shells; a **schedule** on the wall that has the decision's date written on it in advance, in the wrong hand; and the **Nine Tune** in a specimen cradle, being exhibited as evidence, which is a mistake, because the exhibit is a *body sample of a lineage trait* and anyone with Chemoreception 2 can tell.

**How the hearing runs (CORE §11.1, three exchanges, no persuasion rolls).** Registers: **SEMANTIC** (AFF + Signal Discourse vs OSM + Chemoreception) for facts and clauses; **AFFECTIVE** (AFF + Symbiosis or Vigour vs VSC + Pressure Tolerance) for the gallery, the Steward, and the Overseer's dignity; **DECEPTIVE** (DFF + Concealment, or OSM with forgery tools) for the filing error, if they want to commit it rather than find it. **State what a win buys before exchange one**, read the Stakes Ladder (CORE §11.2), and let **Overflow buy rungs**. A stalemate (nobody wins two of three) is *information*: the hearing is adjourned, PAPER +1, and the Cohort learns which Auditor wrote the decision.

**The nine clauses (d9, §13.5).** Every clause is a real rule of this world with a printed answer, and the Cohort can *learn* them by playing. Winning a hearing by reading is the intended victory condition of the Vector, and it pays more Codon than the fight in the corridor (§12.2).

**The counter-claim, and the joke that ends the Vector.** If the Cohort proves prior art (Key 5, Key 10, the exhibit's smell, the wage book, or an Offer accepted at exactly the wrong moment), the Auditors do not lose — they **issue the licence with a counter-claim**, and the counter-claim needs a **surety**, and the room full of organisms who hate the Cohort is *not eligible*, and the Cohort is. The Court is not defeated by being out-argued. It is defeated by being handed a better document, by someone willing to co-sign a person (§10.9).

## 7 · 5 · THE ALLEY BEHIND ORGAN 41 — *the yield, defended by nobody*

**The scene's question:** *Is the thing in the cradle a *what* or a *who*, and who is going to say which one out loud?*
**The bang that opening it:** the print is **tidying**. It has a stack of folded towels, it has swept the alley, it has been waiting nine Beats for someone to tell it whether it is finished, and it is *polite*.

**What is here.** **Foundry Mother "Hush"** (§10.7, §8.3): Level 15, Peptide Constructor, Elite plus a swarm of labour-constructs, and **do not fight this one** — it has been printing in secret for nine Beats because it is *bored of legality* (CODEX §7.1, verbatim), and it has been waiting for somebody to ask it to print something **nobody has licensed**. It will do that for the Cohort, freely, and it will explain exactly why that is a terrible idea, warmly, at length. The **cradle**, and **THE UNLISTED PRINT** (§10.9, with its printed Harvest line, which is the most offensive object in this book and is *meant to be*: the Hegemony has priced a nearly-person, and the price is on the paper, and the Row has a copy).

**Three exits, printed, because an alley you cannot leave is a bug, not a premise:**
1. **the drainage channel** (free; the print follows you if it wants to; it wants to);
2. **Hush's route through organ 41** (costs a construct's parts: 120 S or one Pristine Sequence);

# 14 · RUN IT SHORT OR LONG

## 14.1 Four hours (the compressed Vector)

1. **Scene 1 — The Filing Gate (45 min).** The Form read aloud box by box (Appendix A), the Wage chosen from §4 (offer six, not twelve), the Row Web cut to **questions 1, 2, 6**, BADGE issued. **Say the queue rule and the Mutuality rule here or you will never get to use them.**
2. **Scene 2 — The Row and the hearth (60 min).** Soup, the badge question, E2 as a farce, one Grievance filed, one errand from §13.7. The alley visible, the light on.
3. **Scene 3 — The Print Floor (70 min).** The Line running unasked; the jam (three observations); Kel-Vasht asked properly; the Stage II scene if anyone crossed Level 6; THE RELIEF arrives on time.
4. **Scene 4 — The Court and the alley (85 min).** The hearing as three exchanges (§7·4), then the alley, then the ledger read aloud (CODEX §12.6's discipline is the last scene's punctuation).
**Cut:** SHIFT to **3**, BADGE to **3**, PAPER to **2**, errands to one, the queue to two neighbours. **Never cut:** the Form, the hearth, the Line's output, and the alley. Those four are the Vector.

## 14.2 Six sessions (the crawl)

1 The Form, the queue, the Wage, the dead licence, the badge ledger.
2 The hearth and the picket; the Steward's signature; Grievance 4 or 9 filed.
3 Organ 118: the assessment, the Pristine Sequence, the wrong building, Wage 11's real tenant.
4 The Print Floor: the jam, the Line's shelf, the Parlour, **Level 6 on camera**, THE RELIEF's first pass.
5 The Court: the gallery, the arch, the clauses, the Clerk's Oath, the counter-claim and the surety question.
6 The alley: Hush asked properly, the application on the schedule, the ending, the ledger. Then **Act II** (CODEX §8.5, *The Long Swallow*) is already running: the Cohort has a serial phrase, a surety, and a licence with its name on it.

## 14.3 Five minutes of prep, tonight (CODEX §13.4 applied)

1. What does the compartment **smell of**? — *brine, hot metal, and nine Beats of one fold going slightly wrong.*
2. Which **Clock** advances tonight, and what will the players do about it? — *SHIFT, and it makes the walls start falling down.*
3. What does the enemy want that is **not violence**? — *the Auditor wants to be corrected; the Steward wants to be overruled; Hush wants to be asked.*
4. The **one printable weakness** of the strongest thing here? — *the Line prints what it is fed; the Silence ends when anything starts printing.*
5. Who is **counting** something the Cohort has not noticed? — *the Line is nine Beats into one print, and the Court's schedule already has the alley on it.*

## 14.4 Pacing dials (the Vector is a deck you shuffle yourself)

**Where you put the alley is a choice you make before Session 1, not a fact about the Belt.** Decide the depth — four hours, six sessions, or eleven — and every quantity follows: Wages offered (six compressed, twelve written), Keys in play (three compressed, all twelve crawl), Clock segments (SHIFT 3/6/9; BADGE 3/5/5; PAPER 2/4/6), Grievances (one filed / d12 / d12 twice). This is the free-game trick of putting the End Card wherever you want the last act to fall (*For the Queen*'s SRD, §15.1), and it is the only dial that keeps a 20-page Vector from becoming a 60-page one.

**Frame every site by its question, then cut.** Each node has one agenda; open it with a **bang** (the thing that forces a choice), escalate with sequenced bangs, and **cut the scene the moment the question resolves** — do not fade it out:

| Site | The scene's question | The bang that opens it |
| :-- | :-- | :-- |
| The Filing Gate | *What will we sign to be let through this door?* | a kind clerk handing you a badge and eleven boxes, two of which are lies about the Foundry |
| The Row / the hearth | *Can we be fed and employed in the same afternoon?* | a bowl of soup and a public request to name whose wage you are on |
| The Print Floor | *Do we stop the machine, feed it, or ask what it is making?* | the Line is running, unasked, nine Beats into one print |
| The Fold Parlour | *What are we willing to have fixed, and what will it cost to have it fixed properly?* | an appointment book with entries nine Beats past the stop |
| The Licence Court | *Do we win by their rules, and are we the kind of organism that does?* | the decision already drafted, and the Auditors looking for a finder |
| The alley behind 41 | *Is the thing in the cradle a *what* or a *who*, and who says which first?* | it is tidying, and it asks whether you are the ones who are asked |

**Two borrowed shapes worth knowing, because your table will drift into one of them.** The **labour-dispute shape** — *grievance → stop → scabs → hearing → settlement or sale* — is the Vector's spine, and every free workplace comedy in the hobby runs on it. The **heist shape** (*case → enter → complicate → double-cross → out*) is what your players will reach for in Scene 1, because they have badges and a schedule; if they take it, **let them**: the heist in this Vector is stealing *the filing*, and the double-cross is the Auditor's wrong building. If your session is drifting, ask which of the five words you are in — **wage, picket, print, hearing, cradle** — and play the next one.

## 15.2 The house rules this Vector obeys, line by line

Never let a failure be "nothing happens" — apply a **Cost** (CODEX §2.3). Advance Clocks for **succeeding loudly**, never for a failed roll, and let the Cohort rewind one segment by spending time, Cohesion, or Specimens (CODEX §5.2) — hence §6.6's queue as a resource. Print **one findable weakness** for anything bigger than the Cohort (CODEX §4.4, §13.2). Design a puzzle to fall to **three observations** and a site to have a **spine you say out loud** (CODEX §9.1, §9.4). Budget by **BSP**, pay **Codon** separately, never add the two (CODEX §4). **Never inflate an Activation Threshold because the Cohort has Grown** — change the fiction (+1 die for a good tool, −1 in a hostile tonic; CODEX §2.1): every Threshold in this book is printed once, and §10's header says so. Write a creature **backwards from what it leaves behind**, and make the big kills **cost a faction** (BESTIARY front matter; §11.3). **And the house's own death-spiral guardrails stand** (CORE §5.11; §6.9 only removes surprise, never death).

## 15.3 The comedy's own lineage (nobody invents a body with a payroll)

The inside-a-body tradition has always been a *workplace* tradition, and this Vector is the one in the line that admits it: **Once Upon a Time… Life** (1987, the cartoon that turned the body into a city of workers with uniforms and shifts); **Cells at Work!** (*Hataraku Saibou* — a red blood cell whose entire character is *being a delivery worker who is always late*, white cells as armed police, platelets as a toddler crew, memory cells as veterans who remember the last infection); **Osmosis Jones** (2001, the immune system as a precinct and a hygiene lecture); **Magic School Bus: For Lunch** (a school trip down a throat); **Legend of the Leukocytes** (four white blood cells, real immunology as the mechanics); **Fantastic Voyage** (1966) and **Innerspace** (1987 — the same device played as farce because the wrong person swallowed the ship); and, on the other side of the glass, the biology education video that made the metaphor respectable: Harvard's ***The Inner Life of the Cell***, where the motor proteins **walk**, and the signal peptide is a *address label named Blobby*. The reason the workplace reading is not a joke bolted onto the setting is that **cell biology has always talked like a factory** — the Golgi is "the post office", the ribosome is "the printer", the proteasome is "the shredder", and the immune system **shows identification to itself**. This Vector just takes the metaphor at its word and asks the question a metaphor always eventually asks: **who is on the payroll, and who owns the shape of the tool?**

## 15.5 Safety lineage, stated properly (the comedy version)

The toolkit is the standard one, attributed: **Lines & Veils** (Emily Care Boss, in *Sex & Sorcery*, a supplement for Ron Edwards' *Sorcerer*, from Nordic larp), the **X-card** (John Stavropoulos), **Script Change** (Brie Beau Sheldon: rewind, fast-forward, pause), and a **debrief** after any session that went somewhere heavy. A comedy session adds three things and this Vector prints them in §2.2: **who is allowed to be the butt of the joke** (institutions, not players), **the slapstick dial** (how often hazards throw bodies), and **an explicit offer to play the company man**, because the person who enjoys reading the clause aloud is doing the most dangerous and most valuable work at the table. Content note goes on the invitation, not in an appendix: this Vector is about eviction, licensed ownership of a body's own trait, and a nearly-person with a price on it.

## 15.6 Why this book is laid out the way it is

The published craft literature is blunt about what makes a short module feel expensive, and the list is not art: **real margins and white space**; a **run-this-adventure page** carrying premise, Clocks, ladders, exits, tone, and content note; **one source of truth per fact** with cross-references; **keys short enough to read at a glance**; **stat blocks on the same spread as the scene that needs them**; **labelled** dice tables; a back page of props; and no "GM, when the players arrive…" plot assumptions. Its negatives are the cheapness this Vector avoids: walls of boxed prose, an escalation ladder that is secretly an encounter list, unlabelled tables, and padding by reprint. So: §0 is the run page; §3 prints the Clocks and the ladders before any prose; §7's nodes are keyed and short; §10's blocks are together and §9 tells you which is where; §13 is labelled and always hands the players an action; **Appendix A is a prop you can print**, because the Form *is* the Vector's thesis, and a table that has ticked its own boxes does not need to be told what the story is about.

### THE ONE THING TO REMEMBER TONIGHT

Nobody in the Foundry Belt is winking, and the machine is running. The Overseer is protecting a secret about its own body, the Steward is protecting a signature, the Auditor is protecting a mistake, the strike is protecting a neighbourhood, and the Line is protecting **a specification nobody has the authority to cancel** — and your four organisms have a wage, an apron, and a Form with eleven boxes in front of them. **Read the boxes aloud.
