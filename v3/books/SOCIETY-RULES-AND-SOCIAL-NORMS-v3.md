# The Osmotic Expanse — Society Rules and Social Norms · v3 table edition

*An update to how the Society document is used. Every fact is from the original "SOCIETY RULES AND SOCIAL NORMS", which remains the full reference for the colony's organs and offices, the Tide, budlines and Rites, mutualism, death and the Ash. What changes in v3 is delivery: each law and custom is a card a Director can play from and a new player can follow, and the AI Director looks these same cards up during play.*

## Seven things everyone here knows

Read this to new players. It is all they need before the first scene.

1. **Water moves toward salt.** Being dense and salty is being rich; being watery is being poor. It shows on your body.
2. **You are read at every gate.** Nobody walks in anywhere unexamined.
3. **If it is not written down, it did not happen.** A recorded line beats any argument. A smell is only a rumour.
4. **A promise sworn before a witness is real.** It is written into your body, and breaking it shows.
5. **Nothing dead is wasted.** Every body is harvested or properly buried. Walking away from one is a crime.
6. **You may not lie about what you are.** Masks are illegal, except during the festival of the Swell.
7. **Your body is yours.** Nobody may change it without your consent, and you can prosecute them yourself.

## The Sacred Code: seven Precepts, as cards

### Precept 1 · Containment
**In plain words.** Nobody enters a colony without being read at the gate.
**Enforced by** the Wardens and the Membrane.
**What people say.** "Hold still for the reading." "Unread is unwelcome."
**If you break it.** A fine or a day sealed up; opening an illegal door gets the tool cut out of you.
**How it comes up in play.** a gate that reads everyone; smuggling; a hidden pipe.

### Precept 2 · Non-Waste
**In plain words.** Every body must be harvested or properly buried. Leaving the dead to rot is a crime.
**Enforced by** the Wardens and the Ash.
**What people say.** "Nothing unwasted." "Who is cutting this one?"
**If you break it.** Forced work for the keepers of the dead, a day per body.
**How it comes up in play.** a fight leaves bodies: someone must deal with them; the Ash arrive with a licence; scavengers gather.

### Precept 3 · Catalysis is debt
**In plain words.** If you make or mend something for someone, it must be written down. Gifts create debts.
**Enforced by** the Ash.
**What people say.** "Is that on the Ledger?" "I will owe you a line."
**If you break it.** A fine of twice the gift's value.
**How it comes up in play.** healing a stranger; a favour offered instead of pay; someone refusing help so as not to owe.

### Precept 4 · Tonicity is truth
**In plain words.** You may not lie about what you are: your family line, your nature, your promises. Masks are illegal, except at the Swell festival.
**Enforced by** the Nucleolus.
**What people say.** "State what you are." "Your scent says otherwise."
**If you break it.** Ten days sealed up; worse if done in a public vote.
**How it comes up in play.** disguises; forged papers; being asked to state what you are.

### Precept 5 · The Code is written
**In plain words.** Do not rewrite a region's rules and do not cut the Sequence Forest.
**Enforced by** the Wardens and the Curators.
**What people say.** "It is written."
**If you break it.** Loss of wealth and standing, and powerful people take an interest.
**How it comes up in play.** ancient machinery; someone wanting a record changed.

### Precept 6 · A body is its own argument
**In plain words.** Nobody may alter a body without its consent. The victim can prosecute in person; this is called Argument.
**Enforced by** the victim.
**What people say.** "I did not consent." "I invoke Argument."
**If you break it.** The graft is cut out; repeat offenders are struck from the register.
**How it comes up in play.** patents on living traits; forced grafts; who speaks for someone who cannot.

### Precept 7 · The Membrane is mercy
**In plain words.** Do not break the world's outer wall or trade with what is outside it.
**Enforced by** the Wardens.
**What people say.** "Nothing good is out there."
**If you break it.** Struck from the register.
**How it comes up in play.** things from outside; the Deep Choir, who deny this law.

## Customs, as cards

### The Register
**In plain words.** The official list of who exists. If you are on it you have rights. If you are not, you are Unlisted and have none.
**What people say.** "Are you listed?" "There is no line for that name."
**If you break it.** Forging a line is a serious crime, though ordinary people often sympathise.
**How it comes up in play.** papers checked at any gate; someone offering to make a person legal; the threat of being struck off.

### The Unlisted
**In plain words.** People with no entry on the Register. Invisible to tax and to law, and unprotected by either.
**What people say.** "Nobody saw me." "I cannot sign."
**How it comes up in play.** a helper who cannot be a witness; a newborn or newly made person with no entry; saying where someone came from can unlist them.

### Condition (class)
**In plain words.** Wealth shows on the body. The Dry are dense and rich. The Settled are the middle. The Swollen are watery and poor. Proverb: "Salt calls salt; water finds the floor."
**What people say.** "You look diluted." "She has concentrated nicely."
**How it comes up in play.** who gets served first; who is believed; being ruined is called being diluted.

### Oaths
**In plain words.** A promise written into your body, usually before a witness. Kept, it earns trust. Broken, it scars you and everyone can tell.
**What people say.** "Swear it." "Witnessed."
**If you break it.** The Oath's own cost, and loss of standing.
**How it comes up in play.** paying with a promise instead of money; an official who has been told not to swear; a promise that becomes inconvenient.

### The Ledger
**In plain words.** The world's memory of who owes what. A written, witnessed line beats any argument.
**What people say.** "Show me the line." "That is only a rumour."
**How it comes up in play.** a claim voided by one entry; debts inherited from family; a clerk who can find the page.

### Proof
**In plain words.** A smell or a rumour is not proof. A recorded line is. Showing the truth to a whole crowd at once (a Reveal) also counts.
**What people say.** "A smell is not proof." "Everybody saw."
**How it comes up in play.** players who know something but cannot prove it; choosing where and to whom a truth is said; a truth that would harm the one who tells it.

### The Quorum
**In plain words.** How a colony decides: everybody signals at once, like a vote you can smell. Silencing it is one of the worst crimes.
**What people say.** "The air has it."
**If you break it.** Struck from the register.
**How it comes up in play.** a crowd deciding in front of the Cohort; someone trying to sway or silence a vote.

### Justice is a reading
**In plain words.** An accused person is not only questioned. Their body is read, because a body's chemistry is testimony.
**What people say.** "Submit to assay."
**How it comes up in play.** being accused; evidence that is somebody's body; the three ways out: pay, swear, or run.

### Yielding
**In plain words.** Hurting someone who has given up is against custom, and everyone who watches is shaken by it.
**What people say.** "I yield."
**If you break it.** Ten days sealed up.
**How it comes up in play.** an enemy surrenders; the Cohort surrenders.

### The Hearth
**In plain words.** A shared meal and warmth. Eating together builds trust; refusing a bowl is an insult.
**What people say.** "Sit. Eat."
**How it comes up in play.** being offered food by people who dislike you; a place to rest and talk.

## Manners by condition

| **Dry** (dense, rich) | being seen first and speaking second | making you wait | calling you watered |
| **Settled** (the middle) | naming their trade | splitting the difference | doubting your record |

## The law at the table

When the Cohort is accused of something, the Director needs three answers: how it is proved, what the sentence is, and how to get out of it.

- **How it is proved.** By a recorded line, by a public Reveal, or by reading the accused's body. A smell or a rumour starts trouble but proves nothing.
- **Sentences** are printed in the original's crimes table (fines, days sealed in a cyst, forced work, dilution, Unlisting) and are unchanged.
- **Three ways out**, always: **pay** (a fine or a gift recorded on the Ledger), **swear** (an Oath before a witness to make it right), or **run** (and be remembered).

The written law is strict with the poor and quiet about the powerful. Let players notice.
