# The Ninth Shift · v3 table edition

*Levels 4 to 6. Ready to run in the v3 host.*

This edition is written to be run as a conversation. Players say what they do in their own words; nothing below is a list of permitted moves. All prices, statistics and procedures are in the original Vector and are unchanged.

## The pitch (read this to the players)

A factory district has stopped working. Officially nobody is on strike; officially it is "scheduled maintenance". You are four temps hired to get things moving, standing exactly between the bosses and the workers. It is a comedy: nobody here will attack you, everybody will hand you a form, and the funniest answer is usually the driest one. But one machine never stopped, and the argument everyone is having turns out to be about somebody's body.

## The Clocks (always on the table)

- **SHIFT** (6). The district is seizing up. One step each working day. At the end Shift Nine is broken up and sold. *Who moves it:* The Engine, one per Beat. Helping the Row also advances it.
- **BADGE** (5). How much the Cohort looks like the bosses' people. Taking the good money raises it; helping the Row lowers it. High Badge opens official doors and closes neighbourly ones. *Who moves it:* Printed triggers only. Players can lower it by giving a badge away in public.
- **PAPER** (4). The court's timetable. When it fills, the decision is issued and somebody's body becomes licensed property, unless the Cohort has put a better document in front of the Court. *Who moves it:* Printed triggers, and loud successes. Never a failed roll.

## The truth (Director only)

- The Hegemony patented a shape (the Nine Tune) that is really Overseer Kel-Vasht's own body trait. Kel-Vasht secretly bought it and carries the receipt.
- The Line was never told to stop, so it has kept printing for nine Beats: one small unfinished person, in pieces.
- Foundry Mother Hush is finishing that person in the alley behind organ 41. The court hearing is really about them.
- The Court has already written its decision and needs somebody to blame it on. The filing names the wrong building.
- Nobody is the villain. Whatever side the Cohort helps, the Vector tells them plainly what it cost.

## Secrets, and three ways to each

Give a secret away whenever a player looks, asks or pokes in a way that could find it. The leads are suggestions, not the only doors.

**The Line was never told to stop. In this district a thing keeps going until somebody signs.**
- The Filing Gate: The blank notice board, and the Overseer's note: "the Line has not been asked."
- The Print Floor of Shift Nine: The Line's own output shelf, free to read.
- The Picket Hearth: Any worker on the Row says it in one sentence.

**The stoppage is legal, and there is boring paperwork that proves it.**
- The Picket Hearth: The wage book with Steward Nine-Grudge's signature.
- The Filing Gate: Nine Beats of notices all saying "scheduled maintenance suspension".
- The Licence Court: Clerk Second-to-Last, asked properly, finds the authorisation and reads it aloud.

**The patented shape is a living body's own inherited trait, not a printed invention.**
- The Licence Court: The exhibit smells inherited, not printed, to anyone who checks.
- The Print Floor of Shift Nine: Kel-Vasht's long pause when the word "mine" is nearly said; the receipt it carries.
- The Licence Court: The door arch scans one of the Cohort and finds the same shape in them.

**The patent filing names the wrong building. It is a clerical error and nobody wants to be the one who made it.**
- The Licence Court: Small print on the exhibit document, in the wrong handwriting.
- The Licence Court: Auditor Eleven-Brief's signature mark reads "organ 118".
- The Licence Court: The Auditor will say it privately, once, to a Cohort that has not humiliated it.

**Something nearly finished is waiting in the alley behind organ 41, and the hearing is about it.**
- The Picket Hearth: A light in the alley that should not be on.
- The Print Floor of Shift Nine: The Line's output is nine Beats of the same thing, in pieces.
- The Licence Court: An application on the Court's schedule names a cradle.

**Waiting in a queue for a whole Beat lets the Cohort wind one Clock back a step.** *(optional)*
- The Filing Gate: The clerk says so in the first minute.
- The Picket Hearth: The Row treat queueing as a skill.

**A hired strikebreaking crew called the Relief arrives on the third Beat, on time, and invoices for everything it breaks.** *(optional)*
- The Filing Gate: Their arrival is on the notice board.
- The Print Floor of Shift Nine: A space has been cleared for them.

## The sites

### 1 · The Filing Gate — a queue, a form and a wage

**Open with:** You are at the front of a queue of forty-one and a kind clerk is already holding out a brass badge and a form with eleven boxes: "You will only need three of these to be untrue, and I am not allowed to say which."

**The question this scene answers:** Whose wage does the Cohort take, and what will they sign to get through the gate?

**What is here:** A hiring gate under an official arch. A kind clerk, a rack of nine hundred warm brass badges, an insurance desk, a blank notice board and a queue forty-one long.

**Ways on:** The brine-walk to the Picket Hearth.

**Senses:** brine and warm brass; forty people breathing patiently; the scratch of the clerk's stylus; a notice board with nine days of nothing on it; tea.

**People**

- **Clerk Second-to-Last.** Wants everyone through the gate with the right form. Fears being asked to swear to anything. *Voice:* Gentle, apologetic, makes excellent tea. Answers every question truthfully as far as it knows. *Offers:* Explains any box on the form. Will sign the wrong thing if asked nicely. *Never:* Will not swear an oath.
- **whoever is next in the queue.** Wants to keep their place. Fears losing it. *Voice:* Chatty, resigned. *Offers:* Gossip for company.

**Things players often try** (anything else: rule it the same way)

- *Read the form box by box.* No roll. Read each box aloud and answer questions honestly. Ticking a box is a real choice.
- *Wait in the queue.* No roll. It costs a Beat and winds one Clock back a step. Say so.
- *Check the clerk's sums.* RTC, ordinary. Success finds the error, and the reward printed for it.
- *Take down the notice board, the ledger or the dead queuer's licence.* No roll to take. It is a public act: say who is watching.

**When a roll fails, pick a cost:** a Beat; a box ticked that will matter later; Badge going up; the queue's good opinion.

**Dangers: show the tell before the strike**

- **the form.** Tell: the clerk winces at three of the boxes. Strike: a ticked box is a legal fact.

**If the table stalls:** The clerk refills your tea and taps box four. / The queue shuffles forward and somebody coughs politely. / A notice goes up: the Relief arrives on the third Beat.

**A moment for each character:** *The Wall*: The badge rack is heavy and somebody has to hold it while the clerk looks. *The Ledger*: You spot the arithmetic mistake. *The Rider*: You could be through the arch before anyone reads you. Do you? *The Eyes*: One name in the ledger is crossed out in three different hands.

**Printed in the original (unchanged):** Vector §7.1. Danger: E1: 2 Print-Crew Constructs + 2 Antigen Gnats = 6 BSP, Easy. It ends in one sentence if anybody presents a Wage properly. Nobody here is evil; the clerk is trying to help. If it goes loud the punishment is PAPER +1, not death. Pays: 15 Codon for arriving with a Wage chosen, the Form filled and said out loud; 10 more if they catch the clerk's arithmetic mistake. 30 S in unclaimed wage float if they find the Mutuality's error. The dead queue-member's unclaimed licence is worth 100 S and a conversation.

### 2 · The Picket Hearth — the workers' row, silent

**Open with:** Somebody puts a hot bowl in your hands and asks, loudly and politely, in front of four hundred silent machines: "And whose wage are you on?"

**The question this scene answers:** Can the Cohort get anything here, and are they willing to be seen getting it?

**What is here:** Four hundred living machines stopped mid-task, and a hearth with soup that is not good and is enough. An alley behind organ 41 has a light on.

**Ways on:** Service alley to the Print Floor (a Warden arch: Badge 2+ walks through; Badge 0-1 is challenged and walked through under Warden escort, which is a scene, never a locked door); Back to the Filing Gate; The alley behind organ 41.

**Senses:** soup steam; the silence of four hundred stopped machines; a half-folded shape still held in a still hand; everyone looking without staring; a light where there should be none.

**People**

- **Steward Nine-Grudge.** Wants the Row fed and nobody arrested. Fears being blamed for a strike it did not start. *Voice:* Worn out, exact about names, dry. *Offers:* Food, hiding and a lie on your behalf, once each, for those the Row trusts. *Never:* Will never order anyone to attack.

**Things players often try** (anything else: rule it the same way)

- *Answer honestly about the wage.* No roll. The Row respects it. Their prices follow the Badge.
- *Eat at the hearth.* No roll. It builds Cohesion. Even at high Badge the soup is given, in silence.
- *Buy repairs or supplies.* By Badge: friendly at low, doubled or refused at high. No roll changes that; giving a badge away does.
- *Look into organ 118 or the alley light.* OSM, ordinary, or just walk over.

**When a roll fails, pick a cost:** a service refused; Badge; a Beat; a promise to carry a grievance.

**Dangers: show the tell before the strike**

- **the Silence.** Tell: tools that will not start and wounds that will not close here. Strike: nothing can be repaired, printed or mended on the Row.
- **being unwelcome.** Tell: conversations stop when you arrive. Strike: nobody sells, mends or vouches for you.

**If the table stalls:** The Steward asks the question again, for the people at the back. / A child-sized machine tugs your sleeve and points at the alley. / Two tame scavengers start arguing over a dead machine.

**A moment for each character:** *The Wall*: Carry the soup pot. The Row notices who carries. *The Ledger*: You know what the shape in the stopped machine's hand was going to be. *The Rider*: You could reach the alley unseen. *The Eyes*: You see the light in the alley first.

**Printed in the original (unchanged):** Vector §7.2, §10.8. Danger: E2: THE SILENCE (hazard, 2 BSP) + 1 Gnaw Swarm + 2 Detritus Feeders = 5 BSP, Easy. The Feeders want the corpses, not you. If the Cohort denies them corpses they become a nuisance; if the Cohort feeds them, the Row adopts them. Pays: A Quiet Apron from the scrap bins (free); a Pristine Sequence in organ 118's claim cage (200 S, assessment-bound); the fold a silent organ was holding when it stopped (unlisted, worth a conversation with Kel-Vasht). E2 pays 4.5 Codon.

### 3 · The Print Floor of Shift Nine — the problem room, still running

**Open with:** The Line is running. Nobody is running it. On the far table its output is stacked, folded and very nearly finished, and an attendant with a towel asks whether you have an appointment.

**The question this scene answers:** Does the Cohort stop the machine, feed it, or ask it what it is making?

**What is here:** The Line, running, with nobody running it; the output stacked on the far table, folded and nearly finished; and the Fold Parlour, which is in every respect a spa.

**Ways on:** The pump-lift to the Licence Court; Back by the service alley to the Picket Hearth.

**Senses:** a steady working rhythm in the floor; warm towels; the smell of fresh print; a ticket machine for proteins; one very large person standing very still.

**People**

- **Overseer Kel-Vasht.** Wants the patent to die without having to say why. Fears the receipt it carries being read aloud. *Voice:* Immense, formal, long pauses. Cannot quite say the word "mine". *Offers:* Will back the Cohort at the hearing if they testify. *Never:* Not a fight. It will out-argue anyone and should.
- **the parlour attendant.** Wants the appointment book respected. Fears a queue-jumper. *Voice:* Soothing, relentless, offers towels. *Offers:* Folds things properly, in order.
- **the Line.** Wants to finish its specification. Fears nothing. *Voice:* Does not speak. Answers by what it prints next. *Offers:* Shows its output to anyone who asks. *Never:* Will not stop unless somebody signs.

**Things players often try** (anything else: rule it the same way)

- *Ask the Line what it is making.* No roll. Roll its printed output table and describe it.
- *Clear the jam at organ 118.* RTC with Synthesis, hard. Three printed observations make it easier. Fixing it moves two Clocks: say so first.
- *Argue with Kel-Vasht.* AFF, hard. Losing is fine and still gets its offer.
- *Sign for the Line to stop.* No roll. It is a legal act with a name on it.

**When a roll fails, pick a cost:** a Clock; a place in the parlour queue; Kel-Vasht's patience; the output comes out wrong.

**Dangers: show the tell before the strike**

- **the Relief.** Tell: a space cleared, invoices already pinned up. Strike: they arrive on Beat three and break things by the item.

**If the table stalls:** The Line prints something small and sets it on the table. / The attendant calls a ticket number: it is yours. / Kel-Vasht says, "You are the temps," and waits.

**A moment for each character:** *The Wall*: Stand between the Relief and the Line. *The Ledger*: The jam is yours to read. *The Rider*: Fetch the thing off the moving Line before it is crushed. *The Eyes*: Notice every piece the Line prints fits the last.

**Printed in the original (unchanged):** Vector §7.3, §10.1-10.3. Danger: E3: THE LINE (hazard) + (at Level 4) 1 Print-Crew Construct + 1 Protease Locusts. Standard at Levels 1-4, Easy at 5-8. The Line is the third enemy and the easiest to use: its output table fires whenever anyone is loud near it. Pays: The jam fixed (three observations, Synthesis AT 16): 25 Codon, advances SHIFT and BADGE. The Line's shelf (d10): Specimens, a Pristine Sequence (200 S), forms, and one object that must be played as a person. Construct parts at 2x. E3 pays 17.5 Codon.

### 4 · The Licence Court — where the answer is already written

**Open with:** The arch reads each of you as you walk in and announces what it finds. On the bench three Auditors already have the decision drafted, and the middle one looks at you like someone hoping to be rescued.

**The question this scene answers:** Does the Cohort win by the Court's rules, and what do they become by doing it?

**What is here:** Three Auditors, a scanning arch at the door, a public gallery of forty with numbered tickets, a wall schedule with the decision date already on it, and the patented shape on display as evidence.

**Ways on:** Out across the Row to the alley behind organ 41; The pump-lift back to the Print Floor.

**Senses:** the arch's cold tingle; forty tickets rustling; tea again; a date on the wall in the wrong handwriting; a faint living smell from the exhibit.

**People**

- **Auditor Eleven-Brief.** Wants someone else to find the filing error. Fears being proved wrong in public. *Voice:* Precise, brittle, grateful to anyone who lets it save face. *Offers:* Will correct the record privately for a Cohort that has been courteous. *Never:* Will not admit a mistake aloud.
- **Clerk Second-to-Last.** Wants the paperwork to be true. Fears oaths. *Voice:* As at the gate. *Offers:* Finds and reads documents.

**Things players often try** (anything else: rule it the same way)

- *Argue the case.* The hearing rule as printed: three exchanges. Say what winning buys first.
- *Examine the exhibit.* OSM with Chemoreception, ordinary.
- *Help the Auditor privately.* AFF, ordinary, and only if they have not humiliated it.
- *Forge or fix the filing.* DFF with Concealment, hard. Say plainly it is a crime.

**When a roll fails, pick a cost:** PAPER moves; a witness is called: someone they were kind to; Badge; being named surety for something.

**Dangers: show the tell before the strike**

- **the arch.** Tell: it hums louder near licensed gear. Strike: it reads out what you carry and what you are.
- **the timetable.** Tell: the date on the wall. Strike: the decision issues whether you are ready or not.

**If the table stalls:** The middle Auditor asks if anyone has "noticed anything irregular". / The clerk sets a file within reach and looks away. / The gallery murmurs: ticket nine has been called.

**A moment for each character:** *The Wall*: The arch names your grafts. Stand there anyway. *The Ledger*: The clauses can be learned. You are the one who reads them. *The Rider*: Get the document to the bench before the gavel. *The Eyes*: Smell the exhibit: it was born, not printed.

**Printed in the original (unchanged):** Vector §7.4, §10.5-10.6. Danger: E4: BRACK NINE-BADGES (Elite) + 2 Print-Crew Constructs + 1 registered Acid-Resistant Tank = 10 BSP, Hard at Levels 1-4. They rout at 40% MI because their invoice has a cap of 400 S. Fighting them advances BADGE as much as anything, because the district pays the invoice. Never a new creature type: the same three constructs, better at finding you. Pays: The hearing won by document, not by force: 50 Codon, the patent's fate, the Clerk's Oath, and the counter-claim. E4 ended under the cap: 15 Codon and the invoice (the district pays); if it goes loud and ends in kills: 35 Codon, C8/G3/P2/T1 from Brack, and a Hegemony lien plus a patent claim on anything taken.

### 5 · The Alley behind Organ 41 — the yield, defended by nobody

**Open with:** The thing in the cradle is tidying up. It has swept the alley and folded the towels, and it looks at you and asks, politely, whether it is finished yet.

**The question this scene answers:** Is the thing in the cradle a what or a who, and who says so out loud?

**What is here:** A swept alley, a cradle, a stack of neatly folded towels, and the oldest machine in the district working very quietly.

**Ways on:** The drainage channel (free; the print follows if it wants to); Hush's route through organ 41 (120 S of a construct's parts, or one Pristine Sequence); The front door (Badge 4+, or a warrant).

**Senses:** clean wet stone; a small careful voice; a machine humming a tune; folded towels; four different offers pinned to the wall.

**People**

- **Foundry Mother Hush.** Wants to print something nobody has licensed. Fears being stopped one step from done. *Voice:* Warm, ancient, explains exactly why a thing is a bad idea and then offers to do it. *Offers:* Will finish the print if the Cohort stays and witnesses. *Never:* Do not fight Hush.
- **the Unlisted Print.** Wants to know if it is finished. Fears having made a mess. *Voice:* Sincere, curious, a little embarrassed. Never a joke. *Offers:* Comes with you if asked. It helps.

**Things players often try** (anything else: rule it the same way)

- *Talk to the print.* No roll. Play it straight.
- *Stay and help Hush finish.* No roll: it costs time, and whoever signs becomes responsible for a person.
- *Take it to the Court, or hide it.* A journey with a Clock cost, and a roll only if someone tries to stop them.
- *Sell or harvest it.* It is printed and it pays. Say the price and what it is, plainly, and let them choose.

**When a roll fails, pick a cost:** a Beat; a signature; Badge; who sees them leave.

**Dangers: show the tell before the strike**

- **the offers.** Tell: four factions have already pinned up prices. Strike: whoever the Cohort refuses will come and ask in person.

**If the table stalls:** The print asks again, more quietly. / Hush says, "It needs one more thing, and a witness." / Boots at the mouth of the alley.

**A moment for each character:** *The Wall*: Stand in the alley mouth. *The Ledger*: You can read what Hush still has to do. *The Rider*: Carry the cradle through the drain. *The Eyes*: Be the first to say "who", not "what".

**Printed in the original (unchanged):** Vector §7.5, §10.7, §10.9. Danger: No encounter is budgeted: the alley is a decision. If it goes loud: 1 Locust swarm + Hush's Labour Constructs (as Swarmlings x4) + the cradle (6-8 BSP). The print does not fight; Hush does not strike; her constructs move things, including the Cohort, gently, out of the alley. Pays: The alley seen, priced and decided about: 25 Codon. Hush, asked properly: 20 Codon, one printed item free, one re-fold free, and one thing you did not ask for. Endings pay 40-120 Codon. The print's printed Harvest line (C9 / G5 / P2 / T1, Codon 21) is the Hegemony's own price on paper; if the Cohort takes the money, they name the number aloud as they hand it over.

## v3 rulings (November 2026)

*Made while the AI Director was built and played. The original book carries each one as a note at the end of the section named; the AI Director's Engine follows them.*

- **BADGE in dice** (3.2 BADGE; 17 Nov 2026). BADGE's dice apply to any contest with a Hegemony or Court NPC: −1 at 0, +1 at 2, +2 at 3 and above (Badges 4 and 5 keep the +2 and add what their row gives).
- **Routes and days** (7 · THE FIVE SITES; 17 Nov 2026). A route priced at a Beat costs that day the moment the Cohort takes it (the brine-walk is free to a Cohort at BADGE 0, walking as Kin). If about ten exchanges at the table pass with no Beat, the day has run out: close it, and SHIFT moves.
- **The arch is never a locked door** (7 · 1 · THE FILING GATE; 17 Nov 2026). A Cohort at BADGE 0–1 is challenged at the Warden arch and walked through under escort, which is a scene, never a locked door. A listed exit is always open at a price.

## How it can end, and what comes next

- If the patent dies: Kel-Vasht resigns in public and owes the Cohort its name.
- If the print is finished: the Cohort is legally responsible for a new person.
- If the Row is sold: the Cohort is paid well and called by the wrong name.
- The best ending is one the players invent, such as registering the Line itself as a worker.
