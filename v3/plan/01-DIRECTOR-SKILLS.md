# 01 · What a human Director brings to the table

Research for Osmotic Expanse v3. The question: when a person runs a tabletop
roleplaying game well, what are they actually doing, and which of those skills
must the books, the Vectors and the Engine carry so an AI Director can do them
too?

## The short answer

A roleplaying game is a conversation. Players say what their characters do, in
their own words, and the Director answers with what the world does back. Rules
are called in only when the outcome is in doubt and something is at stake.
Buttons and menus are the opposite of this: they tell the player what is
possible. A Director listens for what the player wants and finds a way for the
world to answer it.

So v3 has one fixed rule: **the player types or says anything; the Director
answers.** Everything else in this document is in service of that.

## The skills

Each skill has three parts: what the human does, what goes wrong when an AI
tries it unaided (taken from our own bench logs where we have them), and what v3
gives the AI so it can do it.

### A. Running the conversation

| # | Skill | What a human does | What the AI needs from v3 |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Listen for intent** | Works out what the player is trying to achieve, not only the words. Asks one short question if it is unclear. | Instruction: restate intent in the fiction before resolving. Engine: the free-text "say" stays the main input. |
| 2 | **Say yes, or roll** | If nothing is at stake, it just happens. Dice only when failure would be interesting. | A printed *ruling ladder* in the Core book (below). The Engine never demands a roll the Director did not call. |
| 3 | **Adjudicate anything** | Maps an unexpected action to a rule on the spot: which stat, how hard, what it costs. | Ruling ladder + a difficulty table in plain words + a "closest printed rule" list per site. |
| 4 | **Fail forward** | A failed roll changes the situation. It never means "nothing happens, try again". | Every site prints *costs of failure* to choose from (time, noise, harm, position, a Clock). |
| 5 | **Telegraph, then follow through** | Shows danger coming (a soft move) before it lands (a hard move). Players can always see the bill before they pay it. | Each threat prints its *tell* and its *strike*. Briefing says which tells have been shown. |
| 6 | **End on a question** | Hands the scene back: "What do you do?" to one named player. | Kept from v1 (one question per reply, the Engine fixes the seat). |

### B. Keeping the game together

| # | Skill | What a human does | What the AI needs from v3 |
|---|---|---|---|
| 7 | **Prep situations, not plots** | Knows who wants what and what happens if nobody interferes. Has no fixed sequence of scenes. | Vectors are rewritten as situations: people, wants, pressures, and what happens if the Cohort does nothing. |
| 8 | **Secrets and clues** | Keeps a list of things worth learning, not tied to one place, and drops them wherever the players look. | Each Vector prints its *secrets*. The Engine tracks which are found; the briefing lists the ones still hidden. |
| 9 | **Three clues for every conclusion** | Anything the players must work out is reachable at least three ways. | Each must-know secret prints three leads in different sites. The check script fails a Vector with fewer. |
| 10 | **Leads when stuck** | Notices a stalled table and offers a way on: a noise, an arrival, a reminder of something unresolved. | Engine counts turns without progress; briefing then says "offer one of these leads". |
| 11 | **Clocks and fronts** | Tracks slow pressures openly so choices have weight and the world moves without the players. | Kept from v1 (CARCASS, TOLL, HUNGER) and made standard for every Vector: three Clocks, each with tells per segment. |
| 12 | **Continuity** | Remembers names, promises, wounds and what was said last week. | Kept from v1 (memory, chronicle). v3 adds NPC cards so a voice stays the same person. |

### C. Making it feel alive

| # | Skill | What a human does | What the AI needs from v3 |
|---|---|---|---|
| 13 | **Strong start** | Opens in motion: something is already happening and needs an answer. | Every Vector and every site prints a *strong start* line. |
| 14 | **Describe with the senses** | Two or three concrete details, one of them not visual. Short. | Each site prints five sense details in plain words to draw from. |
| 15 | **Play NPCs as people** | Each has a want, a fear, a way of speaking, and things they know. They act on their want. | NPC cards: want, fear, voice, knows, will trade, will not do. |
| 16 | **Pacing** | Cuts dull stretches, slows for tense ones, ends scenes when their question is answered. | Each site prints the *question the scene answers*. Briefing nudges to close once answered. |
| 17 | **Spotlight** | Makes sure every player gets turns that matter, and builds moments for each character. | Engine tracks who has not acted lately; each site prints one *moment* for each Hand. |
| 18 | **Be a fan of the characters** | Makes them look competent. Trouble comes from the world, not from making them clumsy. | Instruction, plus failure costs that are about the situation, never the character's foolishness. |

### D. Looking after the table

| # | Skill | What a human does | What the AI needs from v3 |
|---|---|---|---|
| 19 | **Teach as you go** | Explains one rule at the moment it matters, in plain words. Never front-loads. | Kept from v1 (lessons per site). v3 adds a plain-words glossary and a "Words" tab for players. |
| 20 | **Session zero and safety** | Agrees tone and limits first; honours a "stop" at once. | Kept from v1 (table agreement, safety). |
| 21 | **Fair and consistent** | Same ruling for the same situation; never fudges numbers. | Kept from v1: the Engine owns all numbers; the Director never invents one. |
| 22 | **Recap and hooks** | Opens with "last time"; closes with something to look forward to. | Kept from v1 (chronicle). Each Vector prints its closing hooks. |
| 23 | **Speak the players' language** | Uses the table's words. Introduces a new term with its meaning attached, once. | The glossary; rule: first use of a setting word in a session carries a plain gloss. |

## The ruling ladder (skill 2 and 3, made printable)

When a player says what they do, the Director walks down this ladder and stops
at the first rung that fits:

1. **Nothing at stake?** It happens. Describe it.
2. **Impossible here?** Say why in the fiction and offer what is possible.
3. **A printed rule covers it?** (a Strike, a harvest, a toll, a hazard) Use that rule.
4. **Otherwise it is a roll.** Pick the Baseline that matches *how* they do it,
   pick the difficulty from the plain-words table, say what failure will cost
   *before* the dice, then call the roll.
5. **After the roll:** success gives what they wanted. Failure gives the cost
   named, and the situation changes either way.

## What our own bench logs add

Twelve bench runs of the first Director showed where an AI Director drifts
without structure. Each drift maps to a skill above:

- Invented or leaked numbers → skill 21. The Engine must own every number.
- Asked the wrong player the closing question → skill 6 and 17.
- Narrated a death the Engine had not settled → skill 21.
- Spent pressure (TOLL) so the best scene was skipped → skill 16 and 11:
  Clocks need rules about *who* may move them.
- Repeated a hazard that had already fired → skill 12.
- Buttons-only play in v2 → skill 1. It removed the conversation, so it removed the game.
