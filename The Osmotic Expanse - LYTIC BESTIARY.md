## HOW TO READ A BLOCK

```
NAME                    Tier · Role · Order · Size class
MT __  MI __  SOAK __   PP __  Flow __  Frequency +__      attack pool: N dice (homeostatic 3N)
BD __  damage type      Thresholds it defends at: hazard save __
Traits (always true) · Special (spends PP, telegraphed) · Weakness (printed, findable)
HARVEST: C/G/P/T counts · Codon __ · BSP __
Tactics (how it actually plays the Cycle) · Why it exists (what it is *for* in this world)
```

**Conventions.**
* **MI already includes the creature's printed size class.** Do not apply Core size modifiers on top.
* **Reactive Osmosis** (Core IV: +4 to its defending Threshold, 1 Reserve PP) is available to every Standard, Elite, and Catastrophe, and to swarm *clusters* (not segments).
* **Damage** a creature deals = `Overflow + BD + Effect Value − target soak (min 1)`, exactly as for organisms.
* **Special actions cost PP**, and creatures have the same PP economy the players do. A creature that has spent 5 PP has no Reserve. *Say so out loud.*
* **Frequency** is a modifier to `d6 + DFF + discipline`; where a block prints `Freq +2`, use the creature's best relevant Baseline (usually DFF or VSC) for the check.

## SWARM SEGMENTS (recap, Core IV §4.13)

A swarmling is **one token with Segments**. A Strike's Overflow removes `⌈Overflow ÷ 3⌉ + 1` segments; a swarm can make **one Strike per two live segments** (max 3). Segments regenerate 1 per Cycle unless the terrain is Dry or the swarm is Brittle. When segments hit 0, the token dies. **Area effects and Bursts are the answer to swarms**; single-target Strikers are not.

## MODIFYING TEMPLATES

Apply to any block. Each is a *fictional* condition as much as a mechanical one.

| Mod | Change | Who uses it |
| :-- | :-- | :-- |
| **Veteran** | +1 to its attack pool and BD; ×2 Codon; +1 Harvest grade | old survivors, bounty-hunters |
| **Swarmed** | gains 4 Swarmlings of the same Order as Segments | blooms, nursery Deep |
| **Corroded** | soak −3, and at the start of each Cycle takes 3 Lytic damage; Harvest +1 grade (the organs are loosened) | the Wastes, failed harvests |
| **Hijacked** | its Special is replaced by a Pathogenic Wraith Special of the same tier; it can be *recovered* (Sequence Reading AT 20, then Mend) | Wraiths, the Underground's weapons |
| **Register-Forged** | Immune Sentinels read it as **self**: it walks checkpoints | Lysosan assets, Wraith infiltrations |
| **Tonic-Adapted** | immune to one tonic, −2 dice in the opposite one | deep, acidic, cold, or hypertonic locales |
| **Brood-Carrier** | at 50% MI, spawns 1d4 Swarmlings (one action, telegraphed) | Drifters, Swarms, the Abyss |
| **Caged / Wounded** | MI halved, Flow −2, no Reactive Osmosis | a heist, a rescue, a hunt on the Cohort's terms |
| **Elite** | promote to the Elite row and add one Special | captains, champions, named enemies |
| **Young** | MI × ⅔, BD −2, no Special; Harvest −1 grade | nurseries, and the moral problem of killing them |

## ABIOTIC ENTITIES

Hazards and processes are blocked like creatures, with three differences: they have **no Frequency** (they act on their printed trigger or at Cycle end), they cannot be targeted by anything requiring a *body* (Mark, Hijack, Engulfment), and **they harvest nothing** — but many can be *harvested from*: an AE Bloom leaves Pristine Sequences, a Thermal Vent leaves Thermal organs.

An abiotic block's **MI** is its *integrity*: how much damage it takes before the process stops. It is not alive, and it does not go Dormant: at 0 integrity it **fails**, usually loudly.

# ORDER I — LYTIC SWARMS

*Hunger with a body count. Everything in this Order dissolves before it eats, which means it damages armour, structures, and the ground it stands on. They are never evil; they are a digestion that acquired mobility.*

**Order behaviour.** Swarms approach by scent (Chemoreception AT 12 to detect them across a region), attack the *densest* thing in range, and never retreat unless their tonic changes. Every Swarm has **Segments** and cannot be Flank-Breached (there is no flank).

---

### GNAW SWARM
Swarmling · T1 · Mesoscopic cluster · **MT 12 · MI 10 · soak 0 · BD 1 Lytic · pool 3d · PP 2 · Segments 6**
**Traits:** Scent-Led (never Blind for more than one Cycle if it has smelled you) · Corrodes (any hit: target graft −1 soak until repaired)
**Special — Swarm Over (2 PP):** all live segments Strike one creature in Contact. Each Strike at −2 dice. Against a braced target this is a meat grinder; against a moving target it usually misses.
**Weakness:** area damage, Dry Zones, and any Thermal effect end Segments 1-for-1 with no Overflow needed.
**HARVEST:** C3 / G1 · **Codon 1.5 · BSP 1** (the C is worth 15 S; a Cohort that ignores these bodies is throwing money away)
**Tactics:** flows over everything, bites once per Cycle per two segments, and *cannot* be frightened.
**Why it exists:** it is the Expanse's scavenger. Wherever a Gnaw Swarm has been, the ground is bare of old biomass — which is how you find a carcass, and how you know something bigger killed it.

### DETRITUS FEEDER
Swarmling · T1 (T2 in the Wastes) · **MT 12 · MI 10 · soak 0 · BD 1 Lytic · pool 3d · PP 2 · Segments 6**
**Traits:** Acid-Immune (Lytic damage 0) · Harvest Instinct (it will spend its action on a corpse rather than on you — this is exploitable)
**Special — Render (2 PP):** on a corpse within Contact, it Harvests: the corpse's remaining Harvest value drops by one grade, and the Feeder *grows*: +2 Segments permanently.
**Weakness:** if you deny it corpses (burn them, salt them, take them) it becomes a nuisance instead of a threat.
**HARVEST:** C3 / G1 · **Codon 1.5 · BSP 1** (run it as Tier 2 in the Wastes and it yields C4 / G1, Codon 3.5 — which is why Reclaimer crews count the ground before they count the swarm)
**Tactics:** ignores the fight, eats the battlefield. In a long fight, a Feeder becomes four Feeders.
**Why it exists:** the Wastes' sanitation, and the reason the Reclaimers' Ash hire armed escorts to a harvest site.

### PROTEASE LOCUSTS
Swarmling · T2 · **MT 13 · MI 14 · soak 0 · BD 2 Lytic · pool 4d · PP 2 · Segments 8**
**Traits:** Cloud Flight (Flow 5; ignores ground terrain) · Enzyme Rain (when a Strike hits, splashes 1 Lytic to everything in the band)
**Special — Bloom Chorus (3 PP, once per Pulse War):** all Locust swarms in the region (Director's count, usually 2–5 tokens) Strike the same target at once, adding +1 Overflow each.
**Weakness:** Turbid or Dry terrain grounds them; Hypertonic air costs them 1 PP per Cycle.
**HARVEST:** C4 / G1 · **Codon 3.5 · BSP 1**
**Tactics:** arrives as a *sound*, strips a site's organic structure in four Cycles, and leaves before the Wardens do.
**Why it exists:** the Great Flows' population boom, and the reason a colony's cable-network is insured.

### CYTOPLASMIC BLEEDER
Standard · T1 · Microscopic-Mesoscopic · **MT 13 · MI 22 · soak 1 · BD 2 Lytic · pool 4d · PP 3 · Flow 4**
**Traits:** Attach (a hit gives it 1 **Barb**: it rides on the target, and while Barbed it Strikes for free at −1 die each Cycle, taking Reactive Osmosis damage from the host's own efforts)
**Special — Tap (2 PP):** drains 3 MI from the host and heals itself for that amount. Costs 1 ATP the first time (it has no ATP pool; track this as a 3-use limit per fight).
**Weakness:** Detach is one OSM contest (AT 14) or a Thermal/Lytic burn; Phagocyte Engulfment is fatal.
**HARVEST:** C4 / G1 / P1 · **Codon 3 · BSP 2** (its Greater Organ is the *Tap Valve*: Lytic family, +2 soak, and a Minor Cost of hunger)
**Tactics:** finds the slowest organism, attaches, and lets its allies do the killing.
**Why it exists:** mid-strata pest. Colonies pay 15 S a head, which is why Gnats are always hired over PCs.

### ENZYMATIC HORROR
Standard · T2 · Macroscopic · **MT 14 · MI 30 · soak 2 · BD 3 Lytic · pool 5d · PP 3 · Flow 4**
**Traits:** Acidic Surge (Core VII: once per Cycle, everything in Contact takes 5 Lytic) · Corrodes · Testate-Soft (Lytic weapons gain +2 against armour-wearers nearby)
**Special — Dissolve Armour (3 PP):** one target in Contact: its grafts lose **2 soak permanently** (reparable by a Smith or a Nucleoid rite) and it gains **Brittle**.
**Weakness:** Mucoid-family grafts are immune to its Corrodes; Diver Gradient or a Desiccation zone halves its Surge.
**HARVEST:** C5 / G2 / P1 · **Codon 7 · BSP 2** (the Pristine Sequence is *Acid Gland*: the raw material of Acidic Corrosor kit)
**Tactics:** walks at the armour. It will pick the most armoured opponent, every time.
**Why it exists:** an apex grazer in enzyme-rich regions. Where Horrors live, nothing wears Testate.

### AUTOLYTIC HULK
Standard · T3 · Macroscopic · **MT 15 · MI 38 · soak 3 · BD 4 Lytic · pool 6d · PP 3 · Flow 3**
**Traits:** Autophagic (when it takes 8+ damage, its next Strike gains +2 BD — it converts damage to appetite) · Segment Split (at 0 MI it does not die: it splits into 2 Swarmlings at full Segments, and the fight continues)
**Special — Rupture Burst (4 PP, once):** it detonates: `8 + Overflow` Lytic to everything in its band, then becomes a Swarmling with 6 Segments.
**Weakness:** killing it *far* from anyone; Desiccation (no fluid to rupture with); freezing.
**HARVEST:** C6 / G3 / P2 · **Codon 15 · BSP 2**
**Tactics:** it wants to be hit while you are close. Deny it that and it is just slow.
**Why it exists:** the Wastes' defence mechanism — a body that weaponises its own death. The Ash consider eating one a form of suicide.

### SWARM OF NINE MOUTHS
Elite · T2 · Macroscopic colony · **MT 15 · MI 60 · soak 4 · BD 4 Lytic · pool 6d · PP 4 · Flow 5 · Segments 12 (as a single organism)**
**Traits:** Nine Digestions (each Cycle it chooses one damage type — Lytic, Thermal, Osmotic — and its attacks become that type; it reads what the Cohort is armoured against first) · Cannot be Flank-Breached
**Special — Devour (5 PP, once per Pulse War):** a Dormant or grappled creature is consumed: it goes to Total Lysis, and the Swarm gains +12 MI and one of the victim's **Harvest traits** for the rest of the fight.
**Weakness:** its Segment count is its action economy: area effects do not just damage it, they *silence* its specials.
**HARVEST:** C8 / G3 / P2 / T1 · **Codon 14 · BSP 4**
**Tactics:** Cycle 1 reads your armour, Cycle 2 picks the type you cannot soak, Cycle 3 eats someone.
**Why it exists:** a named horror, a colony-killer, and a Tier-2 Catastrophe's bodyguard. The Codex's *Bloom of Ash* ends with one.

### LYSOPHAGE
Elite · T3 · Macroscopic · **MT 16 · MI 76 · soak 5 · BD 5 Lytic · pool 7d · PP 4 · Flow 4**
**Traits:** Enzyme Corridor (the fluid in a line 4 Drifts long becomes Acidic for its allies and costs the Cohort 3 Lytic/Cycle) · Graft-Eater (a hit removes 1 point of a target's graft soak *permanently until repaired*)
**Special — Digest Apparatus (3 PP):** one target's Apparatus in Contact is dissolved (durability roll 2d6, ≤7 destroys). It gains +1 BD for the rest of the fight.
**Weakness:** fight it without Apparatus (Lash-and-soak, Bursts, Desiccation); Smith Overcharge on *its* own dissolved gear.
**HARVEST:** C10 / G4 / P3 / T1 / T1 · **Codon 30 · BSP 4** (the Terminal Codon is why a Hegemony foundry will pay 600 S for this kill)
**Tactics:** disarms, then eats. A Lysophage fight that lasts six Cycles has left the Cohort unarmed.
**Why it exists:** the Wastes' top predator, and the reason Reclaimers travel in crews of eight or not at all.

### RECESSION FEEDER
Standard · T4 · Vast · **MT 16 · MI 46 · soak 4 · BD 5 Lytic · pool 7d · PP 3 · Flow 3**
**Traits:** Anti-Bloom (all regeneration, Segment regrowth, and healing within 6 Drifts of it **stops**) · Starving (it is always at 1 ATP; it will abandon any objective to eat)
**Special — Draw In (4 PP):** everything in a 5-Drift zone is pulled 1 band toward it and takes 6 Osmotic; it then heals 1 MI per creature affected.
**Weakness:** it is losing. A Cohort that feeds it (a corpse, a sacrificial animal, a colony's store) can *lead* a Recession Feeder somewhere useful.
**HARVEST:** C7 / G4 / P2 · **Codon 30 · BSP 2**
**Tactics:** walks into a bloom and ends it, then turns on whoever is standing nearest.
**Why it exists:** the world's own immune answer to overgrowth. Both the Deep Choir and the Flow Guilds try to *herd* them, which is how colonies get destroyed.

### BLOOM MATRIARCH
Catastrophe · T2 · Vast · **PHASE 1 MT 12 · MI 60 · soak 5 · BD 6 Lytic · pool 6d · PP 5 · Flow 2 (rooted)**
**PHASE 2 (the brood frees: MI 60, Flow 5, +4 Segments, gains Swarm Over) · PHASE 3 (the Matriarch herself: MI 30, BD 8, no specials, furious)**
**Traits:** Brood Sac (at the end of each Cycle, spawns 1 Swarmling T2 with half Segments; the sac must be *cut* — a Strike at AT 16, 20 S of acid, or a Breacher's charge to stop the spawning) · Rooted (it cannot be pushed; its Folds anchor it to the ground)
**Special — Bloom Surge (5 PP, once per phase):** all its Swarmlings act immediately, and the ground in a 6-Drift zone becomes a spore bed (4 Lytic/Cycle).
**Special — Chemical Veil (3 PP):** for 2 Cycles, Lytic damage to the Matriarch is halved; the veil can be stripped by any Dry effect, Thermal burn, or a Smith's Tune (AT 16).
**Weakness:** its brood is its clock: kill the sac, not the swarm, and it loses a phase. It *cannot* leave — a Cohort that breaks the ground under it (pump, flood, collapse) can drown the whole fight.
**HARVEST:** C12 / G7 / P3 / T1 / T1 · **Codon 21 · BSP 10** — and the **Serum Signature**: a Pristine Sequence that predates the Closing. This is Act I's smoking gun; do not let it be sold casually.
**Tactics:** arrives, roots, and *grows the fight*. Every Cycle it is unaddressed, the encounter's BSP increases by 2.
**Why it exists:** she should not be possible. Nothing new can be born since the Closing — which is the whole mystery of Act I.

# ORDER II — PROTEIN SENTINELS

*Architecture that defends itself. Sentinels are not animals so much as **organs of a structure**: a ribbon, a shrine, a foundry, a gate. They hold ground, they build ground, and they punish entering. Most will not chase you more than a few Drifts from what they guard — which is the only reason anyone survives meeting one.*

**Order behaviour.** Sentinels brace, mark zones, and prefer Near/Mid bands. They have the highest soak in the Bestiary and the lowest Flow. **They will negotiate** if what they guard is not threatened: a Sentinel is a *function*, and functions can be reasoned with about their function.

---

### CHAPERONE DRONE
Swarmling · T1 · **MT 12 · MI 10 · soak 0 · BD 1 Kinetic · pool 3d · PP 2 · Segments 4 · Flow 3**
**Traits:** Folding (it can *fold* a corpse, a door, or a piece of terrain into a barrier: integrity 12, blocks a line) · Loyal to Structure (it will repair a damaged Sentinel or Fold before attacking)
**Special — Assist Fold (2 PP):** gives an adjacent Sentinel +2 soak until the end of the Cycle.
**Weakness:** ignore the drones and cut what they are repairing. They will spend the whole fight on maintenance.
**HARVEST:** C3 / G1 · **Codon 1.5 · BSP 1** (folded chaperone protein is the cheapest Fibrous graft material there is: 20 S at market)
**Tactics:** builds walls, fixes friends, bites occasionally.
**Why it exists:** the ribbons' maintenance caste. Where there are drones, a bridge is being built — follow them and you find somewhere new.

### FOLD SENTINEL
Standard · T1 · Macroscopic · **MT 13 · MI 22 · soak 1 (+2 while braced) · BD 2 Kinetic · pool 4d · PP 3 · Flow 3**
**Traits:** Brace (Core IV: it may not Drift; all attacks against it use MT +2) · Load-Bearing (if it takes 10+ damage from one hit it does not stagger: it *anchors*, and cannot be pushed for 2 Cycles)
**Special — Present (3 PP):** it declares a zone 3 Drifts wide **Held** for 3 Cycles: anything crossing it is Marked and costs +1 PP per Drift.
**Weakness:** get behind it (Flank Breach: −2 MT) — a braced Sentinel cannot turn, and a Navigator's shear or a Rider's Slip is the answer.
**HARVEST:** C4 / G1 / P1 · **Codon 3 · BSP 2** (the Greater Organ is a *Brace Node*: +2 soak, −1 Flow, Fibrous)
**Tactics:** plants itself at the chokepoint and dares you. It is not stupid; it is *doing its job*.
**Why it exists:** gates, vaults, and the edges of a colony's membrane. A Fold Sentinel is a lock you can talk to.

### RIBBON WARDEN
Standard · T2 · Macroscopic · **MT 14 · MI 30 · soak 2 · BD 3 Kinetic · pool 5d · PP 3 · Flow 5 (on a ribbon only)**
**Traits:** Bridge-Bound (on a ribbon it has Flow 6 and cannot be pushed; off one, Flow 2 and −2 to all pools) · Tether (a hit tethers the target to the bridge: DFF −2 until it breaks the tether, AT 15)
**Special — Shear Call (3 PP):** the ribbon vibrates: everything on it makes a DFF contest at AT 14 or is Jostled and pushed off into the drop.
**Weakness:** fight it off-ribbon, cut the ribbon under it (integrity 30), or hand it a *legal* reason to leave (Bridge law is real: an Oath invoked on a bridge must be answered).
**HARVEST:** C5 / G2 / P1 · **Codon 7 · BSP 2**
**Tactics:** herds you along the bridge into a knot where its allies are.
**Why it exists:** the Hegemony's postal system is protected by things that *are* the postal system.

### LATTICE GUARDIAN
Elite · T2 · Macroscopic · **MT 15 · MI 60 · soak 4 (+2 in its own zone) · BD 4 Kinetic · pool 6d · PP 4 · Flow 3**
**Traits:** Zone Claim (it declares a 4-Drift lattice on entering: it gains +2 soak, +2 MT, and any Cohesion spending inside the lattice costs double) · Unmoving Order (it never Drifts; it *extends*)
**Special — Cage Fold (4 PP):** one creature in Near is **Sequestered** in a protein cage (Core IV Sequester; escape AT 16 or the cage's integrity 40 destroyed).
**Special — Chain of Command (1 PP):** all Sentinels in its lattice gain +1 Overflow on their next hit.
**Weakness:** drag the fight out of the lattice (push, shear, a Rider carrying the Cohort out); Desiccation cracks it (soak −2, and the lattice collapses at integrity 20).
**HARVEST:** C8 / G3 / P2 / T1 · **Codon 14 · BSP 4** (Pristine *Lattice Rule*: needed for Protein Weaver Cathedral Folds and for a Smith's Masterwork bracing)
**Tactics:** arrives, builds, waits. The encounter begins when the Cohort decides whether to fight it on its terms.
**Why it exists:** a Sentinel that has *grown a jurisdiction*. Colonies pay lattice-guardians to hold their borders, and the paper trail is endless.

### TENSILE JAILER
Standard · T3 · Macroscopic · **MT 15 · MI 38 · soak 3 · BD 4 Kinetic · pool 6d · PP 3 · Flow 4**
**Traits:** Restraint (its hits apply **Anchor**: target cannot Drift; escape VSC contest AT 15) · Wall-Build (can raise a sheet wall, integrity 30, in one action — this is how it splits a Cohort apart)
**Special — Deliver (3 PP):** a held creature is *thrown*: 2 bands, 8 Kinetic, and it lands where the Jailer chooses (often into a hazard, a vat, or the outside of a membrane).
**Weakness:** split it from its walls (area effects, a Brute's push); any Lytic damage to its sheets drops their integrity to 0.
**HARVEST:** C6 / G3 / P2 · **Codon 15 · BSP 2**
**Tactics:** takes out the Cohort's *formation*, not its MI: separates, walls, delivers one person to the authority it serves.
**Why it exists:** arrest. Jailer-cells are how the Warden Circles and the Hegemony take prisoners without killing them — the only Order in this book that regularly does that.

### KNOT MARSHAL
Elite · T3 · Macroscopic · **MT 16 · MI 76 · soak 5 · BD 5 Kinetic · pool 7d · PP 4 · Flow 4**
**Traits:** Traffic Law (inside a junction it controls, it decides band order: one ally per Cycle may be *moved* 1 band by the Marshal — including forcibly away) · Register Eye (it sees Marks, forgeries, and Unwalled disguises: +2 to any contest to identify a fake)
**Special — Queue (5 PP, once):** all hostiles in a 5-Drift zone are Held (AT 16 to break) and the Marshal's side acts one extra full Cycle.
**Special — File (2 PP):** it records the Cohort chemically: a Warden or Hegemony response Clock advances by 1 **even if the fight ends in the Cohort's favour**.
**Weakness:** kill it before it Files (Silent strikes, Concealment first, or simply winning in three Cycles); its junction can be destroyed (structure integrity 60).
**HARVEST:** C10 / G4 / P3 / T1 / T1 · **Codon 30 · BSP 4**
**Tactics:** it does not need to win. It needs to *observe and report*, and then the state arrives.
**Why it exists:** a bureaucrat with a fortress body. Killing one is an act of political violence whether you meant it or not.

### CATHEDRAL WARDEN
Catastrophe · T3 · Vast · **PHASE 1 (the shell) MT 13 · MI 80 · soak 6 · BD 7 Kinetic · pool 7d · PP 5 · Flow 2**
**PHASE 2 (the folded interior: MI 60, soak 4, gains two Folds per Cycle) · PHASE 3 (the core, a *living person* the shrine was built around: MI 50, no armour, Begs)**
**Traits:** Living Structure (its Folds are walls with integrity 60 each; it can raise two per Cycle) · Sacred Ground (inside its shrine, all hostiles' Bursts cost +1 ATP and Oath boxes cannot be spent)
**Special — Consecrate (5 PP):** a 8-Drift zone becomes **Held**: viscosity 3, all Sentinel allies gain +2 soak, and the Cohort pays +1 PP per Drift.
**Weakness:** Phase 3 is the answer: the Cathedral was built *around* someone. Stop, talk, or extract them, and the whole structure stops functioning — its walls go slack and its allies leave.
**HARVEST:** C15 / G9 / P4 / T2 / T2 · **Codon 45 · BSP 10** — the Cathedral Fold specimen is the goal of every Protein Weaver at Stage IV, and the Ash will *object* to harvesting the core.
**Tactics:** fortifies, and expects you to come to it. Give it four Cycles and the encounter becomes a siege.
**Why it exists:** a shrine that grew a body. The Deep Choir pilgrims to these; the Hegemony wants to licence them; both are right.

### PRION HUSK
Elite · T4 · Macroscopic · **MT 17 · MI 92 · soak 6 · BD 6 Kinetic · pool 8d · PP 4 · Flow 4**
**Traits:** Misfold (a hit that deals 10+ damage **spreads**: one nearby ally of the *Cohort* — chosen by the Director, not the player — must make an OSM contest at AT 18 or gain **Brittle**) · Wrongness Immunity (Genetic effects, Mark, and Hijack fail: there is nothing there to rewrite)
**Special — Template (4 PP):** it touches a structure or corpse and it *refolds*: integrity halved, and a Swarmling of the Order's choice emerges.
**Weakness:** it cannot regenerate and cannot be reasoned with; Desiccation and Thermal (denaturing) are the only defences that scale. Fire works.
**HARVEST:** C12 / G5 / P4 / T2 · **Codon 60 · BSP 4** (harvesting a Husk is how a Hegemony foundry gets destroyed: the specimen refolds the print line)
**Tactics:** walks into a site and unmaking it. It is not a predator; it is an infection of *form*.
**Why it exists:** a Sentinel that outlived the structure it guarded and kept guarding *everything*. Act II's Foundry Belt stop-terror.

### AMYLOID BULWARK

### BRIDGE BUILDER

# ORDER III — NUTRIENT DRIFTERS

*The world's appetite, migrated. Drifters are the Expanse's biomass: enormous, slow, edible, and legally protected in some regions and infinitely farmable in others. Almost nothing in this Order wants to fight you. Almost all of it will, if it is frightened, hungry, or has something you are trying to take.*

**Order behaviour.** Filter, feed, move with the current, flee at a threshold, and **carry brood**. Their harvest value is the highest per BSP in this book, which is why every colony's economy is a negotiation about who is allowed to kill them.

---

### PLANKTON CLOUD

**HARVEST:** C3 / G1 / — · **Codon 1.5 · BSP 1** (20 S of raw nutrient paste, and the base material of half the Vial catalogue)

**Why it exists:** the bottom of every food chain, and the reason a Bloom event is measured in hours rather than seasons.

### YOLK BLOOM

**Tactics:** walks into a battlefield and eats the terrain. Then uses it.

### TIDAL STRAINER

**Tactics:** eats the scene, then negotiates with what is left.
**Why it exists:** the Abyss' magpie, and one of the few entities in this book you can reasonably *bribe*. Three campaign Vectors begin with something it has taken.

Catastrophe · T4 · Vast (the largest living thing in the Expanse) · **PHASE 1 (the body) MT 14 · MI 90 · soak 7 · BD 8 Osmotic · pool 8d · PP 5 · Flow 4**

**Why it exists:** the Expanse's weather with a will, and the largest moral problem a Cohort can bring home.

# ORDER IV — PATHOGENIC WRAITHS

*Information that wants a body. Everything in this Order fights with your own apparatus: your Reserves, your Marks, your features, your face, your allies. A Wraith fight is rarely decided by damage; it is decided by who still controls their own special actions at Cycle four.*

**Order behaviour.** They prefer hosts to fights. They flee a fight they can infiltrate instead, and they **return later wearing what they learned**. A Cohort that kills a Wraith and leaves the body has not ended anything: the sequence is airborne by then. (Say this. Players need to know the rules of this Order to stop panicking about it.)

---

### HIJACK MITE
Swarmling · T1 · Microscopic · **MT 12 · MI 10 · soak 0 · BD 1 Genetic · pool 3d · PP 2 · Segments 4 · Flow 5**
**Traits:** Attach (a hit installs 1 **Lattice**: at the start of the host's next turn, the Mite spends the host's **Reserve PP** if it holds any, and the Mite heals 4 MI) · Small (MT +2 while unattached)
**Weakness:** Antigen Discipline, Immune Sentinel presence, any Dry Zone, and *burning the host's coat* (a Thermal effect on the host kills attached Mites for 2 Lytic each).
**HARVEST:** C3 / G1 · **Codon 1.5 · BSP 1** (the sequence is worth more than the body: an Underground forger pays 60 S for an intact Mite lattice)
**Tactics:** attaches to whoever uses a Reactive Osmosis, because that is where the Reserve is.
**Why it exists:** the Wastes' most common infection, and the reason Wardens demand a sample at every gate.

### LATTICE LEECH
Swarmling · T2 · **MT 13 · MI 14 · soak 0 · BD 2 Genetic · pool 4d · PP 2 · Segments 6 · Flow 4**
**Traits:** Signal Thief (a hit steals one **class resource point**: an Etch level, a Broadcast Charge, a Turgor charge, a Lock, a Fold Token — the target loses it, the Leech stores it, and can spend it as its own Special) · Detachable (it does not need to survive to have succeeded)
**Special — Spend (2 PP):** uses a stolen resource at +1 Effect Value against its holder.
**Weakness:** resource-light Phenotypes (a Guard with no track to steal is a bad target); Desiccation; anything that makes it spill (Kinetic push) drops stored points.
**HARVEST:** C4 / G1 · **Codon 3.5 · BSP 1**
**Tactics:** picks the specialist, not the warrior.
**Why it exists:** the explanation for why a Cohort's plan stops working halfway through a campaign, and the reason the Sequence Underground keeps them as *weapons* rather than pests.

### RECEPTOR LURKER
Standard · T1 · Mesoscopic · **MT 13 · MI 22 · soak 1 · BD 2 Genetic · pool 4d · PP 3 · Flow 4**
**Traits:** Read (free action: it knows one Burst the target knows, one Vial they carry, and one condition they can shed — it uses this to *announce* its plan, which is genuinely unsettling) · Camouflage (reads as a neutral organism to Chemoreception AT 14)
**Special — Preempt (3 PP):** it **Locks** one of the target's printed features for 2 Cycles (save: OSM at AT 14).
**Weakness:** it must be *near* to read; blind it with Turbid water or a Scent Dampener and it becomes a mediocre brawler.
**HARVEST:** C4 / G1 / P1 · **Codon 3 · BSP 2** (receptor tissue: the base of Antigen Hood and of every Immune Sentinel graft)
**Tactics:** Cycle 1 reads, Cycle 2 announces, Cycle 3 cancels your best thing.
**Why it exists:** the reason high-level organisms travel with an Antigen Detector, and the reason Detectors get murdered first.

### SIGNAL WRAITH

**Traits:** Voice-Theft (it can imitate any Signal action it has heard — including a Pheromological Messenger's Broadcast Charges, a Commander's Cascade call, or an ally's Frequency order) · False Flag (its attacks carry the *victim's* chemical signature: Wardens and Sentinels will hunt the impersonated organism, not the Wraith)

**Special — Wear the Dead (4 PP, once):** takes a corpse's chemistry: it can pass as a specific dead organism for a Tide, including its Oath standing.

**Why it exists:** the most efficient political weapon in the Expanse, and the reason every treaty requires a chemical witness.

### FALSE SELF
Elite · T2 · size of whoever it is wearing · **MT 15 · MI 60 · soak 4 · BD 4 Genetic · pool 6d · PP 4 · Flow 5**

### MIMICRY BLOOM

### THE ALMS

**Why it exists:** a religion of one organism, spreading. The Deep Choir considers it either a saint or a disease, and both positions are held sincerely.

### PRION SHEPHERD
Elite · T4 · Macroscopic · **MT 17 · MI 92 · soak 6 · BD 6 Genetic · pool 8d · PP 4 · Flow 4**

**Why it exists:** what a Wraith becomes when it stops hiding and starts administering — and the clearest argument the Expanse has for why the Sacred Code exists at all.

# ORDER V — OSMOTIC BRUTES

*Pressure with an opinion. The lowest Thresholds, the highest MI, and the only Order that regularly wins a fight without dealing meaningful damage — because it wins by changing where the Cohort is. Every Brute fight is a terrain fight. If the Cohort is braced, anchored, and off a hazard line, half of this Order stops being dangerous.*

**Order behaviour.** They charge, push, throw, and slam things into other things. They are impatient (they act early on the Frequency, and this is the Order that most often wins the Detection Phase). They respect weight and law: a Brute will often *accept* a challenge, a wager, or a formal Oath-duel instead of a fight.

---

### CRYSTAL GROWTH
Swarmling · T2 · Mesoscopic cluster · **MT 13 · MI 14 · soak 0 · BD 2 Osmotic · pool 4d · PP 2 · Segments 6 · Flow 1**

# ORDER VI — IMMUNE SENTINELS

*The world's bureaucracy of violence. Everything in this Order **recognises**: it Marks, registers, cascades, and reports. Sentinels are the only Order that will arrest you, the only one that will stop fighting once you present valid chemistry, and the only one whose tactics get *better* the longer a fight lasts — because they are learning your antigen the whole time.*

**Order behaviour.** They issue challenges (a Challenge is a free action: state the register reason; a Cohort that answers it chemically can avoid the fight entirely). They never attack a **registered citizen** unprovoked in a registered ground. They fight in coordinated groups, and a Sentinel group with a leader is worth double its BSP.

# ORDER VII — ANOMALIES

*A rule that stopped working. Anomalies are not creatures; they are **local edits** to the Expanse's physics, usually with a body because bodies are what the Expanse has to hand. They are the only Order that cannot be learned: the same tactics never work twice, and a Cohort that fights them the way it fought the last one dies.*

**How to run an Anomaly.** Each has one **Broken Rule** (print it, hold it steady, do not change it mid-scene) and one **Denial** (a thing that turns the rule off). Anomalies never chase, never harvest, and never eat. They *do* something else, and the Director should be able to answer, honestly, what they want — even if the answer is "to finish a sentence".

---

### RULEBREAK INSTANCE
Standard · T3 · Mesoscopic · **MT 15 · MI 38 · soak 3 · BD 4 (type: the Cohort's most-used type) · pool 6d · PP 3 · Flow 4**
**Broken Rule:** within 6 Drifts, **Inhibitors do not mark Stress** — for anyone, including the Cohort, and including the Anomaly. (This is a *gift* with teeth: the Cohort can Burst recklessly, and the Anomaly grows each time they do.)
**Denial:** a **Sequence Barb**, a cut protein-tree, or a Smith's Tune applied to the Anomaly's own body (AT 18) restores the rule for 3 Cycles.
**Special — Absorb (2 PP):** each Inhibitor rolled by any creature in its zone heals it 4 MI.
**HARVEST:** C6 / G3 / P2 · **Codon 15 · BSP 2** — the specimen *keeps* the Broken Rule for one Craft project, which is the most desirable and most illegal object in the Atlas's grafting tables
**Why it exists:** something *asked* for this. Anomalies are usually a wish with a body.

### THE SAME, TWICE
Standard · T4 · Mesoscopic · **MT 16 · MI 46 · soak 4 · BD 5 Kinetic · pool 7d · PP 3 · Flow 4**
**Broken Rule:** there are two of it. Not copies: **the same organism, in two places.** Damage dealt to one is dealt to both, but a condition, a Mark, or a Sequester applies to only one — and then it *resolves*: the two bodies decide which one is real, and the other flickers out for a Cycle.
**Denial:** attack both with the *same* action (a Spread or Sweeping Strike, or an area Burst) and it must choose one body permanently: MI halved, no flicker.
**Special — Choose (3 PP):** one body becomes fully real (all MI, all specials), the other becomes **potential**: it can only be hurt, not hurt, and if destroyed the real one loses 20 MI.
**HARVEST:** C7 / G4 / P2 · **Codon 30 · BSP 2** (harvesting *both* bodies at once is the only way to get the P2; the Hegemony has a standing order for it)
**Why it exists:** an organism that has not decided where it is. The Core Clock ticks harder around it.

### THE UNNUMBERED
Elite · T3 · size and shape: whatever the Cohort last counted · **MT 16 · MI 76 · soak 5 · BD 5 Osmotic · pool 7d · PP 4 · Flow 5**
**Broken Rule:** it cannot be counted. Any attempt to state how many of it there are (including a Sense action, a Chemoreception check, or the phrase "there are three") inflicts **Marked** on the speaker and gives the Anomaly +1 Overflow on its next action.
**Denial:** stop counting. Fight it in Turbid water, describe *where* rather than *how many*, and have the Cohort use area effects — the only reliable tactic is to make the question irrelevant.
**Special — Borrow a Number (4 PP):** it takes a number from a creature's stat (player chooses which number to lose 1 from: MI, MT, Flow, or PP for the scene). The Anomaly does not say which it will take. It always takes the one that matters.
**HARVEST:** C10 / G4 / P3 / T1 · **Codon 30 · BSP 4** — the specimen's mass is *variable*: a Smith can make a Masterwork that weighs what you need it to weigh, and will not sell it to anyone
**Why it exists:** the Expanse's registers cannot file it, which is either a mercy or a threat depending on who is being registered.

### REVERSE BLOOM
Elite · T4 · Vast, beautiful, wrong · **MT 17 · MI 92 · soak 6 · BD 6 Lytic · pool 8d · PP 4 · Flow 1**
**Broken Rule:** within 8 Drifts, **damage heals and healing damages.** (Print it. Enforce it in both directions. The Cohort will use it, and should.)
**Denial:** a Desiccation zone (its tissue cannot hold water and the rule stutters: it works only in Contact for 2 Cycles); a Tonic Blessing rite; or the Cohort *reversing their own gear* (a Smith can invert an Apparatus's damage type for one scene).
**Special — Unmake (5 PP):** one structure or organism in its zone returns to an *earlier state*: −1 Stage for a Phenotype NPC, one Graft Slot lost permanently, or a site loses its last four Cycles of construction.
**Weakness:** it cannot move and it is *happy*, which means a Cohort that leaves takes no damage. Its rule is its only defence.
**HARVEST:** C12 / G5 / P4 / T2 · **Codon 60 · BSP 4** (a Reverse Bloom's Pristine Sequence is the only known material that can *un-grow* a Parasitic graft — and the price of one is the reason some colonies have gone to war)
**Why it exists:** an answer to the Closing that went wrong. The Deep Choir sings at it from a respectful distance.

### INVERTED LUNG
Elite · T4 · Vast · **MT 17 · MI 92 · soak 6 · BD 6 Osmotic · pool 8d · PP 4 · Flow 2**
**Broken Rule:** in its zone, **ATP costs are paid backwards**: every action *grants* the actor 2 ATP, and every Rest, Vent, or heal *costs* ATP. (An engine inversion so wrong that Barophils report "hearing breathing from the water".)
**Denial:** Sequence Barb, a Tonic Blessing, or simply **spending**: a Cohort that keeps acting is fine; the Anomaly's real weapon is forcing rest, and rest is how the Cohort clears Stress.
**Special — Suffocate (3 PP):** for 2 Cycles the rule *inverts again* for one target: their actions cost double and their rests heal nothing.
**Weakness:** it wants the Cohort to be careful. Be reckless for three Cycles, and it has nothing.
**HARVEST:** C12 / G5 / P4 / T2 · **Codon 60 · BSP 4** (an Inverted Lung's organ is the core of the **AE Crucible** upgrade line: the Hegemony will trade a favour for one)
**Why it exists:** a metabolism running the wrong way, at the scale of a habitat. It is *breathing the region in*.

### THE LONG ECHO
Elite · T5 · invisible unless it is acting · **MT 18 · MI 108 · soak 7 · BD 7 any (it uses the Cohort's last three damage types, in order) · pool 9d · PP 4 · Flow 6**
**Broken Rule:** it acts on the **previous Cycle**: at the start of each Cycle, its attack lands *before* anyone's Frequency Check, and it is targeting the position the Cohort occupied last Cycle. (Movement matters more than MI: an Echo fight is a dance.)
**Denial:** stop moving in patterns. Break your own rhythm — Split Frequency, hold Reserve, stand still deliberately. A Cohort that acts *against* its habits becomes untargetable, and the Echo will spend its action on a wall.
**Special — Repeat (4 PP):** one action the Cohort took two Cycles ago happens again, at −2 dice, against whoever the Echo chooses.
**HARVEST:** C14 / G6 / P5 / T2 / T1 · **Codon 120 · BSP 4** (its P5 is a **memory of the future**: a Ribozyme Engineer can write it into a template, and the resulting Relic's Gene-Debt is measured in *Beats you will not have*)
**Why it exists:** something is trying very hard to remember a fight it has not lost yet.

### THE QUIET
Catastrophe · T4 · Vast, still, patient · **PHASE 1 MT 14 · MI 80 · soak 7 · BD 8 Genetic · pool 8d · PP 5 · Flow 0**
**PHASE 2 (the Absence: MI 80, invisible, MT +3, attacks without announcing) · PHASE 3 (the Question: MI 70, cannot act, and it *asks one question of each Cohort member*; the answers are binding on the fiction*)**
**Broken Rule:** within 10 Drifts there is **no signal of any kind**: no Broadcast, no Chemoreception, no Signal Discourse, no Cohesion spending, no Symbiote commands, no Bonds. Every Cohort feature that depends on communication stops functioning.
**Denial:** physical only: touch-based features (Contact-band abilities, Engulfment, Oath boxes spent *aloud*), and the Cohesion mechanic's physical exceptions (a shared body-action counts as Cohesion, at the Director's call).
**Special — Silence (5 PP):** a Cohesion spend by the Cohort is *forbidden* for 3 Cycles; a Bonded pair is Locked apart.
**Weakness:** Phase 3. It cannot act then, and whatever it asks is answerable: the Cohort's honest answers end the encounter *in their favour*. This is a fight that the table should be allowed to win by talking, and it will be the best scene of the campaign.
**HARVEST:** C18 / G11 / P5 / T2 / T2 · **Codon 90 · BSP 10** — and the Ash refuse to bury what is left, because there is nothing to bury: the harvest is *the question*, written down
**Why it exists:** a hole where a region's communication used to be. Act III's opening image, and the Curators' calling card.

### CURATOR MANIFESTATION
Catastrophe · T5 (Apotheotic) · size: too large to be in the water · **MT 15 · MI 110 / 110 / 110 (three phases; it does not go Dormant, it *files the phase*) · soak 9 · BD 9 Genetic · pool 10d · PP 5 · Flow 3**
**Not budgeted, deliberately.** The Tier-5 Catastrophe template is MI 270, soak 8, pool 9 — a Curator is **above** its own template, and the numbers are printed so a Cohort of sufficient standing can *count the difference* and understand what they have walked into. There is no BSP figure for this block: a Curator is not an encounter you balance, it is an encounter you survive or negotiate.
**Broken Rule:** within its zone, **the Core's rules are enforced**: no Chimeric or unregistered growth functions, Parasitic grafts activate their Costs at double, and every Relic's Gene-Debt becomes *payable immediately or inert*. It is not attacking you. It is **auditing** you.
**Denial:** there is no Denial. There is **compliance** (pay your debts, surrender the sequence, submit to registration: the Manifestation withdraws, and grants one **Attention mark**, Core XII) or **flight** (it does not pursue past the region's boundary; it never has to).
**Special — Filing (5 PP, once per phase):** one Cohort member is *recorded*: they gain an Attention mark, and the next Manifestation the Cohort meets treats them as a known quantity (its specials target them by name, and its negotiation offers are addressed to them personally).
**Weakness:** **Apotheosis.** A Cohort at Core Clock 5 with Apotheotic Choices can contest its Thresholds at AT 28 (Nucleoid) and, on a Runaway Catalysis, force it to *ask a question instead of acting* — which is the closest thing to a wound a Curator has.
**HARVEST:** none. There is nothing to harvest. Any attempt is a Core Clock segment and a Total Lysis with no body left.
**Tactics:** arrives late, uninvited, and unkillable. It resolves the encounter. Your job as Director is to make it *interesting* rather than fatal: it has a policy, and policies have preferences.
**Why it exists:** it is the reason the Sacred Code is not a metaphor, and the reason the Act III question is "who is doing the filing, and into what?"

# COLONISTS, CONSTRUCTS, AND RELIC-BEARERS

**Colonists.** Use the **Atlas creation rules** for any named NPC organism (they are organisms, not statblocks) and the Bestiary tier templates for *generic* populations. Quick reference for the three cases that come up constantly:

| You need | Use | Numbers |
| :-- | :-- | :-- |
| a **crowd** (market, hab, crew) | no stats; one OSM/AFF contest for the crowd's reaction (AT 12 mixed, 16 hostile, 20 armed) | crowd size matters more than stats: 40 organisms can pin a Cohort with nets and law |
| a **militia squad** (4–8 colonists) | Standard templates at the Cohort's tier, minus 1 to pools, plus 1 to soak if armoured | BSP 2 each; they rout at 40% MI and will surrender if challenged lawfully |
| a **named authority** | Elite template + one Phenotype feature they actually have | BSP 4; they have lawyers, and their real weapon is the Response Clock |

**Symbiote blocks** are in Core §9.6 (eight symbiotes with upkeep and control by AFF). A **Retainer** is a Colonist with a contract: 5–20 S/Beat, one Oath box, and the Core's control rules do not apply to them — they can quit.

### PEPTIDE CONSTRUCT (the Atlas Constructor's print)
Tier 1–5 construct · **MI 8 + 8×Tier · MT 11 + Tier · pool 3 + Tier · BD 1 + Tier · soak Tier − 1 · PP 2 · Flow 4**
Standing orders (assigned at print, changed by a 2-PP Synthesis action); one inserted Harvest trait at −1 tier; control limit 1 + ⌊RTC ÷ 2⌋.
**Weakness:** Lytic damage (Constructs are printed protein: −2 soak to Lytic, always); a Sequence Barb ends its orders for 2 Cycles; an Antigen Gnat Mark makes every Construct in the region confused about it.
**HARVEST:** C(2+Tier) / G⌈Tier÷2⌉ · **Codon ½ × base · BSP 1 per two Tiers** — and the Hegemony considers every unlicensed Construct a *crime scene*

### RELIC-BEARER (template, any Order or Colonist)
Add to any block: **+1 to all pools; one Relic's benefit; one Gene-Debt (4-segment clock).**
When the bearer's Debt clock fills, the Relic's Rule Break activates *unbidden* once, and then the Relic **goes quiet** until a collector arrives. Killing a Relic-Bearer transfers the Debt to the killer (DM Codex §10.6). The relic itself is a Relic per Core §9.8, with its own personality if the table earns one.

### HOST / VESSEL (template, any Colonist or creature)
Add: **one Wraith or Hijacker Insert installed; its own features reduced by one rank; at 0 MI it does not die — the *tenant* leaves** and can flee, negotiate, or take another host within three bands.
Killing a Host is a murder under Precept 6 in every registered region. The correct answer is usually a Mend (Sequence Reading AT 20 + a Pristine Sequence).
