Percentile horror in 1920s coastal New England. Investigators (a doctor, a reporter, a fisherman's widow, a defrocked priest) have fragile minds: the strain track is their sanity, and what they see costs them. The world's mystery: something old sleeps under the harbour of Gull Rock, and a drowned cult of townsfolk is singing it awake.
First adventure, one evening: "The Gull Rock Light". The lighthouse keeper's family vanished during a storm three nights ago; the lamp still burns though no one tends it. Four or five places: the harbour town (safe, an inn and a nervous constable), the causeway to the island at low tide (a hazard: the tide comes in), the keeper's cottage (clues, a diary), the lighthouse tower (a fight with what the keeper's family has become), and the sea cave beneath (the cult's rite and its leader). A countdown: the tide and the rite (when it fills, the thing under the harbour stirs). An ambush when the investigators fail to keep quiet in the cave. A choice at the end: put out the light, or keep it burning.
