### How to use this book

The Core gives you a body. The Atlas gives you a lineage and a trade. The Codex gives you the powers that run the world. **QUORUM gives you the street**: the colony, the family, the market, the office, the court, the feast, and the vote.

The books already print a society. Second Flow is run by a water-tally council; Tally-Keeper Ossune keeps its board; the Tallow Market sells other people's Oaths; the Warden Circles rank their gate-commanders as *Porters*; the Ash Readers read the dead; retainers can quit. What the books do not print is the procedure that lets you **run** those things at the table. That is this book's job: it makes what is already on the page operable first, and invents only where the page is silent.

**Three promises.**

1. **One tool per job.** *Mood* is how a creature smells tonight (Core §11.3). *Standing* is a faction's memory of you (Core §8.7). *Condition* is how much of your interior you can afford to keep (Ch. 5). A *Clock* is what is coming (Codex §5.2). QUORUM adds no second reputation scale, no new die and no new damage type.
2. **Every social scene has a board.** Pulse Wars have bands. A colony deciding has the **Issue Track** (Ch. 4). A room arguing has the **Convocation** (Ch. 4). A trial has both (Ch. 10).
3. **Derived numbers are labelled.** Anything QUORUM invents carries *(derived)* and names the printed figure it was built from, so a Director can check the arithmetic on the back of an envelope.

**A cap on bookkeeping.** A PC carries at most **6 Standings, 1 condition, 9 Ledger lines and the 3 campaign Clocks**. If the table wants more than that, the extra thing is a Vector, and it belongs on the campaign sheet.

# 1 · THE FOUR QUESTIONS

Every social moment in the Expanse asks four questions. Answer them in this order, out loud, and the scene runs itself.

| Question | The tool | Where | Changes |
| :-- | :-- | :-- | :-- |
| **How does it smell tonight?** | **Mood** — twelve moods | Core §11.3 | every exchange; a Chemotactic Rite shifts one NPC one rung per participant |
| **What does it remember of us?** | **Standing** — 0–10 with each faction | Core §8.7, Codex §7 | a Vector's worth of service up; one sentence down |
| **What can we afford to be here?** | **Condition** — Dry, Settled, Swollen, or Unlisted | Ch. 5 | a Drift Season of stores; a sentence; the Tide |
| **What is coming for us?** | **a Clock** — Colony, Order, Core, and Response Clocks | Codex §5.2; Bestiary *Colonists* | something loud, witnessed by its chemistry |

**Mood is not Standing.** Four names appear in both lists — Hostile, Wary, Client, Kin. Say which you mean: *"Its mood is Hostile — that's tonight. Its Standing with you is Wary — that's history."*

## 1.1 The Standing ladder

**Standing** is Core §8.7's 0–10 track with four bands. The Codex names six rungs inside those bands (Codex §7, reconciled in the 10 Nov 2026 printing):

| Standing | Core band | Codex rung | At the table |
| --: | :-- | :-- | :-- |
| 0–1 | **Hostile** | *Hostile* | they shoot first, or will not trade at all |
| 2–3 | **Wary** | *Wary* | they question, block, test |
| 4–5 | **Wary** | *Neutral* | they deal with you as a party, not a person |
| 6–7 | **Client** | *Obliging* | help, for a price; their offices serve you |
| 8 | **Client** | *Confided* | they hide something for you, and tell you one true thing you did not ask for |
| 9–10 | **Kin** | *Bound* | you are inside their Charter: a duty, a voice, protection. The Codex prints a Cohort *Bound* to two factions at once (Codex §12.4): it is a state you are in, not a summit you own |

**Three rules.**

1. **Every act of harvest, mercy or contract is witnessed** (Core §8.7). If a faction's chemistry was in the room, its Standing may move. If it was not, it moves only through a Clock that faction already runs on you.
2. **One rung up, one rung down with its rival** (Codex §5.7). QUORUM names each faction's rival once, in Ch. 15's sponsor table, and nowhere else.
3. **Standing is not friendship.** You can be Kin to the Hegemony and loathed by every foundry mother in it.

**Minor powers.** A colony organ, a guild cell, one Warden household or a Choir circle gets a Standing track only once the Cohort has acted on it twice. Then it is real, and it remembers. *(derived)*

## 1.2 Clocks that run on people

The Codex runs three campaign Clocks — Colony, Order, Core (Codex §5.2). The Bestiary gives every **named authority** a fourth kind: *"their real weapon is the Response Clock"* (Bestiary, *Colonists*). Read them together:

* **Campaign Clocks** count what the world is doing about the Cohort. There are three, always.
* **Response Clocks** belong to a site or an institution — a gate-house, a Tally-Keeper's office, a Host-City's immune system. They are as many as the Cohort has annoyed, and they live on the colony's sheet (Ch. 2), not the PC's.
* **A PC's own Clock** — *a Warden register has a sample of you; a landlord; a creditor; a Choir circle that sang for your budding* — is chosen at creation (Ch. 16) and kept on the PC block (Ch. 18).

# 2 · THE COLONY IS A BODY

Organisms in the Expanse are cells. A colony is what happens when enough of them stop competing and start *specialising*. Folk wisdom says the first colonies copied the Nucleoid Core; the Warden Circles say the Sacred Code commanded it; the Sequence Underground says the copying went the other way. Everyone agrees on the result: **a colony is a body, and every organism in it belongs to an organ.**

Your organ is not your job. It is your *place* — who feeds you, who you answer to, which Hearth you eat at, and who will come looking if you go Dormant in the street. Changing organs is possible, painful, and gossiped about for a Tide.

The Core already licenses this: *"Sieges are Pulse Wars at architecture scale … the same economy, bigger objects"* (Core §7.5). A colony runs on the tracks you already use — health boxes, a Stress-like unrest, Cohesion, walls with MI — at the scale of a body made of bodies.

## 2.1 The six organs

| Organ | What it does | Who staffs it | Sells | Asks | When it fails |
| :-- | :-- | :-- | :-- | :-- | :-- |
| **THE MEMBRANE** | borders, gates, the register: who is *self* | Warden Circles, Immunans, gate-clerks | passage, Antigen Rites, registration | your scent, your Oaths, your lineage | strangers walk in unread; or *nobody* gets in, and the colony starves |
| **THE PUMP** | labour: carrying, pumping, building, moving goods against the gradient | haulers, Flagellans, the Swollen (Ch. 5) | hands, haulage, a strike | wages, rest, a seat at the Hearth | the Vacuole empties, the Membrane's pumps stop, the colony swells |
| **THE VACUOLE** | stores, water rights, the treasury | quartermasters, the Brine Office, Epithalian stewards | loans, rations, storage, water | collateral, contracts, patience | hoarding, rationing, a run on the stores, riot |
| **THE NUCLEOLUS** | the council: reads the Quorum (Ch. 4), keeps the colony's sequence | elders, Quorum Speakers, old budlines | rulings, offices, charters (Ch. 15) | signal, testimony, deference | no decision reaches Quorum; factions rule by default |
| **THE HEARTH** | the commons: shared meals, Rites, healing, the place everyone is equal | Chemotactic priests, healers, Amoebans | Rites (Core §11.6), healing, news | presence, a share of the meal, honesty | Cohesion bleeds out; feuds, Oaths unwitnessed |
| **THE LYSOSOME** | waste, the dead, recycling — and justice | the Reclaimers' Ash, Amoeban digesters, assayers | Assays (Ch. 10), burial grounds, salvage | respect for the dead-cycle (Precept 2) | bodies go unharvested, disease, Precept 2 breaks, the Ash walk out |

**The Lysosome judges** because in the Expanse justice *is* a digestive act: a body is read, broken down into its truths, and the useful parts returned to the colony. Nobody enjoys being near the Lysosome. Nobody can live without it.

## 2.3 Organ health

Each organ has a **health track of four boxes**. Mark boxes left to right.

| Boxes marked | State | At the table |
| --: | :-- | :-- |
| 0 | **Sound** | it works; it endorses one side of each Issue (Ch. 4) if courted |
| 1 | **Strained** | its services cost +50%; its organisms are Wary (Core §11.3) by default |
| 2 | **Failing** | its services need an Obliging Standing or a favour; it cannot endorse; one Colony Trouble roll (Codex §11.3) per Beat |
| 3 | **Necrotic** | it has stopped; the colony suffers its *When it fails* line; **Colony Clock +1** (Codex §5.2) |
| 4 | **Excised** | it is gone. The colony must transplant a new one (a Vector) or become something else |

**Marking boxes.** Mark one when the Colony Clock advances (the Director names *which* organ took it, in front of the players — Codex §5.2 rule 2). Mark one when a Colony Trouble result clearly hits an organ. Mark one when the Cohort does something loud *to* that organ: a strike broken, a register burned, a Hearth fouled.

**Healing boxes.** Clear one when the Cohort resolves a Vector *for* that organ, when a festival honours it (Ch. 12), or at the start of each Drift Season if Colony Cohesion is 6 or more.

## 2.4 Colony Cohesion

The Core promised that the Codex would track **Colony-level Cohesion** (Core §2.5). This is that track.

**Colony Cohesion (0–10)** is the colony's group metabolism: how much its organs still agree. A typical colony sits at **5**.

| Colony Cohesion | The colony | Effect |
| --: | :-- | :-- |
| 9–10 | **Unanimous** | Quorum thresholds −2; all organs heal one box at the Drift Season |
| 6–8 | **Healthy** | heals one organ box per Drift Season |
| 3–5 | **Grumbling** | nothing extra; this is ordinary life |
| 1–2 | **Fractious** | Quorum thresholds +2; every organ's Hearth eats separately |
| 0 | **Lysing** | the colony splits: each organ (or lineage quarter) becomes its own hamlet, or one organ seizes the rest |

**Raise Colony Cohesion (+1):** a festival kept well (Ch. 12); a Vector that heals a Necrotic organ; an Issue reaching Quorum *without* quenching (Ch. 4); a public Mourning Rite for a beloved dead (Core §11.6).
**Lower it (−1):** an organ reaching Necrotic; an exposed chemical lie in the Nucleolus; a body left unharvested in the open (Precept 2); a Quorum stolen by quenching (Ch. 4); an Assay everyone knows was bought (Ch. 10).

## 2.5 Belonging to an organ

Every NPC belongs to one organ; write it beside their name. It tells you, at a glance, who they answer to and what they will trade.

PCs choose an **organ of origin** when they are rooted (Ch. 16). A Cohort holding a Charter (Ch. 15) belongs to its sponsor's organ while it works.

**Induction** — changing organs. A Rite at the new organ's Hearth: 1 Fugue Interval, 30 S, and a sponsor inside that organ at Obliging or better. You lose your old organ's Hearth (and its gossip), and your old organ's Standing drops one rung. Organisms who change organs twice are called **drifters**; three times, **unlisted in all but name**.

## 2.6 The colony's other tracks

A colony also carries the numbers below. They sit on the Colony Sheet (Ch. 18) beside its organs and Cohesion.

| Track | What it is | How to set it |
| :-- | :-- | :-- |
| **Population** | bodies | as §2.2; printed where the Codex gives it (Second Flow: 14,000) |
| **Hearths** | registered households | population ÷ 15 *(derived — the books print no household size)* |
| **Feed** | what the colony grosses | **5 S per colonist per Beat** *(derived from Core §8.4: a Clientele of three colonists earns 1d6 × 5 S a Beat)* |
| **Tally** | what the colony collects | **10 S per hearth per Beat** — about 13% of Feed *(derived; the tally itself is printed, Codex §6.3, §12.2)* |
| **Interior** | unrest, 0–8, on the Stress bands (Core §5.8) | starts at 2; see below |
| **Structure** | walls and gates with MI and Break AT | from Core §7.5's five materials |
| **Mobilised (BP)** | the force it can field | turn out 5–10% of adults; **BP = mobilised ÷ 10** (Codex §3.6: *"every 1 BP lost = ~10 organisms dead"*) |
| **Charter** | 3–5 founding lines | written as sentences; breaking one costs an Office its voice (Ch. 3) |
| **Clocks** | the Colony Clock, and any Response Clocks | Codex §5.2; Ch. 1 |

**Interior** is a colony's Stress. Mark it when an organ goes Necrotic, when a Quorum is stolen, when a strike begins, when the Tide's Zenith bites the wrong condition (Ch. 5). Clear it as Colony Cohesion heals organs.

| Interior | The streets | Effect |
| --: | :-- | :-- |
| 0–2 | calm | nothing |
| 3 | **rationing** | the Vacuole marks a box; every price moves one arrow toward scarce (Ch. 9) |
| 5 | **strike** | the Pump stops (Ch. 9); every Issue the Pump backs starts at +1 |
| 7 | **cascade** | the next failure becomes a **Mass Conflict** inside the walls (Codex §3.6) |
| 8 | **collapse** | Colony Cohesion −2; one organ is Excised; the colony drops to Interior 5 |

**BP is not BSP.** A militia squad is about 1 BP in a mass-conflict line and BSP 2 per colonist in an encounter budget (Bestiary, *Colonists*). The two never add: BP is the battle economy (Codex §3.6), BSP the encounter economy (Codex §4.1).

**The arrears ladder.** A household that cannot pay the tally: one Beat unpaid, 1 Stress; two, registration suspended for a Tide; three, the gate closes and the colony's Clock advances. *(derived)* A Cohort's Niche already includes its tally — the 5 S a Beat of upkeep is it (Core §8.4).

**The Unlisted cannot be assessed at all.** They pay no tally, because the tally cannot see them. That is their edge, and why every railroad and heresy in the Expanse runs through them (Ch. 10).

## 2.7 Running the body

Start every colony scene by asking three questions, and answer them out loud:

1. **Which organ is hurting?** One always is.
2. **What is the colony deciding?** One Issue is always on the air (Ch. 4).
3. **Whose Hearth are we eating at?** It tells the players who is listening.

# 3 · OFFICES

Every organ has a head. The books already name three of them: the Warden Circles rank their gate-commanders **Porter** (Porter-Second Yune, Codex §7.2; Porter-Third Yune, Codex §12.2); Second Flow has a **Tally-Keeper** (Ossune, Codex §12.2); the Ash read the dead as **Readers** (Codex §7.4). QUORUM fills in the rest.

## 3.1 The seven offices

| Office | Organ | Voice | Boon | Burden |
| :-- | :-- | --: | :-- | :-- |
| **Porter** | the Membrane | 2 | chooses who is read at which gate; the colony's gates roll +2 against forged tokens | every admission of a wanted organism is a segment on someone's Clock |

| **Steward** | the Vacuole / the Hearth | 2 | feeds the colony; may pay a household's arrears from the store | a Recession lands here first (Codex §5.4) |
| **Reader** | the Lysosome | 2 | the colony's funerals, last-memory readings and harvest brokerage (the services the Ash sell, Codex §7.4) | bound by the Ash's red lines: no harvest of a body with unspent Oath boxes; no Total Dissolution |

**Fifteen voices.** That is the **Assembly**.

## 3.2 Holding an office

* **Term:** one Osmotic Tide, re-chosen at the **Contract Fair** (Ch. 12). Each organ reaches its own small Quorum (Q 4) on who holds its office.
* **Who:** office-holders use the Bestiary's **named authority** block — Elite template plus one Phenotype feature they actually have, BSP 4 — and each office carries a **Response Clock** of 4 segments (Ch. 1).
* **Losing it:** a broken Charter line (§2.6) revokes the office that broke it. Abandoning an office writes a public Ledger line against you (Ch. 6).
* **A PC in office** gains the boon, carries the burden, and holds a **voice** in the Assembly. Offices are the reward that social play is for.

## 3.3 The Assembly

The Assembly decides the colony's **ordinary** business in one sitting: fees, repairs, who gets the next charter, whether to receive an envoy.

* A **Motion** passes with **8 of 15 voices**. Ties go to the **Tally-Keeper**, because water is the colony's actual life. *(derived)*
* A vacant office's voice is cast by **whoever its holder owed** most — read the vacant holder's Ledger (Ch. 6).
* **Big questions** — war, exile of a budline, the charter, selling the water — go to **Quorum** (Ch. 4): the whole colony must *know* them, not just its offices. An Assembly that tries to decide a Quorum question alone gets an Interior box and a new Issue: *who do the offices think they are?*

## 3.4 The colony contest

When an office-holder (PC or NPC) tries to move one of the colony's tracks directly — raise the tally, clear the rationing, repair a gate — roll **d6 + the office-holder's AFF + Symbiosis rank + terrain** against the AT ladder, the shape Codex §3.6 uses for mass conflict. **Overflow ÷ 4** moves one named track one notch; at most one notch per track per Beat. *(derived)*

# 4 · QUORUM AND CONVOCATION

There are two ways a society decides things, and the Expanse has both.

* **Quorum** — the *whole colony* decides, by chemistry. Use it when everyone must **know** the answer: war, exile, the water, the charter.
* **Convocation** — the *people in a room* decide, by argument. Use it for a market, a council, a trial, a harvest dispute, a wedding, a treaty: three to six parties, each with something to win.

A Quorum often contains Convocations: the scenes where each side wins its allies. A trial is a Convocation whose verdict a Quorum confirms (Ch. 10).

## QUORUM

Real cells decide together by **quorum sensing**: each releases a signal molecule, and when the concentration of signal crosses a threshold, the whole population changes behaviour at once. The Expanse governs itself the same way.

A colony does not vote. Every organism that cares about a question **breathes** its signal into the Hearth air. When the air is thick enough with one answer, the colony *knows* — all of it, at once, in its chemistry — and the decision is made. Quorum Speakers read the air and say it aloud, but they announce; they do not decide.

This is beautiful, and it is not fair: **an organism with more ATP can breathe more signal.** That is the class war of the Expanse in one sentence.

## 4.1 The Issue Track

An **Issue** is a question a colony must answer: *do we let the Flow Guild dam the eastern current? do we hand the Lysosan to the Wardens? does the Pump get a seat in the Nucleolus?*

Draw a track from **−Q** to **+Q**, where **Q** is the colony's Quorum (§2.2: Hamlet 4, Settlement 6, Colony 8, Metropolis 10). The track starts at **0**. One end is **For**, the other **Against**. When the marker reaches either end, the colony has reached **Quorum** and the decision is made — binding, felt by every organism in the colony.

Colony Cohesion moves the ends: at Cohesion 9–10, Q is 2 lower; at 1–2, Q is 2 higher.

**Rounds.** An Issue is argued in **rounds**. A round is one **Beat** for a pressing question, or one **Pulse Ten** (10 Beats) for a slow one. Each round, each side takes its signal actions (§4.2), then the Quorum Speaker reads the air (§4.4).

## 4.2 Signal actions

Each organism (PC or named NPC) may take **one** signal action per round. Bands of supporters count as one organism with a pool of 4 dice.

| Signal action | How | Moves the track |
| :-- | :-- | :-- |
| **Speak at the Hearth** | AFF + Signal Discourse vs **AT 12** (Semantic register, Core §11.1) | 1, +1 per 4 Overflow |
| **Move the crowd** | AFF + Symbiosis vs **AT 12** (Affective register) | 1, +1 per 4 Overflow; on a failure, 1 the *other* way |
| **Breathe heavily** (pay for signal) | spend `10 S × Q` in rendered signal, no roll | 1 (max once per side per round) |
| **Swear it** | encode a public Oath (Core §11.4) tying yourself to the outcome | 2 |
| **Court an organ** | win a three-exchange scene (Core §11.1) with a **Sound** organ's head | 1 per round while it endorses |
| **Call a faction** | a faction where your Standing is Obliging or better (Codex §7) | 1 per round; the faction will want something |
| **False signal** | DFF + Concealment vs **AT 16** (Deceptive register) | 2; but see *Exposure* |
| **Quench** (a crime) | RTC + Synthesis vs **AT 16** | remove 2 of the *other* side's movement this round; see §4.5 |

**Exposure.** False signal is a lie in the air. Any organism with OSM + Chemoreception may test it (contested). If exposed, the false signal's 2 points swing **3 the other way**, Colony Cohesion −1, and the liar faces an Assay (Ch. 10).

**Offices in a Quorum.** An office-holder (Ch. 3) may *Call an office* as their signal action: no roll, the track moves by the office's **voice ÷ 2**, rounded up. *(derived)*

## 4.3 Endorsements

A **Sound** organ (§2.3) endorses one side of each Issue it cares about: +1 per round. Failing and Necrotic organs cannot endorse. This is why factions break organs before a big Quorum: it removes a voice.

A Hearth that the Cohort has eaten at three times this Drift Season (a Chemotactic Rite each time) counts as **courted** without a scene.

## 4.4 The Quorum Speaker

Every Settlement and larger has a **Quorum Speaker**: an organism trusted to read the air and announce. Give each Speaker an **Honesty** of 1–6.

At the end of each round, roll a d6. If it rolls **over** the Speaker's Honesty, the Speaker shades the reading by 1 toward whoever has paid them, frightened them, or shares their Hearth. Players may test a Speaker (OSM + Chemoreception vs AT 12 + Honesty); a Speaker caught shading is a Nucleolus scandal: Colony Cohesion −1, and a new Issue arrives — *who reads the air now?*

## 4.5 Quorum-quenching

Destroying another organism's signal with enzymes is the Expanse's gravest political crime: it is not lying in the air, it is **making the air lie**. A quench removes 2 points of the other side's movement in a round.

Each quench leaves a residue. At the end of the Issue, anyone may hunt for it: OSM + Chemoreception vs **AT 16** (AT 12 if the colony's Lysosome is Sound). A found quench voids the Quorum, drops Colony Cohesion by 2, and sends the quencher to the Assay with the charge *Breaking the Air* (Ch. 10).

## 4.6 What happens at Quorum

The decision is felt, not posted. Every organism in the colony now *knows* it, and acting against a reached Quorum is acting against your own colony's chemistry: **−1 die on all rolls inside the colony** until you leave or recant at the Hearth. Most organisms simply comply. Rebels become interesting.

## 4.8 Running a Quorum, step by step

1. **Name the Issue** and its two answers. Write both ends of the track on a card.
2. **Name the sides**: which organs, factions and faces want which answer. Put the Speaker's Honesty face-down.
3. **Each round**, go around the table: every PC and named NPC takes one signal action. Narrate each one as a *scene*, not a roll.
4. **Read the air.** Add endorsements, roll for the Speaker, move the marker.
5. **At Quorum,** narrate the moment the colony *knows*. Then ask: *who lost, and what will they do tonight?*

> **A Quorum is a Pulse War with Hearths instead of bands.** Run it at the same pace: three to five rounds, fast, loud, with someone always about to cheat.

## CONVOCATION

A Convocation is a room with three to six parties, each wanting something different, and one **Motion** on the table. It uses the Core's conversation exactly: the three registers (Core §11.1), the Stakes Ladder with 4 Overflow buying a rung (Core §11.2), the moods (Core §11.3). It adds only the room.

## 4.9 Setting the room

Write four lines on a card.

1. **The Motion** — one sentence, decided at the end: *who takes the wreck in the sixth hab's channel.*
2. **The parties** (3–6). For each: **Interest** (what they want), **Price** (what they would take instead), **Leverage** (what they can do to the others), and their **Standing** with the Cohort (Ch. 1).
3. **The room's mood** (Core §11.3). It adds +1 die to moves that suit it and −1 to moves that don't: a *Hungry* room favours harvest claims; a *Grieving* room punishes threats.
4. **A Table Clock** of 4–6 segments. Each round advances it by one. **At fill, whoever is furthest up the Stakes Ladder with the most parties wins the Motion.**

## 4.10 The moves

Each round, each party makes **one move**. A round is one **exchange** (Core §11.1); a Convocation is usually three to five rounds, run like a duel.

| Move | The contest | Effect |
| :-- | :-- | :-- |
| **Offer** | **Semantic**: AFF + Signal Discourse vs the target's OSM + Chemoreception | Overflow ÷ 4 = rungs up the Stakes Ladder with the target, against a real price you name |
| **Read** | **Deceptive**, at the target's choice: they roll DFF + Concealment vs your OSM + Chemoreception | learn one party's Price, Leverage, or a secret. If they are not hiding it, don't roll — you already know |
| **Reveal** | produce **evidence**: a witnessed Ledger line (Ch. 6), a register entry, a Memory Vial (Core §11.7), trail-script (Ch. 11) | **one rung, no roll**. A rival may contest with their own evidence: Semantic, AFF + Signal Discourse, winner's evidence stands. Each piece of evidence is revealed once |
| **Alliance** | **Affective**: AFF + Symbiosis vs the target's VSC + Pressure Tolerance | two parties move as one for the rest of the Convocation; costs 1 Cohesion or a Ledger line |
| **Bind** | propose the Motion **as an Oath** (Core §11.4: 1 Fugue Interval, 10 Substrate, a witness with OSM 3+) | if the room accepts, the outcome is enforceable — and recorded (Precept 3) |
| **Pressure** | **Affective**: AFF + Symbiosis or Vigour vs VSC + Pressure Tolerance | one rung; **on a failure the room's mood shifts one step toward Hostile, and the colony's Clock advances** |
| **Walk** | free | withdraw recognition: you drop one rung with the room's host, and every party must answer one question about *why you left* before the next round. The strongest move when the room already leans your way |

**Runaway Catalysis buys two rungs. Never skip a rung a party would not cross** (Core §11.2).

**Proxies and the absent.** A party bound to you — by a Bond (Core §11.5), an Oath, or a Ledger line — can be played **by proxy** at −1 die. You may fight, speak or swear *for* someone: the Codex prints exactly that (*"win an Oath duel as a champion for someone who cannot fight"*, Codex §11.4), and the Ash already speak for the dead (Codex §7.4). A party with no proxy is still in the room: **their Standing is what gets spent in their absence**, and they find out later.

## 4.11 A worked Convocation — market day in Sump Nine

*Sump Nine (Ch. 17) has a wreck in the sixth hab's channel: a Pathogen Scout's body, unharvested, three Beats old.*

**The room.** *Motion:* who takes the wreck. *Parties:* the **Ash crew** (Standing with the Cohort 7; Price: the body; Leverage: Precept 2) · the **toll-collector** (3; Price: 40 S; Leverage: the channel) · the **Registrar** (4; Leverage: the register) · the **Steward** (1; Price: feed for the nursery; Leverage: the nursery itself) · the **Cohort**. *Mood:* **Hungry**. *Table Clock:* 5.

**Round 1.** The Steward **Offers** the wreck to the nursery; the Ash reader contests it, Semantic, and wins by 5: one rung. The Steward is pushed from Refusal to Concession with the Ash — she will hear them now. The Ash reader also **Reads** the Cohort, who conceal and lose: she learns the body was the Cohort's kill, left in the open. Her faction's Clock on the Cohort advances.

**Round 2.** The Registrar **Reveals** a register line: the sixth hab claimed this channel's salvage at the last Rereading. One rung, no roll, and the toll-collector's toll is void. The toll-collector **Walks** — and everyone must say why he was right to.

**Round 3.** The Steward tries **Pressure** on the Ash reader and fails. The room shifts from Hungry toward Hostile; Sump Nine's Colony Clock advances. Her voice in the Assembly is now worth less than her temper.

**Round 4.** The Cohort could **Bind** the Motion as an Oath, but cannot pay for the wreck. Instead they **Reveal** their own evidence — a Ledger line the Ash wrote them last Tide, *"one harvest, freely given, witnessed by the Registrar"* — and offer the body to the Ash as that harvest. The Ash take it. The Table Clock fills with the Ash furthest up the ladder.

**Nothing in this scene was a persuasion roll.** Two Standings moved, one Clock ticked, one Ledger line was paid off and another written, and the Steward lost her authority in the room where she needed it most.

# 5 · CONCENTRATION

In a world made of water, **concentration is wealth**. A rich organism is dense: full of stored salt, sugar and structure, stable in any tonic, hard to swell and hard to shrink. A poor organism is dilute: thin, watery, at the mercy of the next tonic shift. The Expanse's class system is written in osmotic pressure.

## 5.1 The three conditions

| Condition | Called | Lives | Has | Lacks | Organ most often |
| :-- | :-- | :-- | :-- | :-- | :-- |
| **The Dry** | *dense*, *the salted*, *the Concentrate* | hypertonic pockets kept steady by pumps | stores, water rights, signal to burn (Ch. 4) | the habit of labour; patience with the Pump | the Vacuole, the Nucleolus |
| **The Settled** | *the even*, *isotonic folk* | the colony's middle strata | a trade, a Hearth, a little saved | protection from one bad Tide | the Membrane, the Hearth, the Lysosome |
| **The Swollen** | *the dilute*, *the watered* | low, wet districts by the outflows | numbers, solidarity, nothing to lose | stores, signal, standing with the Dry | the Pump |

**To be ruined is to be *diluted*.** To rise is to *concentrate*. The Expanse's proverbs say it plainly: *salt calls salt; water finds the floor.*

## 5.2 Condition at the table

* **Starting Substrate** (Ch. 16): Dry +60 S; Settled +0; Swollen −20 S, but +1 Cohesion at the first session.
* **Speaking across conditions.** In the **Affective** register (Core §11.1), speaking *down* the ladder (Dry to Swollen) gains +1 die on threats and −1 on comfort; speaking *up* gains +1 die on comfort and −1 on threats. Semantic speech ignores condition; contracts do not care who is wet.
* **Signal.** The Dry may *Breathe heavily* (§4.2) at half cost. The Swollen add +1 die to *Move the crowd* in their own district.

**What a condition costs.** Status in the Expanse is bought by the **Tide**, not the day — an Antigen Rite is 25 S for one Tide of being read as *self* (Core §11.6). Each condition has an upkeep per Tide on top of living costs (Ch. 9): **Swollen 0 S · Settled 10 S · Dry 40 S**. *(derived)* An organism that cannot pay at the Tide's turn slips one condition. Condition never buys combat protection — tonic immunity is gear (an Osmotic Filter graft, a Neutraliser, a Barophil's *Hardened*), and so is being unreadable (a Pheromone Mask, an Antigen Mimic).

## 5.3 The Tide turns the ladder

The Osmotic Tide (Codex §5.3) does not only move water; it moves *rank*. When the world's tonicity shifts, the condition that suits it rises.

| Tide phase | Who rises | Who suffers | What society does |
| :-- | :-- | :-- | :-- |
| **Isotonic Rise** | the Settled | nobody much | contracts, conjugations, the Contract Fair (Ch. 12) |
| **Hypertonic Zenith** | **the Dry** (dense bodies thrive) | **the Swollen** (water leaves them) | rationing, the Salt Vigil; the Dry seal their pockets; strikes are broken |
| **Isotonic Fall** | the Settled | the old and the indebted | the Rendering; debts called in before the swell |
| **Hypotonic Zenith** | **the Swollen** (water favours them) | **the Dry** (they bloat; their pumps strain) | the Swell: carnival, masks, riots, the Pump's season of power |

**At the table:** in a Zenith, the rising condition gains **+1 die on all social rolls in the colony** and the suffering condition loses one. In a Hypotonic Zenith, every Issue the Pump backs starts at **+1** on the track.

## 5.4 Moving between conditions

A condition is held up by **four supports**, all things the books already sell:

| Support | What it is | Where |
| :-- | :-- | :-- |
| **a Name** | a registered chemical identity | Codex §7.2; the Naming Rite, Core §11.6 |
| **a licence** | a Hegemony licence, or a guild's | Core §9.2; Codex §7.1 |
| **a hearth** | a Niche, a home, a place at a Hearth | Core §8.4 |
| **a seat** | an office (Ch. 3), or a charter (Ch. 15) | — |

* **Concentrating** (Swollen → Settled, Settled → Dry): hold **two supports** to become Settled, **three** to become Dry; pay the new condition's upkeep for a full Tide; and be received at a Hearth of that condition (a three-exchange scene, AT 12). The old Hearth will call you *salted* behind your back.
* **Diluting** happens when a support is taken — a licence seized (Codex §10.7), a Name suspended, a hearth evicted, a seat lost — and you fall below the count. It happens at the next Tide's turn, or at once if a sentence does it (Ch. 10). Nobody needs your permission to dilute you.

## 5.5 The Unlisted

Below the Swollen is no condition at all. **The Unlisted** are organisms no register can see: the Warden Circles' red line says *"the unregistered — Lysosans, the Ash, most anomalies — have none"* of a registered organism's rights (Codex §7.2), and the heresy of **Unlisted Rights** argues that they should (Codex §8.4).

* **They cannot be assessed.** No tally, no licence, no audit can reach them — which is why railroads, heists and heresies move through them.
* **They have no rights.** A Membrane may refuse them, a Warden may detain them, and their contracts bind no court.
* **They are a network, not a faction.** Archivist Dol erases entries for them (Codex §7.2); the **Body Sovereignty** heresy runs underground railroads for them (Codex §8.4); the Ash find them by refusing to harvest the unlisted dead (Codex §8.4).
* **Getting listed again** takes the **Rereading** (Ch. 12), a Nucleolus pardon, or a forged Name — which is its own Vector.

# 6 · THE LEDGER

Every organism in the Expanse is already owed, already owing, and already remembered. The books keep these debts in many places — Oaths (Core §11.4), Gene-Debt (Core §9.7), retainers who can quit (Bestiary, *Colonists*), the Tallow Market's tradable Oaths (Codex §6.2), the Ash's fee taken from your Harvest rolls (Codex §7.4), the Reclaimer's risen dead who *"owes you a debt the table should absolutely make painful"* (Atlas §3). **The Ledger is one page that holds them all.**

## 6.1 The page

Three columns on the Cohort sheet:

| Owed to you | You owe | Witnessed (you can prove it) |
| :-- | :-- | :-- |
| *Foreman Ock owes us one hard harvest* | *we owe the Brine Office 100 S at 5 S/Beat* | *the Registrar saw Ock promise* |

* **A line is one sentence: who, what, witnessed by whom.** If it needs a stat block, it is a Vector.
* **How many lines:** the first two columns together may hold **Swollen 3 · Settled 5 · Dry 7** lines — your condition is how much obligation the world will let you carry. Never more than **9**, whatever else is true.
* **An unwitnessed line is a rumour, not a line** (Ch. 11). It can still be true. It cannot be called in.

## 6.2 What you can do with a line

| Action | Cost | Effect |
| :-- | :-- | :-- |
| **Open a line** | free if witnessed; an Oath box if it binds you | the line exists |
| **Call it in** | one scene's attempt | one **Concession**-rung result (Core §11.2), one retainer's assist, or one pass through a gate. Once per line |
| **Refuse a called line** | — | the printed Oath-break cost if it was sworn: 2 Lysis Countdown boxes, the witnessing faction drops a rung, one roll on Legacy Scarring (Core §11.4) — otherwise, the witness's faction drops one rung |
| **Pay it off** | the stated price, or a service | clear the line, and **either** clear 1 Stress **or** gain +1 Cohesion *(derived)*. A paid **Gene-Debt**, the heaviest line there is, gives both (Codex §8.2) |
| **Transfer it** | a Rite (Core §11.6) at a Hearth, or the Tallow Market | the obligation binds someone else (Codex §6.2). They did not consent. The Ash call it a theft of grief |
| **Forge it** | **Deceptive**: DFF + Concealment vs the reader's OSM + Chemoreception | a line that never happened. If it is found, the faction that finds it advances its Clock on you |
| **Inherit it** | a Total Lysis | a dead organism's lines pass to its budline (Ch. 7), a charter, or whoever holds the receipt. The Ash do this paperwork, and charge for it |

**Reveal** in a Convocation (Ch. 4) spends a **witnessed** line as evidence.

## 6.3 Ledger or Oath?

An **Oath** is a line you have folded into your own chromatin, with boxes and printed costs (Core §11.4). The **Ledger** is the world's memory of it — and of everything else you owe that is not written into your body. Every Oath is a Ledger line. Most Ledger lines are not Oaths.

# 7 · KIN AND MUTUALISM

Organisms do not have parents the way the old songs pretend. They **divide**. One body becomes two, and both remember being one. Family in the Expanse is the record of those divisions — and the debts, grudges and Oaths that divided with them. And between families, organisms live by **mutualism**: the small, priced, chemical favours that let a body survive among other bodies.

## 7.1 Budlines

A **budline** is every organism descended from one founding division. Budlines are the Expanse's families, houses and clans at once.

* **The scent-root.** Every member carries the founder's chemical signature, faint but readable (OSM + Chemoreception vs AT 8). A budline cannot be hidden from a register; it can only be *forged*.
* **The count.** Names number the divisions. *Siss-of-the-Nine-Smells* (Atlas §1) is the ninth smell of the Siss root: the ninth division to be named.
* **Inheritance.** When an organism divides, both halves keep its Oaths (each half needs only one Oath box per Oath, but both are bound), its Standing with factions, and its debts. **A budline's debt divides with it, and never gets smaller.**
* **Elders.** The oldest living member of a budline is its **root-elder**. A root-elder may call any member to the budline's Hearth once per Tide; refusing costs that member 1 Stress and a rung with whoever the elder knows.

| d6 | Your budline is known for… | Starting effect (Ch. 16) |
| --: | :-- | :-- |
| 1 | an old debt to the Hegemony | owe 100 S at 5 S/Beat; +1 die bargaining with the Brine Office (they know you will pay) |
| 2 | a famous Oath, still unbroken | +1 Cohesion the first time you keep any Oath in a session |
| 3 | an Unlisting, two Great Cycles ago | Wardens test you twice; +1 die with the Sequence Underground |
| 4 | founding this colony | +1 die on Quorum actions at your home colony; enemies in the Nucleolus |
| 5 | a feud with another budline | name it; its members start Wary; your kin start Client |
| 6 | nothing at all | free of debts and grudges, and nobody will vouch for you |

## 7.2 Names

Every organism has three names, used in three registers (Core §11.1):

| Name | Register | Example | Who may use it |
| :-- | :-- | :-- | :-- |
| **Chemical name** | Semantic, exact | the full scent-signature, impossible to pronounce | registers, assayers, Bonds |
| **Called name** | Affective | *Siss*, *Dol*, *Vesk* | anyone |
| **Root name** | Ritual | *of the Nine Smells*, *of Shift Nine* | kin, Hearth rites, Oaths |

A **Naming Rite** (Core §11.6) spreads your chemical name; the Root name is given at the budline Hearth when an organism first divides or first swears. To **call an organism by its root name uninvited** is either intimacy or threat, and the Affective register treats it as a Compliance-rung push (Core §11.2) in either direction.

## 7.3 Conjugation

Two organisms may **conjugate**: link their membranes and exchange sequence. It is the Expanse's most serious promise between two beings — part marriage, part contract, part shared inheritance. It is chemical and legal in tone, never erotic; the table decides how much of it is shown (Codex §1.3).

**The Rite of Conjugation:** 1 Fugue Interval at a Hearth, one witness from each budline, 20 S of rendered Substrate each.

* The two become a **Bond** (Core §11.5) at no Oath-box cost beyond the Bond's own.
* Each gives the other **one plasmid** (§7.4) permanently — it does not fade.
* Their budlines become **kin**: each starts at Client with the other.
* **Dissolving** a conjugation breaks the Bond (Core §11.5) *and* drops both budlines to Wary with each other until the next Rereading (Ch. 12).

Conjugation across lineages is lawful everywhere but blessed only in some places; it is a classic Quorum Issue (§4.7, roll 10).

## 7.4 Plasmids

A **plasmid** is a small, self-contained sequence that can be passed from one organism to another: a knack, a recipe, a trick of digestion, the shape of a song. Giving plasmids is how the Expanse shares culture — horizontal inheritance, older than any school.

**A plasmid** grants **+1 die** to one narrow task you name with the Director (*reading currents in the Rim*, *digesting Lipid*, *haggling in Knot Nine*, *calming Terrified organisms*). It never stacks with another plasmid on the same roll.

* **Carrying:** an organism carries up to **AFF Grade** plasmids.
* **Giving:** 1 Fugue Interval at a Hearth together. A received plasmid lasts **one Osmotic Tide**, then fades — unless the receiver **integrates** it (1 Synthesis Choice or 50 S and a Dormant Cycle), making it permanent.
* **Courtesy:** giving a plasmid shifts the receiver's mood one rung warmer (Core §11.3) and counts as a scene of service toward Standing (Codex §7). **Refusing** one, offered at a Hearth, shifts them one rung colder.
* **Theft:** taking a plasmid without consent is a Precept 6 violation (Codex §8.2). The victim may invoke **Argument**.

## 7.5 Division, age, and succession

An organism divides when it is old, wealthy enough, or desperate. A **division** is a downtime event (1 Beat, half the organism's Substrate) and a story beat: the new organism is a person, with its own will, carrying half of everything — and an NPC the player may hand to the Director or keep as a Legacy (Core Ch. XII).

**Succession** follows the root-elder: when one goes Dormant for good, the budline's Hearth reaches Quorum (Ch. 4, Q 4) on the next elder. Disputed successions are a reliable Vector.

**Childhood has a clock.** A newly divided organism is a **dependent** for **1d6 Drift Seasons**: it rolls with −1 die to every pool, cannot swear Oaths, and sits on a parent's Ledger as a line that cannot be transferred. When it comes of age, it may be **named** at its budline's Hearth (§7.2).

# 8 · LINEAGES IN SOCIETY

The Atlas tells you what each lineage *is* (Atlas §2). This chapter tells you how they **regard each other**, what **pressures** their bodies put on their lives, and where they **live** in a colony. None of it is fair. All of it is common knowledge.

## 8.1 The web of regard

Read across: how the row lineage tends to see the column lineage. **+** warm (+1 die on the first social roll of a scene), **0** neutral, **−** cold (−1 die).

| sees → | Amoeban | Epithalian | Flagellan | Lysosan | Immunan | Barophil |
| :-- | :-: | :-: | :-: | :-: | :-: | :-: |
| **Amoeban** | + | 0 | + | + | − | 0 |

**Why, in one line each:**

* **Amoeban** — *useful, untrustworthy* (Atlas §2). They like the other outsiders (Flagellans, Lysosans) and resent the Immunans, who make them carry a **shape-registry token**.
* **Epithalian** — *civilisation*. Immunans trust them; Flagellans resent them for the toll contracts; they distrust Lysosans as an unreadable library.
* **Flagellan** — *freelancers and smugglers*. The Flow Guilds are Flagellan; the old grievance with Barophils is the deep currents the Barophils tax.
* **Lysosan** — *a legal minority with a very good lawyer*. Founded the Sequence Underground; starts a rung worse with Immunans (their **Registered** Frailty).
* **Immunan** — *the law*: feared, hired, quietly pitied. Trust walls (Epithalians); suspect what changes shape or hides sequence.
* **Barophil** — *geology with opinions*. Outlive everyone; the Deep Choir recruits among them; indifferent to most, tired of Flagellan wake in their trenches.

**These are tendencies, not laws.** A single kept Oath or shared Hearth beats them. A PC whose lineage starts cold has a scene to win, not a wall to accept.

## 8.2 Lineage pressures

Every lineage's Frailty (Atlas §2) is a social fact as well as a physical one. This is what each body does to a career.

## 8.3 Lineage quarters

In any Colony or Metropolis, each lineage keeps a **quarter** with its own Hearth. A quarter is where newcomers of that lineage go first, where its root-elders sit, and where outsiders are guests.

| Quarter | Smells of | Its Hearth | House rule | Taboo |
| :-- | :-- | :-- | :-- | :-- |
| **Amoeban** — *the Melt* | acid, warm broth | a shared digestion pool; meals are eaten *together, inside one membrane* | no fixed walls: rooms are rearranged each Beat | asking an Amoeban what its *real* shape is |
| **Epithalian** — *the Terraces* | resin, clean sheet | a long table built anew by each generation | every guest signs the wall | building without a signed plan |
| **Flagellan** — *the Wake* | brine and motion | a moving Hearth that circles the quarter all Beat | nobody sits still at a meal | anchoring a Flagellan's skiff without asking |
| **Lysosan** — *the Shelves* | paper-dry, many faint scents | a library-Hearth where sequences are read aloud | every guest leaves a sequence (a story, a plasmid) | asking a Lysosan what it is *carrying* |
| **Immunan** — *the Ranks* | cold metal, clean | a register-Hearth: each diner is named before eating | lying at table (they will know) | an unregistered guest |
| **Barophil** — *the Deep End* | pressure, minerals, heat | a slow-cooked meal that takes a whole Beat | silence until the meal is done | hurrying anyone |

## 8.4 Mixed company and mixed bodies

* **A Cohort of mixed lineages** is the norm on a Charter (Ch. 15), and the subject of jokes: *an Amoeban, an Immunan and a Lysosan walk into a register…*
* **Eating at another lineage's Hearth** (a Chemotactic Rite) clears that lineage's cold regard toward you for a Tide.
* **Conjugation across lineages** (Ch. 7) is lawful, frequently argued (§4.7), and the most common way the web of regard changes over Great Cycles.

**Multi-lineage organisms** follow the Atlas rule: a Phenotype outside your lineage costs your Gift its per-scene use, and shows; *"multi-lineage organisms are common in the Hegemony and suspicious everywhere else"* (Atlas §1). **The birth register, not the body, decides your legal lineage.** That makes forged births, unregistered buddings (Codex §11.3) and the Sequence Reading of an infant real crimes and real Vectors.

# 9 · THE UPHILL ECONOMY

Goods in the Expanse behave like molecules. **Downhill** — from where a thing is plentiful to where it is scarce — they drift on their own, slowly, for free. **Uphill** — against the concentration — they must be *carried*, and carrying costs energy. The whole economy is the fight over who pays for uphill.

## 9.1 Price arrows

Every good in every region has a **price arrow**:

| Arrow | Means | Buy price | Sell price |
| :-- | :-- | :-- | :-- |
| **▼ plentiful** | it is made or found here | ×0.5 | ×0.5 |
| **■ even** | it passes through | ×1 | ×0.8 |
| **▲ scarce** | it is wanted here | ×2 | ×1.5 |

| Good | Plentiful (▼) in | Scarce (▲) in |
| :-- | :-- | :-- |
| **Fresh water** (by the cyst) | the Great Flows, the Rim | the Wastes, the Foundry Belt |
| **Salt and minerals** | the Vacuolar Abyss, the Wastes | the Rim, the Mid-Strata |
| **Rendered Substrate** | the Lysosomal Wastes | the Ribbon Bridges, the Plateaus |
| **Lipid** (building sheet) | the Lipid Plateaus | the Foundry Belt, the Rim |
| **Licensed grafts** | the Foundry Belt | everywhere else |
| **Signal** (rendered pheromone, Ch. 4) | the Ribbon Bridges | any colony near Quorum |
| **News** (fresh chemical samples, Core §8.6) | Knot Nine | the Abyss, the Rim |
| **Specimens** | the Wastes, the Abyss | the Foundry Belt, the Nucleoid Approaches |

## 9.2 Drift cargo and carried cargo

* **Drift cargo** (passive): sealed cysts released into a current toward the scarce region. Free, slow (one **Drift Season** to arrive), and at the mercy of the Flow Guilds' tolls and the Tide. Prices equalise as drift cargo arrives; the first cyst sells at ▲, the tenth at ■.
* **Carried cargo** (active): the Cohort hauls it. Profit is the arrow difference. Hauling **uphill** (toward ▲) costs **1 extra Stress per 2 segments** of travel (Core §8.2) for each organism carrying more than half its Bulk, unless it pays **2 ATP per segment** instead — the labour of active transport.

## 9.3 The Pump: haulers and strikes

The **haulers of the Pump** are the colony's carrying organ and its largest working class. A hauler hauls uphill for a wage (§9.5) and keeps the Membrane's pumps turning.

**A strike** is the Pump withholding its labour. Each Beat of strike: the Vacuole marks 1 box (Ch. 2); every good in the colony moves one arrow toward ▲; and an Issue (*meet the Pump's demand*) goes on the air with the Pump's endorsement at +2 per round. Breaking a strike by force is a Pulse War against Colonist blocks (Bestiary) — and Colony Cohesion −2 whatever happens.

## 9.4 Channel-makers

Membranes are supposed to read everything that crosses. **Channel-makers** — smugglers — open illegal pores so goods leak through unread. It is passive transport, stolen.

* **Open a channel:** RTC + Synthesis vs the Membrane organ's AT (**Sound 16, Strained 12, Failing 8**). A channel carries 10 Bulk per Beat until found.
* **Finding channels:** the Membrane rolls OSM + Chemoreception each Pulse Ten (4 dice in a Settlement, 6 in a Colony) vs the channel-maker's original total.
* **Found channels** are a Precept 1 charge (Ch. 10) and a Flow Guild scandal, since half the channels in the Expanse are theirs.

## 9.5 Wages and the cost of a Beat

| Work, per Beat | Pay | Notes |
| :-- | --: | :-- |
| **A colonist's gross** (the baseline) | **5 S** | *(derived from Core §8.4's Clientele line)* — every other wage is read against this |
| Hauler (the Pump) | 4 S | +2 S hauling uphill; the Swollen's living |
| Gate-clerk (the Membrane) | 6 S | registers; bribes on the side |
| Hearth cook or healer | 5 S | plus meals |
| Retainer (Core §8.5; Bestiary *Colonists*) | 5–20 S | crowd hand 5 · militia 5–10 · a named authority 20 *(derived)*. They can quit |
| Quartermaster (the Vacuole) | 10 S | plus what goes missing |
| Assayer or Reader (the Lysosome) | 15 S | feared, well paid |
| Foundry hand (a Hegemony line) | 8 S | licence debt deducted |
| An office-holder (Ch. 3) | 20 S | if honest; much more if not |

**Living costs**, per Beat: Swollen 2 S, Settled 5 S, Dry 15 S. A Niche (Core §8.4) halves them, and its 5 S of upkeep includes the tally. Status upkeep is per Tide (Ch. 5).

**Read the wealth table first.** A Tier 1 Cohort earns 30–60 S per PC per level (Core §9.2). A colonist's whole Beat is worth 5 S. A Cohort is rich by the standards of the street long before it is rich by its own — and the street knows it.

## 9.6 Debt

Debt is the Expanse's second chemistry. The Hegemony lends at **5 S per Beat** (Codex §7.1); budlines lend to kin at nothing, and never forget; the Vacuole lends against water rights.

* **Default** for one Drift Season: the creditor may take you to the Assay (Ch. 10) or sell the debt to the Pump, which collects in labour (4 S a Beat).
* **Debts divide** with their debtor (Ch. 7) and are re-read at the Great Cycle's **Rereading** (Ch. 12), when some are forgiven and some are doubled.

## 9.7 The Hanging Market of Nine Tolls

*The Codex's market strung between four cables in the Mid-Strata, where everything is priced in Cohesion, Oaths and Specimens as well as Substrate (Codex §6.3).* It is the showcase for this chapter:

* **Four currencies at once.** Every stall posts four prices: Substrate, a Specimen grade, an Oath box, or Cohesion. A Cohort may pay any column. *(The Ledger, Ch. 6, records Oath and Cohesion payments.)*
* **Nine tolls.** Each cable-stair charges a toll in a different currency; a Cohort that pays all nine in coin is *Dry* there for a Beat, whatever it is elsewhere.
* **Debts for sale.** A Relic-bearer is selling a Gene-Debt they cannot pay (Codex §6.3) — and the Market's Convocations (Ch. 4) are the most dangerous rooms in the Mid-Strata.

# 10 · CARCASS LAW

Precept 4 says **tonicity is truth**: you may not lie about what you are, and in the Expanse a body can be *read*. Justice follows. An accused organism is not merely questioned; it is **assayed** — tested, sampled, read by the Lysosome — and the colony decides what the reading means. The law is called *carcass law* because, in the end, it is about **what is owed**, and the Expanse settles debts in bodies.

## 10.1 Crimes, by the Code

| Crime | Precept (Codex §8.2) | Prosecuted by | Typical sentence |
| :-- | :-- | :-- | :-- |
| **Crossing unread** — entering without registration | 1 Containment | the Membrane | fine 20–50 S, or Encystment 1 Beat |
| **Channel-making** (Ch. 9) | 1 Containment | the Membrane | Excision of the tool-graft; Unlisting if repeated |
| **Leaving the dead** — a body unharvested | 2 Non-Waste | the Lysosome | Indenture to the Lysosome, 1 Beat per body |
| **Hoarding a gift** — unrecorded synthesis | 3 Catalysis is debt | the Vacuole, the Hegemony | the gift's value as fine, doubled |
| **False scent** — lying about lineage, tonic or Oaths | 4 Tonicity is truth | the Nucleolus | Encystment 1 Pulse Ten; Dilution if in a Quorum |
| **Breaking the Air** — quenching a Quorum (Ch. 4) | 4 Tonicity is truth | the Nucleolus | Unlisting |
| **Cutting the Forest** — rewriting a region's rules | 5 The Code is written | the Warden Circles | Dilution, and the Curators take an interest |
| **Hijack** — altering a body without consent | 6 A body is its own argument | the victim, by **Argument** | Excision; Unlisting for repeat violators |
| **Plasmid theft** (Ch. 7) | 6 | the victim's budline | return it, plus Indenture |
| **Trading beyond** the outer membrane | 7 The Membrane is mercy | the Warden Circles | Unlisting — and Core Clock +1 regardless |
| **Oath-breaking** against the colony | (all) | the Hearth | Dilution; the Oath's own cost (Core §11.4) |
| **Violence against a yielding organism** | (custom, Core §4.14) | the Hearth | Encystment 1 Pulse Ten; witnesses mark Stress anyway |

## 10.2 The trial

A trial is a **Convocation** (Ch. 4) whose Motion is *what is owed*, and whose verdict the colony confirms by **Quorum**.

* **The room.** Accuser, accused, the Reader (or the colony's assayer), a Quorum Speaker, and up to two interested parties — a faction, a budline, a creditor.
* **The oath.** Each party stands and states lineage, tonic and Oaths (Precept 4). An organism that refuses starts one rung down with the room.
* **The Assay.** In the second round, the Reader assays the accused's body: RTC + Sequence Reading vs **AT 12** (**AT 16** for an Elite or a Lysosan). A success is a **Reveal** of the truth (Ch. 4) — the Director knows what it shows. The accused may resist (§10.3).
* **Argument** is open to both sides **on its printed terms and no others**: a sequence altered without consent; AFF vs the violator's RTC, AT 16; once per violator (Codex §8.2, §8.3).
* **Memory Vials** and live extraction carry their printed costs: AT 16, 2 Stress on every participant, and Oath boxes limit what can be pulled (Core §11.7).
* **The verdict.** When the Table Clock fills, the leading side's Motion goes to the colony as an **Issue** (Ch. 4) starting at the side's rung count. The colony *knows* the verdict when it reaches Quorum. A hung Issue means *Clear but watched*: −1 die on all rolls at the Membrane until the next Rereading.

## 10.3 Fooling the Assay

| Method | How | Risk |
| :-- | :-- | :-- |
| **Mask** | a Scent Dampener or Concealment: DFF + Concealment vs the assayer's total | if beaten by 4+, the mask itself is evidence: track 2 toward Guilty |
| **Latency** (Lysosan Gift, Atlas §2) | carry the incriminating chemistry away from the reading | once per scene; an Immunan assayer gets +2 dice to notice |
| **Bought assayer** | 50 S × Q, paid before the trial | the Speaker reads it (Ch. 4): Honesty roll; caught → Colony Cohesion −1, both of you to the Assay |
| **Second assay** | demand another assayer from a different faction | the trial pauses a Beat; the new assayer's faction now owns the verdict |
| **Argument** (Precept 6) | if your sequence was altered without consent, invoke Argument | AFF vs the violator's RTC at AT 16; success voids the evidence drawn from your body |

## 10.4 d12 — Sentences

| d12 | Sentence | Mechanically |
| --: | :-- | :-- |
| 1 | **Fine** | Substrate to the prosecuting organ; unpaid by the next Tide, it doubles and becomes a Ledger line |
| 2 | **Tally conscription** | your labour routed to a creditor or an organ for **3d6 Beats**: no income, 1 Stress per Pulse Ten |
| 3 | **Encystment** | sealed in a cyst under the Lysosome for Beats; afterward −1 Pathway rank of your choice until a Dormant Cycle |
| 4 | **Excision** | a graft surgically removed; the colony keeps it |
| 5 | **Chemical gag** | your emissions are read at −2: you cannot swear Oaths or witness, and you are believed at a disadvantage — the Expanse's civil death *(derived)* |
| 6 | **Register suspension** | no gate, no Hearth, no licence for a Tide (the printed shape: a Gene-Debt made *"a condition of entry"*, Codex §10.5) — you lose one support (Ch. 5) |
| 7 | **Dilution** | exiled into open water for a Tide: the region's tonic state applies to you in full (Core §7.2); you drop one condition; Wardens arrest on sight inside |
| 8 | **Box seizure** | a creditor holds one of your Oath boxes, and you cannot act against them until it is returned *(derived)* |
| 9 | **Forced Reading** | Sequence Reading AT 16; 2 Stress on every participant (Core §11.7) |

**The Apoptotic Oath** is not on the table. A condemned organism may *choose* to dissolve and be harvested, clearing its budline's debts and shame and beginning a Legacy (Core Ch. XII) with +1 Cohesion. Offer it to NPCs; let a player choose it only for their own character's story, and check the table's lines first (Codex §1.3).

## 10.5 After the verdict

* **The guilty's budline** drops one rung with the prosecuting organ.
* **The accuser** gains one rung with it if right, loses one if the Assay clears the accused.
* **A known false verdict** (bought, quenched, forged) is a time bomb: Colony Cohesion −1 now, and when it is exposed, the colony opens an Issue (*reopen the Assay*) at +2.

# 11 · THE SCENT-WEB

*"Chemotaxis is the Expanse's internet"* (Core §8.6). News is a molecule; to know something is to have smelled it; and a message is a thing someone carried.

## 11.1 Four networks

| Network | Run by | Speed | Can it be censored? | Price |
| :-- | :-- | :-- | :-- | :-- |
| **Currentpost** | the Flow Guilds | one region per Beat, on charted currents | yes — the Guilds price and block it | 10–50 S |
| **Bridge post** | the Hegemony, on the Ribbon Bridges (Codex §6.5) | reliable and registered — and **read** | routinely | 10–30 S, stamped |
| **Lipidpost** | the Reclaimers' Ash, by corpse-wagon | one region per Tide | **no** — censoring a funeral breaks Precept 2 | 5–20 S and a Specimen |
| **the Fade** | the Sequence Underground | hours, by decaying molecules; deniable | no — but every relay knows | a favour, always |

## 11.2 Rumour grades

Every rumour has a grade. A rumour's grade is how hard it is to *disprove*, on the AT ladder (Core §1.2).

| Grade | Lives for | AT to disprove |
| :-- | :-- | --: |
| **Fresh** | a Beat | 8 |
| **Repeated** | a Drift Season | 12 |
| **Settled** | until the next Great Cycle | 16 |
| **Law** | until the Rereading | **cannot be argued** — only erased: an archive entry destroyed, or a register line forged (Ch. 10), plus a segment on someone's Clock |

**Buying a rumour** uses the Core's rule: name what you want to know, pay 5–50 S in fresh samples, roll AFF + Signal Discourse at AT 10–20 (Core §8.6). **Forging one** is a Deceptive act (Core §11.1) *and* a Clock segment. A Settled rumour that turns out to be forged advances the Colony Clock: a colony that acted on a lie still acted.

## 11.3 A scent-perch

A Cohort can own a listening post: a **Scent-perch** — 250 S, 5 S a Beat upkeep, established as a colony asset (1 Tide, AT 16, Codex §5.7). It gives **one rumour per Drift Season** off the Codex's rumour table (Codex §11.1, truth rolled by the Director as usual), one extra Core §8.6 query per Beat, and a chance to **notice a planted scent** (OSM + Chemoreception vs the forger's total).

## 11.4 Signal relays

A message carried hand to hand (Ch. 7, *Signal Relay*) moves a region in hours, and every relay organism knows it. Each relay is a chance to leak: the Director rolls a d6 per hop, and on a 1 the message reaches someone it shouldn't.

## 11.5 Trail-script

Speech is emitted (Core Ch. XI). **Writing is laid down**: a trail of scent placed in sequence along a surface, read by following it. Trail-script lasts a Drift Season on lipid, a Tide on chitin, a Great Cycle on crystallised protein.

* **Reading** an ordinary trail: automatic. An old or damaged one: OSM + Chemoreception vs AT 8–16.
* **Forging** a trail (a contract, a register line): RTC + Sequence Reading vs AT 16; an assayer reading it tests against your total. A forged register line is Precept 4 (Ch. 10).
* **Signing** is laying your chemical name at the end. Counterfeiting a signature is the commonest crime in Knot Nine.

## 11.6 Archives

The Expanse remembers in protein. **Archives** are crystallised libraries kept by the Nucleolus (laws, registers) or the Lysosans (everything else — the Shelves, Ch. 8). The oldest library of all is the **Sequence Forest**, and Precept 5 forbids cutting it, which means nobody may edit the oldest record in the world.

**Research** in an archive: 1 Fugue Interval and RTC + Sequence Reading vs AT 12; Overflow buys a second fact (as Core §8.6). Archives charge 5–20 S, or a plasmid (Ch. 7).

## 12.4 The duel that replaces a war

The books print the pieces in five places: two organisms **duelling by Oath, formally, in the open** — *"killing either is a crime; interrupting is a scandal; watching is a legal act"* (Codex §5.5); an **arena** whose walls are registered (Codex §9.2); a Vector to *"win an Oath duel as a champion for someone who cannot fight"* (Codex §11.4); the Osmotic Brutes, who *"will often accept a challenge, a wager, or a formal Oath-duel instead of a fight"* (Bestiary, Order V); and the Hypertonic Zenith, when *"duels are shorter and meaner"* (Codex §5.3). This is the page that joins them.

1. **Name the Motion** the duel decides — a debt, a water right, a grazing claim, an insult. Both sides swear to it as an Oath (Core §11.4).
2. **Champions.** Each side names one. A champion may fight *for* another party, spending that party's Standing, not their own.
3. **Seconds.** Each side may name one second, who can **Reveal** evidence (Ch. 4) once, before blood.
4. **The bout** is a Pulse War between the two champions, in a registered arena or an open circle. It ends at the first **Yield**, Dormancy, or a ring-out.
5. **Stopping.** Either champion may **Yield** — *"a chemical white flag it cannot fake"* (Core §4.14). Whoever kills a yielder marks 2 Stress on every witness and drops Cohesion 1: killing a duellist in front of a crowd is expensive, and that is the world's own argument for stopping. The yielding side concedes the Motion and drops one rung with the champion's faction. *(derived)*
6. **Betting** is in Substrate and in Ledger lines.

A duel settles in one scene what a Mass Conflict (Codex §3.6) would settle in a Tide and a hundred dead. The colonies prefer it, and the Muster's widows prefer it most of all.

# 13 · HOST-CITIES

The largest organisms in the Expanse are not fought. They are **lived in**. A **Host-City** is a Vast organism (Core §4.5) that has accepted tenants: thousands of smaller organisms living in its body cavities, trading in its fluids, paying it for the privilege. Some are ancient, some are sick, all of them have moods.

## 13.1 What a Host-City is

* **A body with districts.** Its anatomy is its map. The *mouth* is the market; the *gut* is the foundry; the *vacuoles* are the stores; the *membrane* is the wall; the *nucleus* is untouchable.
* **A landlord with an immune system.** The host's immune cells are the city's police. They do not take bribes. They take *scent*.
* **A traveller.** Host-Cities migrate with the Tide (§13.4). A city's address changes every Drift Season.
* **Organs and Host at once.** A Host-City is a colony (Ch. 2) — six organs, a Quorum, a Colony Cohesion — living inside a creature that has its own **Temper** (§13.3).

## 13.2 Tenancy

* **Rent** is paid to the host in ATP: **1 ATP per tenant per Beat**, fed into the host at the Rent Wells. A Cohort pays as one tenant per member.
* **Tenancy marks.** A paid-up tenant carries the host's tolerance-scent. Without it, the immune cells treat you as *not-self*: a Pulse War against Immune Sentinel blocks (Bestiary) at the next checkpoint.
* **Unpaid rent** — the host's mood worsens (§13.3), and your scent fades: one Beat of grace, then you are not-self.

## 13.3 The Host Temper

Every Host-City has a **Temper Clock** of 6 segments. It advances when the tenants hurt the host and recedes when they tend it.

| Segments | Temper | The city |
| --: | :-- | :-- |
| 0–1 | **Content** | warm, slow currents; rent may be forgiven at festivals |
| 2 | **Restless** | districts shift; one route closes each Beat (roll a district) |
| 3 | **Feverish** | Thermal hazards in the gut district; immune patrols double |
| 4 | **Purging** | the host expels one district's tenants into open water |
| 5 | **Rejecting** | every tenant marks 1 Stress per Fugue Interval inside; the Nucleolus calls an Issue: *leave the host?* |
| 6 | **Lysing** | the host is dying or moving on. Whatever happens next is the end of a city |

**Advances (+1):** unpaid rent across a district; a Burst or Pulse War inside the body; mining the host's tissue; a Lysosome that stops removing waste. **Recedes (−1):** a festival held at the host's mouth (Ch. 12); a Cohort Vector that heals a wound or clears an infection; a Chemotactic Rite fed *to* the host (100 S of rendered Substrate).

## 13.4 Migration

Host-Cities follow the Tide (Codex §5.3): toward fresh water in the Hypertonic Zenith, away from bloom-swarms in the Hypotonic. At the start of each Drift Season, the Director moves each Host-City one region along the map (Codex §6) and notes the route. A Cohort that knows a city's route can **ride it** — free travel between regions, paid in rent.

## 13.5 Three Host-Cities

| Host-City | Body | Route | Temper now | Known for | Secret |
| :-- | :-- | :-- | --: | :-- | :-- |
| **Old Mother Brine** | a Vast saline amoeboid, slow, motherly | Great Flows → Mid-Strata → Rim → back | 1 | the best market in the Flows; Flow Guild headquarters live in her left vacuole | she is old enough to remember the Seeding — and she is starting to divide |
| **The Tessellate** | a lattice-bodied colony-organism, geometric, cold | Lipid Plateaus ↔ Foundry Belt | 2 | Hegemony licence courts; the cleanest registers in the Expanse | the Hegemony is quietly mining her structural lipid, and the Temper Clock is being hidden |
| **Kettle** | a heat-loving Barophil giant that never stops boiling | Vacuolar Abyss ↔ Lysosomal Wastes | 3 | Deep Choir pilgrims; thermal foundries; hard, honest tenants | Kettle is not one host but two, conjugated; they disagree about where to go |

# 14 · CULTURE

What do organisms *do* when they are not fighting, trading or being judged? They make things beautiful, they teach, they remember, they eat together, and they race.

## 14.1 Art

| Form | What it is | Who loves it |
| :-- | :-- | :-- |
| **Chromatophore murals** | living walls whose colours shift with the viewer's mood | Epithalians; the Dry |
| **Pheromone chords** | music you smell: three or more scents released in timed sequence | Flagellans; the Swell |
| **Drift choreography** | dance in moving water, read by the wake it leaves | the Rim; Flagellans |
| **Shape-theatre** | Amoeban actors becoming each character in turn | the Melt; the Swollen |
| **Long notes** | one sustained pressure-song, sung over a Beat | Barophils; the Deep Choir |
| **Register-poetry** | verse written as a list of names, read aloud at the Hearth | Immunans (they call it the only honest poetry) |

**A great work** is a downtime project (Codex §5.7): 1 Drift Season, an appropriate Pathway at AT 16. Success gives a **Naming Rite** (Core §11.6) for free in that colony.

## 14.2 Learning: Lodges and Induction

Pathways are taught in **Lodges**. A student is **Induced**; a master is the Lodge's **Source**. Each of the 24 Phenotypes (Atlas §3) keeps a Lodge in every Colony-sized settlement: a bunk, a meal, and gossip for members of the trade. A Lodge counts as a Hearth for its Phenotype.

* **Training** follows the Core's prices — Pathway ranks at 60, 150 and 400 S, with rank 3 and above needing a teacher or a P-grade Specimen (Core §9.9) — and the Codex's time and AT (Codex §5.7). What QUORUM adds is that **the teacher is a person**: a Lodge's Source teaches for a Ledger line (Ch. 6), usually one service a Tide.
* **Leaving a Lodge** badly is a line you owe it.

## 14.3 Memory strands

A **memory strand** is a Memory Vial (Core §11.7) kept as an heirloom — a budline's recorded moments, played back at the Hearth on feast nights. Strands are sacred and very valuable: 100–1,000 S to the right buyer, and stealing one is plasmid theft at budline scale (Ch. 10).

## 14.4 Food

Meals are chemical, not culinary (Core §8.1), which does not stop anyone from having opinions. A good Chemotactic meal (Core §11.6) is judged by **balance** (no one's tonic is upset), **memory** (it tastes of the budline's Hearth) and **honesty** (nothing in it is masked). Serving a masked meal is an insult; serving an *exquisite* masked meal at the Swell is an art form.

## 14.5 Play

* **Current-racing:** organisms ride a Nutrient Current through a marked course — DFF + Fluid Navigation, three checkpoints, Momentum allowed (Core §4.3). The Swell's great sport; bets run to thousands of Substrate.
* **Quorum games:** children play at reaching consensus — a game that teaches every organism to smell a cheat.
* **Shape-riddles:** an Amoeban becomes something; you guess it. Losing costs a plasmid.

# 15 · COHORT CHARTERS

Why does a band of four strange organisms get to wander between colonies, fight Orders, harvest the dead and break into vesicle complexes? Because someone **chartered** them.

Why does a band of four strange organisms get to wander between colonies, fight Orders, harvest the dead and break into vesicle complexes? Because someone **chartered** them.

Precept 1 forbids anything crossing a colony's membrane unread, and Precept 7 forbids crossing the outer one at all. Ordinary organisms stay inside. A **Cohort Charter** is a licence to work *outside the membrane*: in the open fluid, between colonies, in the places nobody registers. It is rare, it is powerful, and it comes with strings.

## 15.1 What a charter is

* **A consortium.** A chartered Cohort counts in law as **one organism made of several** — which is *why* it keeps a shared Cohesion track (Core §2.5). Its members may be registered as one, tried as one, and paid as one.
* **A sponsor.** Every charter is issued by a sponsor: a faction (Codex §7) or a colony's organ (Ch. 2). The sponsor vouches for the Cohort at every Membrane — and the Cohort answers to the sponsor.
* **A seal.** The charter is a scent-seal worn by every member. It lets them pass registered gates without an Antigen Rite (Core §11.6), once per Beat each.

## 15.2 Sponsors, and each one's rival

| Sponsor | The charter grants | It asks | It is revoked if | Its rival |
| :-- | :-- | :-- | :-- | :-- |
| **The Ribosome Hegemony** | licensed gear at −20%; Foundry Belt gates open | one licence-enforcement job per Tide | you print or carry unlicensed sequence | the **Sequence Underground** (unlicensed sequence) |
| **The Warden Circles** | the right to detain; registers answer your questions | every unregistered organism you meet, reported | you hide one | the **Sequence Underground** (the Unlisted) |
| **The Flow Guilds** | free passage on Guild currents; drift cargo at no toll | carry one Guild cargo per Drift Season, no questions | you dam, own or sell a current | the **Ribosome Hegemony** (tolls and owned currents) |
| **The Reclaimers' Ash** | harvest rights on any unclaimed body | every harvest rendered properly (Precept 2) | you leave the dead | the **Warden Circles** (who call the Ash unregistered) |
| **The Sequence Underground** | safe houses; a forged register line once per Tide | carry a sequence, now and then, and do not read it | you hand a Lysosan to a register | the **Warden Circles** |
| **The Deep Choir** | shelter in the Abyss and the Wastes; Barophil guides | sing at the Salt Vigil; carry a Choir pilgrim | you help keep the tonic "soft" | the **Flow Guilds** (who keep the currents soft) |
| **A colony organ** (any) | that colony's organ services at Obliging; a home Hearth; one support (Ch. 5) | the organ's Vectors come first | the organ goes Necrotic, or you break its Quorum | whichever organ it is fighting this Tide |

*The rival column is the Codex's "one rung down with its rival" (Codex §5.7), named once, here. It is drawn from each faction's own asks and red lines.*

## 15.3 Charter rank

| Rank | Earned by | Grants |
| :-- | :-- | :-- |
| **Provisional** | issuing (a Contract Fair, or a sponsor's Vector) | the seal; the sponsor's first-rung Standing |
| **Sealed** | three Vectors completed for the sponsor | the seal opens **any** registered gate once per Beat; the Cohort may sponsor retainers (Core §8.5) |
| **Named** | a Naming Rite (Core §11.6) for the Cohort as one organism | social pools +1 in the sponsor's territory; songs; enemies who know your chemical name |
| **Ancient** | the charter survives a Rereading (Ch. 12) | the Cohort may sponsor *other* Cohorts; one Nucleolus seat in the sponsor's home colony |

## 15.4 Losing a charter

A revoked Cohort is, from that moment, **a band of unregistered organisms outside the membrane**: Precept 1 violators at every gate, and fair game for Wardens. Revocation is a Vector, not an ending: find a new sponsor, clear the charge at the Assay (Ch. 10), or go to the Underground.

**When a colony defaults.** A colony can lose its *own* charter: *"the colony's charter is bought by a Guild"* (Codex §12.5). The buyer becomes a **sponsor of the whole colony** — every office now answers to it, and its rival's Standing with every colonist drops one rung.

## 15.5 Starting a campaign with a charter

Choose the Cohort's **sponsor** in the first session, together. It gives you:

1. **the opening Vector** (the sponsor's need);
2. **the first Standing rung** (Codex §7);
3. **the first conflict of loyalty** — the moment, a few sessions in, when the sponsor's interest and the Cohort's conscience part ways. Plan for it. It is the best scene you will run.

## 16.1 A Beat in a colony

| --: | :-- | :-- |

## 16.3 Rooting a character

The Atlas makes an organism (Atlas §1). These six choices put it **inside** the world. Add them after Step 6 (*Write three things down*). Each takes a minute.

| # | Choice | Options | Gives |
| --: | :-- | :-- | :-- |
| 1 | **Budline** | roll or choose (Ch. 7) | a root name, two relatives (one Wary, one Kin), and the budline's d6 effect |
| 2 | **Condition of birth** | Dry, Settled or Swollen (Ch. 5) | starting Substrate +60 / +0 / −20 (Swollen: +1 Cohesion at the first session) |
| 3 | **Organ of origin** | one of the six (Ch. 2) | +1 die on social rolls with that organ; its Hearth will feed you; one enemy in another organ |

Then choose the **Cohort's sponsor** together (Ch. 15).

**Worked example — Siss-of-the-Nine-Smells** (Atlas §1). *Budline:* a famous unbroken Oath (+1 Cohesion the first time Siss keeps any Oath in a session). *Condition:* Settled. *Organ of origin:* the Pump — Siss grew up running messages for haulers, and the Vacuole's quartermasters still remember the strike she carried word of. *Plasmid:* read a Speaker's honesty. *Owed:* the haulers of Hab Six owe her a hiding place. *Owes:* 40 S to a Flow Guild courier-mother, witnessed at the Contract Fair. *Clock:* the Brine Office has a sample of her — she carried the strike's word through their gate. *Sponsor (with the table):* the Flow Guilds.
