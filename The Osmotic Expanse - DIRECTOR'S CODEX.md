# 1 · THE DIRECTOR'S MANDATE

## 1.1 Four jobs, in order

1. **Be the medium.** Describe the fluid: what it tastes of, what it carries, what it costs to cross. Every scene has tonicity, viscosity, and flow. Write them on your card before you write the monster.
2. **Be honest about numbers.** Thresholds do not move to help or hurt. If a player has built a 7-dice pool, they have *earned* 99% at AT 16 — let them have it, and make the next thing 7 dice harder to see, not luckier to fail.
3. **Be a witness, not a referee of taste.** The Expanse is a game about bodies: consumption, assimilation, parasitism, breeding, decay, and the politics of who counts as "self". Your table will need to know where its membranes are.
4. **Ask what happens next, then who smells it.** Nothing in this world is secret for long. A fight is a chemical announcement.

## 1.2 The three tensions to play against

| Tension | How it shows up | What it gives you |
| :-- | :-- | :-- |
| **Containment vs. Exchange** | every benefit requires letting something in: grafts, Specimens, Oaths, symbiotes | a clean way to say *yes, and here is the price* |
| **Colony vs. Organism** | what is good for the settlement is often fatal to a person | politics without villains |
| **Registration vs. Freedom** | the Warden Circles keep the antigen registers; the Lysosans refuse them | a lawful/chaos axis that is actually about bodies |

## 1.3 Lines, veils, and the comfort of a membrane

Run a **session zero** with these four questions. They are not soft; they are what makes body horror playable for a whole group.

1. **What bodies are on the table?** Consent around: forced grafting, parasitism, reproductive/hijack content, ingestion of people, Total Lysis of named NPCs, and identity loss (Viral Mimic, Replicator).
2. **How graphic is a death?** Total Lysis can be clinical ("your contents join the world") or explicit. Agree on the register and stay in it.
3. **What is the tone dial?** The Expanse supports grim survival, baroque political intrigue, and absurd xenobiological comedy — but rarely all three at once.
4. **Who can pause, and how?** Use a single word at the table. When it is said, the fiction rewinds one sentence and the scene restarts adjusted. No explanation required, ever.

**A Director in the Expanse should never spring a body-horror beat on a player.** The engine already does it mechanically (Parasitic Grafts, Hijacked, Latency, Chimeric Growth). Make those beats *chosen*, and they become the best scenes of the campaign.

## 1.4 Engine Notes (why the numbers are the numbers)

The Core's resolution engine was tuned by direct enumeration and a 500-fight Monte Carlo per encounter budget (1d6 pools, 1s scoring zero, 2+PP strikes, reserved Reactive Osmosis, 35% burst usage). Every published threshold, MI curve, and Biomass budget in this book comes from that calibration. The short version:

* A **4-dice pool** (Grade 3 + rank 1, the Stage I specialist) clears **AT 12 68% of the time** and AT 16 30% of the time. That is the intended "competent but not certain" band.
* A Cohort of four at any tier, spending **Standard** budget, wins ~90% of fights with **0–1 Dormancies** across **3–6 Cycles**.
* **Deadly** budgets average **1.5–2.5 Dormancies** and an 83–95% party win rate. Deadly means *somebody's body gets harvested*. Announce it in-fiction.
* **Inhibitors are the true difficulty curve:** a 7-dice pool takes ≥1 Inhibitor **72%** of the time and ≥2 **33%** of the time. Stress is what kills experienced organisms, not misses.

# 2 · ADJUDICATING THE ENGINE

## 2.1 Choosing a Threshold

Ask one question: **who could do this, if they tried?**

| The answer | AT |
| :-- | --: |
| any adult organism with the pathway | 8 |
| a competent specialist | 12 |
| a colony's best | 16 |
| a Hegemony institution | 20 |
| something written into the Sacred Code | 24 |
| one organism per generation | 28 |

Then modify the **fiction**, not the number: give the attempt a tool (+1 die), a hostile tonic (−1 die), an ally (+1 die), or a consequence. **Never inflate an AT because the party is high Level.** High Level organisms clear higher ATs with *more dice*, which the game gives them honestly through Pathways, Baselines, and grafts.

## 2.2 When not to roll

* **No chance of failure, no cost.** Do it.
* **Unopposed, unhurried, consequence-free:** compare the **Homeostatic Baseline** (3 × dice) to the AT. No dice, no Stress, no argument.
* **Failure is more interesting than success:** roll.
* **The player is improvising something the rules do not cover:** pick an existing rule and stretch it. "That's a Burst at Tier II cost." "That's a Membrane Penetration attempt at AT 14." Say the number, say why, move on.

## 2.3 Failures that improve the game

Never "nothing happens". Pick one, in the moment:

| Type | Shape of it |
| :-- | :-- |
| **Cost** | it works, and the price is ATP, Stress, a broken Vial, noise, or an Oath box |
| **Turn** | it fails and the world moves: a wall closes, a current shifts, a scent marks them |
| **Complication** | it works for the wrong party: the acid eats the lock *and* the hinge; the signal reaches the wardens |
| **Reveal** | it fails, and the failure *teaches*: "the gate is not locked, it is registered. It is waiting for an antigen." |

**Never** punish a clever plan with a hidden requirement the player could not have known. If you did not write it down before the roll, it was not there.

## 2.5 Three useful rules of thumb

* **+1 die ≈ +15–20% success and +2 Overflow.** At Stage III, one extra die is worth more than +4 damage. Say this out loud to players who are min-maxing BD; it will change their builds.
* **Soak is exponential, MT is linear.** +2 MT is a shrug; +2 soak against the enemy's main damage type is a whole fight. When a player's build seems too strong, attack the type they are not armoured against — that is the Graft Matrix doing its job.
* **A creature's real defence is MI × (1 ÷ hit chance).** A high-MT foe with 20 MI is worth less than a low-MT foe with 60 MI and 4 soak against the party's favoured type. Use MI and soak to make encounters *read* differently, not just harder.

## 2.6 Reading Stress out loud

Stress is the Expanse's tension meter and it should be **spoken**, like the Countdown. When the table hears "Stress 6, ATP costs +1" they start playing differently: Venting, spending Cohesion, choosing not to Burst. That shift *is* the game's pacing. If a session has no Stress on the table, you have not been honest about Inhibitors.

# 3 · RUNNING A PULSE WAR

## 3.1 Before the dice: the scene card

Write four things when a fight might happen. They take twenty seconds and they change everything.

```
TONIC: isotonic | VISC: clear (1 PP/Drift) | FLOW: 2 toward the door
ZONES: spore bed (Contact band) · membrane wall (blocks Far line)
HAZARD: pore charge (Osmotic, 10 dmg, AT 14 to spot) at the second band
```

If you only remember one thing: **decide the terrain before initiative**, because terrain decided after initiative is a lie the players can hear.

## 3.2 The Cycle, run fast

1. **Detection Phase** — both sides roll (Core IV §4.1). Announce what each side learned. In the Expanse *both* sides usually learn something: the ambush rarely fully works, and that is the flavour.
2. **Frequency Check** — one roll per side if the enemies are simple: roll **once per Order-group**, not per creature. A pack of Lytic Swarmlings shares a Frequency.
3. **Act in order.** Ask each player for their **intent and cost** ("two PP Strike, then hold one") *before* their turn comes. Then resolve in twenty seconds: pool, dice, Threshold, Overflow, damage, conditions.
4. **Track out loud.** Say "Stress 3", "Countdown four boxes", "you have 2 PP and 6 ATP". The players will start playing the economy instead of the dice, and the fight gets better.

**Shortcuts that never break the game:**

* **Batch the small things.** A 6-segment swarm is one token, one Frequency, up to three Strikes (§Core 4.13).
* **Average damage** for mobs: take mean Overflow from the odds table (§2.4) instead of rolling. For a 5-dice pool at MT 14 that is **4.5 + BD**.
* **Players roll everything that matters.** Attacks against them: they roll defence? No — keep the Core's MT model: *you* roll their attack, but **let them roll their own saves, Harvest checks, Stress Vents, and Overdrive decisions**. Player hands touching dice = player attention.
* **Telegraph, always.** A Bestiary creature's Special is a *described* action. If a Wraith is preparing a Hijack, say the Wraith's lattice is unfolding. Reactive Osmosis exists because the fiction warned them.

## 3.3 Running the seven Orders (how each feels at the table)

| Order | Its plan | How you run it | What breaks it |
| :-- | :-- | :-- | :-- |
| **Lytic Swarms** | eat everything, always hungry | Segments; never target-prioritise; the *zone* is the attack | area Bursts, Desiccation zones, fire/Thermal |
| **Protein Sentinels** | hold the line, punish entering | brace, cover, Mark, build | Breachers, spatial tricks, Navigator shear |
| **Nutrient Drifters** | feed, flee, return later | disengage constantly; harvest *you* if you fall | chase rules, currents, area denial |
| **Pathogenic Wraiths** | rewrite, hijack, possess | steal Reserves, turn minions, use your own features | Sequester (Phagocyte), Genetic-immune grafts, Hijacker pre-empts |
| **Osmotic Brutes** | move you into a problem | push, throw, slam into walls and hazards | Anchors, Immovables, braces, Gel terrain |
| **Immune Sentinels** | recognise, register, purge | Mark, cascade, coordinate, retreat to report | Lysosan masks, forged antigen tokens, Misdirection |
| **Anomalies** | be wrong; make you wrong | change one rule per scene, never the same one twice | preparation, Denial of the rule, Desiccation, walking away |

## 3.4 Hazards act too

A hazard is a participant with a trigger and a Cycle clock. Put a mark on your sheet for anything with a timer and **resolve it at the end of the Cycle, after everyone**, so players can react to it. Hazards are also how you make an "unwinnable" fight survivable: the swarm falls into the cavitation snap, the Brute throws a Sentinel into the enzyme mine, and the Cohort learns that terrain is an ally.

## 3.5 Ending the fight, and what it announces

A Pulse War ends when one side stops being able to catalyse. When it ends, ask three questions:

1. **What is harvestable?** Say the yield out loud (§Bestiary yields column). Loot in the Expanse is *decided* by the players, not by you.
2. **What did the fight announce?** Every Pulse War is a chemical shout: who smelled it, and what will they do about it? Put a clock on it (Codex §5.2).
3. **What is the Cohort's state?** MI, Stress, Countdown boxes, ATP, and Specimen Bulk. If they are at Stress 7 and 3 boxes, the next scene should not be a fight — it should be a *choice* about rest. That is the pacing tool nobody uses enough.

**Never restart a fight the players already won** because the roll went badly. If a Total Lysis happens, let it happen (Atlas XII): the Legacy system exists for exactly this moment.

## 3.6 Mass Conflict (colonies, swarms, and wars)

When the unit is too big for a Cycle, run it in **Tides** (one Tide = one Fugue Interval) with a **line** and a **Biomass count**.

```
LINE: a strip of 6 sectors (bands). Each side fields Biomass (BP) in sectors.
TIDE resolution, per contested sector:
  roll d6 + commander's AFF + Pathway(Symbiosis) + sector terrain modifier
  winner's Overflow removes that many BP from the loser's sector
  if a sector's BP hits 0, the line shifts one sector toward the loser
ROUT: the side whose BP falls below 25% of start, or whose line collapses on 3 sectors
CASUALTIES: every 1 BP lost = ~10 organisms dead (or 1 Elite, or 1/4 Catastrophe)
```

**Players intervene** by taking a sector personally: their Cohort counts as **BP 4** and they fight a normal Pulse War inside that Tide (win = 8 BP removed, lose = the sector falls). This is how a Level 8 Cohort can decide a colony war without becoming an army: they break the *one sector* that matters.

The Warden Circles, the Flow Guilds, and the Deep Choir all field **BP 40–200** in a regional war. A Lytic Swarm bloom fields **BP 300+** and does not rout — which is why blooms are solved with terrain, tonicity, and sacrifice, not with swords.

# 4 · ENCOUNTER ARCHITECTURE

## 4.1 Biomass Points (BSP)

Every Bestiary block carries a BSP cost. The budget is per **Cohort of four**:

| Creature type | BSP | What it is |
| :-- | --: | :-- |
| **Swarmling** | 1 | 4–8 segments, MI ≈ 6 + 4·Tier |
| **Standard** | 2 | a normal adult of the Order, MI ≈ 14 + 8·Tier |
| **Elite** | 4 | a specialist/captain, MI ≈ 28 + 16·Tier |
| **Catastrophe** | 10 | a phase-bearing boss, MI ≈ 70 + 40·Tier |

| Level band | Easy | Standard | Hard | Deadly |
| :-- | --: | --: | --: | --: |
| **1–4** (Tier 1) | 5 | 7 | 10 | 13 |
| **5–8** (Tier 2) | 7 | 9 | 12 | 15 |
| **9–12** (Tier 3) | 8 | 10 | 12 | 16 |
| **13–16** (Tier 4) | 9 | 11 | 14 | 17 |
| **17–20** (Tier 5) | 10 | 12 | 14 | 16 |

Adjust for Cohort size: **3 organisms × 0.75**, **5 organisms × 1.2**, **6+ organisms × 1.4**.

## 4.2 What the simulations showed

500 Monte Carlo fights per budget, four PCs, standard play (2-PP Strikes, one reserved Reactive Osmosis, Bursts ~35% of Cycles). Numbers are **average Cycles** and **average Dormancies per fight**.

| Tier | Encounter | Cycles | Dormancies | Read it as |
| :-- | :-- | --: | --: | :-- |
| 1 | 3 Standard (6 BSP) | 2.8 | 0.01 | Standard, feels easy |
| 1 | 6 Swarmlings (6) | 2.8 | 0.0 | Easy: swarmlings die to area |
| 1 | 2 Elite (8) | 4.6 | 0.4 | Hard |
| 1 | 5 Standard (10) | 5.1 | 0.4 | Hard, long |
| 1 | 1 Catastrophe (10) | 3.5 | 0.03 | Standard — the *phases* are the danger |
| 2 | 6 Standard (12) | 4.5 | 0.5 | Hard |
| 2 | 3 Elite (12) | 5.0 | 0.8 | Hard→Deadly |
| 3 | 6 Standard (12) | 3.0 | 0.3 | Standard |
| 3 | 3 Elite (12) | 3.0 | 0.6 | Hard |
| 4 | 7 Standard (14) | 3.1 | 0.5 | Hard |
| 4 | 4 Elite (16) | 3.7 | 1.1 | Deadly |
| 5 | 14 Swarmlings (14) | 5.8 | 1.9 | Deadly |
| 5 | 8 Standard (16) | 4.5 | 2.0 (83% win) | Deadly, three organisms will lose bodies |

**Three lessons.** (1) A fight is **3–6 Cycles**: short in cycles, dense in fiction — do not stretch it. (2) **Elites are more dangerous than numbers** until Tier 5, where swarms become lethal because the Cohort's MI has not kept pace with area damage. (3) **Catastrophes at their own tier are survivable but their phases are not**: budget 10 BSP for the boss and then *add* 4–8 BSP of adds, and warn the players in-fiction.

## 4.3 Build an encounter in five moves

1. **Pick the Order** (Bestiary VII). That gives you tactics, terrain appetite, and harvest.
2. **Pick the theatre.** Tonic + viscosity + flow, two zone traits, one hazard (§3.1). For a Hard or Deadly fight, the terrain should *favour the Order*, not the Cohort.
3. **Spend the budget.** Use §4.1. Prefer **2 Elites + 4 Standard** over 6 Elite: mixed pools make mixed Frequencies, and Frequencies are where fights are won.
4. **Add one twist.** Roll or choose:
   - the enemy has **prepared** (a Fold, a chart, a Vector seeded, an antigen register on the Cohort);
   - the objective is **not survival** (escort, harvest, sabotage, escape a Tide, keep a specimen alive);
   - a **third party** is present (another Order, a colony patrol, a rival Cohort);
   - the terrain is **changing** (a Bloom surge, a current shift, a collapsing bridge, a pump starting).
5. **Set the yield** before you run it: Codon (Bestiary yields), Substrate, one **Specimen that enables the next plan**, and one **consequence** (who heard, what broke, what is now owed).

## 4.4 Catastrophes: designing a boss

A Catastrophe is not a bigger block. It is **three blocks wearing one name.**

| Element | Rule |
| :-- | :-- |
| **Phases** | 2–3 phases, each with its own MI, and each phase **announced** by the fiction ("its lattice slides open; the second body unfolds from inside"). End a phase at 0 MI; do not let a boss go Dormant mid-phase. |
| **Phase gaps** | between phases, give the Cohort one free Reconstitution-like beat: 2 Cycles where the boss rebuilds. They will use it to reposition, Vent, and plant Vectors. That beat is the fight's rhythm. |
| **Lair action** | once per Cycle, after everyone: the *place* acts (a current surges, a pump fires, the wall closes). Always telegraphed the Cycle before. |
| **Reserve denial** | bosses should threaten, but never permanently deny, the Cohort's Reactive Osmosis — that is the rule that makes a boss feel unfair rather than dangerous |
| **A way to end it early** | every boss has one exploitable print (a harvested organ, a named Oath, a tonic switch). Players who find it should be able to skip a phase. Reward reading. |
| **Codon and harvest** | a Catastrophe pays Codon at ×3 and 4 Harvest rolls. Give the Pristine Sequence a **name** and a use: the Cohort should be able to buy the thing that beats the *next* boss with it. |

## 4.5 When the encounter is not a fight

Roughly one session in three, the threat should be something else. The same budget rules apply:

| Kind | Budget equivalent | Play |
| :-- | :-- | :-- |
| **Hazard crossing** | Easy–Standard | terrain ATs, Stress, one or two forced contests |
| **Harvest dispute** | Standard | social + a claim to defend; Immune Sentinels may show up |
| **Infiltration** | Standard | Detection Phase without a fight; antigen registers; Oaths |
| **Chase** | Standard | 6 Cycles, band shifts, terrain choices |
| **Outbreak** | Hard | Pathogen vectors, a colony dying, time pressure |
| **Contract** | Easy | an Oath, a debt, a faction rung — and the Codex's Clocks start |

## 4.6 A worked build (Tier 2, Hard, 12 BSP)

**The Pump Chamber of Anchor's Reach.** Three **Protease Wardens** (Elite, 4 BSP each — Immune Sentinels) plus four **Antigen Gnats** (Swarmling) in a pumping hall.

```
TONIC isotonic | VISC clear | FLOW 3 toward the outflow gate (a real hazard: swept = 1 band + 6 Osmotic)
ZONES: antigen register arch (Wardens see Marked through it; it flags Lysosan organisms automatically)
HAZARD: cavitation snap (Kinetic 8, AT 12 to sense, fires when the pump cycles: Cycle 2, 5, 8)
TWIST: the pump is scheduled. If the Cohort does not jam it (Synthesis AT 14, 2 PP, 20 S of Substrate),
       Cycle 8 flushes the whole chamber: 3 band shift and 14 Osmotic damage to everyone left standing.
YIELD: Codon 56 (3 Protease Wardens at 14 each + 4 Antigen Gnats at 3.5), plus 3 Wardens × (2 Greater
       Organs, 1 Pristine Sequence carrying *Antigen Register* — exactly the Specimen a Viral Mimic
       needs for Stage III Hive Mask)
CONSEQUENCE: the Warden Circles now have a registered chemical sample of the Cohort (a Clock starts)
```

Run it in four minutes: read the tonic and the arch aloud, roll one Frequency for the Wardens and one for the Gnats, and let the pump be the third combatant.

# 5 · THE OSMOTIC TIDE

The Expanse is not a place that stays. Its water moves, its salt rises and falls, and its seasons are chemical. This chapter is the machine that makes the world change **without you deciding it should**.

## 5.1 Calendars

| Unit | Length | Felt as |
| :-- | :-- | :-- |
| **Pulse Cycle** | 6 s | combat |
| **Fugue Interval** | ~1 h | a rest, a scene, a job |
| **Metabolic Beat** | ~20 lit hours + 4 dim | a day; sleep is a Dormant Cycle |
| **Drift Season** | ~30 Beats | currents reroute; tolls rewrite; contracts expire |
| **Osmotic Tide** | ~4 Drift Seasons | the world's tonicity swings. Everything downstream of this chapter happens on a Tide. |
| **Great Cycle** | 16 Tides (~1,900 Beats) | a generation. Registers are purged. Old debts are re-read. |

## 5.2 Clocks: the only world-state you need to track

A **Clock** is a track of 4–6 segments with a promise attached. Advance it when the fiction says something is progressing; resolve it when it fills. Write the segments on index cards and put them where the players can see them: **a visible clock is a threat the players can plan against, and planning is what they want.**

Three clocks per campaign, always:

| Clock | Segments | Advances when | Fills → |
| :-- | --: | :-- | :-- |
| **COLONY CLOCK** | 6 | the Cohort's actions disturb a settlement: violence in a market, unlicensed synthesis, a corpse left unharvested, a broken Oath | the colony acts: wardens arrive, tolls rise, a contract is voided, a mob forms |
| **ORDER CLOCK** | 5 | the Cohort harms, harvests, or imitates an Order of the Bestiary | that Order responds: swarming, register-hunt, mimicry of *the Cohort*, or (rarely) an offer |
| **CORE CLOCK** | 4 | touching Nucleoid material, using a Relic's Rule Break, Apotheotic Choices, Deep Naming | the Nucleoid notices: Curators, sequence storms, a region's rules soften |

**Rules for clocks.** (1) Advance at most one segment per session per clock. (2) Advance it *in front of the players* and name it: "the Warden Circles have a sample of you. That's a segment." (3) Never advance a clock because the players failed a roll — advance it because they **succeeded at something loud**. (4) Players may spend time, Cohesion, or Specimens to *rewind* a segment. Let them. That is a whole category of play.

## 5.3 The Osmotic Tide, phase by phase

Each Tide phase lasts one Drift Season. The whole four-phase cycle is a Tide. **Every region's tonicity moves with the Tide** — which means a plan that worked in the Hypertonic season can be suicidal in the Hypotonic one.

| Phase | Regions trend | Mechanical truth | What people do |
| :-- | :-- | :-- | :-- |
| **ISOTONIC RISE** | neutral → slightly hypertonic | travel is cheap; healing works normally; PP are full | trade season; contracts signed; wars start (everyone can afford it) |
| **HYPERTONIC ZENITH** | all regions hypertonic | DFF pools −2, PP −1, Drift costs +1, healing halved, VSC +2 | defensive posture; duels are shorter and meaner; the Deep Choir is strongest; colonies ration water |
| **ISOTONIC FALL** | neutral → slightly hypotonic | recovery; regeneration effects strengthen; Harvest yields +1 grade | mourning and harvesting season; expeditions launch |
| **HYPOTONIC ZENITH** | all regions hypotonic | DFF +2, PP +1, **MT −2**, Turgid on heavy hits, Dormant organisms gain +1 box/Cycle | raids and swarming; blooms explode; guards fight worse; the Flow Guilds run everything |

**Run the Tide as a season of consequences, not a penalty.** If the Hypertonic zenith makes the players slower, it also makes *everything else* slower — which is why the Deep Choir picks that season to walk, and why the Hegemony schedules its foundry overhauls then.

## 5.4 Blooms and Recessions

A **Bloom** is an uncontrolled reproductive surge: biomass everywhere, everything regenerating, everything hungry. A **Recession** is the opposite: nothing grows, nothing heals, dead things stay dead.

Roll 1d6 at the start of each Drift Season, or whenever the Cohort does something that involves biomass (a Total Dissolution, a PANDORA, a Foundry, a Salt-the-Earth):

| d6 | Season event |
| --: | :-- |
| 1 | **Recession.** Regeneration and Segments stop. Harvest grades drop by one. A colony's food stores fail: the Colony Clock advances 2. |
| 2 | **Thin Bloom.** Nutrient Drifters swell; travel encounters +50%. Healing effects gain +2. |
| 3 | **Great Flow.** Currents strengthen by 1 region-wide for a Season. The Flow Guilds' prices triple. |
| 4 | **Antigen Panic.** A Warden register finds something. Immune Sentinels sweep the region for a Season; Lysosan PCs are in real trouble. |
| 5 | **Deep Bloom.** A swarm Order's population doubles in one region. A BP 200+ bloom begins marching somewhere. |
| 6 | **AE Surge.** A radiant region opens somewhere (Runaway Catalysis softening). Every Catalyst in the Expanse hears about it within a Beat. Anomalies appear (roll on the Anomaly table, Bestiary VII). |

## 5.7 Downtime between Vectors

Give the Cohort **two Fugue Intervals per Beats-worth of downtime** and resolve their projects with a single contest each (AT per the ladder), plus the cost.

| Project | Time | AT | Outcome |
| :-- | :-- | --: | :-- |
| Craft a Rare+ item | 2 intervals | 16 | item; Overflow improves quality or cuts cost |
| Learn a Burst / Sequence Reading | 1–2 | 12–16 | feature; failure costs Stress and a Specimen |
| Establish a colony asset | 1 Tide | 16 + cost | asset online; Colony Clock gains a segment (you are now a thing to be taxed) |
| Heal a scar / lost rank | 1 Tide | 16 (Severe scars: 20+) | healed; failure means the scar has *opinions* now |
| Build reputation with a faction | 2 intervals + rite | 12–20 | one rung up; one rung down with its rival |
| Investigate a Clock | 1–3 intervals | 14–18 | rewind one segment, or learn what it counts to |
| Hunt a specific creature | 1 Tide | its tier × 4 + 8 | a confrontation on your terms; the Codex's Regions chapter gives you its lair |

**Always end downtime by asking what changed.** Downtime that does not change the world is a shopping list, and shopping lists are how campaigns stall.

# 6 · REGIONS OF THE GREAT VESICLE

Nine playable regions. Each is written to be run in one session or to hold a whole campaign arc. **All tonicity values are the Isotonic-Fall baseline**; apply the Tide shift from §5.3.

Travel between regions is measured in **Beats at Flow 2** with a loaded Cohort. Distances are political as much as physical: a toll arch can turn three Beats into nine.

```
Rim ──3── Mid-Strata ──4── Foundry Belt
 │            │  ╲4
 │4           │3   ╲ Ribbon Bridges ──5── Nucleoid Approaches ──2── Core
 └──4 Plateaus┘
      │4              Mid-Strata ──5── Great Flows ──6── Abyss
      └──5 Wastes ──3──┘                     ╲
                          Wastes ──7── Abyss  ╲──(pump line, 2)── Foundry Belt
```

## 6.1 THE PERI-MEMBRANE RIM

*The outer wall of everything. A curved plain of membrane overhead and to every side, scarred by old breaches, bristling with toll arches. The only place in the Expanse where you can see the boundary of the world, and the only place the Warden Circles truly govern.*

**Medium:** isotonic · clear · Flow 1 (but a constant *inward pull*: Fluid Navigation AT 12 or −1 Drift). Viscosity 2 PP/Drift beyond the third arch.
**Who:** Warden Circles (registers, arch-gates), Flow Guilds (export), Reclaimers' Ash (gravediggers at the breach-scars), Lytic Swarmlings (Order: drawn to old breach sites).
**Services:** antigen registration (free, costs a Clock if you have enemies), toll bonds, skiff hire, Warden inns (safe, expensive, watched).

| Site | What it is | Hook |
| :-- | :-- | :-- |
| **The Nine Arches** | nine toll gates in a row, each run by a different Warden household | someone is forging arch-tokens; the Cohort's sample is already in three registers they never visited |
| **The Scar of Anchor's Fall** | a 400-Drift healed breach, its edges folded into a shantytown | a body was found *outside the membrane*, half-digested, carrying a sealed vial of something nobody can name |
| **The Listening Wall** | a stretch of membrane where Barophil pilgrims press their shells and report what they hear | whatever is on the other side has started answering in a language three of them now speak |

**Local Clock (Order):** the Rim's registers hold a sample of the Cohort. Every time they use a gate, advance. At 5: a Warden *Curator-escort* is assigned to them permanently.
**Harvest:** breach-scar tissue (Fibrous grafts), membrane scrap (Lipid), old registered dead (payable to the Wardens — a legal harvest, which means a *taxed* one).
**Threat:** Lytic Swarmling blooms gather at old scars. A Rim bloom is a Mass Conflict (§3.6) with the Warden Circles fielding BP 120.

## 6.2 THE LIPID PLATEAUS

*A desert of fat: vast pale plateaus of congealed lipid, slick and heatless, split by dark seams of free oil. Barophil herds walk them. The air of the world here is thick and sweet, and everything that dies does so slowly.*

**Medium:** hypotonic · **viscous (3 PP/Drift)** · Flow 0 except in the oil seams (Flow 4, and they burn).
**Who:** Barophil herders and their colonies, the Deep Choir (pilgrimage roads), Epithalian ranch-farms on the plateau edges.
**Services:** Testate and Mucoid graft work, heat-sink gear, Herd Chorus training, water at obscene prices.

| Site | What it is | Hook |
| :-- | :-- | :-- |
| **Tallow Market** | a floating bazaar on a plateau raft, 400 organisms, no weapons, no registers | an Oath market: you can buy *someone else's* Oath obligation, and it binds. Someone wants the Cohort to hold a specific debt. |
| **The Seam of Nine Fires** | an oil seam that has been lit for eleven Beats | the Deep Choir believes it is the world's wound; a Reclaimer crew believes it is a forge; the fire is spreading toward a colony of 3,000 |
| **The Long Grazing** | Barophil herd-territory, 90 Drifts wide | a herder collective hires the Cohort to find what is eating calves from inside — a **Parasitic** infestation, not a predator |

**Local Clock (Colony):** each time the Cohort burns lipid or starts a Thermal catastrophe, the Plateaus advance. At 4: the herders expel them from all grazing roads.
**Harvest:** lipid slabs (Lipid family, cheap), Thermal organs, Testate shells (the only place to buy Full Testate Carapaces at Common price).
**Threat:** the Plateaus are where **Thermal Vent Fields** and Barophil catastrophes nest. Do not let a Diver use Gradient here without a discussion.

## 6.3 THE CYTOPLASMIC MID-STRATA

*The heartland. Endless suspended settlement: colony-habs the size of hills, tethered by grown cables, moving with the current. Most organisms alive are here, and most organisms alive have never seen the membrane.*

**Medium:** isotonic · clear · Flow 2 (the Mid Drift, predictable, charted).
**Who:** everybody. Flow Guilds, Warden Circles (weak), Reclaimers' Ash (strong), Sequence Underground (hidden), six hundred named colonies.
**Services:** everything at standard price, and every Synthesis service in Core §9.9.

| Site | What it is | Hook |
| :-- | :-- | :-- |
| **Second Flow** | a mid-sized colony (14,000) run by a water-tally council; the default home for a campaign Cohort | its water supply is failing (Recession? Sabotage? a Desiccation specialist with a grudge?) and the council's three answers are all crimes |
| **The Ash Rows** | the Reclaimers' Ash quarter: licensed corpse-processing rows where the dead are read for their last hours | a dead organism in the row is *still reading people back*, and it knows things about the Cohort's lineage |
| **The Hanging Market of Nine Tolls** | a market strung between four cables where everything is priced in Cohesion, Oaths, and Specimens as well as Substrate | a relic-bearer is selling a Gene-Debt they cannot pay, and the buyer is not a person |

**Local Clock (Colony):** Second Flow's clock advances when the Cohort breaks water-law, violence in a hab, unlicensed synthesis.
**Harvest:** nothing wild here except pests; the harvest is **the dead**, and the dead are property.
**Threat:** infestations (Pathogenic Wraiths riding the cables), and the quiet horror of a hab that has been *replaced* one organism at a time.

## 6.4 THE GREAT FLOWS (NUTRIENT CURRENTS)

*Rivers inside the world: channels of fast, nutrient-thick fluid that carry everything from skiff-caravans to swarm invasions. To be Flagellan here is to be free; to be anything else is to be cargo.*

**Medium:** isotonic (hypertonic in the deep channels) · clear · **Flow 3–5**, never still, and each channel has a *season* when it reverses.
**Who:** Flow Guilds (tolls, charts, muscle), Flagellan colonies on the banks, Nutrient Drifters (Order), and every smuggler alive.
**Services:** charts (§Core 8.2), skiffs, cargo space, hired current, information — the Flows are where rumours are made.

| Site | What it is | Hook |
| :-- | :-- | :-- |
| **The Toll Reach** | 60 Drifts where three channels merge and the Guilds tax everything | a toll barge has been hijacked by people wearing the Guild's own chemistry (Viral Mimic work); the Guild wants the Cohort to be deniable |
| **The Backward Season** | a channel that reverses for 11 Beats every Great Cycle | something is travelling *against* the reversal, upstream, and it is not a ship |
| **Nursery Deep** | a Drifter nursery field, legally protected, morally impossible | a colony is starving; the field is the only food. Do the Cohort protect it, harvest it, or price it? |

**Local Clock (Order):** the Flow Guilds remember whoever breaks a channel. At 4: the Cohort is *untollable* — no Guild service anywhere, no passage, no legal sale of anything they carry.
**Harvest:** Drifter tissue (Nutrient pastes, ATP-rich), Thermal vents along the deep channels, and **salt** (Desiccation gold).
**Threat:** a BP 300 Lytic Swarm bloom riding a channel is the classic Mid-Strata apocalypse: Mass Conflict with the Cohort holding one sector.

## 6.5 THE RIBBON BRIDGES

*Protein highways spun across thousands of Drifts by things that have been building for longer than any colony has existed. Under them: the long drop and the drift. On them: toll-hams, caravans, and the Hegemony's entire postal system.*

**Medium:** isotonic · clear · Flow 1 alongside, **0 on the ribbon surface** (adhesion only; DFF checks to stay on when the bridge vibrates).
**Who:** Protein Sentinels (Order, the *builders*, not the Hegemony), Epithalian maintenance clans, Hegemony couriers.
**Services:** structural synthesis, Fold work, Bridge law (a whole jurisdiction: on a bridge, an Oath is enforced by the bridge).

| Site | What it is | Hook |
| :-- | :-- | :-- |
| **The Unfinished Span** | a bridge that stops in mid-water, 4,000 Drifts short. Nobody knows who stopped it | a Protein Sentinel work-crew has resumed building, and it is building *toward something new* that just appeared in the water |
| **Knot Nine** | a junction where seven ribbons cross: a city, a market, a courtroom | a Warden register at Knot Nine has recorded a Cohort member as **two different organisms**. The law says one of them must be lysed. |
| **The Singing Span** | a ribbon that hums at a frequency which causes Stress 1 per Fugue Interval, permanently | a Deep Choir shrine, a Hegemony listening post, and a Reclaimer crew all want the same bar of that song |

**Local Clock (Order):** damage to a ribbon is damage to a *being*. The Sentinels will repair, then respond. At 3: they stop repairing the Cohort's roads.
**Harvest:** ribbon offcuts (Fibrous grafts, Protein Lattice gear), Bridge Echo specimens for Smiths.
**Threat:** shear zones, and the fact that everyone on a bridge is standing on something that can *decide to move*.

## 6.6 THE FOUNDRY BELT (RIBOSOME HEGEMONY)

*Ten thousand foundry-organs fused into a working wall of synthesis, each one printing apparatus, grafts, and licence-stamped sequences at industrial scale. The Hegemony does not conquer regions; it makes regions need its catalogue.*

**Medium:** hypertonic (waste brine) · **turbid** · Flow 2 (pump-driven, and the pumps stop at shift-change).
**Who:** the Ribosome Hegemony (Amino Smiths, Constructors, Engineers), Immune Sentinels (their guards), contract labour from every lineage.
**Services:** the whole Core §9 gear catalogue at list price; Synthesis discounts (+2 dice); **licences** (the real currency here).

| Site | What it is | Hook |
| :-- | :-- | :-- |
| **Shiftless Row** | a dead shift, 400 foundry organs silent for 9 Beats; production resumed on the next shift and nobody was disciplined | what was printed in the silence is in the Cohort's hands already, and it is *unlisted*: no Gene-Debt, no serial chemistry, no registration |
| **The Brine Market** | where the Hegemony sells its waste and buys back scrap | a Reclaimer crew is buying something the Hegemony says does not exist: intact **Pristine Sequences** in bulk, from a supplier inside the Belt |
| **Licence Court** | where disputes over apparatus patents are settled by chemical audit | someone has patented a *lineage trait*. The Cohort is asked to prove it was prior art, or to burn the patent holder's foundry |

**Local Clock (Order):** the Hegemony's enforcement is **economic**, not martial. At 3: gear the Cohort owns begins to fail (their Apparatus loses 1 BD; their grafts develop a Minor Cost the Hegemony can "fix" for a debt). At 5: a Warden *and* Hegemony joint register-hunt.
**Harvest:** foundry organs are worth more alive; the Belt buys Constructs and Construct parts at 2×.
**Threat:** a Constructor runaway (an unlicensed Foundry making its own line), and the classic **Peptide Constructor revolt** scenario.

## 6.7 THE LYSOSOMAL WASTES

*A region that digests. Acid-lit, enzyme-thick, full of the partially dissolved remains of everything the Expanse has decided to stop being. The only place a Reclaimer can become rich or terrible.*

**Medium:** **acidic / hypertonic** · viscous (2 PP/Drift, 3 in the deep) · Flow 3 (drainage channels), Turbid.
**Who:** Reclaimers' Ash (pilgrim crews), Amoeban clans, Lytic Swarmlings (Order, and they are *winning* here).
**Services:** legal harvesting, corpse-brokerage, acid-proof gear, Neutralisers at 1 S (a fact that will save your Cohort once per arc).

| Site | What it is | Hook |
| :-- | :-- | :-- |
| **The Vat Road** | a drainage channel full of half-dissolved cargo wrecks, worked by hundreds of Reclaimers | a wreck is *still broadcasting its Oaths*, and the Oaths belong to someone currently in high office |
| **Ash of the Second Choir** | a mass grave of Deep Choir pilgrims, half-dissolved, not dead | they were digested *by the region* and survived; they want the Cohort to carry a word out |
| **The Undissolvable** | a single organism that has been in the Wastes for nine Great Cycles, being digested forever | it is *choosing* to stay, it remembers the Era of Closing, and it wants a specific person's help to finally stop |

**Local Clock (Order):** every unlicensed Total Dissolution advances it. At 4: the Wastes themselves begin to *target* the Cohort (hazard AT +4, blooms spawn in their camps).
**Harvest:** the best in the Expanse — 2 extra Harvest rolls, and Pristine Sequences are common here. Also the most illegal.
**Threat:** **Catastrophes** (Bestiary VII) rise here during HYPOTONIC ZENITH; a Waste bloom is a Mass Conflict nobody wins without sacrificing a region.

## 6.8 THE VACUOLAR ABYSS

*Bounded sacs of stored world: pressure, cold, and long memory. Down there, the fluid is older than the colonies and the pressure is a law that no Oath can appeal.*

**Medium:** isotonic core, **hypertonic layers** (stratified) · cold · viscous (3+) · Flow 1, with sudden **pressure drops** (Kinetic 12, AT 16 to brace).
**Who:** Barophil divers and their Deep Houses, Warden Circles (storage vaults — the Expanse's banks), Sequence Underground smugglers.
**Services:** vault rental (the only safe place for a Relic), pressure gear, Deep Tonic Vials, silence.

| Site | What it is | Hook |
| :-- | :-- | :-- |
| **Vault Nine** | a Warden storage sac holding the Expanse's chemical memory, including the last samples of extinct lineages | a heist, and the moral problem that the sample being stolen is a *person's* grandfather, still chemically alive |
| **The Deep House of Nine Doors** | a Barophil colony inside a pressure gradient, one door per generation | the Cohort is invited as witnesses to a Door rite, and one of the doors opens onto something that is not storage |
| **The Cold Field** | a region of absolute stillness where nothing decays | dead things down there are perfect, and someone is harvesting them to make organisms that cannot be harmed by Lytic damage at all |

**Local Clock (Core):** opening a sealed old sac advances it. At 3: something in the Abyss starts counting the Cohort's actions.
**Harvest:** pressure organs, Deep Sample Vials (250 S each), and old dead with intact Harvest traits (Legendary grade possible).
**Threat:** pressure, cold, and the Abyss dwellers (Bestiary VII: Anomalies and Osmotic Brutes of the deep).

## 6.9 THE NUCLEOID CORE AND ITS APPROACHES

*The world's written centre. Approaches: a maze of dense sequence-forest, forbidden to most, guarded by things with no faces. Core: where the Sacred Code is kept and where the Prime Serum's last vials are rumoured to be.*

**Medium:** hypertonic (approach) → **hypercharged** (core) · viscous (2) · Flow 0 (the Core does not move; everything else moves around it).
**Who:** Nucleoid Curators (unplayable, uncaring, absolute), the Sequence Underground, Deep Choir pilgrims, every regime that wants to rewrite the Code.
**Services:** nothing legal. Black-market: Deep Naming (1,200 S, 1 P), sequence theft, Curator rumour, and the only place to buy **AE** (Core §9.3).

| Site | What it is | Hook |
| :-- | :-- | :-- |
| **The Sequence Forest** | dense protein-trees encoding the region's rules; cut one and the region's physics changes | someone is *planting* new rules: a grove where Lytic damage heals, a grove where Oaths physically bind. The Underground wants the Cohort to plant one. |
| **The Quiet Gate** | the last checkpoint before the Core, guarded by Curators who do not speak | it has begun admitting organisms. It has begun *returning* them changed. Nobody can name what changed. |
| **The Ninth Archive** | an Underground cell holding the Era of Closing's true record | the record says the Prime Serum was used, and who used it, and the Deep Choir and the Hegemony will both lise the Cohort for carrying it |

**Local Clock (Core):** every approach advances it, always. At 4: Curators take an interest; at 5: **Attention** marks (Core XII Apotheosis), which is how a Level 15 Cohort becomes a campaign-ending force or a dead one.
**Harvest:** forbidden. Nucleoid-grade material makes Legendary grafts and is a crime against the Code by possession.
**Threat:** the Core's own defences, and the fact that the deepest truth in the Expanse is stored in a place that does not care who reads it.

# 7 · FACTIONS AND FACES

Six powers run the Expanse. None of them are villains, which is why all of them are dangerous. Each entry gives you: what they say they want, what they actually want, what they will sell you, what they will ask, what they will **lyse you for**, and three faces.

**Standing ladder.** Standing is Core §8.7's 0–10 track and its four bands (Hostile 0–1 · Wary 2–5 · Client 6–8 · Kin 9–10). The Codex names six rungs inside those bands: *Hostile* (0–1) · *Wary* (2–3) · *Neutral* (4–5) · *Obliging* (6–7) · *Confided* (8) · *Bound* (9–10). Each rung up costs a Vector's worth of service and one scene where you chose the faction over your own comfort. Each rung down costs one sentence.

---

## 7.1 THE RIBOSOME HEGEMONY

**Says:** *we make the Expanse livable. Catalogue, licence, feed.*
**Actually:** *we make the Expanse depend on us, and dependency is the only lawful power there is.*

The Hegemony is an industrial organism, not a government: ten thousand foundry-organs, a patent chemistry, and the only legal print-shop for grafts above Uncommon. It has never conquered a region. It has only ever made a region unable to equip itself.

**Sells:** everything in Core §9 at list price; Synthesis discounts (+2 dice); **licences** (the real currency); loans at 5 S/Beat; bulk Specimen purchase.
**Asks:** registration of your gear and grafts; patent testimony; foundry labour (a Beat of your RTC for their line); suppression of unlicensed printing.
**Will lise you for:** printing an unlisted sequence, or proving that a patented trait is *prior art owned by a lineage*.
**Red line:** they never break a contract in writing. They will reinterpret it for a Tide.

**Internal splits.** *Catalogue Loyalists* (print everything, licence everything) vs *Line Purists* (refuse to print lineage traits; they consider the Smith tradition heretical) vs *the Brine Office* (waste logistics, and quietly the most powerful desk in the Belt because it decides which foundry gets feedstock).

| Face | Role | Block | Wants now | Secret |
| :-- | :-- | :-- | :-- | :-- |
| **Overseer Kel-Vasht of Shift Nine** | foundry overseer, 40 Beats old | Immunan Complement Commander, Level 12 (Elite block) | the Cohort to testify that a Smith's tune is prior art, so the patent dies | its own lineage trait was bought, not inherited; it carries the receipt |
| **Ninth Vellum** | patent-auditor, chemically polite | Immunan Antigen Detector, Level 9 (Standard) | to audit the Cohort's gear; will pay 250 S for full cooperation | the Brine Office pays it to lose specific audits |
| **Foundry Mother "Hush"** | a Peptide Constructor who has been printing for 11 Great Cycles | Epithalian Peptide Constructor, Level 15 (Elite + Construct swarm) | to be asked to print something nobody has licensed; it is *bored of legality* | it has been printing one unlisted organism, slowly, for nine Beats, and it is nearly finished |

## 7.2 THE WARDEN CIRCLES

**Says:** *nothing enters a colony that has not been read.*
**Actually:** *the register is the colony. To be unlisted is to be outside the world's legal body.*

A federation of gate-houses, each independent, connected by shared **antigen registers** — a chemical common law. They are the only faction with a monopoly on lawful violence, and they use it mostly on paperwork.

**Sells:** registration (and its enormous benefits: contract enforcement, safe passage through arches, legal harvest rights); Warden escort; archive access; a **Name** (a chemical identity, which is worth more than gold in the Rim).
**Asks:** samples, toll bonds, cooperation against unregistered organisms, and the surrender of anything Lysosan you are carrying.
**Will lise you for:** masking an antigen, forging an arch-token, or harvesting without a licence in a protected nursery.
**Red line:** a registered organism has rights. This is real and it is also the trap: the unregistered — Lysosans, the Ash, most anomalies — have none.

**Internal splits.** *Nine Arches* (hereditary gate households, aristocratic, paranoid) vs *Circuit Wardens* (mobile, pragmatic, hire adventurers) vs *the Register Reformists* (want lineage traits de-registered; hated by everyone above them).

| Face | Role | Block | Wants now | Secret |
| :-- | :-- | :-- | :-- | :-- |
| **Porter-Second Yune of Arch Four** | gate commander | Immunan Phagocyte Guard, Level 8 (Elite) | the Cohort to identify a forged arch-token bearer without killing them | it has been carrying an unregistered sibling in Engulfment for six Beats |
| **Archivist Dol** | register keeper at Knot Nine | Immunan Antigen Detector, Level 6 (Standard) | a chemical sample from a Lysosan PC, *voluntarily* | it has been quietly erasing entries for the Sequence Underground since its lineage was de-registered |
| **The Third Vigil** | a Warden hunting-cell of four | Immunan squad (Archer ×2, Guard, Commander), Level 10 | to bring in a Viral Mimic that wore a Warden for a Tide and killed no one | one of the Vigil *wants* the Mimic to succeed |

## 7.3 THE FLOW GUILDS

**Says:** *we keep the currents charted and the cargo moving.*
**Actually:** *we own the charts, and whoever owns the charts owns the war.*

Fifty-odd guilds under a shifting **Tide Compact**: mutually recognised tolls, shared charts, one rule no guild breaks — *never block a channel that carries water.*

**Sells:** passage, charts, skiffs, hired currents, cargo, and **rumour** (the Rumour Economy, Core VIII).

## 7.7 How to voice a chemistry

Give each lineage one physical tic and keep it. The Expanse's people do not gesture; they **emit**.

| Lineage | Speech tic | First impression (roll d6 or choose) |
| :-- | :-- | :-- |
| **Amoeban** | mid-sentence they *taste* a word: they repeat your last word back, altered | curious, invasive, already holding something of yours |
| **Epithalian** | slow, structured, sentences with load-bearing clauses; they build a wall before the point | solid, slow, faintly architectural; you feel inspected for gaps |
| **Flagellan** | fast, half-finished, they orient while talking (facing away, drifting) | impatient, warm, already three Drifts ahead of you |
| **Lysosan** | indirect, quoting someone else's words; they answer a question with a similar question | evasive, funny, and the only one in the room who notices the door |

**For factions, add a second tic:** Hegemony speakers cite clause numbers; Wardens use the word *listed*; Guilds price things out loud; Ash say "unwasted"; Underground never use names; Choir answer in second person plural.

# 8 · THE SACRED CODE AND ITS HERESIES

## 8.1 The Three Eras

Every faction, every grudge, and every law in the Expanse comes from one of these. Teach them as **folk chronology**, not as a lecture: any old organism knows them, and each tells them differently.

| Era | Folk length | What happened | What it left behind |
| :-- | :-- | :-- | :-- |
| **THE MAKING** | the first ~24 million Beats | warm mindless chemistry learned to *keep*; the first enclosed volumes; the ancestral lineages diverged; the Catalyst Choir built the physical scaffolding: membranes, ribbons, pumps, the Sequence Forest | the **laws of physics as literal architecture**; the lineages' Frailties (which the faithful call *honesty*); the ribbons, which are still building |
| **THE SEEDING** | ~15 million Beats, ending ~17,000 Beats ago | a **drop of Prime Serum** — repeated, in some accounts, many times — made novelty cheap: bursts, grafts, speciation explosions; the first colonies; the first harvest wars | the six lineages' Gifts, the Burst domains, and the **Serum vials** (rumoured: nine remain); the Hegemony's founding claim that it can *print* what the Serum did |
| **THE CLOSING** | the last ~9 Great Cycles | the Serum stopped. Something closed the door: the Nucleoid's approaches sealed, the Curators appeared, and the Sacred Code was **issued as enforceable law** | the Warden registers, the Curators, the Gene-Debt, and every religion's central wound: *why did it stop?* |

**The Era of Closing is the campaign's engine.** Something ended novelty, and the entire political order is built on that ending. Whoever can explain *why* the Serum stopped can rewrite the Expanse.

## 8.2 The Sacred Code: seven precepts, each with a mechanic

The Code is not a metaphor to the faithful, and it is not entirely a metaphor to you either: each precept has a mechanical edge, because the Nucleoid *can* read a body.

| # | Precept | What it forbids or commands | Mechanical edge |
| --: | :-- | :-- | :-- |
| 1 | **Containment** | nothing crosses a colony's membrane unread | registers grant +2 dice to find an unregistered organism; a registered organism gains +1 Cohesion per scene it is lawful in |
| 2 | **Non-Waste** | a body must be harvested or buried in a licensed ground | leaving a body unharvested advances the Colony Clock; a harvested body pays Codon to *whoever holds the licence* (usually not the killer) |
| 3 | **Catalysis is debt** | a gift of synthesis must be recorded | the Gene-Debt on Relics; a Cohort that pays a debt clears 1 Stress and +1 Cohesion (Core §9.7) |
| 4 | **Tonicity is truth** | you may not lie about what you are: state your lineage, your tonic, your Oaths | an organism that voluntarily states all three gains +1 die on its next contest that Beat; refusal costs 1 Cohesion among the faithful |
| 5 | **The Code is written** | do not rewrite a region's rules; do not cut the Sequence Forest | Deep Naming and Rule Break (Relics) advance the Core Clock; Curators act at 4 |
| 6 | **A body is its own argument** | do not hijack, do not rewrite a body without consent | the Underground's weaponised version: an organism whose sequence was altered without consent may invoke **Argument** — a free contested contest (AFF vs violator's RTC) at AT 16 against any claim the violator makes anywhere |
| 7 | **The Membrane is mercy** | do not breach the outer membrane; do not trade with what is beyond it | the only precept the Deep Choir openly denies; breaching it opens a Core Clock at 3 and never closes it |

**Play the Code as law, not as scenery.** Wardens enforce 1, 2, and 5. The Ash enforce 2 and 3. The Underground enforce 6. The Choir ignore 7 and think 5 is a lie.

## 8.3 Homeostasis and Entropy: the two poles

Every religion in the Expanse is a position on one question: *is the world trying to stay the same, or trying to end?*

**HOMEOSTATISTS** (Warden Circles, most colonies, the Hegemony's public face) hold that the world's good is *stability*: correct tonicity, honest registers, harvested dead, no novelty. Rites are maintenance rites. Their priesthood is administrative and their heresy-hunt is a *scheduling problem*.
**Play them:** calm, reasonable, and capable of any atrocity to keep a region's tonicity nominal. Their best line: *"We are not cruel. We are hydrated."*

**ENTROPISTS** (Deep Choir, the Unwasted Congregation, most Lysosan hermits) hold that the world is *already ending* and that the only dignity is in choosing how. Rites are acceleration rites: pilgrimages into lethal regions, voluntary digestion, naming the dying.
**Play them:** frighteningly kind. They never threaten. They *accompany*. Their best line: *"You will be reused. We would like you to be remembered first."*

**Rites** (Core XI lists six; these are the Director's variants with costs):

| Rite | Who | Effect | Cost |
| :-- | :-- | :-- | :-- |
| **Naming of the Unwasted** | Ash | a Total-Lysed organism's Harvest trait becomes usable by one PC (graft, one Scene per Long rest) | 1 Oath box; Ash standing +1 rung |
| **Tonic Blessing** | Homeostasist | for one Tide, the recipient treats one hostile tonic as neutral | a rite, 1 hour, +1 Cohesion to the performing colony |
| **The Bare Walk** | Choir | for one Tide, the recipient treats one thermal or pressure hazard as neutral and gains +2 dice on Pressure Tolerance | must be witnessed; mark 1 Stress |
| **Argument** | anyone | invoke Precept 6 as a legal weapon | see above; can only be invoked once per violator |
| **Deep Naming** | Underground/Nucleoid | permanently rewrite one of your own features (swap a Phenotype Stage feature, or take a Chimeric Growth for free) | 1,200 S + 1 P; Core Clock +1; roll on the Legacy Scarring table |
| **The Second Drop** (heresy) | Choir heretics | at a Nucleoid-grade site: force a Runaway Catalysis on any one roll, any time, once per Tide | Core Clock +2; every Curator in the region knows; one PC loses 1 Codon Level permanently |

## 8.5 THE LONG ARGUMENT — a campaign spine (Levels 1→20)

Three acts, one question: **why did the novelty stop, and who is allowed to restart it?**

### ACT I — SOMETHING IS SEEDED (Levels 1–5)
**Region:** Mid-Strata, Rim, or the Wastes (pick the Cohort's home).
**Surface plot:** a Bloom is eating a colony's food chain (run *The Bloom of Ash*, Ch. 12).
**Underneath:** blooms in this era should be *impossible* — nothing new can breed. Every clue is a seed of the truth:
- the bloom's organisms carry a **Serum signature**: a sequence pattern that predates the Closing;
- whoever seeded it is **harvesting the bloom** behind the Cohort, systematically (find the harvest-site: Pristine Sequences stacked like cordwood);
- a Warden register already had the bloom's chemistry *before* it appeared, dated nine Great Cycles ago.
**Act I bosses:** a Bloom Matriarch (tier 2 Catastrophe), a harvest crew of Reclaimers who are not Ash, and a Curator-adjacent anomaly that only watches.
**Reveal to hand them:** a vial-shaped absence. Something was poured here.
**Ends when:** the Cohort can name who seeded the bloom, or has the vial.

### ACT II — THE ERA OF CLOSING (Levels 6–12)
**Region:** Foundry Belt, Ribbon Bridges, the Vacuum Abyss, the Nucleoid Approaches.
**Plot:** three powers want the same thing — the record of the Closing — and each will hire the Cohort:
- the **Hegemony** wants the Serum recipe (to print it, licence it, and end novelty permanently on its own terms);
- the **Deep Choir** wants the *reason* it stopped (they believe the world was promised a second drop and was lied to);
- the **Sequence Underground** wants the record destroyed (because the record proves who consented).
**Stops:** Vault Nine (the chemical archive of extinct lineages, where a Serum sample is *stored as a person*); the Ninth Archive; the Unfinished Span (built by something that stopped when the Closing started); the Quiet Gate.
**Act II bosses:** Foundry Mother Hush's unfinished print (a Catastrophe that is *one of the Cohort's own lineage*, unlicensed); a Curator-escort (unwinnable, survivable — it takes a sample and leaves); the Third Vigil, if the Cohort became Bound to the Wardens in Act I.
**Reveal:** at the Closing, a **consent** was given: some organism or coalition *asked* for the Serum to stop, and the Sacred Code is the receipt. The Ninth Archive names them. The name is a lineage. The lineage is one of the Cohort's.
**Ends when:** the Cohort holds the receipt, or has destroyed it, or has become the thing the Curators protect.

### ACT III — THE MEMBRANE (Levels 13–20)
**Region:** Peri-Membrane Rim, the Listening Wall, the Nucleoid Core, everywhere that will have them.
**Plot:** the membrane is not a wall. It is a **cell wall**, and the Expanse is one compartment in a larger body whose chemistry the Cohort has no word for. The Listening Wall's answer is not a message: it is a **gradient**, and it has been slowly equalising for nine Great Cycles. Something outside is *using up the world's difference*.
**Factions' final positions:**
- Hegemony: print a Second Drop and seal the membrane behind it (survival as a factory).
- Wardens: close the Nine Arches forever, and lise anyone outside (survival as a fortress).
- Choir: answer the gradient with a Drop of the world's own — a wholesale, uncontrolled novelty event (survival as a mutation, and millions die).
- Underground: open the membrane and let the world become something else entirely (survival is the wrong question).
- Curators: they have done this before. There are other vesicles. They are *counting* this one.
**Act III bosses:** Curator manifestations (Apotheotic thresholds, Core Clock 5); the **Catabolic Tide** itself (a region-wide entropy hazard with a BP line); whichever faction the Cohort betrayed.
**Four endings** (choose by what the table actually does; see Core XII for Apotheosis consequences):
1. **The Sealed World** — the Cohort survives, novelty ends, the Expanse stabilises at a lower ceiling. Everyone keeps their scars. The next generation will never Grow past Level 10.
2. **The Second Drop** — the world changes permanently: new lineages, new domains, new catastrophes. The Cohort are the ancestors of the next era. Apotheosis marks on everyone.
3. **The Answered Gradient** — the membrane opens; the Expanse is absorbed by the larger body. Bittersweet, cosmically strange, and the final scene is one organism describing what it finally *tastes*.
4. **The Refusal** — the Cohort rejects all four options and does something the Director did not write. **This is the intended ending.** Write it with them in the last ten minutes of the last session, out loud, and it will be the best twenty minutes of your year.

### Running the spine without railroading
The three acts are **questions**, not scenes. Each Act can be completed by any path the table invents. Your only obligations: (a) the bloom's Serum signature exists somewhere findable; (b) three powers want the Closing record and will pay; (c) the Core Clock always runs. If the Cohort ignores the spine for six sessions, let them — the Tide and the Clocks keep moving, and the spine will come to them wearing a Warden's face.

# 9 · SITES, TRAPS, AND CHEMISTRY PUZZLES

## 9.1 The five-room site

Any vesicle complex, wreck, grove, or foundry wing is five rooms plus a spine. Build it in ten minutes:

1. **Approach** — a toll, a hazard, or a scent. (What does the site want from you before you enter?)
2. **First room, wrong** — a space that has changed purpose: a nursery that is now a store, a store that is now a grave.
3. **The problem room** — the site's actual function, still running: a pump, a register, a print line, a digestion vat.
4. **The cost room** — what the site's function has been consuming. (This is where the horror is.)
5. **The yield room** — the thing that makes the trip worth it, defended by the site's owner or by the site itself.
**Spine:** one *flow* that connects them (current, drainage, cable, sound). Say it aloud at the start: every good site has a direction.

| **Codon** | killing, surviving, objectives | Growth (Levels) | the campaign's pace. Never give it for shopping. |
| **Substrate (S)** | selling Specimens, tolls, contracts, salvage | gear, grafts, upkeep, colony assets | the cost of being equipped. Watch it: it is the only axis where you can take things away. |
| **Specimens** C/G/P/T | harvesting the dead | gear crafting, Bursts (AE), Deep Synthesis | the reason the Cohort lingers at a battlefield. Give them bodies worth looting. |
| **Standing / Oaths / Cohesion** | choosing, keeping promises, rites | passage, services, legal personhood, *information* | the reward that turns a murder-hobo Cohort into a political one. |

**Rule:** a session should pay in **at least three** of the four. A session that pays only Codon produces characters who are powerful and friendless, which is a valid genre but not usually the one the players signed up for.

## 10.2 The equipment check

If you want to know whether your Cohort is under- or over-geared, compare against this (per PC, cumulative Substrate *earned*, before upkeep and sinks):

| At Level | Earned total | Should own, realistically |
| --: | --: | :-- |
| **5** | ~280 S | one good Apparatus (20–60), one graft (5–60), 4–6 Vials, a pack, a loan outstanding |
| **10** | ~1,080 S | Rare Apparatus (75–200), 2 grafts (one Rare), a vessel share or colony niche, a licence, an AE supply |
| **15** | ~3,530 S | Very Rare gear (250–600), 3 grafts, a Relic *conversation*, a Retainer, a colony asset, real debt |
| **20** | ~11,180 S | Epic/Legendary (800–3,000+), 4 grafts, a Named status somewhere, and a Gene-Debt that has collectors |

Above the line: **take something away** (licence audit, Corrodes, a rival buys first, upkeep climbs, a colony taxes the asset). Below the line: hand them a **hoard** (Core X §10.2) or a Specimen stack — under-geared Cohorts die to soak, not to damage.

## 10.5 Gene-Debt and its collectors

A **Gene-Debt** (Core §9.7) is a Relic's price written into your own sequence: it can be *bequeathed*, *traded*, and *collected*. Track debts on the Cohort sheet as a clock of 4 segments. Advance when: a Tide passes without a payment, the Cohort uses the Relic's Rule Break, or a collector makes contact.

**d10 — who comes for the debt**

| d10 | Collector | What they want |
| --: | :-- | :-- |
| 1 | a Hegemony audit-crew (Immunan squad) | the Relic, or 40% of its value, plus a licence fee |
| 2 | the debt's **heir** — someone to whom the debt was legally left | to be handed the Relic, politely, in public |
| 3 | a **Reclaimers' broker** | to buy the debt at 60% and become very pleasant about it |
| 4 | a **Sequence Underground forger** | to swap the debt onto a dead organism (illegal, effective, and a Clock) |
| 5 | the **Warden register** | the debt is now a *condition of entry*: no arches until paid |
| 6 | a **Deep Choir listener** | to sing the debt aloud in a colony, which makes it *social* instead of legal |
| 7 | the Relic's **previous owner**, still alive | it wants the Relic back and will take a finger chemistry as interest |
| 8 | the **Curators** (Core Clock 4+) | not payment. A *measurement*. Something is learned about the Cohort that cannot be unlearned. |
| 9 | a **rival Cohort** who bought the debt as leverage | a favour, and the favour is a crime |

**At 4 segments:** the debt becomes **Metabolic**: the Relic's Minor Cost doubles, and any Long rest in a registered colony triggers an arrest attempt.

## 10.6 The social price of harvesting

Everything in the Expanse is harvestable, and nothing is harvestable *everywhere*. Make the Cohort choose where its loot comes from:

| You harvested | Who objects | Consequence |
| :-- | :-- | :-- |
| a protected nursery | Flow Guilds, Wardens, the Order Clock | Order Clock +2; a lawful bounty; also: the region's food chain thins (Recession) |
| a registered person | Wardens, Ash, their family | murder under Precept 1; the Argument can be invoked against the Cohort |
| an unlicensed body (the Ash's property) | Reclaimers' Ash | harvest-rights dispute (§11.5); Ash standing −1 rung; bodies start going missing from *your* camps |
| a colony's own dead (legally) | nobody — and that is the horror | the colony thrives, and owes you, and expects you to do it again |
| a living organism (graft-by-force) | everyone with a law, and the Sequence Underground | Body Sovereignty crime; Core Clock +1 if the technique used Nucleoid material |
| a Relic-bearer | the debt's holders (§10.5) | the debt transfers to whoever harvested. Read that twice before rolling Harvest. |
| an Anomaly | physics | the specimen does not behave. Roll d6: it grows, it copies, it hums, it is warm, it is counted, it is not there in the morning. |

## 10.7 Breaking gear honestly

Players remember the fights where they *lost something*. Do it fairly:

| Method | Works on | Fair warning |
| :-- | :-- | :-- |
| **Corrodes** (Lytic Acidic surge, Bony Flail) | grafts (−1 soak until repaired) | the creature's print says Acidic; the Mucoid family is immune |
| **Overcharge** (an enemy Smith) | Apparatus (durability 2d6 ≤6 destroys) | an enemy Smith tuning its weapon is a telegraphed action |
| **Licence seizure** (Hegemony) | any registered item | they show the clause. They always show the clause. |
| **Desiccation / Cold field** | Vials and liquid goods | a Dry Zone is announced the Cycle before |
| **The Tide** | colony assets, vessels, stored Specimens | a Season of Hypertonic Zenith breaks anything improperly stored |

**Always offer a route to repair or replacement** — the Hegemony, the Ash, or a Smith with opinions. Gear loss is a Vector generator, not a punishment.

## 10.8 Twelve sinks that create stories

1. **Upkeep** (Symbiotes 2–10 S/Beat (Core §9.6), assets 5 S/Beat, Retainers 5–20 S/Beat)
2. **Licence fees** for gear and for your own Name
3. **Toll arches** and Guild passage
4. **Harvest rights** purchased before you loot (the legal way)
5. **Neutralisers, Ampules, AE** (consumables; AE is 60–90 S each and the Cohort always needs one more)
6. **Colony assets** (workshop, vat, nursery — cheap to build, expensive to keep, worth stealing)
7. **Oaths** (a rite costs something real; keeping it costs more)
8. **Debts and interest** (5 S/Beat, and the Hegemony compounds)
9. **A vessel** (a drift-skiff: 400–1,200 S, crewed, toll-paying, and *somebody's* property)
10. **Graft re-tuning** (Smiths charge 10% and a Specimen)
11. **Funerals** (the Ash will not do it free, and the Cohort will pay anyway)
12. **Bribes** (never legal, always effective, always a Clock)

# 12 · THE BLOOM OF ASH
### A ready-to-run Vector for four organisms, Levels 1 → 5

**Region:** the Cytoplasmic Mid-Strata, at the edge of the Lysosomal Wastes.
**Tone:** investigation that becomes a siege that becomes a decision.
**Spine link:** this is Act I of *The Long Argument* (§8.5). If you are not running a campaign, it still works — and the last scene will make your players ask for one.

## 12.1 The situation, in one paragraph

The colony of **Second Flow** (14,000 organisms, four outer farming rings, one water tally) is being eaten by a Bloom. The Bloom's organisms are not in any register, not in any Bestiary order the wardens can name cleanly, and — this is the thing nobody says out loud — **they are new**. Nothing has been new since the Closing. Something is pouring a *Serum signature* into the outer rings, and the bloom is what happens when a closed world is reminded that it used to be open.

Three powers want this kept quiet: the **Warden Circles** (because a bloom proves their registers are incomplete), the **Flow Guilds** (because the bloom is eating a toll channel), and a **Hegemony foundry office** (because they know what the signature is). One power wants it known: the **Deep Choir**. The Cohort will be hired by someone, and it will not be the right someone.

## 12.2 Faces

| Name | Role | Block | What they want | What they will hide |
| :-- | :-- | :-- | :-- | :-- |
| **Tally-Keeper Ossune** | Second Flow's water council | Colonist, AFF-led; Argument available | the bloom gone before the water tally fails and the colony defaults | the council *sold* the outer rings' biomass to a Guild three Tides ago; the bloom is eating sold goods |
| **Reader-Second Vash** (niece of Reader-Grandmother Vash) | Ash field-reader at the Ash Rows | Lysosan Viral Mimic, Level 5 (Standard + Camouflage) | a true reading of the bloom's dead, and the Ash's claim on them | the readings keep coming back with the *same* chemical phrase, and it is a Hegemony serial mark |
| **Porter-Third Yune** | Warden gate escort assigned to the Cohort | Immunan Phagocyte Guard, Level 5 (Standard, Elite if you want a hard fight) | to *observe*, not to fight; the Wardens will not act without a register match | the register already matched, nine Great Cycles ago. The Wardens have known for a Tide. |
| **Nine Fathoms** | Chartwright, hired by the Guilds | Flagellan Turbulence Navigator, Level 4 (Standard) | one true observation of the bloom's spread rate | the bloom is spreading *against* the current, and it is following a line — a pipe |
| **The Keeper of the Deep** | a nursery-tender in the outer ring, with 400 juveniles | Colonist, non-combat, Oath boxes 3 | to keep the juveniles alive, by any means | they have been feeding the bloom. It *listens* to them. They are not evil; they are desperate and curious. |
| **Vell of Shift Nine** | the seeder: a Ribozyme Engineer, exiled, funded quietly by the Hegemony's Brine Office | Epithalian Ribozyme Engineer, Level 7 (Elite) | to prove that novelty can be made again, and to be *credited* for it | the bloom is a **failure**: it cannot be controlled, and Vell knows it, and is too proud to stop |

**Vell is the adventure.** Everything before Act 3 is Vell's evidence; everything after is what the table does with a person who broke the world's oldest law out of *vanity and love of the work*. Let them be brilliant, let them be right about the science, and let them be terrified of what they made.

## 12.3 Five sites

**1 · THE ASH ROWS (Second Flow's licensed dead).** A quiet quarter of hanging corpse-husks, read for their last hours. Vash's crew is working a bloom-kill.
*Play it:* a **reading scene**. The Cohort may pay (25 S) or help (3 Cycles of labour) for a read. The read gives: the serial phrase **"print-run nine, unlisted"**, and the dead organism's last sight — a pipe mouth in the outer ring, pouring something glowing.
*Danger:* **Detritus Feeders** have arrived with the corpses. 3 Feeders (3 BSP). They ignore the Cohort unless the Cohort is standing near a body. Yune will not start a fight in a licensed ground.

**2 · THE OUTER RING (the bloom's edge).** Farmland of cultivated Drifter ropes, half of it *wrong*: too fat, too fast, too new.
*Play it:* a **survey**. Chemoreception and Synthesis contests (AT 12/14) reveal: the bloom's tissue carries a sequence pattern with **no terminal codon decay** — it is new, measurably new. Also a **pipe**: a Hegemony print-line feed, unmarked, running from the deep.
*Danger:* 1 Gnaw Swarm + 1 Cytoplasmic Bleeder (5 BSP, Easy) — and the *real* problem is that killing the bloom here **spreads its spores** (advance the Bloom Clock by 1: violence makes it worse. This is the adventure's thesis, taught mechanically in the first session.)

**3 · THE PUMP STATION OF ANCHOR'S REACH.** The pipe runs to a pump station held by a Guild-hired security crew — three **Protease Wardens** (Elite, and the Wardens will *insist* they are lawful: they have a Hegemony print-licence for the pump) and four **Antigen Gnats**.
*Play it:* the DM Codex §4.6 worked build, verbatim, including the antigen arch and the Cycle-8 pump flush. **This is the Hard fight of the adventure** and it should happen at Level 4–5.
*The lever:* the pump can be **jammed** (Synthesis AT 14, 20 S Substrate) instead of the crew defeated — the Wardens then have a lawful reason to arrest their own employers, and Vell's cover starts to crack.

**4 · NURSERY DEEP.** The keeper's domain: 400 juveniles in protected protein cradles, and the keeper's long conversation with the bloom.
*Play it:* **no combat.** The keeper shows the Cohort that the bloom's organisms *obey a written rule* they can repeat, and that they came up through the pipe, "singing the same note". If the Cohort is hostile, the keeper triggers the cradles to release the juveniles *into* the bloom — a moral catastrophe, not a fight.
*What they can get:* the **Bloom Chorus** (a Broadcast pattern that can *call the bloom back* along its pipe line for 2 Beats — the Act 3 lever), and a choice: do they protect the nursery (Warden/Hegemony legal cover for the keeper) or expose it (the keeper is lysed under Precept 2, and the Cohort carried it there)?

**5 · THE PRINT-CELLAR.** Beneath the pump station: a working print line, nine Great Cycles of old Hegemony hardware, one Engineer, and the thing the run was building — a **Bloom Matriarch**, rooted, and a swarm of **Protease Locusts** filling the ceiling.

## 12.4 Act 3: the cellar, run as three Cycles of choice

**Encounter (Tier 2, Deadly for a Level 5 Cohort of four):** Bloom Matriarch (10 BSP) + 3 Protease Locust swarmlings (3) + 4 Cytoplasmic Bleeders (8) = **21 BSP**.
Do not run this as "kill everything". The Matriarch's Brood Sac is the fight: **cut it** (Strike at AT 16, a Breacher's charge, or 20 S of acid poured into the sac) and it loses its spawning phase. The print line can be **destroyed** (integrity 40) or **seized** (Vell will surrender to a Cohort that can name the crime).

**The three endings, and what each costs:**

| Ending | How | Consequence |
| :-- | :-- | :-- |
| **Seal it** | destroy the line, hand Vell to the Wardens, let the bloom starve | Second Flow survives; the Wardens take the Serum signature and *bury it*; the Cohort is paid 400 S and made **Bound** to two factions who now both consider them a leak. The Deep Choir sings at the Cohort's name. |
| **Publish it** | carry proof to a Warden arch *and* a Choir shrine *and* a Guild chart-hall | the Expanse learns that novelty can be made. **Core Clock +2 immediately.** Every faction opens a Serum Vector. Act II begins, and it begins with the Cohort famous. |
| **Finish the work** | take the print line, take Vell's templates, and *try better* | the Cohort has a Serum signature and a moral horizon they will never get back. Advance the Colony Clock to fill: Second Flow's next bloom is theirs. |

**Vell's own ending is up to the table.** They will not beg. If asked what they wanted, they say: *"I wanted to be the one who asked the world to grow again."* Whatever happens to them, the print run's nine-cycle date is now in evidence — and the Closing's record (Act II) has a name attached to it.

## 13.2 Instant conversion: any creature in sixty seconds

1. **One sentence → Tier + Role.** Match the Cohort's level band (Tier) and decide how dangerous it should feel: nuisance = Swarmling, threat = Standard, named = Elite, event = Catastrophe. Read the numbers off BEST-01's template table.
2. **Choose the Order by its *verb*, not its appearance.** Push → V. Dissolve → I. Recognise/cancel → VI. Rewrite/possess → IV. Hold ground/build → II. Eat/filter/flee → III. Break a rule → VII. Then steal **one Special** from the closest printed block.
3. **Print one weakness the players can find.** Say it in the first Cycle of the fight. That is the whole craft: a Bestiary entry is a *puzzle with MI*.

*Worked example.* "A thing that hides in foam and spits acid." Tier 2 Standard, Order I (dissolve) → Enzymatic Horror's numbers (MI 30, soak 2, BD 3 Lytic, pool 5d, MT 14, PP 3), steal Acidic Surge, give it Concealment in Turbid/foam terrain, and print the weakness: **foam is drying air — a Dry Zone or Thermal effect strips its cover and halves its Surge.** Done. Twenty seconds, calibrated, and the Cohort can beat it.
